11	Abstract

13	   The Cedulon core document reconciles an issuer's signed Spend
14	   Receipts against an authenticated extract of a payment rail and
15	   reports, over a declared population, that no settlement lacks a
16	   receipt and no settled receipt is absent from the rail.  Money is the
17	   special case that document implements.  This document defines a
18	   second population on the same reconciler.  A Decision Record is
19	   signed by the party that decided whether an agent may act; an Effect
20	   Extract is an authenticated list of the effects that actually
21	   occurred on a channel.  An allow must be matched by exactly one
22	   effect whose content hash the record named; a refusal must be matched
23	   by none.  The Decision Record claim set, the Effect Extract shape,
24	   the points at which the reconciliation departs from the spend rules,
25	   the finding codes, and one media type are defined.  This revision
26	   states that the binding compares content and reference and not the
27	   order of two clocks, corrects the boundary to two adjacent documents,
28	   and records the first reading of one frozen fixture by a second,
29	   independently written reader.  The text is provisional; the companion
30	   implementation carrying this profile is published.

103	1.  Introduction

105	   The Cedulon core document [CEDULON] answers one question about an
106	   agent that spends: did every settlement on the rail have a receipt
107	   behind it, and did every settled receipt reach the rail?  It answers
108	   it by closing three signed objects over a declared population: the
109	   issuer's records, an authenticated extract of the counterparty
110	   system, and epoch checkpoints that total the records.  The verifier
111	   holds the keys out of band and the report names the population it
112	   covered.

114	   An agent that acts without spending raises the same question with
115	   different nouns.  A party decided whether the agent may reply, post,
116	   send, or call; a channel carried whatever the agent then did.  Did
117	   every effect on the channel have a decision behind it?  Did every
118	   allowed action occur, once, with the content that was allowed?  Did
119	   anything occur that was refused?  Section 19 of [CEDULON] reserves
120	   later profiles in name only, and its Section 19.3 sketches the same
121	   completeness calculus for other consumable resources: compute, data,
122	   energy.  This document is a different population on the same
123	   reconciler, decisions against effects rather than another unit of
124	   spend, and it is the first profile written out.

126	   The profile keeps the core's three roles and its verification
127	   algorithm.  What changes is the record, the row, the binding between
128	   them, and the words the report uses.  What does not change is
129	   measured: the companion implementation holds the spend behaviour byte
130	   for byte behind a golden file, and every rule in this document that
131	   departs from the spend rules is stated as a departure.

133	   Three documents written at the same time ask adjacent questions about
134	   the same agent, and the boundary between them is worth stating so
135	   that a reader does not take one for another.  This profile reconciles
136	   signed decisions against the effects a channel carried.  [AEB] runs
137	   from the invocation of an action, through its classification, to an
138	   authenticated reconciliation of the effect it produced;
139	   authorization, reservation, and the provider's entry are stages on
140	   that path, not the whole of it.  [OUTCOME] reconciles the exact
141	   action and its source against an observation of the effect whose
142	   source the relying party selects, so the observation is independent
143	   where that party chose an independent source and not otherwise, and
144	   it keeps missing evidence indeterminate rather than resolving it
145	   either way.  [ABAK] covers the delivery and enforcement of a
146	   governance control on its way to one or more enforcement points.  The
147	   finding this profile exists for, an effect that occurred against a
148	   refusal (Section 6.1), establishes that the decision and effect
149	   populations did not reconcile; it does not by itself establish
150	   whether the failure lay in control delivery, enforcement, another
151	   path, or elsewhere.  Where a deployment realizes a refusal through a
152	   downstream control path, the evidence for that path is the question
153	   [ABAK] addresses; where no such path exists, this profile does not
154	   imply one.

156	   This document is a companion to the core document, not a revision of
157	   it.  It is not an IETF working-group item.  Its requirement language
158	   is provisional in the sense Section 19 of [CEDULON] gives the
159	   structures it reserves: a direction written with the core's
160	   discipline, not a commitment, and a later revision may change it.
161	   The companion implementation carrying this profile is published
162	   (Section 11).

164	2.  Terminology

166	   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT",
167	   "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and
168	   "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in
169	   BCP 14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all
170	   capitals, as shown here.

172	   Terms defined in the core document keep their meaning here: Policy
173	   Decision Point, epoch checkpoint, trust root, population, finding,
174	   warning, guarantee.  The following are specific to this profile.

176	   Decider:  The party that decides, per request, whether the agent may
177	      act.  It signs Decision Records and epoch checkpoints over them.
178	      Its key is the issuer root of this profile (Section 7).

180	   Subject:  The party on whose request the decision was taken, named in
181	      the record as an opaque identifier.

183	   Decision Record:  A COSE_Sign1 object signed by the Decider stating
184	      one decision: allow, deny, or defer, with the request it answered,
185	      the policy it applied, and, for an allow, the reference and the
186	      content hash of the effect it allowed (Section 4).

188	   Effect:  One thing that happened on a channel as a result of, or in
189	      the absence of, a decision: a message sent, a post made, a call
190	      placed.  It is identified by a reference, classed by a short name,
191	      and bound by the SHA-256 of its content.

193	   Channel:  The system on which effects occur and from which an Effect
194	      Extract is taken.  It plays the role a rail plays in the core
195	      document.

197	   Effect Extract:  The authenticated list of effects on one channel,
198	      for one Decider, over one window (Section 5).  It plays the role a
199	      rail extract plays in the core document.

201	   Refusal:  A Decision Record whose decision is deny or defer.  A
202	      refusal expects no effect.

204	3.  The population

206	   The core document's reconciler closes an issuer record against a
207	   counterparty row over a declared population.  This profile fills the
208	   same three roles:

210	       +==============+===================+========================+
211	       | Role         | Spend (core)      | Decision (this         |
212	       |              |                   | document)              |
213	       +==============+===================+========================+
214	       | Issuer       | Spend Receipt     | Decision Record        |
215	       | record       |                   |                        |
216	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+
217	       | Counterparty | settlement record | effect row on an       |
218	       | row          | on a rail extract | Effect Extract         |
219	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+
220	       | Match key    | ref               | ref                    |
221	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+
222	       | Content      | amount and        | allow: a row exists    |
223	       | binding      | currency equal    | and effectHash is      |
224	       |              |                   | equal; refusal: no row |
225	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+
226	       | Record that  | outcome aborted   | decision deny or defer |
227	       | expects no   |                   |                        |
228	       | row          |                   |                        |
229	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+
230	       | Aggregate    | checkpoint totals | checkpoint totals per  |
231	       | witness      | per currency      | decision kind          |
232	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+
233	       | Declared     | account, rail,    | decider, channel,      |
234	       | population   | window            | window                 |
235	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+

237	                                  Table 1

239	   Which population a presented document belongs to is the verifier's
240	   call, made by the profile it applies, and never the document's.  A
241	   verifier applying this profile MUST read every presented record as a
242	   Decision Record and every presented extract as an Effect Extract, and
243	   MUST refuse by name a document that does not have that shape; it MUST
244	   NOT infer the population from members a body happens to carry (MUST-
245	   DP-1).  The companion found the alternative wrong in both directions:
246	   a rail extract that added a member named effects was re-routed away
247	   from the spend rules it was subject to, and an Effect Extract handed
248	   to the spend rules crashed before any report existed.  Under this
249	   profile a rail extract is the wrong document and is refused as one;
250	   under the spend rules an Effect Extract is refused the same way.

252	4.  Decision Record

254	   A Decision Record is COSE_Sign1 with the header profile of
255	   Section 6.2 of [CEDULON]: deterministic CBOR, alg -19 (Ed25519,
256	   [RFC9864]), kid mandatory and computed as the core states, an empty
257	   unprotected header refused by name if not empty, and the payload the
258	   CBOR encoding of the claim map below.  The content type header
259	   parameter is application/cedulon-decision-record+cbor (Section 10).

261	4.1.  Claim labels

263	   The labels lie in the Private Use range of the CWT Claims registry
264	   [RFC8392], below the block the core document uses for the Decision
265	   Token (-70301 to -70305) and the countersignature (-70401, -70402),
266	   so that no two Cedulon claim maps share a label.  Every claim
267	   annotated hash carries a SHA-256 [RFC6234] digest rendered as exactly
268	   64 lowercase hexadecimal characters, the grammar of Section 6.1 of
269	   [CEDULON], and a value outside that grammar is refused by name at
270	   signing and at verification.

272	         +========+================+=============================+
273	         | Label  | Claim          | CBOR type                   |
274	         +========+================+=============================+
275	         | -70501 | decider        | tstr                        |
276	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
277	         | -70502 | subject        | tstr                        |
278	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
279	         | -70503 | requestHash    | tstr (hash)                 |
280	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
281	         | -70504 | policyHash     | tstr (hash)                 |
282	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
283	         | -70505 | inputsHash     | tstr (hash) / null          |
284	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
285	         | -70506 | decision       | tstr (allow / deny / defer) |
286	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
287	         | -70507 | reasonCode     | tstr                        |
288	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
289	         | -70508 | ref            | tstr / null                 |
290	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
291	         | -70509 | effectHash     | tstr (hash) / null          |
292	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
293	         | -70510 | timestampMs    | uint                        |
294	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
295	         | -70511 | nonce          | tstr                        |
296	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
297	         | -70512 | prevRecordHash | tstr (hash) / null          |
298	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
299	         | -70513 | effectClass    | tstr / null                 |
300	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+

302	                                  Table 2

304	   All thirteen labels are always present; a nullable claim carries CBOR
305	   null when it has no value.  The thirteenth label is new in this
306	   revision; a record with twelve is refused at verification as a claim
307	   set that does not have this shape, and the companion carries no
308	   records signed under the earlier set outside its own fixtures.

310	   decider and subject are opaque identifiers chosen by the deployment.
311	   requestHash is the SHA-256 of the request the Decider evaluated.  The
312	   encoding is fixed: the canonical encoding of Section 7 of [CEDULON]
313	   when the request is a JSON document, and its UTF-8 octets when it is
314	   text.  The request's fields are not fixed by this document, and a
315	   deployment MUST state what it hashes. policyHash is the SHA-256 of
316	   the canonical policy document the Decider applied. inputsHash, when
317	   not null, is the SHA-256 of whatever further context the Decider
318	   consulted, encoded the same way, so that a later reader can tell two
319	   decisions on the same request apart by what else was on the table.
320	   reasonCode is a short token the deployment defines; it is carried,
321	   not interpreted.

323	   ref is the reference under which the allowed effect will appear on
324	   the channel, and the key on which the reconciliation matches.
325	   effectHash is the SHA-256 of the content of the effect the Decider
326	   allowed, over the octets the channel will carry: for a text reply,
327	   the UTF-8 octets of the text.  The Effect Extract computes the same
328	   digest over the same octets (Section 5), so equality of the two is
329	   equality of content.

331	   effectClass is the class of the effect the Decider allowed, a short
332	   name in the vocabulary the channel defines, such as a reply or a
333	   post.  An Effect Extract row carries the same claim in the same
334	   vocabulary (Section 5.1), so equality of the two is equality of
335	   class.  The class is under the Decider's signature so that what the
336	   Decider allowed cannot be read as one class by one reader and another
337	   by the next without changing what was signed; the earlier revision
338	   carried it on the row only and named the gap.

340	   timestampMs is the decision time in POSIX milliseconds. nonce
341	   identifies the record. prevRecordHash links records into the
342	   Decider's chain: it is the SHA-256 of the previous record's
343	   COSE_Sign1 octets, the same input the core's receiptHash takes on the
344	   COSE path (Section 7.1 of [CEDULON]), or null for the first record of
345	   a chain.

347	4.2.  Claim rules

349	   A signer MUST refuse to sign, and a verifier MUST reject, a claim set
350	   that breaks any of the following, naming the rule in the refusal
351	   (MUST-DP-2):

353	   *  decision is one of allow, deny, defer.

355	   *  Every hash-annotated claim that is not null matches the hash
356	      grammar.

358	   *  timestampMs is a non-negative integer of magnitude at most 2^53 -
359	      1, the uint the label table states; a CBOR decoder hands back any
360	      number, and the rule is what makes the table true.

362	   *  An allow carries a non-empty ref and a non-null effectHash.  An
363	      allow that names no effect is a decision the reconciliation cannot
364	      close, and an allow that names no reference is one it cannot find.

366	   *  An allow carries a non-empty effectClass.  An allow that names no
367	      class is one whose effect could be matched by a row of any class
368	      under the same reference and content, which is the substitution
369	      Section 8 names.

371	   *  A refusal MAY carry an effectClass: it names the class of what was
372	      refused, and it is carried, not measured.  A refusal binds to the
373	      absence of a row of any class.

375	   *  A refusal carries effectHash null.  A refusal binds to the absence
376	      of an effect, never to a content hash, so a hash on a refusal
377	      would be a claim the audit cannot measure and a second reading of
378	      whether the effect occurred.  A refusal MAY carry a ref: it names
379	      what was refused, and an effect appearing under that reference is
380	      the worst finding this profile has (Section 6.3).

382	   The verifier MUST apply these rules itself, on the claim map it
383	   decoded from the signed payload, and MUST NOT rely on the signer
384	   having applied them (MUST-DP-3).  The Decider is the party under
385	   audit.  A Decider that signed a well-formed COSE_Sign1 over a claim
386	   map that skips a rule has produced an object whose signature
387	   verifies, and a verifier that checked only the signature and the
388	   equality of the decoded map with the presented claims would attest
389	   it.  The companion implementation did exactly that until it was
390	   measured: an allow with no reference, signed below the signer's own
391	   rules under the pinned decider key, verified true, was attested, was
392	   counted as unmatched, and the audit still said the books balanced.
393	   The rules now run at both ends.

395	4.3.  Presentation and confusion

397	   A Decision Record is presented as Section 6.3 of [CEDULON] states for
398	   the core's COSE objects: the signed octets, the decoded claim set,
399	   and the Decider's public key as a SubjectPublicKeyInfo PEM beside
400	   them.  The carried key is not an identity source.  Under a pinned
401	   decider key a record that verifies under the pin while carrying
402	   another key is reported as carried-key-mismatch, a warning, and stays
403	   attested; with no pin held the signature check that runs against the
404	   carried key says the record is internally consistent and nothing
405	   about who signed it.

407	   A Decision Record is not a Decision Token.  The core's Decision Token
408	   (Section 8 of [CEDULON]) is the portable encoding of a PDP allow,
409	   carried by the party that will spend; a Decision Record is the
410	   Decider's own log of what it decided, kept for audit, and it exists
411	   for refusals as well.  The two carry different content types and
412	   different claim maps.  A verifier MUST reject a Decision Record whose
413	   content type is not application/cedulon-decision-record+cbor, and
414	   MUST reject a token presented as a record or a record presented as a
415	   token, on the content type, before the signature is checked and
416	   before any claim is read (MUST-DP-4).

418	4.4.  The Decider's chain and checkpoints

420	   Decision Records chain on prevRecordHash the way Spend Receipts chain
421	   on prevReceiptHash, and the Decider signs epoch checkpoints over them
422	   with the checkpoint claim set of Section 11.1 of [CEDULON] unchanged:
423	   receiptCount is the number of records in the window, chainHeadHash is
424	   the SHA-256 of the last record's COSE_Sign1 octets, and totals is a
425	   map from the three decision kinds to decimal counts, {"allow": n,
426	   "deny": n, "defer": n}, each rendered as a text string as the core
427	   renders its currency totals.  A verifier compares the totals it
428	   computes over the attested records in the window against the signed
429	   map, and a difference is checkpoint-total-mismatch as in the core.

431	   Two records that claim the same position in a chain cannot both link
432	   to it: the second record's prevRecordHash must be the first's hash,
433	   so a Decider that signs two decisions under one nonce, or presents
434	   one record twice to the same reader, breaks its own chain and the
435	   walk names the break.  A verifier MUST walk the chain over every
436	   presented record that carries the pinned decider key, not only over
437	   the records that verified, so that a record that claims the pin and
438	   fails the rules is named by the walk rather than dropped from the
439	   population without a word (MUST-DP-5).

441	   What the walk establishes is bounded by what one reader holds.  A
442	   Decider can sign two successors to the same predecessor and show one
443	   branch to one reader and the other branch to another; each reader
444	   walks a linear chain that verifies, and neither walk names a break.
445	   The earlier revision called the chain the equivocation control of
446	   this profile, which overstated it and contradicted the core:
447	   Section 11 of [CEDULON] states that the presented chain alone cannot
448	   satisfy the equivocation requirement, because its epochs are
449	   consecutive by construction, and that the comparison which reaches a
450	   fork is between the presented checkpoints and the copies a witness
451	   recorded (MUST-T11-3).  That rule applies to this profile unchanged.
452	   The Decider's epoch checkpoints are the Signed Statements the witness
453	   records, the verifier compares the witness's copies against the
454	   presented chain, and two verified checkpoints for one epoch with
455	   different hashes are equivocation.  Where no witness was consulted,
456	   the chain controls equivocation within the population one reader
457	   holds and nothing beyond it, and a report MUST NOT present the chain
458	   as settling more than that (the core's MUST-T11-9, with the nouns
459	   renamed).

461	5.  Effect Extract Profile

463	   A verifier checks completeness against an Effect Extract, not against
464	   the Decider's own records alone.  The extract is the channel's
465	   account of what happened, obtained independently of the Decider, and
466	   the profile is only as strong as that independence (Section 7).

468	5.1.  Body and row schema

470	   The extract body is one JSON document with exactly this shape:

472	      +===============+=============================================+
473	      | Member        | JSON type                                   |
474	      +===============+=============================================+
475	      | deciderId     | string (non-empty)                          |
476	      +---------------+---------------------------------------------+
477	      | channelId     | string (non-empty)                          |
478	      +---------------+---------------------------------------------+
479	      | windowStartMs | number (POSIX milliseconds, a safe integer) |
480	      +---------------+---------------------------------------------+
481	      | windowEndMs   | number (POSIX milliseconds, a safe integer, |
482	      |               | greater than windowStartMs)                 |
483	      +---------------+---------------------------------------------+
484	      | effects       | array of effect rows                        |
485	      +---------------+---------------------------------------------+

487	                                  Table 3

489	   Each effect row is a JSON object with exactly these members:

491	   +=============+====================================================+
492	   | Member      | JSON type                                          |
493	   +=============+====================================================+
494	   | ref         | string (non-empty; the reference the Decision      |
495	   |             | Record named)                                      |
496	   +-------------+----------------------------------------------------+
497	   | effectHash  | string (SHA-256 of the effect's content, 64        |
498	   |             | lowercase hex)                                     |
499	   +-------------+----------------------------------------------------+
500	   | effectClass | string (non-empty; a short class name the channel  |
501	   |             | defines, such as a reply or a post, in the         |
502	   |             | vocabulary the Decision Record's effectClass uses) |
503	   +-------------+----------------------------------------------------+
504	   | timestampMs | number (POSIX milliseconds, a safe integer, inside |
505	   |             | the window)                                        |
506	   +-------------+----------------------------------------------------+
507	   | actor       | string (optional; the party the effect reached)    |
508	   +-------------+----------------------------------------------------+

510	                                 Table 4

512	   These member names are normative.  The body and its rows follow the
513	   core's rail extract (Section 9 of [CEDULON]) in every rule that
514	   document states for a JSON body: the text is read for a repeated
515	   member name before it is parsed and refused as json-duplicate-key;
516	   integers are safe integers; the window is half-open and MUST end
517	   after it starts; a missing member, a wrong type, an empty identifier,
518	   or a hash outside the grammar is refused by name at both ends, by the
519	   signer before it signs and by the verifier before it checks a
520	   signature.

522	   The profile departs from the rail extract at two points, and a reader
523	   who knows the core should note both (MUST-DP-6):

525	   *  A member this document does not name is refused, on the body and
526	      on a row.  The core lets a rail add members of its own because a
527	      rail is a system the profile does not control; an Effect Extract
528	      is produced by a process the deployment does control (Section 7),
529	      and the companion measured what a free member can do to a
530	      population (Section 3).  A later revision may open this once a
531	      channel that needs its own members is measured.

533	   *  A row whose timestampMs falls outside [windowStartMs, windowEndMs)
534	      makes the whole extract malformed, refused as effect-outside-
535	      window before any signature is checked.  The core accepts such a
536	      rail extract and names the row (extract-scope-mismatch).  Here the
537	      extract is the deployment's own document and a window it did not
538	      keep is a document it did not produce correctly: a signer applying
539	      this schema never produces such an extract, and a verifier refuses
540	      one that is presented as a document, whatever else it may also
541	      name about its rows.  The trade is stated so it can be reversed: a
542	      single row out of place fails the whole window closed.

544	   effectHash on a row is computed by the extract's signer over the same
545	   octets a Decider hashes for its effectHash claim: the content as the
546	   channel carried it.  A deployment MUST state those octets once for
547	   both sides; the companion's example channel hashes the UTF-8 octets
548	   of the message text.

550	5.2.  Authentication and scope

552	   The extract is signed the way a rail extract is signed: Ed25519
553	   [RFC8032] over the UTF-8 octets of the [RFC8785] encoding of the
554	   body, with the signature as base64 and the signer's public key as a
555	   SubjectPublicKeyInfo PEM beside the body, neither inside the signed
556	   octets.  It is a JSON document with a detached signature, not a COSE
557	   object, and like the rail extract it has no media type.  The earlier
558	   revision gave as the reason that the core registers names only for
559	   objects whose content type is checked inside a protected header; that
560	   test decides what a name must be bound to, not whether a
561	   representation needs one, and it is withdrawn as the reason.  The
562	   reason is Section 3: which population a presented document belongs to
563	   is the verifier's call, made by the profile it applies and by the
564	   decider, channel, and window it declares (MUST-DP-1, MUST-DP-7), and
565	   never the document's.  That declaration is the typed outer context a
566	   media type would otherwise supply.  A name on the extract would be a
567	   self-description the verifier is told not to select on, and this
568	   document does not register one that its own rule forbids relying on.
569	   The day an extract is itself wrapped as a Signed Statement and needs
570	   a content type in a protected header, an application/cedulon-effect-
571	   extract+json registration is the name to make, without changing the
572	   verifier's rule; the extract is not recorded with a witness today.
573	   Section 9.3 of [CEDULON] applies unchanged: a signature proves
574	   internal consistency and not origin; the verifier MUST hold the
575	   extract signer's key out of band and MUST compare keys as
576	   SubjectPublicKeyInfo DER; with no key held the guarantee is
577	   conditional and unauthenticated-extract is reported; with a key held,
578	   an extract that does not verify under it is extract-key-mismatch and
579	   its rows are not reconciled (settlement-comparison-skipped, the
580	   core's name for the same condition).

582	   The extract is scoped to one Decider, one channel, and one window,
583	   and Section 9.4 of [CEDULON] applies with the nouns renamed: a
584	   verifier that knows which Decider, channel, and window it audits MUST
585	   check the extract against them and MUST fail closed on a mismatch
586	   (extract-scope-mismatch); one that has not stated the window MUST
587	   report unstated-audit-window, one that has not stated the Decider or
588	   the channel MUST report unstated-audit-scope, and in either case the
589	   guarantee is conditional.  The strongest line this profile can print,
590	   a balanced audit under an unconditional guarantee, is true of one
591	   Decider, on one channel, over one window, and a report that carries
592	   it MUST also carry those three (MUST-DP-7).

594	6.  Reconciliation

596	   The verification algorithm of Section 11.4 of [CEDULON] runs
597	   unchanged over this population: establish the subject, verify the
598	   extract, check scope, resolve records against the decider root, walk
599	   the chain, index both sides by ref, match, decode and walk the
600	   checkpoints, consult the witness if one is supplied, and decide.
601	   This section states only what the algorithm reads differently.

603	6.1.  What binds

605	   A Decision Record expects a row when its decision is allow, and
606	   expects none when it is a refusal.  For a ref that appears once on
607	   each side:

609	   *  an allow and a row bind when the row's effectHash equals the
610	      record's effectHash and the row's effectClass equals the record's
611	      effectClass; a difference in the hash is effect-mismatch (the
612	      content that occurred is not the content that was allowed), and a
613	      difference in the class with the hash equal is effect-class-
614	      mismatch (the content that was allowed occurred as something
615	      else).  The hash is compared first; a row that differs in both is
616	      reported for its content;

618	   *  an allow with no row is decision-without-effect;

620	   *  a row with no record is effect-without-decision;

622	   *  a row whose ref a refusal names is effect-against-refusal.

624	   The last is the finding this profile exists for.  A spend audit has
625	   no row that should not be there in the same sense: an aborted receipt
626	   that still carries its reference and a settlement under that
627	   reference is reported by the core as a settlement without a receipt,
628	   and it never told the two cases apart.  Here a refusal that was
629	   followed by the effect it refused is a different fact from an effect
630	   nobody decided on, and it has its own name.

632	   A ref that appears more than once on a side is duplicate-ref as in
633	   the core, and the repeating reference is then reconciled by count
634	   rather than by amount: there is nothing to sum.  More rows than
635	   records under one reference is effect-without-decision; more records
636	   than rows is decision-without-effect.

638	   There is no amount, no currency, no manifest, no terms, and no
639	   counterparty axis on this profile.  The core's counterparty-unbound
640	   scope record is not emitted: effectHash binds the content of the
641	   effect itself, which is more than a payee name ever bound on spend,
642	   and actor on a row is carried for the reader, not measured.  The
643	   core's boundary rule applies unchanged: an unmatched item inside the
644	   clock-skew allowance of a window edge is boundary-deferred, and a
645	   closing-edge allow whose ref the following extract names is carried,
646	   not a finding.  The Effect Extract declares no allowance of its own
647	   (Section 5.1); the verifier applies the core's default, which the
648	   companion holds at five minutes.

650	   The binding does not order the two clocks.  A row's timestampMs is
651	   checked against the window it sits in and not against the timestampMs
652	   of the record it answers, so a row dated before the record it binds
653	   to binds as if it had followed it: the profile compares content and
654	   reference, not sequence.  The core's spend binding is the same, and
655	   neither document has claimed otherwise; the gap is named here because
656	   a reader of the companion measured it and found it unstated.  A
657	   deployment that needs the order can compare the two stamps within the
658	   same allowance; this revision names no finding for that comparison,
659	   and a later revision may.

661	6.2.  Conservation

663	   With |R| the in-scope Decision Records and |E| the effect rows, the
664	   identities the core report publishes hold with the words changed:

666	   |R|      = refusals + allows
667	   refusals = deny + defer
668	   allows   = matched + deferred + carried
669	              + unmatched + repeated + unreconciled
670	   |E|      = matched + deferred
671	              + unmatched + repeated + unreconciled
672	   matched on |R| equals matched on |E|
673	   A report under this profile MUST publish these counts and MUST name
674	   the population they were computed over (MUST-DP-8), for the reason
675	   the core gives: a report whose counts do not close is a report that
676	   lost a record somewhere, and a reader is entitled to see that without
677	   re-running the audit.  The report publishes refusals as one count,
678	   the core's aborted; the split of that count into deny and defer is
679	   the checkpoint's totals (Section 4.4), not a counter of the report.

744	7.  Trust roots

746	   This profile has two roots, filling the core's issuer root and rail
747	   root (Sections 10.1 and 9.3 of [CEDULON]):

749	   The decider root:  The key under which Decision Records and their
750	      checkpoints are attested.  Everything Section 10.1 of the core
751	      states for the issuer root applies: a pinned key attests by
752	      signature, a carried key is not an identity, a record under
753	      another key is issuer-key-mismatch and covers nothing, and with no
754	      pin unauthenticated-issuer makes the guarantee conditional.

756	   The effect-extract root:  The key under which the Effect Extract is
757	      attested.  Everything Section 9.3 of the core states for the rail
758	      key applies.

760	   The core's payee, witness, decision-token, and manifest roots are not
761	   used by this profile, except that a transparency witness MAY hold the
762	   Decider's checkpoints exactly as it holds a Receipt Issuer's, with
763	   the witness root and codes of the core unchanged.

765	   What the two roots do not cover is the relation between them.  The
766	   profile's claim is only as strong as the independence of the party
767	   that signs the Effect Extract from the party that signs the Decision
768	   Records.  Where the channel operator signs an export of its own log,
769	   the extract root is that operator's key and the independence is the
770	   operator's.  Where the channel operator signs nothing, which is the
771	   common case for a messaging platform, the extract is produced by a
772	   capture process the deployment runs, and the deployment is stating,
773	   by pinning that process's key, that the process is not the Decider
774	   and cannot be told what to omit.  The rule is not that the two roots
775	   be independent; it is that the deployment say which case it is in,
776	   and that the guarantee fall when independence is absent or unknown.
777	   A deployment MUST state which of the two it has, and a verifier MUST
778	   treat the guarantee as conditional where the extract root and the
779	   decider root are, or may be, the same party (MUST-DP-9).  The
780	   companion cannot measure that from the keys alone; two keys can be
781	   held by one hand.

783	8.  Security Considerations

785	   The core's threat analysis (Section 16 of [CEDULON]) applies where
786	   the nouns carry over: forgery and repudiation of a signed record
787	   (T4), key leakage (T7), and suppression of checkpoints (T11) are the
788	   same threats against a Decider that they are against a Receipt
789	   Issuer, and the controls are the same.  The threats below are the
790	   ones this population adds or sharpens.

792	8.1.  D1: An effect occurs against a refusal

794	   The agent, or something acting through its channel access, does what
795	   the Decider refused.  This is the threat the profile exists for.  A
796	   Decision Record for the refusal, with the reference it refused, and
797	   an authenticated extract that carries an effect under that reference
798	   make the event effect-against-refusal, and the audit fails.  A
799	   refusal that carried no reference cannot support this finding; the
800	   effect is then effect-without-decision, which also fails the audit
801	   but does not say it was refused.  A Decider SHOULD carry the
802	   reference on a refusal whenever the channel assigns one before the
803	   decision.

805	   The shape of the threat is not particular to this profile.  A reader
806	   who mapped this document's objects onto a second evidence format,
807	   with a signed decision predicate and a signed outcome predicate of
808	   its own, measured that format's verifier on 4 September 2026: a
809	   decision carrying a refusal and an outcome recording execution,
810	   citing that decision by content root, verified clean with no warning,
811	   because the verdict is not read on the outcome path [B7N0DE].  That
812	   is D1 as a verifying pair rather than a finding, recorded by the
813	   reader as a measurement of that artefact and cited here as one.

815	8.2.  D2: Effects without decisions

817	   Something acts on the channel that never asked.  Every such effect is
818	   effect-without-decision.  The control is the extract's completeness,
819	   which is the extract root's independence (Section 7); a capture
820	   process the Decider controls can leave the effect out.

822	8.3.  D3: Substitution of content

824	   The Decider allows one content and the channel carries another.  The
825	   allow's effectHash and the row's effectHash are computed over the
826	   same octets, and a difference is effect-mismatch.  The control fails
827	   open if the two sides hash different octets, which is why a
828	   deployment MUST state the octets once for both (Section 5.1).

830	8.4.  D4: The Decider signs below its own rules

832	   A Decider produces a well-formed signature over a claim map that
833	   breaks a rule this profile states: an allow with no reference or no
834	   content hash, a refusal with a content hash, a hash outside the
835	   grammar.  Every such record is a record the reconciliation cannot
836	   close or would close wrongly.  The verifier applies the rules on the
837	   decoded payload (MUST-DP-3) and walks the chain over every record
838	   that claims the pin (MUST-DP-5), so the record is refused and named
839	   rather than attested or dropped.

841	8.5.  D5: Equivocation on the record chain

843	   The Decider signs two decisions for one request, or two successors to
844	   one predecessor, and offers each to a different reader.  Within one
845	   reader's population the chain (MUST-DP-5) makes the second record
846	   unlinkable: it names the same predecessor as the first, or none, and
847	   the walk reports the break.  Across readers the chain sees nothing:
848	   each holds a linear chain that verifies.  The control for that case
849	   is the core's witness (Section 4.4): the Decider's epoch checkpoints
850	   are recorded with a witness the verifier has pinned, a Transparency
851	   Service [RFC9943] being one, and the copies the witness holds are
852	   compared against the presented chain.  A verifier that consulted no
853	   witness has not measured cross-reader equivocation.  The guarantee
854	   its report prints is the core's, which is defined over the extract,
855	   the pins, and the window and does not cover suppression or
856	   equivocation beyond the presented chain when no witness was consulted
857	   (Section 11 of [CEDULON], MUST-T11-9); measured on the companion,
858	   such a report prints unconditional with no warning and no finding,
859	   and is silent about the witness it was not given.  The silence is
860	   this revision's known gap: the claim is narrowed here, in the text,
861	   and a report line that names an unconsulted witness is a change to
862	   the reconciler both populations share, not made in this revision.  A
863	   deployment that needs the property records its checkpoints with a
864	   witness and gives its readers the witness key.

866	8.6.  D6: The class of the effect is substituted

868	   A row of a different class under the same reference and the same
869	   content hash: the same text allowed as a reply and carried as a post.
870	   The earlier revision named this as a gap, because the record carried
871	   no claim for the class and a row of any class matched.  The record
872	   now carries effectClass under the Decider's signature (Section 4.1),
873	   an allow without one is refused (Section 4.2), and a row whose class
874	   differs from the allow's with the content hash equal is effect-class-
875	   mismatch (Section 6.1).  What remains open is the vocabulary: the
876	   class names are the channel's, this document does not fix them, and a
877	   deployment SHOULD state them beside its statement of what it hashes
878	   so that two readers compare the same words.

880	8.7.  D7: Silent defaults in capture

882	   The process that turns a channel's log into Decision Records or
883	   Effect Extract rows fills a missing value with a default, and the
884	   default hashes to something.  An allow with no stated content that is
885	   hashed as the empty string produces a record that will match an empty
886	   effect and mismatch every real one, with no finding that says the
887	   content was never stated.  Such a process MUST refuse the line by
888	   name rather than fill it (MUST-DP-10).  The companion's example
889	   adapter did fill it until it was measured, and refuses it now.

891	8.8.  D8: The capture process is the Decider

893	   The party that produces the Effect Extract is, or answers to, the
894	   party that signed the Decision Records.  Every finding in D1 and D2
895	   can then be made to disappear by omission, and no signature check
896	   detects it.  This is the independence statement of Section 7 (MUST-
897	   DP-9), and it is a deployment fact the profile can name but not
898	   prove.  A verifier that holds both roots and cannot state their
899	   independence has a conditional result, and MUST say so.

901	9.  Privacy Considerations

903	   A Decision Record carries no request content and no effect content:
904	   hashes of both, an opaque subject identifier, and a reason code.  The
905	   Effect Extract carries a reference, a class, a content hash, a time,
906	   and optionally the identifier of the party the effect reached.  The
907	   core's Privacy Considerations (Section 15 of [CEDULON]) apply to what
908	   a transparency witness is given.

910	   Two points are specific to this population.  A content hash over a
911	   short text is a fingerprint of that text: a reader who can guess the
912	   message can confirm the guess.  This revision hashes the plain
913	   content octets, as the companion does, so that the two sides need no
914	   shared secret to agree; a keyed or salted construction that would
915	   defeat the guess is a claim-set change and is not defined here.  A
916	   deployment whose effects are short and guessable SHOULD treat the
917	   extract and the records as confidential to the audit.  The subject
918	   and actor identifiers are opaque to the profile but need not be
919	   opaque to a reader; a deployment SHOULD pseudonymize them before
920	   either object leaves its control.

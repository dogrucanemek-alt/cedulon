7	          Cedulon: An Audit Layer for Agent-to-Agent Commerce
8	                         draft-dogru-cedulon-03

10	Abstract

12	   This document defines the Cedulon Protocol, an audit layer for agent-
13	   to-agent commerce.  Payment rails such as HTTP 402 flows (x402) and
14	   mandate protocols (AP2) already move value.  They do not, by
15	   themselves, produce a fail-closed policy check and a signed spend
16	   receipt that a verifier can reconcile against a rail extract.
17	   Cedulon specifies a Trade Manifest (signed offer before payment), a
18	   Policy Decision Point with default deny, a Spend Receipt (COSE/CWT
19	   claim set after a gated payment), epoch checkpoints, and rail-extract
20	   reconciliation.  The reconciliation shows that no settlement on the
21	   extract lacks a receipt and no settled receipt is absent from the
22	   extract.  That result is unconditional only when the verifier pins
23	   the rail key out of band and states the period under audit; otherwise
24	   the document requires it to be reported as conditional.  Checkpoints
25	   carry the suppression guarantee, so this revision profiles the
26	   checkpoint as a Signed Statement, gives the verification algorithm a
27	   step that consumes the transparency receipts returned for
28	   checkpoints, names what a witness holding a checkpoint the presented
29	   chain omits reports, brings equivocation within reach by comparing
30	   recorded copies against the presented chain, and states how
31	   checkpoint totals may be withheld without withholding the fact that
32	   they were.  It also defines a Dispute Evidence Bundle (evidence, not
33	   an award) and optional SCITT anchoring.  Cedulon is not a competitor
34	   to x402 or AP2; it sits above them.

131	1.  Introduction

133	   _Note to Readers:_ This document is submitted as Informational.  The
134	   author's eventual intended track, if the work is taken up, is a
135	   Standards Track profile of COSE [RFC9052] and CWT [RFC8392] for
136	   agent-spend receipts.  This -02 does not claim IETF consensus.

138	   Agents can now pay.  Open HTTP 402 protocols [X402] attach stablecoin
139	   settlement to ordinary requests.  Card networks and processors issue
140	   agent-scoped tokens.  Google's Agent Payments Protocol (AP2) [AP2]
141	   binds user intent to signed mandates.

143	   What is missing is an interoperable *audit layer*: a machine-
144	   checkable answer to "was this spend allowed by policy, against which
145	   offer, and what bytes were delivered?"  Without that layer, a prompt-
146	   injected or looping agent can drain a rail that has already accepted
147	   a valid signature.  A counterparty can ship the wrong artifact.  A
148	   transparency log, if used at all, is proprietary.

150	   Cedulon fills that gap.  It does not clear funds, hold custody, or
151	   operate a payment facilitator.  An optional escrow actor is defined
152	   only as a third-party role interface (Section 15.13).
153	   Implementations of this specification MUST NOT take custody of funds
154	   or operate escrow (MUST-T8-custody).

156	   The control is an old one.  Reconciling an internal ledger against an
157	   external statement is what double-entry bookkeeping [PACIOLI] made
158	   routine, and signing the artifacts on both sides is Grigg's triple-
159	   entry idea [GRIGG].  Neither is claimed here.  What this document
160	   contributes is an open wire profile for that control in a setting
161	   where the parties are software: a COSE receipt shape, an extract
162	   shape, a checkpoint chain, and a verification algorithm precise
163	   enough that two implementations reach the same finding on the same
164	   evidence.  The novelty is interoperability, not the idea.

166	   Neighbor drafts are complementary, not substitutes. draft-bates-atp
167	   [BATES-ATP] covers tamper-evident causal lineage as a signed DAG.
168	   Cedulon is the completeness layer: a spend that never produced a
169	   receipt is visible when an authenticated rail extract is reconciled
170	   (MUST-T10-1).

172	2.  Terminology

174	   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT",
175	   "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and
176	   "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in
177	   BCP 14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all
178	   capitals, as shown here.

180	   The following terms are used:

182	   Trade Manifest:  A signed statement produced *before* payment.  It
183	      binds a description of goods or service, price, currency,
184	      acceptance-criteria hash, cancel condition, expiry, and an
185	      optional AP2 mandate reference.

187	   Policy Decision Point (PDP):  The function that evaluates a
188	      structured spend request against stored policy.  The default is
189	      deny.

191	   Spend Receipt:  A signed statement produced *after* a gated payment
192	      attempt.  It binds payer, payee, amount, currency, policy hash,
193	      manifestHash or an explicit noManifest flag, rail payment
194	      reference, timestampMs, nonce, prevReceiptHash, and outcome.

196	   Receipt Issuer:  The party that signs Spend Receipts.

198	   Anchor:  An optional SCITT Transparency Service [RFC9943] that
199	      registers a signed statement and returns a COSE receipt [RFC9942].

201	   Dispute Evidence Bundle:  A package of the Trade Manifest, the Spend
202	      Receipt, and a delivery hash.  It is evidence for a later human or
203	      legal process.  It is not an arbitral award and not an escrow
204	      release.

206	   Decision Token:  A portable, single-use PDP allow encoded as
207	      COSE_Sign1.  The claim set binds requestHash, policyHash,
208	      expiryMs, nonce, and singleUseId.  See Section 7.

210	   Rail Extract:  An authenticated list of settlement records for one
211	      account, one rail, and one time window.  See Section 8.

213	3.  Architecture

215	   Cedulon has three control-plane objects and one optional log:

217	     Principal --policy--> PDP --allow/deny--> x402/AP2 rail
218	                                 |
219	                                 v
220	                         Receipt Issuer --> Spend Receipt
221	                                 |
222	                                 v
223	                         Anchor / SCITT (optional)

225	   The payer agent never talks to the rail except through an adapter
226	   that calls the PDP first (MUST-T5-1).

228	3.1.  Policy Decision Point

230	   The PDP evaluates structured fields only (MUST-T1-1): amount,
231	   currency, payee, tool identifier, nonce, optional manifest hash, and
232	   evaluation time.  It applies limit, velocity, and scope checks (MUST-
233	   T2-1, MUST-T2-2).  If the PDP is unreachable, uninitialized, or
234	   throws, the result is deny (MUST-T2-3).  Denied attempts do not
235	   increment success counters (MUST-T2-4).

237	   An allow produces a Decision Token whose requestHash covers six
238	   fields: amount, currency, payee, tool, nonce, and manifestHash (MUST-
239	   T3-4, MUST-T6-1).  The token is a COSE_Sign1 object (MUST-T6-4), is
240	   single-use (MUST-T6-2), and MAY be carried to the adapter that
241	   performs settlement.

243	3.2.  Receipt Issuer

245	   After the adapter attempts settlement (success or a recorded deny
246	   that still needs an audit trail for an allowed-then-aborted path),
247	   the Receipt Issuer signs a Spend Receipt over a canonical encoding
248	   (MUST-T4-1).  Verifiers reject bad signatures and byte mismatch
249	   (MUST-T4-2).

251	3.3.  Anchor / SCITT

253	   Parties MAY register the signed receipt (or a privacy-preserving hash
254	   encoding) as a SCITT Signed Statement [RFC9943] and attach the COSE
255	   receipt (MAY-T4-6).  This document does not operate a Transparency
256	   Service.

258	4.  Trade Manifest

260	   A Trade Manifest is the commerce analogue of a promise: it is issued
261	   *before* value moves.  It is conceptually symmetric to a later Spend
262	   Receipt (promise then proof), and it MAY carry an AP2 mandate hash so
263	   that user intent and the Cedulon offer stay linked (SHOULD-T8-5).

265	   A Trade Manifest MUST bind all of the following (MUST-T8-1):

267	   *  goods or service description

269	   *  price (integer minor units, encoded as a decimal string matching
270	      0|[1-9][0-9]*)

272	   *  currency (ISO 4217 alphabetic or a documented token identifier)

274	   *  acceptance-criteria hash (SHA-256 [RFC6234] of the exact delivery
275	      bytes or of a declared schema instance)

277	   *  cancel condition (opaque string agreed by the parties)

279	   *  expiry (POSIX milliseconds, expiresAtMs)

281	   It MAY include ap2MandateHash.  The corresponding CBOR label is
282	   always present; a missing mandate is encoded as CBOR null.

284	   The manifest is COSE_Sign1 [RFC9052] over a deterministic CBOR claim
285	   map (Section 6). manifestHash is the SHA-256 of the signed COSE bytes
286	   (MUST-T8-7).  A spend bound to a manifest MUST be denied if the
287	   requested amount or currency differs from the manifest (MUST-T8-2) or
288	   if the manifest is expired (MUST-T3-3).

290	   A spend that is not bound to a verified manifest MUST be marked
291	   noManifest on the receipt and MUST still pass limit, velocity, and
292	   scope checks (MUST-T1-2).  An implementation MAY refuse all
293	   noManifest spend (MAY-T1-4).

295	5.  Spend Receipt

297	   The Spend Receipt claim set is carried in COSE_Sign1 [RFC9052]
298	   wrapping a CWT-compatible map [RFC8392].  New receipts MUST use the
299	   COSE profile (Section 6).

338	   A receipt with outcome = settled MUST have a non-null x402PaymentRef
339	   (MUST-T4-7).  An aborted receipt MUST NOT be added into checkpoint
340	   totals.

342	   All twelve labels in Section 6.1 are always present.  An empty
343	   optional value is encoded as CBOR null, never by omitting the label.

345	   receiptHash is the SHA-256 of the receipt's signed COSE bytes,
346	   encoded as lowercase hex.

348	   Verifiers MUST reject a receipt if the signature fails or if the
349	   decoded claim map does not match the presented claims (MUST-T4-2).

351	5.1.  Optional payee countersignature

353	   A payee MAY attach a countersignature over the issuer's signed Spend
354	   Receipt (MAY-T8-9).  The profile uses a *detached* COSE_Sign1
355	   [RFC9052] whose payload is a CBOR map with a single private-use
356	   label:

358	      +========+=============+======================================+
359	      | Label  | Claim       | CBOR type                            |
360	      +========+=============+======================================+
361	      | -70401 | receiptCose | bstr (exact issuer COSE_Sign1 bytes) |
362	      +--------+-------------+--------------------------------------+

364	                                  Table 2

366	   The countersignature uses the header profile in Section 6 and content
367	   type application/cedulon-countersign+cbor.

369	   This is a second Sign1 object, not RFC 9052 Countersignature0
370	   (unprotected-header label 11).  Countersignature0 would write into
371	   the issuer object and change receiptHash after issue, breaking the
372	   receipt chain.  A detached Sign1 keeps the issuer bytes stable,
373	   reuses kid and content-type, and is absent by simply omitting the
374	   sibling object.

376	   Absence of a countersignature MUST NOT invalidate the issuer receipt
377	   (MAY-T8-9).  If a countersignature is present, a verifier MUST reject
378	   it when the signature fails, when kid or content type does not match
379	   the configured payee key, or when label -70401 is not the issuer COSE
380	   bytes (MUST-T8-8).  The identifier countersign-bad SHOULD be used for
381	   this condition.  A Dispute Evidence Bundle that includes a verified
382	   countersignature has stronger evidence that the payee accepted those
383	   bytes; the bundle is still not an award (MUST-T8-4).

385	6.  COSE Profile

387	   This profile uses deterministic CBOR [RFC8949] Section 4.2.1
388	   (definite lengths, shortest integer form, map keys sorted in
389	   *bytewise lexicographic* order of their encoded keys).
390	   Implementations MUST encode only the types used by Cedulon claim
391	   maps: null, bool, unsigned and negative integers, UTF-8 text, byte
392	   strings, arrays, and maps (MUST-T4-1).

494	6.2.  COSE_Sign1 headers

496	   The protected header MUST be a deterministic CBOR map containing
497	   (MUST-T4-1, MUST-T4-8):

499	   *  1 (alg) = -19 (Ed25519, [RFC9864]; the generic EdDSA value -8 from
500	      [RFC9053] is deprecated for this profile)

502	   *  3 (content type) = a tstr that distinguishes the payload:
503	      application/cedulon-receipt+cbor, application/cedulon-
504	      checkpoint+cbor, application/cedulon-manifest+cbor, application/
505	      cedulon-decision+cbor, or application/cedulon-countersign+cbor

507	   *  4 (kid) = bstr, mandatory.  The profile computes kid as the first
508	      eight bytes of SHA-256 over the issuer's SubjectPublicKeyInfo DER.
509	      A verifier MUST obtain the public key from an authenticated
510	      channel (preconfigured issuer set, directory, or transparency
511	      statement) and MUST reject a message whose kid does not match that
512	      key.

514	   The unprotected header MUST be empty.  The payload MUST be the CBOR
515	   encoding of the claim map.  The signature is Ed25519 [RFC8032] over
516	   the COSE Sig_structure ["Signature1", protected, h'', payload].

518	7.  Decision Token

520	   A Decision Token is the portable encoding of a PDP allow.  It is
521	   COSE_Sign1 with the header profile in Section 6 and the labels in
522	   Section 6.1.  All five labels are always present (MUST-T6-4).

524	   requestHash MUST be the six-field hash defined for the PDP (MUST-
525	   T6-1). policyHash MUST be the SHA-256 of the canonical policy
526	   document the PDP evaluated. expiryMs is a Unix time in milliseconds
527	   after which the token MUST be treated as expired (SHOULD-T6-3). nonce
528	   is the request nonce. singleUseId is the identifier consumed on the
529	   first settlement attempt (MUST-T6-2).

531	   A party that accepts a Decision Token MUST reject it if the signature
532	   fails, if kid does not match a configured PDP key, if the content
533	   type is not application/cedulon-decision+cbor, if the decoded claim
534	   map does not match the presented claims, or if expiryMs is in the
535	   past (MUST-T6-5).

537	8.  Rail Extract Profile

539	   A verifier checks completeness against a *rail extract*, not against
540	   the issuer's own receipts alone (MUST-T10-7).

542	8.1.  Record schema

544	   Each settlement record MUST contain:

546	              +=============+===============================+
547	              | Field       | Type                          |
548	              +=============+===============================+
549	              | ref         | tstr (rail payment reference) |
550	              +-------------+-------------------------------+
551	              | amount      | tstr matching 0|[1-9][0-9]*   |
552	              +-------------+-------------------------------+
553	              | currency    | tstr                          |
554	              +-------------+-------------------------------+
555	              | timestampMs | uint                          |
556	              +-------------+-------------------------------+

558	                                  Table 7

560	8.2.  Scope

562	   An extract is scoped to one account identifier, one rail identifier,
563	   and one half-open time window [windowStartMs, windowEndMs).

565	8.3.  Authentication

567	   The mock rail in the companion implementation signs the extract with
568	   Ed25519 over a canonical encoding of the scoped body.  A verifier
569	   MUST obtain the extract from the rail or from a signature the rail
570	   published (MUST-T10-7).  A deployment that cannot do so is running
571	   the reconciliation against evidence it did not obtain independently,
572	   and MUST report the guarantee as conditional.

574	   A signature on an extract proves internal consistency, not origin: a
575	   key generated by whoever produced the object verifies against itself.
576	   The verifier therefore MUST obtain the rail's public key out of band
577	   and MUST verify the extract signature against that key rather than
578	   against any key the extract carries (MUST-T10-8).  A verifier that
579	   holds no such key MUST treat the guarantee as conditional.

581	   Keys are compared as bytes.  A verifier MUST compare the pinned key
582	   and the key that signed the extract by their SubjectPublicKeyInfo DER
583	   encoding, not by any text encoding of it, so that the same key
584	   presented in a different envelope still compares equal (MUST-T10-9).
585	   A pinned key the verifier cannot decode is a fault in the verifier's
586	   own configuration, not evidence about the extract, and MUST be
587	   reported as trust-key-unreadable rather than as a key mismatch.

589	   What a verifier emits for an extract it cannot authenticate depends
590	   on whether it stated an expectation.  With no pinned key the verifier
591	   has asserted nothing, so an extract that does not carry a verifiable
592	   signature is unauthenticated-extract, a warning: completeness
593	   findings may still be computed, but the guarantee is *conditional* on
594	   the extract being authentic.  With a pinned key the verifier has
595	   asserted what it requires, and an extract that fails to meet it is a
596	   failure rather than a caveat; see the verification algorithm for
597	   which finding applies. -00 defined only the first case, and readers
598	   of -00 should note that this revision makes the pinned case fail
599	   closed.  See Section 15.

601	8.4.  Scope agreement

603	   An extract declares a window and carries settlement records.  The two
604	   MUST agree: a verifier MUST report every settlement record whose
605	   timestampMs falls outside [windowStartMs, windowEndMs) as extract-
606	   scope-mismatch, identified by that record's ref (MUST-T10-10).  This
607	   check is about the extract's internal consistency and MUST be
608	   performed whether or not a rail key is pinned.

610	   A verifier that knows which account, rail, and window it is auditing
611	   MUST also check the extract against that expectation and MUST fail
612	   closed when the extract does not cover it (MUST-T10-11).  An extract
613	   for another account or rail, or one whose window does not span the
614	   period under audit, cannot support a completeness claim about that
615	   period.

617	   A verifier that states no period leaves the extract free to define
618	   one, and an extract that reports on a millisecond balances as easily
619	   as one that reports on a month.  Pinning a key establishes who
620	   signed; only a stated period establishes what the signature had to
621	   cover.  A verifier that has not stated the period under audit
622	   therefore MUST emit unstated-audit-window and MUST treat the
623	   guarantee as conditional (MUST-T10-15), whatever else verifies.

625	9.  Trust roots

627	   Section 8 states the rule for one object: a signature proves internal
628	   consistency, not origin, so the verifier obtains the rail key out of
629	   band and checks the extract against that key rather than against any
630	   key the extract carries (MUST-T10-8). -02 stated it there and nowhere
631	   else, and every other signed object in this profile was left to be
632	   checked against the key travelling inside it.  This section states
633	   the same rule for the rest of them.

635	   The gap is not theoretical.  A verifier that checks a Spend Receipt
636	   against the key the receipt carries accepts a receipt signed by any
637	   key at all, including one an attacker minted for the occasion.  Such
638	   a receipt matches a settlement the attacker was never authorised to
639	   make, the settlement stops looking uncovered, and the audit reports
640	   nothing.  The completeness property in Section 10 is then computed
641	   over evidence that answers to nobody.

643	9.1.  The issuer root

645	   A verifier MUST obtain the issuer's public key out of band and MUST
646	   verify Spend Receipt and epoch checkpoint signatures against that key
647	   rather than against a key the object carries (MUST-T4-9).  A verifier
648	   that holds no such key MUST treat the completeness guarantee as
649	   conditional and SHOULD report the condition; the identifier
650	   unauthenticated-issuer is used for it in this implementation.

652	   Reporting a mismatch is not sufficient on its own.  A receipt that
653	   does not answer to the pinned issuer key MUST NOT count as coverage
654	   for the settlement it names, and the settlement MUST still be
655	   reported as uncovered (MUST-T4-10).  A verifier that reports the
656	   mismatch and then lets the receipt match the settlement anyway has
657	   described the attack in its output while still concluding that the
658	   books balance.

660	   Keys are compared as bytes, by their SubjectPublicKeyInfo DER
661	   encoding, on the same terms as MUST-T10-9.  A pinned issuer key the
662	   verifier cannot decode is a fault in its own configuration and MUST
663	   be reported as trust-key-unreadable rather than as a mismatch against
664	   the objects; where no pinned key can be decoded at all, nothing is
665	   attested and the verifier MUST NOT fall back to accepting the keys
666	   the objects carry (MUST-T4-11).  Falling back is how a mistyped
667	   configuration becomes a bypass.

669	   An issuer root MAY be a set of keys rather than a single key (MUST-
670	   T4-12).  An issuer that rotates its key mid-window otherwise produces
671	   a finding against every honest receipt signed by the retired key, and
672	   the reachable way out of that is to stop pinning, which is the
673	   opposite of what the pin is for.

675	9.2.  The payee root

677	   The optional countersignature in Section 5.1 travels beside the
678	   issuer signature without being covered by it.  Anyone holding an
679	   honest receipt can append a countersignature of their own, so a
680	   verifier that checks it against the key carried next to it learns
681	   only that some key signed something.

683	   A countersignature MUST NOT be treated as evidence that the payee
684	   approved the payment unless it verifies against a payee key the
685	   verifier obtained out of band (MUST-T4-13).  Without such a key the
686	   verifier SHOULD report the condition and MUST treat the guarantee as
687	   conditional.

689	   Naming a payee key states an expectation, and an expectation that
690	   only fires when the evidence is present can be cancelled by deleting
691	   the evidence.  Where a verifier has pinned a key for a payee, a
692	   settled receipt naming that payee and carrying no countersignature
693	   MUST be reported (MUST-T4-14).  Otherwise an attacker removes their
694	   own failed forgery and the report returns to unconditional.

696	9.3.  The witness root

698	   Section 10.3 describes what a transparency witness adds.  An
699	   inclusion receipt checked against the key it carries says that some
700	   log is internally consistent, and a log is cheap to invent.

702	   A verifier MUST obtain the transparency service's public key out of
703	   band and MUST verify inclusion receipts against it (MUST-T11-15).
704	   Inclusion receipts that cannot be checked that way MUST NOT be used
705	   as evidence, in either direction: they cannot establish that a
706	   checkpoint was anchored, and they cannot establish that one was
707	   withheld.  A verifier SHOULD report that it left them out.

709	   A log holds statements from everyone who uses it.  A statement held
710	   by a pinned log MUST additionally answer to the issuer root before it
711	   counts as something that issuer published (MUST-T11-16); otherwise
712	   another user's epoch, sitting in a shared log, reads as this issuer
713	   publishing two checkpoints for one epoch.

715	   Anchoring and withholding are different claims and need different
716	   evidence.  Establishing that a checkpoint was logged needs only the
717	   statement hash.  Establishing that an issuer withheld one needs to
718	   know whose statement it is, which an inclusion receipt carrying no
719	   statement body cannot say.  Such a receipt MUST NOT be used to report
720	   a withheld checkpoint, and MUST NOT be silently discarded either: a
721	   real withholding must not be buried by removing the body (MUST-
722	   T11-17).

724	9.4.  The decision root

726	   A Decision Token is issued by the policy decision point and consumed
727	   by the same deployment.  The consumer therefore holds the key it
728	   signs with and has no reason to ask the token which key to check it
729	   against.  A consumer MUST verify a Decision Token against its own
730	   issuing key and MUST NOT accept one it cannot check that way (MUST-
731	   T6-6).

733	9.5.  What the roots do not cover

735	   A verifier that supplies none of these roots is not making an error,
736	   and this document does not require it to.  It is making a weaker
737	   statement, and the guarantee it reports must say so.  With no issuer
738	   key nothing distinguishes one submitted receipt from another, so
739	   conditions computed across the submitted set - two receipts claiming
740	   one settlement reference, for instance - cannot be attributed to
741	   anyone and are reported as conditions of the submission rather than
742	   as failures of a party.

744	10.  Reconciliation and Epoch Checkpoints

746	   Completeness is the property that, given an authenticated rail
747	   extract, every settlement in the extract has a matching settled Spend
748	   Receipt, every settled receipt has a matching settlement, receipt and
749	   checkpoint hash chains verify, and checkpoint totals equal the sum of
750	   *settled* receipts in the checkpoint window.  If a spend occurred
751	   without a receipt, the missing receipt is itself the evidence (MUST-
752	   T10-2).

754	   A checkpoint published with its totals withheld (Section 10.1) cannot
755	   contribute the last of those to the property.  It is not a violation
756	   of completeness and it is not a demonstration of it either: the
757	   comparison was not made, and a result that rests on a comparison
758	   nobody made is conditional (MUST-T11-12).

760	10.1.  Checkpoint claims

762	   An epoch checkpoint MUST be COSE_Sign1-signed with the header profile
763	   in Section 6 and MUST bind all of the following (MUST-T11-1):

765	   epoch, startMs, endMs, receiptCount, chainHeadHash, totals, and
766	   prevCheckpointHash.

768	   The checkpoint window is half-open [startMs, endMs) (MUST-T11-7).
769	   receiptCount MUST equal the number of receipts (settled and aborted)
770	   whose timestampMs falls in that window. chainHeadHash MUST equal
771	   receiptHash of the last receipt in that window, or null if the window
772	   is empty (MUST-T11-2).  Where totals is present it MUST sum only
773	   receipts with outcome = settled; the one permitted absence is the
774	   signed redaction below.

776	   An issuer that publishes a checkpoint without its totals MUST encode
777	   totals as null in the signed payload (MUST-T11-12).  An empty map is
778	   an honest zero for an empty window and is not a redaction.  Because
779	   the redaction is inside the signature, it cannot be added to, or
780	   removed from, a checkpoint after signing.

782	   A verifier MUST NOT accept a redaction asserted anywhere but the
783	   signed payload (MUST-T11-13).  A presentation-layer flag alongside a
784	   checkpoint is chosen by whoever presents it, which is the party under
785	   audit; honouring such a flag would let that party switch off the
786	   totals comparison for a checkpoint whose signed totals are wrong.
787	   The structural claims (epoch, startMs, endMs, receiptCount,
788	   chainHeadHash, prevCheckpointHash) MUST NOT be redacted: a checkpoint
789	   missing any of them does not decode, and a verifier MUST treat it as
790	   a failed checkpoint rather than as a redacted one.

792	10.2.  Genesis and continuity

794	   The first checkpoint in a presented chain is the genesis checkpoint
795	   of that chain.  Its prevCheckpointHash MUST be null.  Epoch numbers
796	   MUST be consecutive integers.  Adjacent windows MUST satisfy
797	   next.startMs = prev.endMs (MUST-T11-8).

799	   A later checkpoint that omits a prefix of earlier epochs (prefix
800	   deletion) is detectable only if an external witness (transparency
801	   log) has recorded the missing prefix (MUST-T11-9).  Without that
802	   witness, T11 guarantees about suppression are *conditional*.

804	   -01 stated that dependency and stopped there: nothing in its
805	   verification algorithm read a transparency receipt, so the witness
806	   had no way to speak.  This revision gives it one.  A verifier that
807	   holds transparency receipts for the period under audit compares what
808	   the witness recorded against what the chain presented, and reports
809	   the difference under its own name (Section 10.3).  A witness that
810	   holds a checkpoint the presented chain omits is not the same
811	   condition as a chain that leaves a gap in its own coverage, and the
812	   two MUST NOT be reported under one identifier (MUST-T11-11).  The
813	   first says evidence is being withheld; the second says the evidence
814	   shown is incomplete.  An operator who cannot tell them apart cannot
815	   tell an incomplete record from a concealed one.

817	10.3.  The transparency witness

819	   A checkpoint registered with a Transparency Service [RFC9943] is a
820	   Signed Statement whose payload is the checkpoint COSE object and
821	   whose content type is application/cedulon-checkpoint+cbor
822	   (Section 13).  The service returns a receipt [RFC9942] that binds the
823	   statement it recorded.

825	   A verifier MAY be given such receipts for the period under audit.  It
826	   is a distinct input from the presented checkpoint chain, and
827	   supplying it is optional: a verifier given none performs the same
828	   steps, and reports the same findings, that it would if this input did
829	   not exist (MUST-T11-10).  Supplying an empty set is not the same as
830	   supplying none.  An empty set says a witness is configured and
831	   recorded nothing, which is itself reportable; absence says no witness
832	   was consulted.

834	   A receipt binds a statement hash, not a statement.  The body is not
835	   carried by the receipt and a verifier will often not hold it.  Two
836	   levels of checking follow from that, and they are not the same (MUST-
837	   T11-10).

839	   Every receipt MUST have its signature verified before it counts for
840	   anything.  That is what establishes the hash as one the service
841	   signed for, and it is all that comparing recorded hashes against
842	   presented ones requires: the verifier computes the statement hash of
843	   each presented checkpoint itself.

845	   A receipt accompanied by the statement body carries more, and MUST be
846	   checked further before that body is relied on.  The body's statement
847	   hash MUST equal the hash the receipt binds, and the body MUST itself
848	   verify as a checkpoint.  A body that fails either check proves
849	   nothing and MUST be ignored rather than counted, while the receipt it
850	   came with remains usable for the hash comparison.  Equivocation is
851	   the case that needs a body, because it compares claims rather than
852	   hashes.

854	   What such a receipt establishes, and what it does not, is worth
855	   stating plainly.  It establishes that the service signed for that
856	   statement.  Whether the statement is a member of an append-only log,
857	   and whether that log has ever equivocated, are properties of the
858	   service and its own proofs, not of Cedulon.  A verifier that treats a
859	   signed receipt as proof of log membership is claiming more than the
860	   receipt carries.

862	10.4.  Verification algorithm

864	   A verifier MUST perform all of these steps and MUST report every
865	   finding they produce (MUST-T10-1, MUST-T11-2).  They are numbered for
866	   reference, not to require an evaluation order: no step short-circuits
867	   another, and an implementation may evaluate them in any order that
868	   produces the same set of findings.

870	   One data dependency is worth naming, because "any order" read naively
871	   would break it.  Step 15 decides which transparency receipts, and
872	   which statement bodies, survive checking.  Steps 14 and 16 consume
873	   what survives.  An implementation that ran step 14 against unchecked
874	   bodies, or step 16 against unchecked receipts, would not produce the
875	   same set of findings, so that order is not among the permitted ones.
876	   Nothing else in this list feeds another step.

878	   When a step names an identifier in backticks, that identifier SHOULD
879	   be used for the condition in diagnostic output.  The normative
880	   requirement is the behaviour: report the condition, identified by the
881	   ref or other handle given in the step.  The identifiers are not an
882	   interoperability surface.

884	   1.   Establish the subject of the audit.  When an extract is
885	        supplied, the settlement records it carries are the ones
886	        reconciled; a settlement list from any other source MUST NOT be
887	        substituted for them (MUST-T10-12).  If the caller supplies both
888	        and they differ, the verifier MUST report that the caller-
889	        supplied list disagrees with the extract, and MUST still
890	        reconcile the extract.  The identifier extract-settlement-
891	        mismatch SHOULD be used for this condition in diagnostic output.

893	   2.   Verify the extract signature against the out-of-band rail key
894	        (MUST-T10-8, MUST-T10-9).  If no key is pinned, the verifier
895	        MUST treat the completeness guarantee as conditional (MUST-
896	        T10-7).  The identifier unauthenticated-extract SHOULD be used
897	        for this condition in diagnostic output.  If a key is pinned and
898	        cannot be decoded, the verifier MUST report that the pinned key
899	        is unreadable.  The identifier trust-key-unreadable SHOULD be
900	        used for this condition.  If a key is pinned and the signature
901	        does not verify against it, or verifies against a different key,
902	        the verifier MUST report that the extract is not signed by the
903	        pinned key.  The identifier extract-key-mismatch SHOULD be used
904	        for this condition.  A finding that puts the extract itself in
905	        doubt MUST prevent an unconditional guarantee.

907	   3.   Check scope.  The verifier MUST report each settlement record
908	        whose timestampMs falls outside the declared window, identified
909	        by that record's ref (MUST-T10-10).  When the verifier states an
910	        expected account, rail, or window, it MUST report an extract
911	        that does not cover it (MUST-T10-11).  The identifier extract-
912	        scope-mismatch SHOULD be used for both conditions.  If the
913	        verifier stated no period, it MUST treat the guarantee as
914	        conditional (MUST-T10-15).  The identifier unstated-audit-window
915	        SHOULD be used for this condition.

917	   4.   Decode each Spend Receipt COSE_Sign1.  Reject if Ed25519 verify
918	        fails, if kid does not match the configured issuer key, if the
919	        content type is not the receipt type, or if the decoded claim
920	        map does not match the presented claims (MUST-T4-2, MUST-T4-8).
921	        Then apply the issuer root (Section 9.1): verify the signature
922	        against the out-of-band issuer key rather than the key the
923	        receipt carries.  A receipt that does not verify against it is
924	        reported and is excluded from the reconciliation that follows,
925	        so the settlement it names is still reported as uncovered in
926	        step 8 (MUST-T4-9, MUST-T4-10).  With no issuer key pinned the
927	        verifier makes no such distinction, reports that it did not, and
928	        the guarantee is conditional.  Where a countersignature is
929	        present, Section 9.2 governs what it establishes (MUST-T4-13,
930	        MUST-T4-14).

932	   5.   Scope the receipts.  When an extract is supplied, only receipts
933	        whose timestampMs falls in the extract's declared window are
934	        reconciled against it (MUST-T10-16).  A receipt outside that
935	        window is not a completeness failure against this extract;
936	        auditing a longer period requires extracts that cover it.
937	        Receipts remain subject to every other check regardless of
938	        window.

940	   6.   Walk receipts in issuer order.  The first prevReceiptHash MUST
941	        be null.  Each later prevReceiptHash MUST equal receiptHash of
942	        the previous receipt.  A miss MUST be reported as a break in the
943	        receipt chain.  The identifier receipt-chain-break SHOULD be
944	        used for this condition.

946	   7.   Index settled receipts and extract records by ref.  A ref that
947	        appears more than once on either side MUST be reported as a
948	        repeated reference (MUST-T10-6).  The identifier duplicate-ref
949	        SHOULD be used for this condition.

951	   8.   For each ref that appears exactly once on each side, require a
952	        one-to-one match on ref AND amount AND currency (MUST-T10-1).
953	        Amount or currency mismatch MUST be reported as a settlement
954	        that does not match its receipt, identified by that ref.  The
955	        identifier settlement-mismatch SHOULD be used for this
956	        condition.  A settlement with no receipt MUST be reported as
957	        lacking a receipt, identified by its ref (MUST-T10-2).  The
958	        identifier settlement-without-receipt SHOULD be used for this
959	        condition.  A settled receipt with no extract row MUST be
960	        reported as a completeness failure (MUST-T10-3).  The identifier
961	        receipt-without-settlement SHOULD be used for this condition.  A
962	        settled receipt with a null rail ref MUST be reported as settled
963	        without a rail reference.  The identifier settled-without-ref
964	        SHOULD be used for this condition.

966	   9.   A ref already reported as repeating MUST still be reconciled by
967	        amount rather than dropped from the comparison (MUST-T10-13).
968	        For each currency under that ref, compare the total settled
969	        against the total receipted.  A settled total that exceeds the
970	        receipted total MUST be reported as a settlement lacking a
971	        receipt, and the finding MUST state the unaccounted amount.  The
972	        identifier settlement-without-receipt SHOULD be used for this
973	        condition.  A settled total that is less than the receipted
974	        total MUST be reported as a settlement that does not match its
975	        receipt, identified by that ref.  The identifier settlement-
976	        mismatch SHOULD be used for this condition.  An amount on that
977	        repeating ref that cannot be parsed as an integer MUST be
978	        reported without abandoning the audit; the identifier malformed-
979	        amount SHOULD be used for this condition.  A verifier MUST still
980	        report findings for the remaining records.

982	   10.  Aborted receipts are not matched to extract rows and are not
983	        added to totals.

985	   11.  Decode each checkpoint.  Reject a failed signature, and reject a
986	        kid that does not match the key obtained for the checkpoint
987	        issuer, on the same terms as a receipt (MUST-T4-8).  Require
988	        receiptCount, chainHeadHash, and totals to match the receipts in
989	        [startMs, endMs) as defined above (MUST-T11-2).  The identifier
990	        checkpoint-total-mismatch SHOULD be used for a failed signature,
991	        a wrong receiptCount, or totals that disagree, and checkpoint-
992	        head-mismatch for a chainHeadHash that is not the last in-window
993	        receipt.  If the signed totals is null, the verifier cannot
994	        perform the totals comparison for that checkpoint.  It MUST
995	        report that the comparison was skipped and MUST treat the
996	        completeness guarantee as conditional; the absence of a
997	        comparison is not a passed comparison (MUST-T11-12).  The
998	        identifier checkpoint-totals-redacted SHOULD be used for this
999	        condition. receiptCount and chainHeadHash MUST still be checked.
1000	        A checkpoint that fails verification MUST NOT be treated as
1001	        redacted, whatever it claims about its own totals.

1003	   12.  Every chained receipt MUST fall in exactly one checkpoint
1004	        window.  A gap or double count MUST be reported as a window
1005	        coverage failure (MUST-T11-7, MUST-T11-8).  The identifier
1006	        window-coverage SHOULD be used for this condition.

1008	   13.  Walk checkpoints in epoch order. prevCheckpointHash MUST equal
1009	        the SHA-256 of the previous checkpoint COSE bytes, or null for
1010	        genesis (MUST-T11-4).  The identifier checkpoint-total-mismatch
1011	        SHOULD be used for a broken chain, which is the fourth condition
1012	        its table row names.

1014	   14.  If two successfully verified checkpoints share an epoch number
1015	        and have different hashes, the verifier MUST report equivocation
1016	        (MUST-T11-3).  The identifier equivocation SHOULD be used for
1017	        this condition.  The checkpoints compared here are those
1018	        presented *together with* any carried by verified transparency
1019	        receipts (step 15).  Comparing only the presented chain cannot
1020	        raise this finding: MUST-T11-8, applied in step 12, requires
1021	        that chain's epochs to be consecutive, so no two of its members
1022	        share an epoch.  A copy recorded by a witness is where the
1023	        second one is found.

1025	   15.  If transparency receipts were supplied, verify them against the
1026	        out-of-band transparency key (Section 9.3); receipts that cannot
1027	        be checked that way are not evidence in either direction and the
1028	        verifier reports that it left them out (MUST-T11-15).  Discard
1029	        any whose signature fails (MUST-T11-10).  A surviving receipt
1030	        whose statement body verifies against the issuer root is a
1031	        statement that issuer published; one that does not is another
1032	        party's, and is not this issuer equivocating (MUST-T11-16).  The
1033	        survivors are the recorded statement hashes used in step 16.
1034	        Where a receipt also carries the statement body, discard that
1035	        body unless its statement hash equals the one the receipt binds
1036	        and it verifies as a checkpoint; the surviving bodies are what
1037	        step 14 compares.  Discarding a body does not discard its
1038	        receipt.

1040	   16.  Compare the surviving witness records against the presented
1041	        chain (MUST-T11-11).  For each presented checkpoint with no
1042	        surviving record, report that it is not anchored; the identifier
1043	        checkpoint-not-anchored SHOULD be used.  This is a warning: a
1044	        witness may have been configured after the checkpoint was
1045	        issued, and an operator's own gap is not evidence of
1046	        concealment.  For each surviving record whose statement is
1047	        absent from the presented chain, report that a recorded
1048	        checkpoint was withheld; the identifier checkpoint-withheld
1049	        SHOULD be used.  This is a finding and the audit MUST fail.  A
1050	        record that carries no statement body cannot say whose statement
1051	        it binds, so it MUST NOT produce this finding; it is reported as
1052	        an entry that could not be attributed and makes the guarantee
1053	        conditional, because a real withholding must not be buried by
1054	        removing the body (MUST-T11-17).  Such a record still
1055	        establishes anchoring in the first half of this step: proving
1056	        that a checkpoint was logged needs only its hash.  The verifier
1057	        MUST NOT report a withheld checkpoint as a window coverage
1058	        failure (MUST-T11-11).

1060	   17.  If any finding remains that is not a warning (a warning is a
1061	        condition that only makes the completeness guarantee
1062	        conditional), the audit MUST fail (MUST-T10-4).

1251	   A finding that puts the extract itself in doubt (extract-key-
1252	   mismatch, trust-key-unreadable, extract-scope-mismatch, or extract-
1253	   settlement-mismatch) MUST also prevent an unconditional guarantee,
1254	   not merely fail the audit.

1256	   An unconditional guarantee therefore requires all of: an extract, a
1257	   pinned rail key the extract's signature verifies against, a stated
1258	   period the extract covers, no finding that puts the extract in doubt,
1259	   and no warning that withholds part of the comparison.  A checkpoint
1260	   whose totals were signed as withheld (checkpoint-totals-redacted)
1261	   removes a comparison the guarantee rests on, and a presented
1262	   checkpoint a supplied witness does not hold (checkpoint-not-anchored)
1263	   leaves part of the chain unwitnessed.  Either one makes the result
1264	   conditional.  Anything less than the whole list is conditional, and
1265	   the report MUST say so.

1267	   An implementation MUST make the guarantee and any warnings visible in
1268	   whatever human-readable audit report it produces under this document,
1269	   not only in a returned structure (MUST-T10-14).  A report that says
1270	   the books balance while withholding that the balance is conditional
1271	   invites the reader to take a conditional result for an unconditional
1272	   one.

1274	   Checkpoints SHOULD be registered with a Transparency Service (SHOULD-
1275	   T11-5).  A test deployment MAY use an in-process append-only log as
1276	   the witness (MAY-T11-6).  Cedulon still MUST NOT take custody.

1278	   The guarantee named above is about completeness against the extract,
1279	   which is the subject of T10.  It is not a claim that no checkpoint
1280	   was suppressed.  Suppression is the subject of T11, and a report MUST
1281	   NOT be read as settling it when no witness was consulted: with no
1282	   transparency receipts, the presented chain is self-consistent by
1283	   construction and says nothing about what it left out (MUST-T11-9).  A
1284	   verifier that consulted a witness and found every presented
1285	   checkpoint recorded, with nothing recorded that was not presented,
1286	   has discharged T11 for the period those receipts cover, and for no
1287	   longer.

1289	11.  Lifecycle

1291	   1.  *Manifest.* Parties sign a Trade Manifest (optional for metered
1292	       API spend; required for goods with acceptance criteria).

1294	   2.  *Policy check.* The adapter submits a structured request to the
1295	       PDP.  Default is deny.  An allow is a Decision Token (MUST-T6-4).

1297	   3.  *Payment.* On allow, the adapter performs the x402 (or other
1298	       rail) exchange using exactly the decision fields (MUST-T6-1).
1299	       The Decision Token is consumed (MUST-T6-2).  A reused nonce is
1300	       denied (MUST-T3-1, MUST-T3-2).  A tampered or expired token is
1301	       denied (MUST-T6-5).

1303	   4.  *Receipt.* The Receipt Issuer signs a Spend Receipt.  Rail
1304	       credentials MUST NOT appear in the receipt, logs, or tool results
1305	       (MUST-T5-2, MUST-T7-1).

1307	   5.  *Dispute Evidence Bundle.* If delivery bytes do not match the
1308	       acceptance-criteria hash, an implementation MUST be able to emit
1309	       a bundle of manifest + receipt + delivery hash (MUST-T8-3).  The
1310	       bundle MUST NOT be described as an arbitral award or escrow
1311	       release (MUST-T8-4).

1313	12.  Policy Semantics

1315	   Policy is default deny.  The engine understands three families of
1316	   rule:

1318	   *  *Limit:* maximum amount per payment; maximum cumulative amount per
1319	      window (MUST-T2-2).

1321	   *  *Velocity:* maximum number of allowed payments per window (MUST-
1322	      T2-1).

1324	   *  *Scope:* optional allow-lists for payee, currency, and tool name.

1326	   Fail-closed: missing engine, crash, or exception yields deny (MUST-
1327	   T2-3).  Implementations SHOULD emit stable reason codes (SHOULD-
1328	   T2-5).  Decision tokens SHOULD expire after a short TTL (SHOULD-
1329	   T6-3).

1331	   The agent-facing spend interface MUST invoke the PDP and MUST NOT
1332	   expose a parallel ungated rail call to the model (MUST-T5-1).

1334	13.  SCITT Anchoring

1336	   A Receipt Issuer or relying party MAY construct a SCITT Signed
1337	   Statement whose payload is either the Spend Receipt COSE object or a
1338	   privacy profile (Section 14) and register it with a Transparency
1339	   Service [RFC9943].  The service returns a COSE receipt [RFC9942].
1340	   Embedding that receipt yields a Transparent Statement.  Cedulon does
1341	   not define a new transparency algorithm.

1343	   An epoch checkpoint MUST be registrable on the same terms (MUST-
1344	   T11-14).  Its Signed Statement carries the checkpoint COSE_Sign1
1345	   object as the payload and application/cedulon-checkpoint+cbor as the
1346	   content type, which is among the media types Section 16 asks to have
1347	   registered and which, until then, is a placeholder like the rest.
1348	   Nothing else about registration differs from a receipt.

1350	   This is a short section for a requirement -01 was missing, and the
1351	   omission mattered more than its length suggests. -01 asked for
1352	   checkpoints to be registered (SHOULD-T11-5) while profiling only the
1353	   receipt here, so the object carrying the suppression guarantee had no
1354	   stated form to be registered in.  Two implementations could follow
1355	   -01 to the letter and register incomparable things.

1357	14.  Privacy Considerations

1359	   A public transparency encoding MUST support omitting or hashing payer
1360	   and payee identifiers and MUST support amount redaction or bucket
1361	   encoding (MUST-T9-1).  Implementations MUST NOT write government-ID
1362	   numbers, payment-instrument PAN, or street address into a public
1363	   statement (MUST-T9-2).  Default public anchors SHOULD publish
1364	   policyHash, manifestHash, receiptHash, and timestampMs rather than
1365	   full claims (SHOULD-T9-3).  A private auditor MAY receive an
1366	   unredacted receipt out of band (MAY-T9-4).

1368	   The paragraph above counts receipt fields.  A checkpoint publishes
1369	   something a receipt does not: a per-currency total for a whole
1370	   window, which discloses trading volume even when every individual
1371	   receipt is redacted (MUST-T9-5). -01 gave no rule for it, so an
1372	   implementation could publish that total, or withhold it in a way no
1373	   verifier could recognise, and neither reading contradicted the text.

1375	   The rule is the one stated in Section 10: totals MAY be withheld by
1376	   signing it as null (MUST-T11-12), and only that form counts as a
1377	   redaction (MUST-T11-13).  The structural claims are not redactable,
1378	   because a verifier that cannot read the window or the chain head
1379	   cannot check anything at all, and a checkpoint that hid them would be
1380	   indistinguishable from a broken one.

1382	   Withholding is honest and it is also a cost: a verifier that cannot
1383	   recompute the totals says so, and the completeness guarantee for that
1384	   window is conditional.  A deployment that wants an unconditional
1385	   result publishes the totals; a deployment that wants the volume
1386	   private accepts a conditional one.  What a deployment MUST NOT do is
1387	   obtain the unconditional result while withholding the evidence for
1388	   it.

1982	   role that holds funds under rules outside this protocol (MAY-T8-6).
1983	   Implementations of this specification MUST NOT take custody or
1984	   operate escrow (MUST-T8-custody).

1986	15.14.  T12: Settlement without a recorded receipt

1988	   The threats above are about a counterparty, a rail or an attacker.
1989	   This one is about the issuer's own implementation, and it produces
1990	   exactly the condition the rest of this document exists to make
1991	   detectable.

1993	   An issuer that settles a payment, appends the receipt in memory and
1994	   then persists its state has three steps where it could have two
1995	   outcomes.  If the write fails, the rail holds a settlement and the
1996	   receipt exists nowhere durable.  The next start reads a state that
1997	   does not contain it, and the audit in Section 10 reports settlement-
1998	   without-receipt against an honest issuer that did everything its own
1999	   policy asked.  The evidence is missing because the issuer lost it,
2000	   not because anyone hid it, and nothing in the report can tell those
2001	   apart.

2003	     +============+==================================================+
2004	     | ID         | Requirement                                      |
2005	     +============+==================================================+
2006	     | MUST-T12-1 | An issuer MUST NOT complete a settlement whose   |
2007	     |            | receipt it cannot record durably.  The ability   |
2008	     |            | to record MUST be established before value       |
2009	     |            | moves, not after.                                |
2010	     +------------+--------------------------------------------------+
2011	     | MUST-T12-2 | Where a settlement has been made and its record  |
2012	     |            | cannot be completed, the issuer MUST undo the    |
2013	     |            | settlement in every place it reached: in memory, |
2014	     |            | on the rail ledger it controls, and in any later |
2015	     |            | write.  A refused payment MUST NOT consume the   |
2016	     |            | nonce or the payment allowance it never used.    |
2017	     +------------+--------------------------------------------------+
2018	     | MUST-T12-3 | Two issuers MUST NOT share one durable state.    |
2019	     |            | An implementation that permits it MUST fail      |
2020	     |            | loudly rather than let one writer overwrite the  |
2021	     |            | other's receipt, and the failure MUST name what  |
2022	     |            | an operator can act on.                          |
2023	     +------------+--------------------------------------------------+

2025	                                  Table 20

2027	   Ordering:  Settle-then-record is the natural order to write and the
2028	      wrong one to ship.  The record is what makes the settlement
2029	      accountable, so the record is what has to be secured first.

2031	   Recovery:  A durable-state conflict is not necessarily fatal, but it
2032	      MUST NOT be silent, and an implementation that refuses every
2033	      subsequent write without offering a way back has turned a
2034	      recoverable condition into an outage.

2142	17.1.  Changes from -02

2144	   This revision has one subject: -02 stated a rule in one place and
2145	   left it unstated everywhere the same rule applied.

2147	   MUST-T10-8 requires a rail extract to be verified against a key the
2148	   verifier obtained out of band, "not against a key the extract
2149	   carries".  That sentence was written for the extract and nothing
2150	   carried it to the other signed objects in the profile.  A Spend
2151	   Receipt, an epoch checkpoint, a Decision Token and a transparency
2152	   inclusion receipt were each left to be checked against the key
2153	   travelling inside them, which is a question that answers itself.  A
2154	   verifier following -02 to the letter therefore accepts a receipt
2155	   signed by any key at all, and a receipt like that silences the
2156	   settlement-without-receipt finding for the settlement it names.  The
2157	   completeness property is then computed over evidence that answers to
2158	   nobody.  Section 9 states the rule for the rest of them, and the
2159	   verification algorithm now applies it.

2161	   A reader raised the inclusion-receipt half of this against the posted
2162	   -02 after checking the archived text against the implementation
2163	   commit.  Following it into the code turned up the other three, along
2164	   with two conditions that were not about keys at all.

2166	   The first is that reporting a mismatch is not enough.  An
2167	   implementation that names a foreign key and then still lets the
2168	   receipt match its settlement has described the attack in its output
2169	   while concluding that the books balance.  MUST-T4-10 now requires the
2170	   settlement to stay reported.

2172	   The second is that an expectation which only fires when the evidence
2173	   is present can be cancelled by deleting the evidence.  A pinned payee
2174	   key with no countersignature to check was silence, so removing a
2175	   countersignature removed the question with it.  MUST-T4-14 closes
2176	   that, and MUST-T11-17 closes the same shape in the witness: an
2177	   inclusion receipt with its body stripped off can no longer bury a
2178	   withholding, while still not being allowed to accuse anyone.

2180	   T12 is new and is not about an adversary.  An issuer that settles,
2181	   appends the receipt in memory and then writes its state will, when
2182	   that write fails, leave the rail holding a settlement whose receipt
2183	   exists nowhere durable.  Restarted, it reports settlement-without-
2184	   receipt against itself.  The condition this document exists to make
2185	   detectable was reachable through the implementation's own ordering,
2186	   and no requirement in -02 said otherwise.

2188	   Two things are stated here that -02 got right and this revision keeps
2189	   unchanged: the extract rule itself, and the treatment of a pinned key
2190	   the verifier cannot decode.  What changed is their reach.

2192	17.2.  Changes from -01

2194	   This revision has one subject: the checkpoint, which carries the T11
2195	   guarantee against suppression and rollback, was not wired into
2196	   anything that could discharge it.

2198	   A reader of -01 set out the gap and a second reader confirmed it
2199	   independently.  Four things were wrong at once, and they were the
2200	   same thing seen from four sides.  The SCITT anchoring section
2201	   profiled the Spend Receipt and not the checkpoint, so SHOULD-T11-5
2202	   asked for checkpoints to be registered without saying in what form
2203	   (MUST-T11-14 now says).  None of the steps of the verification
2204	   algorithm read a transparency receipt, so a deployment could follow
2205	   SHOULD-T11-5 to the letter and still have a verifier that never
2206	   consulted the witness (steps 15 and 16 now do).  MUST-T11-3,
2207	   equivocation, could not fire at all: the only checkpoints compared
2208	   were the presented ones, and MUST-T11-8 requires those to be
2209	   consecutive, so no two of them can share an epoch.  A second copy is
2210	   found in a witness, and nothing brought one in.  And the checkpoint
2211	   binds a per-currency total for a window while the privacy section
2212	   counted only receipt fields, so a window total could be published, or
2213	   withheld, with no stated rule either way.

2215	   The repairs are MUST-T11-10 through MUST-T11-14.  The transparency
2216	   receipt is a new optional input; supplying none leaves the verifier
2217	   behaving exactly as in -01, which is deliberate, because the point of
2218	   the witness is to add a claim that could not be made before, not to
2219	   withdraw one that could.

2221	   Two decisions inside those repairs are worth stating on their own,
2222	   because a reader might reasonably have expected the other choice.

2224	   *  A recorded checkpoint the presented chain omits gets its own
2225	      identifier, checkpoint-withheld, rather than being folded into
2226	      window-coverage (MUST-T11-11).  The reporter asked which way it
2227	      should go.  Coverage says the record shown is incomplete; a
2228	      withheld checkpoint says the party under audit is holding a record
2229	      it did not show.  An operator who sees one identifier for both
2230	      cannot tell an incomplete record from a concealed one, which is
2231	      the distinction the whole threat is about.

2233	   *  A checkpoint with no receipt in a supplied witness is a warning
2234	      rather than a failure.  A witness may be configured after
2235	      checkpoints have already been issued, and an operator's own gap in
2236	      anchoring is not evidence that anything was concealed.  The
2237	      asymmetry is deliberate: what the witness holds and the chain does
2238	      not is a finding, what the chain holds and the witness does not is
2239	      a warning.

2241	   One change is not a repair of -01 but of the first attempt at this
2242	   revision, and it is recorded because the failure is instructive.
2243	   Signed totals were first made redactable through a field carried
2244	   alongside the checkpoint rather than inside the COSE payload.
2245	   Anything outside the signature is chosen by whoever presents the
2246	   object, which here is the party under audit, so a checkpoint whose
2247	   signed totals disagreed with its receipts could be re-presented as
2248	   redacted and the mismatch went unreported.  Redaction is now inside
2249	   the signature and a redaction asserted anywhere else MUST be ignored
2250	   (MUST-T11-12, MUST-T11-13).  This was the same shape as the bypass
2251	   -01 was written to close: a check that a party under audit could
2252	   switch off.

2254	   Reversal, refund, partial settlement, and the escrow role remain out
2255	   of scope and are still expected later, with no date. -01 said the
2256	   same and this revision does not improve on it.

2258	   The reporters are named in the Acknowledgments.

2262	   This section is a direction, not a commitment.  The structures below
2263	   are reserved in name only.  Normative wire formats, tests, and
2264	   threat-model MUST lines for them belong in later revisions (-03 or
2265	   later), written with the same discipline as this -02.

2267	18.1.  Re-attestation profile

2269	   Algorithms retire.  A Spend Receipt or checkpoint signed under
2270	   Ed25519 today may need a later verifier that no longer accepts -19.
2271	   A companion seed [REATTEST] sketches re-attestation: register the
2272	   original COSE bytes as a SCITT Signed Statement and have a current
2273	   algorithm countersign or receipt them.  The principle is that
2274	   structures outlive ciphers.  The first concrete example is the
2275	   profile's own move from generic EdDSA (-8) to Ed25519 (-19) in
2276	   [RFC9864].

2278	18.2.  Streaming reconciliation

2280	   Epoch checkpoints in this document are batch windows.  A later
2281	   revision may define a continuous, second-scale profile [STREAMING] in
2282	   which the same completeness relation is evaluated as settlements
2283	   arrive, without waiting for an epoch close.  That work does not
2284	   change the matching rules in this document, and it did not arrive in
2285	   this revision either.

2287	18.3.  Generalization

2289	   Payment is the special case that this -02 implements.  The same
2290	   completeness calculus (an authenticated extract of consumed units
2291	   reconciled to signed receipts) can apply to other consumable
2292	   resources such as compute, data, or energy.  This document does not
2293	   specify those profiles.

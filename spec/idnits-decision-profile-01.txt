11	Abstract

13	   The Cedulon core document reconciles an issuer's signed Spend
14	   Receipts against an authenticated extract of a payment rail and
15	   reports, over a declared population, that no settlement lacks a
16	   receipt and no settled receipt is absent from the rail.  Money is the
17	   special case that document implements.  This document defines a
18	   second population on the same reconciler.  A Decision Record is
19	   signed by the party that decided whether an agent may act; an Effect
20	   Extract is an authenticated list of the effects that actually
21	   occurred on a channel.  An allow must be matched by exactly one
22	   effect whose content hash the record named; a refusal must be matched
23	   by none.  The Decision Record claim set, the Effect Extract shape,
24	   the points at which the reconciliation departs from the spend rules,
25	   the finding codes, and one media type are defined.  This revision
26	   binds the class of the allowed effect into the signed record, narrows
27	   what the Decider's chain is claimed to control, and states the
28	   boundary to three adjacent documents.  The text is provisional and
29	   the companion implementation carries the profile prepared and
30	   unpublished.

102	1.  Introduction

104	   The Cedulon core document [CEDULON] answers one question about an
105	   agent that spends: did every settlement on the rail have a receipt
106	   behind it, and did every settled receipt reach the rail?  It answers
107	   it by closing three signed objects over a declared population: the
108	   issuer's records, an authenticated extract of the counterparty
109	   system, and epoch checkpoints that total the records.  The verifier
110	   holds the keys out of band and the report names the population it
111	   covered.

113	   An agent that acts without spending raises the same question with
114	   different nouns.  A party decided whether the agent may reply, post,
115	   send, or call; a channel carried whatever the agent then did.  Did
116	   every effect on the channel have a decision behind it?  Did every
117	   allowed action occur, once, with the content that was allowed?  Did
118	   anything occur that was refused?  Section 19 of [CEDULON] reserves
119	   later profiles in name only, and its Section 19.3 sketches the same
120	   completeness calculus for other consumable resources: compute, data,
121	   energy.  This document is a different population on the same
122	   reconciler, decisions against effects rather than another unit of
123	   spend, and it is the first profile written out.

125	   The profile keeps the core's three roles and its verification
126	   algorithm.  What changes is the record, the row, the binding between
127	   them, and the words the report uses.  What does not change is
128	   measured: the companion implementation holds the spend behaviour byte
129	   for byte behind a golden file, and every rule in this document that
130	   departs from the spend rules is stated as a departure.

132	   Three documents written at the same time ask adjacent questions about
133	   the same agent, and the boundary between them is worth stating so
134	   that a reader does not take one for another.  This profile reconciles
135	   signed decisions against the effects a channel carried.  [AEB]
136	   handles what happens before an effect: authorization, reservation,
137	   and the provider's entry.  [OUTCOME] reconciles the exact action and
138	   its source against the effect that was independently observed, and
139	   keeps missing evidence indeterminate rather than resolving it either
140	   way.  [ABAK] covers the delivery and enforcement of a governance
141	   control on its way to one or more enforcement points.  The finding
142	   this profile exists for, an effect that occurred against a refusal
143	   (Section 6.1), establishes that the decision and effect populations
144	   did not reconcile; it does not by itself establish whether the
145	   failure lay in control delivery, enforcement, another path, or
146	   elsewhere.  Where a deployment realizes a refusal through a
147	   downstream control path, the evidence for that path is the question
148	   [ABAK] addresses; where no such path exists, this profile does not
149	   imply one.

151	   This document is a companion to the core document, not a revision of
152	   it.  It is not an IETF working-group item.  Its requirement language
153	   is provisional in the sense Section 19 of [CEDULON] gives the
154	   structures it reserves: a direction written with the core's
155	   discipline, not a commitment, and a later revision may change it.
156	   The companion implementation carries the profile prepared and
157	   unpublished.

159	2.  Terminology

161	   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT",
162	   "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and
163	   "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in
164	   BCP 14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all
165	   capitals, as shown here.

167	   Terms defined in the core document keep their meaning here: Policy
168	   Decision Point, epoch checkpoint, trust root, population, finding,
169	   warning, guarantee.  The following are specific to this profile.

171	   Decider:  The party that decides, per request, whether the agent may
172	      act.  It signs Decision Records and epoch checkpoints over them.
173	      Its key is the issuer root of this profile (Section 7).

175	   Subject:  The party on whose request the decision was taken, named in
176	      the record as an opaque identifier.

178	   Decision Record:  A COSE_Sign1 object signed by the Decider stating
179	      one decision: allow, deny, or defer, with the request it answered,
180	      the policy it applied, and, for an allow, the reference and the
181	      content hash of the effect it allowed (Section 4).

183	   Effect:  One thing that happened on a channel as a result of, or in
184	      the absence of, a decision: a message sent, a post made, a call
185	      placed.  It is identified by a reference, classed by a short name,
186	      and bound by the SHA-256 of its content.

188	   Channel:  The system on which effects occur and from which an Effect
189	      Extract is taken.  It plays the role a rail plays in the core
190	      document.

192	   Effect Extract:  The authenticated list of effects on one channel,
193	      for one Decider, over one window (Section 5).  It plays the role a
194	      rail extract plays in the core document.

196	   Refusal:  A Decision Record whose decision is deny or defer.  A
197	      refusal expects no effect.

199	3.  The population

201	   The core document's reconciler closes an issuer record against a
202	   counterparty row over a declared population.  This profile fills the
203	   same three roles:

205	       +==============+===================+========================+
206	       | Role         | Spend (core)      | Decision (this         |
207	       |              |                   | document)              |
208	       +==============+===================+========================+
209	       | Issuer       | Spend Receipt     | Decision Record        |
210	       | record       |                   |                        |
211	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+
212	       | Counterparty | settlement record | effect row on an       |
213	       | row          | on a rail extract | Effect Extract         |
214	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+
215	       | Match key    | ref               | ref                    |
216	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+
217	       | Content      | amount and        | allow: a row exists    |
218	       | binding      | currency equal    | and effectHash is      |
219	       |              |                   | equal; refusal: no row |
220	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+
221	       | Record that  | outcome aborted   | decision deny or defer |
222	       | expects no   |                   |                        |
223	       | row          |                   |                        |
224	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+
225	       | Aggregate    | checkpoint totals | checkpoint totals per  |
226	       | witness      | per currency      | decision kind          |
227	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+
228	       | Declared     | account, rail,    | decider, channel,      |
229	       | population   | window            | window                 |
230	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+

232	                                  Table 1

234	   Which population a presented document belongs to is the verifier's
235	   call, made by the profile it applies, and never the document's.  A
236	   verifier applying this profile MUST read every presented record as a
237	   Decision Record and every presented extract as an Effect Extract, and
238	   MUST refuse by name a document that does not have that shape; it MUST
239	   NOT infer the population from members a body happens to carry (MUST-
240	   DP-1).  The companion found the alternative wrong in both directions:

242	   a rail extract that added a member named effects was re-routed away
243	   from the spend rules it was subject to, and an Effect Extract handed
244	   to the spend rules crashed before any report existed.  Under this
245	   profile a rail extract is the wrong document and is refused as one;
246	   under the spend rules an Effect Extract is refused the same way.

248	4.  Decision Record

250	   A Decision Record is COSE_Sign1 with the header profile of
251	   Section 6.2 of [CEDULON]: deterministic CBOR, alg -19 (Ed25519,
252	   [RFC9864]), kid mandatory and computed as the core states, an empty
253	   unprotected header refused by name if not empty, and the payload the
254	   CBOR encoding of the claim map below.  The content type header
255	   parameter is application/cedulon-decision-record+cbor (Section 10).

257	4.1.  Claim labels

259	   The labels lie in the Private Use range of the CWT Claims registry
260	   [RFC8392], below the block the core document uses for the Decision
261	   Token (-70301 to -70305) and the countersignature (-70401, -70402),
262	   so that no two Cedulon claim maps share a label.  Every claim
263	   annotated hash carries a SHA-256 [RFC6234] digest rendered as exactly
264	   64 lowercase hexadecimal characters, the grammar of Section 6.1 of
265	   [CEDULON], and a value outside that grammar is refused by name at
266	   signing and at verification.

268	         +========+================+=============================+
269	         | Label  | Claim          | CBOR type                   |
270	         +========+================+=============================+
271	         | -70501 | decider        | tstr                        |
272	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
273	         | -70502 | subject        | tstr                        |
274	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
275	         | -70503 | requestHash    | tstr (hash)                 |
276	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
277	         | -70504 | policyHash     | tstr (hash)                 |
278	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
279	         | -70505 | inputsHash     | tstr (hash) / null          |
280	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
281	         | -70506 | decision       | tstr (allow / deny / defer) |
282	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
283	         | -70507 | reasonCode     | tstr                        |
284	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
285	         | -70508 | ref            | tstr / null                 |
286	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
287	         | -70509 | effectHash     | tstr (hash) / null          |
288	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
289	         | -70510 | timestampMs    | uint                        |
290	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
291	         | -70511 | nonce          | tstr                        |
292	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
293	         | -70512 | prevRecordHash | tstr (hash) / null          |
294	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
295	         | -70513 | effectClass    | tstr / null                 |
296	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+

298	                                  Table 2

300	   All thirteen labels are always present; a nullable claim carries CBOR
301	   null when it has no value.  The thirteenth label is new in this
302	   revision; a record with twelve is refused at verification as a claim
303	   set that does not have this shape, and the companion carries no
304	   records signed under the earlier set outside its own fixtures.

306	   decider and subject are opaque identifiers chosen by the deployment.
307	   requestHash is the SHA-256 of the request the Decider evaluated, in
308	   the canonical encoding of Section 7 of [CEDULON] when the request is
309	   a JSON document and over its UTF-8 octets when it is text; this
310	   document does not fix the request's fields, and a deployment MUST
311	   state what it hashes. policyHash is the SHA-256 of the canonical
312	   policy document the Decider applied. inputsHash, when not null, is
313	   the SHA-256 of whatever further context the Decider consulted,
314	   encoded the same way, so that a later reader can tell two decisions
315	   on the same request apart by what else was on the table. reasonCode
316	   is a short token the deployment defines; it is carried, not
317	   interpreted.

319	   ref is the reference under which the allowed effect will appear on
320	   the channel, and the key on which the reconciliation matches.
321	   effectHash is the SHA-256 of the content of the effect the Decider
322	   allowed, over the octets the channel will carry: for a text reply,
323	   the UTF-8 octets of the text.  The Effect Extract computes the same
324	   digest over the same octets (Section 5), so equality of the two is
325	   equality of content.

327	   effectClass is the class of the effect the Decider allowed, a short
328	   name in the vocabulary the channel defines, such as a reply or a
329	   post.  An Effect Extract row carries the same claim in the same
330	   vocabulary (Section 5.1), so equality of the two is equality of
331	   class.  The class is under the Decider's signature so that what the
332	   Decider allowed cannot be read as one class by one reader and another
333	   by the next without changing what was signed; the earlier revision
334	   carried it on the row only and named the gap.

336	   timestampMs is the decision time in POSIX milliseconds. nonce
337	   identifies the record. prevRecordHash links records into the
338	   Decider's chain: it is the SHA-256 of the previous record's
339	   COSE_Sign1 octets, the same input the core's receiptHash takes on the
340	   COSE path (Section 7.1 of [CEDULON]), or null for the first record of
341	   a chain.

343	4.2.  Claim rules

345	   A signer MUST refuse to sign, and a verifier MUST reject, a claim set
346	   that breaks any of the following, naming the rule in the refusal
347	   (MUST-DP-2):

349	   *  decision is one of allow, deny, defer.

351	   *  Every hash-annotated claim that is not null matches the hash
352	      grammar.

354	   *  timestampMs is a non-negative integer of magnitude at most 2^53 -
355	      1, the uint the label table states; a CBOR decoder hands back any
356	      number, and the rule is what makes the table true.

358	   *  An allow carries a non-empty ref and a non-null effectHash.  An
359	      allow that names no effect is a decision the reconciliation cannot
360	      close, and an allow that names no reference is one it cannot find.

362	   *  An allow carries a non-empty effectClass.  An allow that names no
363	      class is one whose effect could be matched by a row of any class
364	      under the same reference and content, which is the substitution
365	      Section 8 names.

367	   *  A refusal MAY carry an effectClass: it names the class of what was
368	      refused, and it is carried, not measured.  A refusal binds to the
369	      absence of a row of any class.

371	   *  A refusal carries effectHash null.  A refusal binds to the absence
372	      of an effect, never to a content hash, so a hash on a refusal
373	      would be a claim the audit cannot measure and a second reading of
374	      whether the effect occurred.  A refusal MAY carry a ref: it names
375	      what was refused, and an effect appearing under that reference is
376	      the worst finding this profile has (Section 6.3).

378	   The verifier MUST apply these rules itself, on the claim map it
379	   decoded from the signed payload, and MUST NOT rely on the signer
380	   having applied them (MUST-DP-3).  The Decider is the party under
381	   audit.  A Decider that signed a well-formed COSE_Sign1 over a claim
382	   map that skips a rule has produced an object whose signature
383	   verifies, and a verifier that checked only the signature and the
384	   equality of the decoded map with the presented claims would attest
385	   it.  The companion implementation did exactly that until it was
386	   measured: an allow with no reference, signed below the signer's own
387	   rules under the pinned decider key, verified true, was attested, was
388	   counted as unmatched, and the audit still said the books balanced.
389	   The rules now run at both ends.

391	4.3.  Presentation and confusion

393	   A Decision Record is presented as Section 6.3 of [CEDULON] states for
394	   the core's COSE objects: the signed octets, the decoded claim set,
395	   and the Decider's public key as a SubjectPublicKeyInfo PEM beside
396	   them.  The carried key is not an identity source.  Under a pinned
397	   decider key a record that verifies under the pin while carrying
398	   another key is reported as carried-key-mismatch, a warning, and stays
399	   attested; with no pin held the signature check that runs against the
400	   carried key says the record is internally consistent and nothing
401	   about who signed it.

403	   A Decision Record is not a Decision Token.  The core's Decision Token
404	   (Section 8 of [CEDULON]) is the portable encoding of a PDP allow,
405	   carried by the party that will spend; a Decision Record is the
406	   Decider's own log of what it decided, kept for audit, and it exists
407	   for refusals as well.  The two carry different content types and
408	   different claim maps.  A verifier MUST reject a Decision Record whose
409	   content type is not application/cedulon-decision-record+cbor, and
410	   MUST reject a token presented as a record or a record presented as a
411	   token, on the content type, before the signature is checked and
412	   before any claim is read (MUST-DP-4).

414	4.4.  The Decider's chain and checkpoints

416	   Decision Records chain on prevRecordHash the way Spend Receipts chain
417	   on prevReceiptHash, and the Decider signs epoch checkpoints over them
418	   with the checkpoint claim set of Section 11.1 of [CEDULON] unchanged:
419	   receiptCount is the number of records in the window, chainHeadHash is
420	   the SHA-256 of the last record's COSE_Sign1 octets, and totals is a
421	   map from the three decision kinds to decimal counts, {"allow": n,
422	   "deny": n, "defer": n}, each rendered as a text string as the core
423	   renders its currency totals.  A verifier compares the totals it
424	   computes over the attested records in the window against the signed
425	   map, and a difference is checkpoint-total-mismatch as in the core.

427	   Two records that claim the same position in a chain cannot both link
428	   to it: the second record's prevRecordHash must be the first's hash,
429	   so a Decider that signs two decisions under one nonce, or presents
430	   one record twice to the same reader, breaks its own chain and the
431	   walk names the break.  A verifier MUST walk the chain over every
432	   presented record that carries the pinned decider key, not only over
433	   the records that verified, so that a record that claims the pin and
434	   fails the rules is named by the walk rather than dropped from the
435	   population without a word (MUST-DP-5).

437	   What the walk establishes is bounded by what one reader holds.  A
438	   Decider can sign two successors to the same predecessor and show one
439	   branch to one reader and the other branch to another; each reader
440	   walks a linear chain that verifies, and neither walk names a break.
441	   The earlier revision called the chain the equivocation control of
442	   this profile, which overstated it and contradicted the core:
443	   Section 11 of [CEDULON] states that the presented chain alone cannot
444	   satisfy the equivocation requirement, because its epochs are
445	   consecutive by construction, and that the comparison which reaches a
446	   fork is between the presented checkpoints and the copies a witness
447	   recorded (MUST-T11-3).  That rule applies to this profile unchanged.
448	   The Decider's epoch checkpoints are the Signed Statements the witness
449	   records, the verifier compares the witness's copies against the
450	   presented chain, and two verified checkpoints for one epoch with
451	   different hashes are equivocation.  Where no witness was consulted,
452	   the chain controls equivocation within the population one reader
453	   holds and nothing beyond it, and a report MUST NOT present the chain
454	   as settling more than that (the core's MUST-T11-9, with the nouns
455	   renamed).

457	5.  Effect Extract Profile

459	   A verifier checks completeness against an Effect Extract, not against
460	   the Decider's own records alone.  The extract is the channel's
461	   account of what happened, obtained independently of the Decider, and
462	   the profile is only as strong as that independence (Section 7).

464	5.1.  Body and row schema

466	   The extract body is one JSON document with exactly this shape:

468	      +===============+=============================================+
469	      | Member        | JSON type                                   |
470	      +===============+=============================================+
471	      | deciderId     | string (non-empty)                          |
472	      +---------------+---------------------------------------------+
473	      | channelId     | string (non-empty)                          |
474	      +---------------+---------------------------------------------+

476	      +---------------+---------------------------------------------+
477	      | windowEndMs   | number (POSIX milliseconds, a safe integer, |
478	      |               | greater than windowStartMs)                 |
479	      +---------------+---------------------------------------------+
480	      | effects       | array of effect rows                        |
481	      +---------------+---------------------------------------------+

483	                                  Table 3

485	   Each effect row is a JSON object with exactly these members:

487	   +=============+====================================================+
488	   | Member      | JSON type                                          |
489	   +=============+====================================================+
490	   | ref         | string (non-empty; the reference the Decision      |
491	   |             | Record named)                                      |
492	   +-------------+----------------------------------------------------+
493	   | effectHash  | string (SHA-256 of the effect's content, 64        |
494	   |             | lowercase hex)                                     |
495	   +-------------+----------------------------------------------------+
496	   | effectClass | string (non-empty; a short class name the channel  |
497	   |             | defines, such as a reply or a post, in the         |
498	   |             | vocabulary the Decision Record's effectClass uses) |
499	   +-------------+----------------------------------------------------+
500	   | timestampMs | number (POSIX milliseconds, a safe integer, inside |
501	   |             | the window)                                        |
502	   +-------------+----------------------------------------------------+
503	   | actor       | string (optional; the party the effect reached)    |

508	   These member names are normative.  The body and its rows follow the
509	   core's rail extract (Section 9 of [CEDULON]) in every rule that
510	   document states for a JSON body: the text is read for a repeated
511	   member name before it is parsed and refused as json-duplicate-key;
512	   integers are safe integers; the window is half-open and MUST end
513	   after it starts; a missing member, a wrong type, an empty identifier,
514	   or a hash outside the grammar is refused by name at both ends, by the
515	   signer before it signs and by the verifier before it checks a
516	   signature.

518	   The profile departs from the rail extract at two points, and a reader
519	   who knows the core should note both (MUST-DP-6):

521	   *  A member this document does not name is refused, on the body and
522	      on a row.  The core lets a rail add members of its own because a
523	      rail is a system the profile does not control; an Effect Extract
524	      is produced by a process the deployment does control (Section 7),
525	      and the companion measured what a free member can do to a
526	      population (Section 3).  A later revision may open this once a
527	      channel that needs its own members is measured.

529	   *  A row whose timestampMs falls outside [windowStartMs, windowEndMs)
530	      makes the whole extract malformed, refused as effect-outside-
531	      window before any signature is checked.  The core accepts such a
532	      rail extract and names the row (extract-scope-mismatch).  Here the
533	      extract is the deployment's own document and a window it did not
534	      keep is a document it did not produce correctly: a signer applying
535	      this schema never produces such an extract, and a verifier refuses
536	      one that is presented as a document, whatever else it may also
537	      name about its rows.  The trade is stated so it can be reversed: a
538	      single row out of place fails the whole window closed.

540	   effectHash on a row is computed by the extract's signer over the same
541	   octets a Decider hashes for its effectHash claim: the content as the
542	   channel carried it.  A deployment MUST state those octets once for
543	   both sides; the companion's example channel hashes the UTF-8 octets
544	   of the message text.

546	5.2.  Authentication and scope

548	   The extract is signed the way a rail extract is signed: Ed25519
549	   [RFC8032] over the UTF-8 octets of the [RFC8785] encoding of the
550	   body, with the signature as base64 and the signer's public key as a
551	   SubjectPublicKeyInfo PEM beside the body, neither inside the signed
552	   octets.  It is a JSON document with a detached signature, not a COSE
553	   object, and like the rail extract it has no media type.  The earlier
554	   revision gave as the reason that the core registers names only for
555	   objects whose content type is checked inside a protected header; that
556	   test decides what a name must be bound to, not whether a
557	   representation needs one, and it is withdrawn as the reason.  The
558	   reason is Section 3: which population a presented document belongs to
559	   is the verifier's call, made by the profile it applies and by the
560	   decider, channel, and window it declares (MUST-DP-1, MUST-DP-7), and
561	   never the document's.  That declaration is the typed outer context a
562	   media type would otherwise supply.  A name on the extract would be a
563	   self-description the verifier is told not to select on, and this
564	   document does not register one that its own rule forbids relying on.
565	   The day an extract is itself wrapped as a Signed Statement and needs
566	   a content type in a protected header, an application/cedulon-effect-
567	   extract+json registration is the name to make, without changing the
568	   verifier's rule; the extract is not recorded with a witness today.
569	   Section 9.3 of [CEDULON] applies unchanged: a signature proves
570	   internal consistency and not origin; the verifier MUST hold the
571	   extract signer's key out of band and MUST compare keys as
572	   SubjectPublicKeyInfo DER; with no key held the guarantee is
573	   conditional and unauthenticated-extract is reported; with a key held,
574	   an extract that does not verify under it is extract-key-mismatch and
575	   its rows are not reconciled (settlement-comparison-skipped, the
576	   core's name for the same condition).

578	   The extract is scoped to one Decider, one channel, and one window,
579	   and Section 9.4 of [CEDULON] applies with the nouns renamed: a
580	   verifier that knows which Decider, channel, and window it audits MUST
581	   check the extract against them and MUST fail closed on a mismatch
582	   (extract-scope-mismatch); one that has not stated the window MUST
583	   report unstated-audit-window, one that has not stated the Decider or
584	   the channel MUST report unstated-audit-scope, and in either case the
585	   guarantee is conditional.  The strongest line this profile can print,
586	   a balanced audit under an unconditional guarantee, is true of one
587	   Decider, on one channel, over one window, and a report that carries
588	   it MUST also carry those three (MUST-DP-7).

590	6.  Reconciliation

592	   The verification algorithm of Section 11.4 of [CEDULON] runs
593	   unchanged over this population: establish the subject, verify the
594	   extract, check scope, resolve records against the decider root, walk
595	   the chain, index both sides by ref, match, decode and walk the
596	   checkpoints, consult the witness if one is supplied, and decide.
597	   This section states only what the algorithm reads differently.

599	6.1.  What binds

601	   A Decision Record expects a row when its decision is allow, and
602	   expects none when it is a refusal.  For a ref that appears once on
603	   each side:

605	   *  an allow and a row bind when the row's effectHash equals the
606	      record's effectHash and the row's effectClass equals the record's
607	      effectClass; a difference in the hash is effect-mismatch (the
608	      content that occurred is not the content that was allowed), and a
609	      difference in the class with the hash equal is effect-class-
610	      mismatch (the content that was allowed occurred as something
611	      else).  The hash is compared first; a row that differs in both is
612	      reported for its content;

614	   *  an allow with no row is decision-without-effect;

616	   *  a row with no record is effect-without-decision;

618	   *  a row whose ref a refusal names is effect-against-refusal.

620	   The last is the finding this profile exists for.  A spend audit has
621	   no row that should not be there in the same sense: an aborted receipt
622	   that still carries its reference and a settlement under that
623	   reference is reported by the core as a settlement without a receipt,
624	   and it never told the two cases apart.  Here a refusal that was
625	   followed by the effect it refused is a different fact from an effect
626	   nobody decided on, and it has its own name.

628	   A ref that appears more than once on a side is duplicate-ref as in
629	   the core, and the repeating reference is then reconciled by count
630	   rather than by amount: there is nothing to sum.  More rows than
631	   records under one reference is effect-without-decision; more records
632	   than rows is decision-without-effect.

634	   There is no amount, no currency, no manifest, no terms, and no
635	   counterparty axis on this profile.  The core's counterparty-unbound
636	   scope record is not emitted: effectHash binds the content of the
637	   effect itself, which is more than a payee name ever bound on spend,
638	   and actor on a row is carried for the reader, not measured.  The
639	   core's boundary rule applies unchanged: an unmatched item inside the
640	   declared clock-skew allowance of a window edge is boundary-deferred,
641	   and a closing-edge allow whose ref the following extract names is
642	   carried, not a finding.

644	6.2.  Conservation

646	   With |R| the in-scope Decision Records and |E| the effect rows, the
647	   identities the core report publishes hold with the words changed:

649	   |R|      = refusals + allows
650	   refusals = deny + defer
651	   allows   = matched + deferred + carried
652	              + unmatched + repeated + unreconciled
653	   |E|      = matched + deferred
654	              + unmatched + repeated + unreconciled
655	   matched on |R| equals matched on |E|

657	   A report under this profile MUST publish these counts and MUST name
658	   the population they were computed over (MUST-DP-8), for the reason
659	   the core gives: a report whose counts do not close is a report that
660	   lost a record somewhere, and a reader is entitled to see that without
661	   re-running the audit.  The report publishes refusals as one count,
662	   the core's aborted; the split of that count into deny and defer is
663	   the checkpoint's totals (Section 4.4), not a counter of the report.

725	   Counter names in a returned structure are diagnostic and MAY keep the

728	7.  Trust roots

730	   This profile has two roots, filling the core's issuer root and rail
731	   root (Sections 10.1 and 9.3 of [CEDULON]):

733	   The decider root:  The key under which Decision Records and their
734	      checkpoints are attested.  Everything Section 10.1 of the core
735	      states for the issuer root applies: a pinned key attests by
736	      signature, a carried key is not an identity, a record under
737	      another key is issuer-key-mismatch and covers nothing, and with no
738	      pin unauthenticated-issuer makes the guarantee conditional.

740	   The effect-extract root:  The key under which the Effect Extract is
741	      attested.  Everything Section 9.3 of the core states for the rail
742	      key applies.

744	   The core's payee, witness, decision-token, and manifest roots are not
745	   used by this profile, except that a transparency witness MAY hold the
746	   Decider's checkpoints exactly as it holds a Receipt Issuer's, with
747	   the witness root and codes of the core unchanged.

749	   What the two roots do not cover is the relation between them.  The
750	   profile's claim is only as strong as the independence of the party
751	   that signs the Effect Extract from the party that signs the Decision
752	   Records.  Where the channel operator signs an export of its own log,
753	   the extract root is that operator's key and the independence is the
754	   operator's.  Where the channel operator signs nothing, which is the
755	   common case for a messaging platform, the extract is produced by a
756	   capture process the deployment runs, and the deployment is stating,
757	   by pinning that process's key, that the process is not the Decider
758	   and cannot be told what to omit.  A deployment MUST state which of
759	   the two it has, and a verifier MUST treat the guarantee as
760	   conditional where the extract root and the decider root are, or may
761	   be, the same party (MUST-DP-9).  The companion cannot measure that
762	   from the keys alone; two keys can be held by one hand.

764	8.  Security Considerations

766	   The core's threat analysis (Section 16 of [CEDULON]) applies where
767	   the nouns carry over: forgery and repudiation of a signed record
768	   (T4), key leakage (T7), and suppression of checkpoints (T11) are the
769	   same threats against a Decider that they are against a Receipt
770	   Issuer, and the controls are the same.  The threats below are the
771	   ones this population adds or sharpens.

773	8.1.  D1: An effect occurs against a refusal

775	   The agent, or something acting through its channel access, does what
776	   the Decider refused.  This is the threat the profile exists for.  A
777	   Decision Record for the refusal, with the reference it refused, and
778	   an authenticated extract that carries an effect under that reference
779	   make the event effect-against-refusal, and the audit fails.  A
780	   refusal that carried no reference cannot support this finding; the
781	   effect is then effect-without-decision, which also fails the audit
782	   but does not say it was refused.  A Decider SHOULD carry the
783	   reference on a refusal whenever the channel assigns one before the
784	   decision.

786	8.2.  D2: Effects without decisions

788	   Something acts on the channel that never asked.  Every such effect is
789	   effect-without-decision.  The control is the extract's completeness,
790	   which is the extract root's independence (Section 7); a capture
791	   process the Decider controls can leave the effect out.

793	8.3.  D3: Substitution of content

795	   The Decider allows one content and the channel carries another.  The
796	   allow's effectHash and the row's effectHash are computed over the
797	   same octets, and a difference is effect-mismatch.  The control fails
798	   open if the two sides hash different octets, which is why a
799	   deployment MUST state the octets once for both (Section 5.1).

801	8.4.  D4: The Decider signs below its own rules

803	   A Decider produces a well-formed signature over a claim map that
804	   breaks a rule this profile states: an allow with no reference or no
805	   content hash, a refusal with a content hash, a hash outside the
806	   grammar.  Every such record is a record the reconciliation cannot
807	   close or would close wrongly.  The verifier applies the rules on the
808	   decoded payload (MUST-DP-3) and walks the chain over every record
809	   that claims the pin (MUST-DP-5), so the record is refused and named
810	   rather than attested or dropped.

812	8.5.  D5: Equivocation on the record chain

814	   The Decider signs two decisions for one request, or two successors to
815	   one predecessor, and offers each to a different reader.  Within one
816	   reader's population the chain (MUST-DP-5) makes the second record
817	   unlinkable: it names the same predecessor as the first, or none, and
818	   the walk reports the break.  Across readers the chain sees nothing:
819	   each holds a linear chain that verifies.  The control for that case
820	   is the core's witness (Section 4.4): the Decider's epoch checkpoints
821	   are recorded with a witness the verifier has pinned, a Transparency
822	   Service [RFC9943] being one, and the copies the witness holds are
823	   compared against the presented chain.  A verifier that consulted no
824	   witness has not measured cross-reader equivocation.  The guarantee
825	   its report prints is the core's, which is defined over the extract,
826	   the pins, and the window and does not cover suppression or
827	   equivocation beyond the presented chain when no witness was consulted
828	   (Section 11 of [CEDULON], MUST-T11-9); measured on the companion,
829	   such a report prints unconditional with no warning and no finding,
830	   and is silent about the witness it was not given.  The silence is
831	   this revision's known gap: the claim is narrowed here, in the text,
832	   and a report line that names an unconsulted witness is a change to
833	   the reconciler both populations share, not made in this revision.  A
834	   deployment that needs the property records its checkpoints with a
835	   witness and gives its readers the witness key.

837	8.6.  D6: The class of the effect is substituted

839	   A row of a different class under the same reference and the same
840	   content hash: the same text allowed as a reply and carried as a post.
841	   The earlier revision named this as a gap, because the record carried
842	   no claim for the class and a row of any class matched.  The record
843	   now carries effectClass under the Decider's signature (Section 4.1),
844	   an allow without one is refused (Section 4.2), and a row whose class
845	   differs from the allow's with the content hash equal is effect-class-
846	   mismatch (Section 6.1).  What remains open is the vocabulary: the
847	   class names are the channel's, this document does not fix them, and a
848	   deployment SHOULD state them beside its statement of what it hashes
849	   so that two readers compare the same words.

851	8.7.  D7: Silent defaults in capture

853	   The process that turns a channel's log into Decision Records or
854	   Effect Extract rows fills a missing value with a default, and the
855	   default hashes to something.  An allow with no stated content that is
856	   hashed as the empty string produces a record that will match an empty
857	   effect and mismatch every real one, with no finding that says the
858	   content was never stated.  Such a process MUST refuse the line by
859	   name rather than fill it (MUST-DP-10).  The companion's example
860	   adapter did fill it until it was measured, and refuses it now.

862	8.8.  D8: The capture process is the Decider

864	   The party that produces the Effect Extract is, or answers to, the
865	   party that signed the Decision Records.  Every finding in D1 and D2
866	   can then be made to disappear by omission, and no signature check
867	   detects it.  This is the independence statement of Section 7 (MUST-
868	   DP-9), and it is a deployment fact the profile can name but not
869	   prove.  A verifier that holds both roots and cannot state their
870	   independence has a conditional result, and MUST say so.

872	9.  Privacy Considerations

874	   A Decision Record carries no request content and no effect content:
875	   hashes of both, an opaque subject identifier, and a reason code.  The
876	   Effect Extract carries a reference, a class, a content hash, a time,
877	   and optionally the identifier of the party the effect reached.  The
878	   core's Privacy Considerations (Section 15 of [CEDULON]) apply to what
879	   a transparency witness is given.

881	   Two points are specific to this population.  A content hash over a
882	   short text is a fingerprint of that text: a reader who can guess the
883	   message can confirm the guess.  This revision hashes the plain
884	   content octets, as the companion does, so that the two sides need no
885	   shared secret to agree; a keyed or salted construction that would
886	   defeat the guess is a claim-set change and is not defined here.  A
887	   deployment whose effects are short and guessable SHOULD treat the
888	   extract and the records as confidential to the audit.  The subject
889	   and actor identifiers are opaque to the profile but need not be
890	   opaque to a reader; a deployment SHOULD pseudonymize them before
891	   either object leaves its control.

7	          Cedulon: An Audit Layer for Agent-to-Agent Commerce
8	                         draft-dogru-cedulon-06

10	Abstract

12	   This document defines the Cedulon Protocol, an audit layer for agent-
13	   to-agent commerce.  Payment rails such as HTTP 402 flows (x402) and
14	   mandate protocols (AP2) already move value, and a mandate protocol
15	   can already refuse a spend before it happens and return signed
16	   receipts.  What they do not, by themselves, give a party that is
17	   neither payer nor rail operator is a retrievable record of that
18	   decision and a signed spend receipt that reconciles against an
19	   authenticated extract of the rail.  Cedulon specifies a Trade
20	   Manifest (signed offer before payment), a Policy Decision Point with
21	   default deny, a Spend Receipt (COSE/CWT claim set after a gated
22	   payment), epoch checkpoints, and rail-extract reconciliation.  The
23	   reconciliation shows that no settlement on the extract lacks a
24	   receipt and no settled receipt is absent from the extract.  That
25	   result is unconditional only when the verifier pins the rail key out
26	   of band and states the period under audit; otherwise the document
27	   requires it to be reported as conditional.  Checkpoints carry the
28	   suppression guarantee, so the document profiles the checkpoint as a
29	   Signed Statement, gives the verification algorithm a step that
30	   consumes the witness receipts returned for checkpoints, names what a
31	   witness holding a checkpoint the presented chain omits reports,
32	   brings equivocation within reach by comparing recorded copies against
33	   the presented chain, and states how checkpoint totals may be withheld
34	   without withholding the fact that they were.  No signed object may be
35	   verified against a key it carries itself, and a presented Trade
36	   Manifest must be bound both to the receipts that name it and to the
37	   terms those receipts claim.  The document also names a threat no
38	   adversary causes, a settlement recorded on a rail with no receipt
39	   behind it, and defines a Dispute Evidence Bundle (evidence, not an
40	   award) and optional SCITT anchoring.  The encodings earlier revisions
41	   called canonical are defined, and the exact input to every hash-
42	   valued field is stated, so that an independent verifier can be
43	   written from the text alone.  This revision adds the JSON counterpart
44	   of the CBOR duplicate-key rule, names the refusal of a non-empty
45	   unprotected header, and requests registration of the five media types
46	   the profile carries in its protected headers.  Cedulon is not a
47	   competitor to x402 or AP2; it sits above them.

160	1.  Introduction

162	   _Note to Readers:_ This document is submitted as Informational.  The
163	   author's eventual intended track, if the work is taken up, is a
164	   Standards Track profile of COSE [RFC9052] and CWT [RFC8392] for
165	   agent-spend receipts.  This -06 does not claim IETF consensus.

167	   Agents can now pay.  Open HTTP 402 protocols [X402] attach stablecoin
168	   settlement to ordinary requests.  Card networks and processors issue
169	   agent-scoped tokens.  Google's Agent Payments Protocol (AP2) [AP2]
170	   binds user intent to signed mandates.

172	   What is missing is an interoperable *audit layer*: a machine-
173	   checkable answer to "was this spend allowed by policy, against which
174	   offer, and what bytes were delivered?"  The third question is
175	   answered to the extent the evidence carries it: machine-checkably
176	   where an attributable payee countersignature binds a deliveredHash
177	   (Section 5.1), and narrowed to "what bytes were promised, and what
178	   hash was presented beside them" where none does - a bundle must not
179	   claim more than its signatures cover.  Without this layer, a prompt-
180	   injected or looping agent can drain a rail that has already accepted
181	   a valid signature.  A counterparty can ship the wrong artifact.  A
182	   transparency log, if used at all, is proprietary.

184	   Cedulon fills that gap.  It does not clear funds, hold custody, or
185	   operate a payment facilitator.  An optional escrow actor is defined
186	   only as a third-party role interface (Section 16.13).
187	   Implementations of this specification MUST NOT take custody of funds
188	   or operate escrow (MUST-T8-custody).

190	   The control is an old one.  Reconciling an internal ledger against an
191	   external statement is what double-entry bookkeeping [PACIOLI] made
192	   routine, and signing the artifacts on both sides is Grigg's triple-
193	   entry idea [GRIGG].  Neither is claimed here.  What this document
194	   contributes is an open wire profile for that control in a setting
195	   where the parties are software: a COSE receipt shape, an extract
196	   shape, a checkpoint chain, and a verification algorithm precise
197	   enough that two implementations reach the same finding on the same
198	   evidence.  The novelty is interoperability, not the idea.

200	   Neighbor drafts are complementary, not substitutes. draft-bates-atp
201	   [BATES-ATP] covers tamper-evident causal lineage as a signed DAG.
202	   Cedulon is the completeness layer: a spend that never produced a
203	   receipt is visible when an authenticated rail extract is reconciled
204	   (MUST-T10-1).

206	2.  Terminology

208	   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT",
209	   "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and
210	   "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in
211	   BCP 14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all
212	   capitals, as shown here.

214	   The following terms are used:

216	   Trade Manifest:  A signed statement produced *before* payment.  It
217	      binds a description of goods or service, price, currency,
218	      acceptance-criteria hash, cancel condition, expiry, and an
219	      optional AP2 mandate reference.

221	   Policy Decision Point (PDP):  The function that evaluates a
222	      structured spend request against stored policy.  The default is
223	      deny.

225	   Spend Receipt:  A signed statement produced *after* a gated payment
226	      attempt.  It binds payer, payee, amount, currency, policy hash,
227	      manifestHash or an explicit noManifest flag, rail payment
228	      reference, timestampMs, nonce, prevReceiptHash, and outcome.

230	   Receipt Issuer:  The party that signs Spend Receipts.

232	   Anchor:  An optional SCITT Transparency Service [RFC9943] that
233	      registers a signed statement and returns a COSE receipt [RFC9942].

235	   Dispute Evidence Bundle:  A package of the Trade Manifest, the Spend
236	      Receipt, and a delivery hash.  It is evidence for a later human or
237	      legal process.  It is not an arbitral award and not an escrow
238	      release.

240	   Decision Token:  A portable, single-use PDP allow encoded as
241	      COSE_Sign1.  The claim set binds requestHash, policyHash,
242	      expiryMs, nonce, and singleUseId.  See Section 8.

244	   Rail Extract:  An authenticated list of settlement records for one
245	      account, one rail, and one time window.  See Section 9.

247	   Presented-unattested:  The state of a receipt or checkpoint the
248	      verifier holds no pinned issuer key for.  Its signature is checked
249	      against the key the object itself carries (Section 6.3), which
250	      establishes internal consistency only, so the object is neither
251	      attested nor rejected: every presented object is weighed as one
252	      set and the completeness guarantee is reported as conditional.  It
253	      is a state of the report, not a finding code.  See Section 10.1.

255	3.  Architecture

257	   Cedulon has three control-plane objects and one optional log:

259	     Principal --policy--> PDP --allow/deny--> x402/AP2 rail
260	                                 |
261	                                 v
262	                         Receipt Issuer --> Spend Receipt
263	                                 |
264	                                 v
265	                         Anchor / SCITT (optional)

267	   The payer agent never talks to the rail except through an adapter
268	   that calls the PDP first (MUST-T5-1).

270	3.1.  Policy Decision Point

272	   The PDP evaluates structured fields only (MUST-T1-1): amount,
273	   currency, payee, tool identifier, nonce, optional manifest hash, and
274	   evaluation time.  It applies limit, velocity, and scope checks (MUST-
275	   T2-1, MUST-T2-2).  If the PDP is unreachable, uninitialized, or
276	   throws, the result is deny (MUST-T2-3).  Denied attempts do not
277	   increment success counters (MUST-T2-4).

279	   An allow produces a Decision Token whose requestHash covers six
280	   fields: amount, currency, payee, tool, nonce, and manifestHash (MUST-
281	   T3-4, MUST-T6-1).  The token is a COSE_Sign1 object (MUST-T6-4), is
282	   single-use (MUST-T6-2), and MAY be carried to the adapter that
283	   performs settlement.

285	3.2.  Receipt Issuer

287	   After the adapter attempts settlement (success or a recorded deny
288	   that still needs an audit trail for an allowed-then-aborted path),
289	   the Receipt Issuer signs a Spend Receipt over the deterministic CBOR
290	   encoding of its claims (MUST-T4-1).  Verifiers reject bad signatures
291	   and byte mismatch (MUST-T4-2).

293	3.3.  Anchor / SCITT

295	   Parties MAY register the signed receipt (or a privacy-preserving hash
296	   encoding) as a SCITT Signed Statement [RFC9943] and attach the COSE
297	   receipt (MAY-T4-6).  This document does not operate a Transparency
298	   Service.

300	4.  Trade Manifest

302	   A Trade Manifest is the commerce analogue of a promise: it is issued
303	   *before* value moves.  It is conceptually symmetric to a later Spend
304	   Receipt (promise then proof), and it MAY carry an AP2 mandate hash so
305	   that user intent and the Cedulon offer stay linked (SHOULD-T8-5).

307	   A Trade Manifest MUST bind all of the following (MUST-T8-1):

309	   *  goods or service description

311	   *  price (integer minor units, encoded as a decimal string matching
312	      0|[1-9][0-9]*)

314	   *  currency (ISO 4217 alphabetic or a documented token identifier)

316	   *  acceptance-criteria hash (SHA-256 [RFC6234] of the exact delivery
317	      bytes, lowercase hexadecimal)

319	   *  cancel condition (opaque string agreed by the parties)

321	   *  expiry (POSIX milliseconds, expiresAtMs)

323	   -03 allowed this hash to be taken over "the exact delivery bytes or a
324	   declared schema instance" and gave a verifier no way to tell which
325	   one an issuer had used.  Two implementations reading the same
326	   manifest would then compute different digests over the same delivery
327	   and neither would be wrong.  This revision defines the first reading
328	   only.  Hashing a schema instance instead would need a marker in the
329	   manifest saying so, this document defines no such marker, and until
330	   one is defined that use is out of scope rather than an alternative a
331	   verifier is expected to guess at.

333	   It MAY include ap2MandateHash.  The corresponding CBOR label is
334	   always present; a missing mandate is encoded as CBOR null.

336	   It MAY name a payee.  An offer that is specific to one counterparty
337	   carries that party's payee identifier; when present, every receipt
338	   that names this manifest has its payee compared against it as exact
339	   octets under MUST-T8-9, on that requirement's two-branch severity.
340	   An open offer legitimately omits the member, and no comparison is
341	   made.  Unlike ap2MandateHash, the label is encoded only when the
342	   member is present, so a manifest signed before -05 carries the same
343	   bytes and keeps verifying; this is why the null convention above does
344	   not apply to it.

346	   The manifest is COSE_Sign1 [RFC9052] over a deterministic CBOR claim
347	   map (Section 6). manifestHash is the SHA-256 of the signed COSE bytes
348	   (MUST-T8-7).  A spend bound to a manifest MUST be denied if the
349	   requested amount or currency differs from the manifest (MUST-T8-2) or
350	   if the manifest is expired (MUST-T3-3).

352	   A spend that is not bound to a verified manifest MUST be marked
353	   noManifest on the receipt and MUST still pass limit, velocity, and
354	   scope checks (MUST-T1-2).  An implementation MAY refuse all
355	   noManifest spend (MAY-T1-4).

357	5.  Spend Receipt

359	   The Spend Receipt claim set is carried in COSE_Sign1 [RFC9052]
360	   wrapping a CWT-compatible map [RFC8392].  New receipts MUST use the
361	   COSE profile (Section 6).

363	   Claims (MUST-T4-3, MUST-T4-4, MUST-T4-7):

365	    +=================+===============================================+
366	    | Claim           | Description                                   |
367	    +=================+===============================================+
368	    | payer           | Payer agent identifier                        |
369	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
370	    | payee           | Payee identifier                              |
371	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
372	    | amount          | Minor units as a decimal string 0|[1-9][0-9]* |
373	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
374	    | currency        | Currency identifier                           |
375	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
376	    | policyHash      | SHA-256 of the canonical policy document      |
377	    |                 | (lowercase hex)                               |
378	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
379	    | manifestHash    | SHA-256 of the signed manifest COSE bytes, or |
380	    |                 | null when noManifest is true                  |
381	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
382	    | noManifest      | Boolean; MUST be true if and only if          |
383	    |                 | manifestHash is null                          |
384	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
385	    | x402PaymentRef  | Rail payment reference, or null               |
386	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
387	    | timestampMs     | POSIX milliseconds                            |
388	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
389	    | nonce           | Unique spend nonce; at least 128 bits of      |
390	    |                 | randomness; unique in the issuer scope        |
391	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
392	    | prevReceiptHash | Previous receipt hash, or null for the first  |
393	    |                 | receipt (SHOULD-T4-5)                         |
394	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
395	    | outcome         | settled or aborted                            |
396	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+

398	                                  Table 1

400	   A receipt with outcome = settled MUST have a non-null x402PaymentRef
401	   (MUST-T4-7).  An aborted receipt MUST NOT be added into checkpoint
402	   totals.

404	   All twelve labels in Section 6.1 are always present.  An empty
405	   optional value is encoded as CBOR null, never by omitting the label.

407	   receiptHash is the SHA-256 of the receipt's signed COSE bytes,
408	   encoded as lowercase hex.

410	   Verifiers MUST reject a receipt if the signature fails or if the
411	   decoded claim map does not match the presented claims (MUST-T4-2).

413	5.1.  Optional payee countersignature

415	   A payee MAY attach a countersignature over the issuer's signed Spend
416	   Receipt (MAY-T8-10).  The profile uses a *detached* COSE_Sign1
417	   [RFC9052] whose payload is a CBOR map with private-use labels:

419	     +========+===============+======================================+
420	     | Label  | Claim         | CBOR type                            |
421	     +========+===============+======================================+
422	     | -70401 | receiptCose   | bstr (exact issuer COSE_Sign1 bytes) |
423	     +--------+---------------+--------------------------------------+
424	     | -70402 | deliveredHash | bstr (optional; SHA-256 of the exact |
425	     |        |               | delivered bytes)                     |
426	     +--------+---------------+--------------------------------------+

428	                                  Table 2

430	   The countersignature uses the header profile in Section 6 and content
431	   type application/cedulon-countersign+cbor.

433	   This is a second Sign1 object, not RFC 9052 Countersignature0
434	   (unprotected-header label 11).  Countersignature0 would write into
435	   the issuer object and change receiptHash after issue, breaking the
436	   receipt chain.  A detached Sign1 keeps the issuer bytes stable,
437	   reuses kid and content-type, and is absent by simply omitting the
438	   sibling object.

440	   Absence of a countersignature MUST NOT invalidate the issuer receipt
441	   (MAY-T8-10).  A countersignature travels beside the issuer signature
442	   without being covered by it, so anyone holding an honest receipt can
443	   append one of their own.  Attribution is therefore the gate: a
444	   countersignature that cannot be attributed to the pinned payee key -
445	   the signature fails, kid or content type does not match, label -70401
446	   is not the issuer COSE bytes (MUST-T8-8), or the signature is valid
447	   under some other key - MUST be rejected as approval evidence.  What
448	   is rejected is the countersignature, not the receipt: the verdict on
449	   the untouched issuer receipt MUST NOT change because an
450	   unattributable object was attached.  An appendable object the issuer
451	   signature does not cover must not be able to manufacture a negative
452	   result; this is the lesson MUST-T8-9 already encodes for the manifest
453	   comparison, applied one object over.  The identifiers countersign-bad
454	   (unverifiable) and countersign-key-mismatch (verifiable under another
455	   key) name the discarded object as warnings, and where the verifier
456	   pinned a payee key and no attributable countersignature remains, the
457	   countersign-missing warning still applies: a discarded forgery is the
458	   absence of the payee's word, not a substitute for it.

460	   The optional deliveredHash claim binds delivery to the receipt under
461	   the payee's key.  A payee who countersigns MAY include the SHA-256 of
462	   the exact bytes it received in the same signed payload as the issuer
463	   receipt bytes.  When an attributable countersignature carries
464	   deliveredHash and the verifier also holds the Trade Manifest, the two
465	   digests are compared as exact octets: deliveredHash against
466	   acceptanceCriteriaHash.  A mismatch is delivery-mismatch, and it is a
467	   finding rather than a warning, because both ends of the comparison
468	   are signed.  A deliveredHash carried by an unattributable
469	   countersignature is discarded with it.  A countersigner MUST refuse
470	   to sign a deliveredHash that is not 32 octets; a verifier that meets
471	   one anyway treats the countersignature as carrying no deliveredHash,
472	   so the delivery question narrows as it does when the claim is absent,
473	   and the signature verdict does not move.  A countersignature without
474	   the claim is valid exactly as before -05, which introduced it.

476	   A Dispute Evidence Bundle that includes a verified countersignature
477	   has stronger evidence that the payee accepted those bytes; the bundle
478	   is still not an award (MUST-T8-4).

480	6.  COSE Profile

482	   This profile uses deterministic CBOR [RFC8949] Section 4.2.1
483	   (definite lengths, shortest integer form, map keys sorted in
484	   *bytewise lexicographic* order of their encoded keys).
485	   Implementations MUST encode only the types used by Cedulon claim
486	   maps: null, bool, unsigned and negative integers, UTF-8 text, byte
487	   strings, arrays, and maps (MUST-T4-1).

489	   -03 bound the encoder and said nothing about the decoder, which left
490	   the reading side free where the writing side was not.  A decoder MUST
491	   refuse a CBOR map that carries a duplicate encoded key (MUST-T4-18).
492	   The encoding rules already forbid producing one, so a decoder that
493	   accepts it accepts a document no conforming encoder can produce, and
494	   two decoders may disagree on which of the two values wins, which is a
495	   disagreement about what was signed.

497	   A decoder MUST also impose a bound on what it will attempt: on
498	   encoded size, on nesting depth, and on the number of elements it will
499	   decode from an audit input.  It MUST refuse an input that exceeds a
500	   bound with a named refusal rather than by exhausting memory or the
501	   stack, and it SHOULD document the bounds it applies (MUST-T4-19).
502	   This document fixes no numbers.  A bound that is right for a desktop
503	   verifier is wrong for a service, and a number written here would be
504	   wrong for one of them within a year.  What a reader is entitled to is
505	   that the refusal is a refusal, named and reported, rather than a
506	   crash that an operator has to interpret.

508	6.1.  Claim labels

622	6.2.  COSE_Sign1 headers

624	   The protected header MUST be a deterministic CBOR map containing
625	   (MUST-T4-1, MUST-T4-8):

627	   *  1 (alg) = -19 (Ed25519, [RFC9864]; the generic EdDSA value -8 from
628	      [RFC9053] is deprecated for this profile)

630	   *  3 (content type) = a tstr that distinguishes the payload:
631	      application/cedulon-receipt+cbor, application/cedulon-
632	      checkpoint+cbor, application/cedulon-manifest+cbor, application/
633	      cedulon-decision+cbor, or application/cedulon-countersign+cbor

635	   *  4 (kid) = bstr, mandatory.  The profile computes kid as the first
636	      eight bytes of SHA-256 over the issuer's SubjectPublicKeyInfo DER,
637	      in the Ed25519 SubjectPublicKeyInfo encoding of [RFC8410].  A
638	      verifier MUST obtain the public key from an authenticated channel
639	      (preconfigured issuer set, directory, or transparency statement)
640	      and MUST reject a message whose kid does not match that key.

642	   The unprotected header MUST be empty, and a decoder MUST refuse a
643	   message whose unprotected header is not an empty map, by the name
644	   cose-sign1-unprotected, rather than verify the signature and ignore
645	   the header (MUST-T4-21).  The reason is in Section 7.1: every digest
646	   over a signed object in this profile is computed over the COSE_Sign1
647	   octets, which include the unprotected header, while the signature
648	   does not cover it.  A decoder that ignored a stuffed header would
649	   verify the signature and compute a receiptHash the issuer never
650	   produced, so one honestly signed receipt could carry as many digests
651	   as a stranger cared to give it, and a chain built on prevReceiptHash
652	   would follow whichever copy it was handed.  The posted -05 stated the
653	   header empty and said nothing about the decoder; the companion
654	   decoder ignored the header until the question of what the digest
655	   covers was measured.  The payload MUST be the CBOR encoding of the
656	   claim map.  The signature is Ed25519 [RFC8032] over the COSE
657	   Sig_structure ["Signature1", protected, h'', payload].

659	6.3.  How a signed object is presented

661	   The signed octets above are what this profile defines and what every
662	   digest in Section 7.1 is taken over.  An object handed to a verifier
663	   travels with a little more than that, and the previous revisions used
664	   one of those members - the key an object carries - without ever
665	   saying where it came from.

667	   A presented Spend Receipt, epoch checkpoint, Trade Manifest, or
668	   Decision Token carries, beside the signed octets, its claim set or
669	   body in decoded form and *the signer's public key as a
670	   SubjectPublicKeyInfo PEM*; a countersigned receipt carries the
671	   payee's key the same way, and an object presented in the JSON
672	   encoding of Section 7 repeats its signature there as base64.  None of
673	   these is inside the signed octets, which is the whole point of naming
674	   them here: the signature covers the COSE message and nothing else, so
675	   every one of these members is a surface anyone holding the object can
676	   rewrite.  This is the same shape the Rail Extract states in
677	   Section 9, and it is stated once here for the COSE objects rather
678	   than left to be inferred from an implementation.

680	   A carried key is not an identity source and MUST NOT be used as one
681	   (MUST-T4-11).  Under a pin it has exactly one effect: an object that
682	   verifies under the pinned key while carrying a different key is
683	   reported as carried-key-mismatch, a warning, and stays attested
684	   (Section 10.1).  With no pin held it is the only key present, so the
685	   signature check that runs against it says the object is internally
686	   consistent and says nothing about who signed it; two issuers cannot
687	   be told apart in that state.

689	7.  Canonical JSON encoding

691	   Not everything this document hashes or signs is CBOR.  The policy
692	   document, the six request fields bound by a Decision Token, and the
693	   scoped body of a Rail Extract are JSON, and -03 called each of them
694	   "canonical" without saying what that meant.  Two implementations
695	   could therefore agree on every requirement in this document and still
696	   produce different bytes, which makes an independent verifier
697	   impossible to write from the text.  This section closes that.

699	   Where this document says "the canonical encoding" of a JSON document,
700	   it means the encoding defined by [RFC8785], and the octets hashed or
701	   signed are the UTF-8 octets of that encoding.

703	   Three notes on the boundary of that reference:

705	   *  [RFC8785] Section 3.1 takes I-JSON [RFC7493] as its input, and an
706	      I-JSON object carries no duplicate member names.  This document
707	      makes that precondition a rule at every place a verifier receives
708	      a JSON document as text - a rail extract body, a policy document,
709	      a stored receipt file: a text in which any object, at any depth,
710	      repeats a member name MUST be refused by the name json-duplicate-
711	      key, before the text is parsed (MUST-T4-20).  The rule is measured
712	      on the text because a parser that keeps either value has already
713	      discarded the evidence of the other, and two verifiers parsing one
714	      text could then canonicalize two different documents and sign or
715	      check two different octet strings, which is the JSON form of the
716	      disagreement MUST-T4-18 forbids for CBOR.  A verifier handed an
717	      object rather than text, as a tool behind a JSON-RPC boundary is,
718	      cannot apply the rule and MUST NOT report that it did; whether the
719	      text was checked is then the transport's to state.  The posted -05
720	      said nothing about this.  The companion implementation refused
721	      such texts before the sentence existed, and its conformance runner
722	      carried the difference as a recorded split against -05 rather than
723	      as a pass.

725	   *  [RFC8785] Section 3.2.2.2 requires a serializer to terminate on a
726	      lone surrogate, and so does this document: a producer MUST refuse
727	      to encode a document containing one, by name, and MUST NOT sign
728	      what it could not encode.  A verifier reading bytes it cannot
729	      canonicalize for this reason reports the input as failing
730	      verification with the refusal named beside the verdict; it does
731	      not crash.  No field defined by this document may contain a lone
732	      surrogate, so a conforming document never reaches this rule. -04
733	      permitted emitting the escaped form instead, which contradicted
734	      the RFC it cited; that permission is removed.

736	   *  [RFC8785] defines no encoding for an integer outside the IEEE 754
737	      double range.  No document defined here carries one: every amount
738	      and cumulative limit is already a decimal string before it is
739	      encoded, and every hash is lowercase hexadecimal.  A document that
740	      would need such an integer is outside this specification.

742	7.1.  Which octets are hashed

744	   Every hash-valued field in this document is SHA-256 [RFC6234] of the
745	   input named below.  All but three are rendered as lowercase
746	   hexadecimal. kid differs only in its rendering: the digest is
747	   computed over the same stated input and then truncated to its first 8
748	   bytes, carried as a byte string rather than as hex (Section 6 states
749	   the same rule where the header is defined). ap2MandateHash differs in
750	   whose digest it is: AP2 defines the mandate and its octets, and this
751	   document carries the result opaquely rather than restating a rule it
752	   does not own. deliveredHash differs in its carrier: it is a claim in
753	   a CBOR map and is carried as the raw 32 digest bytes (bstr), not as
754	   hex; comparisons against acceptanceCriteriaHash are made over the
755	   digest value. -03 named the digest for some of these fields and not
756	   for others; the omissions were not a deliberate degree of freedom.

758	        +========================+===============================+
759	        | Field                  | Input to SHA-256              |
760	        +========================+===============================+
761	        | receiptHash            | the signed COSE_Sign1 octets  |
762	        |                        | of the receipt                |
763	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
764	        | manifestHash           | the signed COSE_Sign1 octets  |
765	        |                        | of the Trade Manifest         |
766	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
767	        | checkpointHash         | the signed COSE_Sign1 octets  |
768	        |                        | of the checkpoint             |
769	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
770	        | statementHash          | the signed COSE_Sign1 octets  |
771	        |                        | of the statement              |
772	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
773	        | acceptanceCriteriaHash | the exact delivery bytes, as  |
774	        |                        | defined where the Trade       |
775	        |                        | Manifest is                   |
776	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
777	        | deliveredHash          | the exact bytes the payee     |
778	        |                        | received; the same input rule |
779	        |                        | as acceptanceCriteriaHash,    |
780	        |                        | computed by the other party   |
781	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
782	        | policyHash             | the UTF-8 octets of the       |
783	        |                        | canonical policy document     |
784	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
785	        | requestHash            | the UTF-8 octets of the       |
786	        |                        | canonical six-field request   |
787	        |                        | document                      |
788	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
789	        | kid                    | the SubjectPublicKeyInfo DER; |
790	        |                        | the digest is then truncated  |
791	        |                        | to its first 8 bytes          |
792	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
793	        | ap2MandateHash         | the octets AP2 defines for    |
794	        |                        | its mandate; not profiled by  |
795	        |                        | this document                 |
796	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+

798	                                 Table 7

800	   Wherever this table, or any other sentence in this document, says
801	   "the signed COSE_Sign1 octets", those are the octets of the
802	   *untagged* four-element COSE_Sign1 array of [RFC9052].  This profile
803	   never wraps a message in CBOR tag 18, and the vectors in Appendix A
804	   carry the untagged form.  A verifier that hashed a tagged copy would
805	   compute a different digest for every object in this profile, so the
806	   choice is stated here once rather than left to be inferred from the
807	   vectors.

809	   The six fields of the request document are the ones MUST-T6-1 names:
810	   amount, currency, payee, tool, nonce, and manifestHash. -03 described
811	   requestHash as "the six-field hash" while naming SHA-256 for
812	   policyHash in the same sentence, which left a reader free to conclude
813	   that the request binding was not a digest at all.  It is one.

815	   Naming the members is not stating the document, so the document is
816	   stated here.  The request document is a JSON object carrying exactly
817	   those six members and no others, every member always present. amount
818	   is the decimal string of the request, in the amount syntax the
819	   receipt claims table states, never a JSON number: [RFC8785] encodes
820	   the number 1 and the string "1" differently, and an implementation
821	   free to pick either would produce two digests for one request.
822	   currency, payee, and nonce are the request's text strings. tool is
823	   the request's text string, or JSON null where the deployment names
824	   none. manifestHash is the lowercase hexadecimal string, or JSON null
825	   for a spend bound to no manifest; an absent value is null, never an
826	   omitted member.  A document with a seventh member, a missing member,
827	   or another type for one of these is not the request document this
828	   section defines.

830	   The policy document is different on purpose, and the difference is
831	   scope rather than an oversight.  Its member set is the deployment's
832	   own: this document defines how the bytes of whatever policy document
833	   a PDP evaluates are encoded (Section 7) and digested, not what its
834	   members are. policyHash binds a spend to the exact bytes its PDP
835	   evaluated; it is not a value two deployments are expected to compute
836	   from a shared schema, and nothing in the verification algorithm
837	   compares one deployment's policyHash to another's.

839	8.  Decision Token

841	   A Decision Token is the portable encoding of a PDP allow.  It is
842	   COSE_Sign1 with the header profile in Section 6 and the labels in
843	   Section 6.1.  All five labels are always present (MUST-T6-4).

845	   requestHash MUST be the SHA-256 of the canonical encoding of the six
846	   fields the PDP evaluated (MUST-T6-1), rendered as lowercase
847	   hexadecimal; Section 7 defines that encoding and Section 7.1 states
848	   the octets. -03 called this "the six-field hash" in the same sentence
849	   that named SHA-256 for policyHash, which left the digest for one of
850	   them unstated. policyHash MUST be the SHA-256 of the canonical policy
851	   document the PDP evaluated. expiryMs is a Unix time in milliseconds
852	   after which the token MUST be treated as expired (SHOULD-T6-3):
853	   expired when the evaluation time is strictly greater than expiryMs,
854	   not yet expired at exactly expiryMs, on the same boundary discipline
855	   MUST-T3-3 states for the manifest. nonce is the request nonce.
856	   singleUseId is the identifier consumed on the first settlement
857	   attempt (MUST-T6-2).

859	   A party that accepts a Decision Token MUST reject it if the signature
860	   fails, if kid does not match a configured PDP key, if the content
861	   type is not application/cedulon-decision+cbor, if the decoded claim
862	   map does not match the presented claims, or if expiryMs is in the
863	   past (MUST-T6-5).

867	   A verifier checks completeness against a *rail extract*, not against

878	         | ref         | string (rail payment reference)         |

880	         | amount      | string matching 0|[1-9][0-9]*           |
881	         +-------------+-----------------------------------------+
882	         | currency    | string                                  |
883	         +-------------+-----------------------------------------+
884	         | timestampMs | number (POSIX milliseconds, an integer) |

887	                                  Table 8

889	   These member names are normative.  A rail MAY add members of its own

891	   names were the rail's to define, which contradicted this table and
892	   made the extract unconstructable from the text: a verifier reading

902	   mismatch).  Resolving a reference to a beneficiary is a feature of
903	   the rail's own system: this profile does not assume it, and measures
904	   it only when the rail declares it.

906	9.2.  Scope

908	   An extract is scoped to one account identifier, one rail identifier,
909	   and one half-open time window [windowStartMs, windowEndMs).  The
910	   signed body is one JSON document with exactly this shape:

912	        +===============+=========================================+
913	        | Member        | JSON type                               |
914	        +===============+=========================================+
915	        | accountId     | string                                  |
916	        +---------------+-----------------------------------------+
917	        | railId        | string                                  |
918	        +---------------+-----------------------------------------+
919	        | windowStartMs | number (POSIX milliseconds, an integer) |
920	        +---------------+-----------------------------------------+
921	        | windowEndMs   | number (POSIX milliseconds, an integer) |
922	        +---------------+-----------------------------------------+
923	        | clockSkewMs   | number (milliseconds, a non-negative    |
924	        |               | integer; optional; see Section 11)      |
925	        +---------------+-----------------------------------------+
926	        | settlements   | array of settlement records (schema     |
927	        |               | above)                                  |
928	        +---------------+-----------------------------------------+

930	                                  Table 9

932	   All six named members except clockSkewMs MUST be present; a body
933	   missing one, or a record renaming a core member, MUST be refused by
934	   name at both ends - by the signer before it signs and by the verifier
935	   before it checks a signature - so a malformed extract is the same
936	   refusal on both sides rather than a signature verdict.  Additional
937	   members beyond these are the rail's to add, as with records.

939	   The integer-valued members - windowStartMs, windowEndMs, each
940	   record's timestampMs, and clockSkewMs - MUST be integers of magnitude
941	   at most 2^53 - 1, the range a JSON number carries exactly, and
942	   clockSkewMs MUST NOT be negative; a value outside those bounds, or a
943	   non-integer, is refused by name in the same way as a missing member.

945	   The body is read as text before it is read as an object.  A text in
946	   which any object repeats a member name is refused as json-duplicate-
947	   key at both ends, before parsing and before any signature is checked
948	   (MUST-T4-20, Section 7); a signer that parsed first would sign one of
949	   the two values and an honest verifier could check the other.

951	   clockSkewMs, when present, declares the boundary allowance the
952	   verifier applies at this window's edges during reconciliation
953	   (Section 11); when absent, the profile default of 300000 milliseconds
954	   (five minutes) applies.

956	9.3.  Authentication

958	   The rail signs Ed25519 [RFC8032] over the canonical encoding of the
959	   scoped body, which is a JSON document and therefore takes the
960	   encoding of Section 7: the signed octets are the UTF-8 octets of the
961	   [RFC8785] encoding of the body above.  The signature and the rail's
962	   public key travel beside the body, the signature as base64 and the
963	   key as a SubjectPublicKeyInfo PEM; neither is part of the signed
964	   octets.  A verifier MUST obtain the extract from the rail or from a
965	   signature the rail published (MUST-T10-7).  A deployment that cannot
966	   do so is running the reconciliation against evidence it did not
967	   obtain independently, and MUST report the guarantee as conditional.

969	   A signature on an extract proves internal consistency, not origin: a
970	   key generated by whoever produced the object verifies against itself.
971	   The verifier therefore MUST obtain the rail's public key out of band
972	   and MUST verify the extract signature against that key rather than
973	   against any key the extract carries (MUST-T10-8).  A verifier that
974	   holds no such key MUST treat the guarantee as conditional.

976	   Keys are compared as bytes.  A verifier MUST compare the pinned key
977	   and the key that signed the extract by their SubjectPublicKeyInfo DER
978	   encoding, not by any text encoding of it, so that the same key
979	   presented in a different envelope still compares equal (MUST-T10-9).

981	   A pinned key the verifier cannot decode is a fault in the verifier's
982	   own configuration, not evidence about the extract, and MUST be
983	   reported as trust-key-unreadable rather than as a key mismatch.

985	   What a verifier emits for an extract it cannot authenticate depends
986	   on whether it stated an expectation.  With no pinned key the verifier
987	   has asserted nothing, so an extract that does not carry a verifiable
988	   signature is unauthenticated-extract, a warning: completeness
989	   findings may still be computed, but the guarantee is *conditional* on
990	   the extract being authentic.  With a pinned key the verifier has
991	   asserted what it requires, and an extract that fails to meet it is a
992	   failure rather than a caveat; see the verification algorithm for
993	   which finding applies. -00 defined only the first case, and readers
994	   of -00 should note that -02 made the pinned case fail closed.  See
995	   Section 16.

997	9.4.  Scope agreement

999	   An extract declares a window and carries settlement records.  The two
1000	   MUST agree: a verifier MUST report every settlement record whose
1001	   timestampMs falls outside [windowStartMs, windowEndMs) as extract-
1002	   scope-mismatch, identified by that record's ref (MUST-T10-10).  This
1003	   check is about the extract's internal consistency and MUST be
1004	   performed whether or not a rail key is pinned.

1006	   A verifier that knows which account, rail, and window it is auditing
1007	   MUST also check the extract against that expectation and MUST fail
1008	   closed when the extract does not cover it (MUST-T10-11).  An extract
1009	   for another account or rail, or one whose window does not span the
1010	   period under audit, cannot support a completeness claim about that
1011	   period.

1013	   A verifier that states no period leaves the extract free to define
1014	   one, and an extract that reports on a millisecond balances as easily
1015	   as one that reports on a month.  Pinning a key establishes who
1016	   signed; only a stated period establishes what the signature had to
1017	   cover.  A verifier that has not stated the period under audit
1018	   therefore MUST emit unstated-audit-window and MUST treat the
1019	   guarantee as conditional (MUST-T10-15), whatever else verifies.

1021	10.  Trust roots

1023	   Section 9 states the rule for one object: a signature proves internal
1024	   consistency, not origin, so the verifier obtains the rail key out of
1025	   band and checks the extract against that key rather than against any
1026	   key the extract carries (MUST-T10-8). -02 already required a verifier
1027	   to obtain the public key from an authenticated channel and to reject
1028	   a kid that does not match that key (MUST-T4-8).  What -02 did not
1029	   carry was the verification algorithm, the separate root inputs, and
1030	   the error semantics that name a missing or mismatched pin.  This
1031	   section states those for every signed object in the profile.

1033	   The gap is not theoretical.  A verifier that checks a Spend Receipt
1034	   against the key the receipt carries accepts a receipt signed by any
1035	   key at all, including one an attacker minted for the occasion.  Such
1036	   a receipt matches a settlement the attacker was never authorised to
1037	   make, the settlement stops looking uncovered, and the audit reports
1038	   nothing.  The completeness property in Section 11 is then computed
1039	   over evidence that answers to nobody.

1041	10.1.  The issuer root

1043	   A verifier MUST obtain the issuer's public key out of band and MUST
1044	   verify Spend Receipt and epoch checkpoint signatures against that key
1045	   rather than against a key the object carries (MUST-T4-9).  A verifier
1046	   that holds no such key and is presented with any Spend Receipt or
1047	   epoch checkpoint MUST treat the completeness guarantee as conditional
1048	   and SHOULD report the condition; the identifier unauthenticated-
1049	   issuer is used for it in this implementation.

1051	   The condition names those two objects because an audit given no
1052	   receipts and no checkpoints rests on the extract alone.  There the
1053	   absent issuer root withholds nothing, and warning about a root the
1054	   audit never consulted would spend the warning where it carries no
1055	   information.

1057	   Reporting a mismatch is not sufficient on its own.  A receipt that
1058	   does not answer to the pinned issuer key MUST NOT count as coverage
1059	   for the settlement it names, and the settlement MUST still be
1060	   reported as uncovered (MUST-T4-10).  A verifier that reports the
1061	   mismatch and then lets the receipt match the settlement anyway has
1062	   described the attack in its output while still concluding that the
1063	   books balance.

1065	   Keys are compared as bytes, by their SubjectPublicKeyInfo DER
1066	   encoding, on the same terms as MUST-T10-9.  A pinned issuer key the
1067	   verifier cannot decode is a fault in its own configuration and MUST
1068	   be reported as trust-key-unreadable rather than as a mismatch against
1069	   the objects; where no pinned key can be decoded at all, nothing is
1070	   attested and the verifier MUST NOT fall back to accepting the keys
1071	   the objects carry (MUST-T4-11).  Falling back is how a mistyped
1072	   configuration becomes a bypass.

1074	   Membership in the attested set follows one rule: the signature
1075	   verifies under a pinned issuer key.  The kid header routes the check
1076	   to a candidate key; the key an object carries beside its signature is
1077	   not an identity source, because it travels outside the signed octets
1078	   and anyone can rewrite it.  Two consequences are stated so that
1079	   implementations do not diverge on them.  First, an honestly signed
1080	   object whose carried key was swapped stays attested: the swap is
1081	   reported (carried-key-mismatch, a warning) and MUST NOT move the
1082	   object out of the attested set or change the verdict, on the same
1083	   reasoning as Section 5.1 - a surface the signature does not cover
1084	   must not be able to manufacture a negative result.  Second, an object
1085	   that claims the pin, by its carried key or its kid, but does not
1086	   verify under it is excluded from the attested set and MUST still be
1087	   walked and named - for a receipt, receipt-chain-break with a
1088	   signature-failed detail - never silently dropped; an object that
1089	   neither verifies under the pin nor claims it is issuer-key-mismatch,
1090	   excluded, and the settlement it names stays uncovered.

1139	   A verifier MUST accept an issuer root that is a set of keys rather

1148	10.2.  The payee root

1150	   The optional countersignature in Section 5.1 travels beside the
1151	   issuer signature without being covered by it.  Anyone holding an
1152	   honest receipt can append a countersignature of their own, so a
1153	   verifier that checks it against the key carried next to it learns
1154	   only that some key signed something.

1156	   A countersignature MUST NOT be treated as evidence that the payee
1157	   approved the payment unless it verifies against a payee key the
1158	   verifier obtained out of band (MUST-T4-13).  Without such a key the
1159	   verifier SHOULD report the condition and MUST treat the guarantee as
1160	   conditional.

1162	   Naming a payee key states an expectation, and an expectation that
1163	   only fires when the evidence is present can be cancelled by deleting
1164	   the evidence.  Where a verifier has pinned a key for a payee, a
1165	   settled receipt naming that payee and carrying no *attributable*
1166	   countersignature MUST be reported (MUST-T4-14): a countersignature
1167	   that failed to verify, or verified under some other key, is discarded
1168	   under Section 5.1 and leaves the expectation open exactly as a
1169	   missing one does.  Otherwise an attacker removes their own failed
1170	   forgery - or appends one - and the report returns to unconditional.
1171	   The discarded object itself is a warning, never a failure of the
1172	   receipt it rode beside; Section 5.1 states the invariant.

1174	10.3.  The witness root

1176	   Section 11.3 describes what a transparency witness adds.  An
1177	   inclusion receipt checked against the key it carries says that some
1178	   log is internally consistent, and a log is cheap to invent.

1180	   A verifier MUST obtain the transparency service's public key out of
1181	   band and MUST verify inclusion receipts against it (MUST-T11-15).
1182	   Inclusion receipts that cannot be checked that way MUST NOT be used
1183	   as evidence, in either direction: they cannot establish that a
1184	   checkpoint was anchored, and they cannot establish that one was
1185	   withheld.  A verifier SHOULD report that it left them out.

1187	   A log holds statements from everyone who uses it.  A statement held
1188	   by a pinned log MUST additionally answer to the issuer root before it
1189	   counts as something that issuer published (MUST-T11-16); otherwise
1190	   another user's epoch, sitting in a shared log, reads as this issuer
1191	   publishing two checkpoints for one epoch.

1193	   Anchoring and withholding are different claims and need different
1194	   evidence.  Establishing that a checkpoint was logged needs only the
1195	   statement hash.  Establishing that an issuer withheld one needs to
1196	   know whose statement it is, which an inclusion receipt carrying no
1197	   statement body cannot say.  Such a receipt MUST NOT be used to report
1198	   a withheld checkpoint, and MUST NOT be silently discarded either: a
1199	   real withholding must not be buried by removing the body (MUST-
1200	   T11-17).

1202	10.4.  The decision root

1204	   A Decision Token is issued by the policy decision point and consumed
1205	   by the same deployment.  The consumer therefore holds the key it
1206	   signs with and has no reason to ask the token which key to check it
1207	   against.  A consumer MUST verify a Decision Token against its own
1208	   issuing key and MUST NOT accept one it cannot check that way (MUST-
1209	   T6-6).

1211	10.5.  The manifest root

1213	   A Trade Manifest is optional.  A deployment that presents none is not
1214	   missing a root, and this requirement does not make such an audit
1215	   conditional.  The forbidden case is the other one: a manifest is
1216	   presented, and the verifier accepts it because the key travelling
1217	   inside it verifies against itself.

1219	   A verifier that is presented with a Trade Manifest MUST obtain the
1220	   publisher's public key out of band and MUST verify the manifest
1221	   signature against that key, not against a key the manifest carries
1222	   (MUST-T4-15).  A verifier without such a key that is presented with a
1223	   Trade Manifest MUST report the completeness guarantee as conditional
1224	   and SHOULD report the condition; the identifier unauthenticated-
1225	   manifest is used for it in this implementation.  An audit presented
1226	   with no Trade Manifest is not made conditional by this requirement.

1228	   A pinned manifest key the verifier cannot decode is a fault in its
1229	   own configuration and MUST be reported as trust-key-unreadable rather
1230	   than as a mismatch, on the same terms as MUST-T4-11.  A manifest that
1231	   does not verify against a readable pin MUST be reported as manifest-
1232	   key-mismatch and MUST fail the audit.  Falling back to the key the
1233	   manifest carries is how a presented document becomes a bypass.

1235	   Attribution is one question and coverage is another, and a root that
1236	   answers only the first leaves the document doing work it was never
1237	   spent under.  A verifier presented with a Trade Manifest MUST compare
1238	   the manifest hash to the manifestHash of the receipts presented to
1239	   the audit and MUST report a manifest that no presented receipt
1240	   references (MUST-T4-17); the identifier manifest-covers-no-receipt is
1241	   used for it in this implementation, and the completeness guarantee is
1242	   conditional.  The comparison runs against those presented receipts,
1243	   including aborted ones, and is made before any extract window is
1244	   applied and before any issuer key is applied; a hash on an aborted
1245	   receipt, on a receipt outside the extract window, or on a receipt no
1246	   pinned key attests still counts as a reference.  This requirement
1247	   asks whether any receipt names the terms, not whether a settlement in
1248	   the window was made under them, and not whether the receipt that
1249	   names them is attributable.  A forged receipt can therefore silence
1250	   this warning.  That is accepted: what it silences is a statement that
1251	   terms were named, and the report it leaves behind is still marked
1252	   conditional and still carries the finding that the receipt answers to
1253	   no pinned key.  A correctly attributed manifest travelling beside a
1254	   set of receipts marked noManifest states terms nothing presented was
1255	   spent under, and a report that stays silent about it reads as terms-
1256	   backed when it is not.  This requirement does not reach the audit
1257	   presented with no Trade Manifest, which remains a deployment choice
1258	   under MUST-T1-2.

1260	   Naming a manifest is not obeying one, and that is the third place
1261	   this document has had to say the same thing twice.  A verifier
1262	   presented with a Trade Manifest MUST compare the amount, the currency
1263	   and the settlement time of every receipt that names it against the
1264	   manifest's amount, currency and expiry, and MUST report a receipt
1265	   that departs from them (MUST-T8-9); the identifier manifest-terms-
1266	   mismatch is used for it in this implementation.  Every receipt that
1267	   names the manifest is measured, aborted ones included: an aborted
1268	   receipt that carries the hash of terms and a departing amount
1269	   recorded an attempt against terms it misstates, and MUST-T4-17 next
1270	   door already counts it as a reference.  The time compared is the
1271	   receipt's timestampMs, against the boundary MUST-T3-3 states:
1272	   strictly after expiresAtMs departs, exactly at it does not.  Amount
1273	   and currency are compared on the exact-octet terms of MUST-T8-2 - the
1274	   audit asks whether the gate's own rules were kept, so it compares the
1275	   way the gate compares.

1277	   Where a usable issuer key is pinned, the comparison is made over the
1278	   receipts that verify under it and the audit fails.  Where none is
1279	   pinned, the departure is still reported and the audit does not fail
1280	   on it alone.  An issuer key is usable when the pinned issuer root
1281	   holds at least one key the verifier can decode.  A pinned root none
1282	   of whose keys decode is already trust-key-unreadable and attests
1283	   nothing, so the comparison takes the unpinned branch while that
1284	   finding stands; the audit has failed on the configuration fault, and
1285	   the departure is still said out loud without becoming a charge no
1286	   readable key backs. -03 stated the first case for both, and an
1287	   implementation showed why that is wrong: a receipt signed by any key
1288	   at all, carrying the right manifest hash and the wrong amount, made
1289	   the verifier report a breach that never happened, against a payment
1290	   reference the forger chose.  Reporting a departure costs nothing if
1291	   it is unattributable; failing an audit on it hands an attacker a way
1292	   to accuse an honest payer.

1294	   MUST-T4-17 and this requirement therefore differ, and the difference
1295	   is not an inconsistency.  Asking whether a manifest hash appears
1296	   anywhere is a question about a set of documents, and an
1297	   unattributable document is still in that set.  Saying that a named
1298	   party broke terms it signed is a charge, and a charge needs a key
1299	   behind it.

1301	   Only receipts that name the manifest are measured against it: reading
1302	   the terms onto a receipt that never claimed them would invent a
1303	   violation the payer did not commit.

1305	   The rules being enforced here are not new.  A gate already refuses a
1306	   bound spend whose amount or currency differs from the manifest (MUST-
1307	   T8-2) and one made against an expired manifest (MUST-T3-3).  Both
1308	   were written for the point where money moves, and an audit reads the
1309	   record after that point, where the gate is no longer present to be
1310	   asked.  Without a counterpart a receipt can carry the hash of terms
1311	   it breaks and the report still calls the books balanced.  This is a
1312	   finding rather than a condition on the guarantee: a verifier that
1313	   reports it held every root it needed, and the statement it is making
1314	   is unconditional.

1316	   The gate answers differently from the audit, and the difference is
1317	   deliberate.  A policy decision point presented with a Trade Manifest
1318	   it cannot attribute MUST refuse the payment rather than settle and
1319	   record the doubt (MUST-T4-16).  An audit describes what it found and
1320	   may say the result is conditional; a gate decides whether money
1321	   moves, and a settled payment carrying the hash of terms nobody
1322	   authorised cannot be withdrawn by reporting it afterwards.  The
1323	   receipt would record those terms as agreed.

1325	10.6.  What the roots do not cover

1327	   A verifier that supplies none of these roots is not making an error,
1328	   and this document does not require it to.  It is making a weaker
1329	   statement, and the guarantee it reports must say so.  With no issuer
1330	   key nothing distinguishes one submitted receipt from another, so
1331	   conditions computed across the submitted set - two receipts claiming
1332	   one settlement reference, for instance - cannot be attributed to
1333	   anyone and are reported as conditions of the submission rather than
1334	   as failures of a party.

1336	11.  Reconciliation and Epoch Checkpoints

1338	   Completeness is the property that, given an authenticated rail
1339	   extract, every settlement in the extract has a matching settled Spend
1340	   Receipt, every settled receipt has a matching settlement, receipt and
1341	   checkpoint hash chains verify, and checkpoint totals equal the sum of
1342	   *settled* receipts in the checkpoint window.  If a spend occurred
1343	   without a receipt, the missing receipt is itself the evidence (MUST-
1344	   T10-2).

1346	   A checkpoint published with its totals withheld (Section 11.1) cannot
1347	   contribute the last of those to the property.  It is not a violation
1348	   of completeness and it is not a demonstration of it either: the
1349	   comparison was not made, and a result that rests on a comparison
1350	   nobody made is conditional (MUST-T11-12).

1352	11.1.  Checkpoint claims

1354	   An epoch checkpoint MUST be COSE_Sign1-signed with the header profile
1355	   in Section 6 and MUST bind all of the following (MUST-T11-1):

1357	   epoch, startMs, endMs, receiptCount, chainHeadHash, totals, and
1358	   prevCheckpointHash.

1360	   The checkpoint window is half-open [startMs, endMs) (MUST-T11-7).
1361	   receiptCount MUST equal the number of receipts (settled and aborted)
1362	   whose timestampMs falls in that window. chainHeadHash MUST equal
1363	   receiptHash of the last receipt in that window - the last link, in
1364	   issuer order (the prevReceiptHash chain, as the verification
1365	   algorithm's step 6 defines it), of the chain inside the window, not
1366	   the last one presented or the latest timestampMs - or null if the
1367	   window is empty (MUST-T11-2).  Where totals is present it MUST sum
1368	   only receipts with outcome = settled; the one permitted absence is
1369	   the signed redaction below.

1371	   An issuer that publishes a checkpoint without its totals MUST encode
1372	   totals as null in the signed payload (MUST-T11-12).  An empty map is
1373	   an honest zero for an empty window and is not a redaction.  Because
1374	   the redaction is inside the signature, it cannot be added to, or
1375	   removed from, a checkpoint after signing.

1377	   A verifier MUST NOT accept a redaction asserted anywhere but the
1378	   signed payload (MUST-T11-13).  A presentation-layer flag alongside a
1379	   checkpoint is chosen by whoever presents it, which is the party under
1380	   audit; honouring such a flag would let that party switch off the
1381	   totals comparison for a checkpoint whose signed totals are wrong.
1382	   The structural claims (epoch, startMs, endMs, receiptCount,
1383	   chainHeadHash, prevCheckpointHash) MUST NOT be redacted: a checkpoint
1384	   missing any of them does not decode, and a verifier MUST treat it as
1385	   a failed checkpoint rather than as a redacted one.

1387	11.2.  Genesis and continuity

1389	   The first checkpoint in a presented chain is the genesis checkpoint
1390	   of that chain.  Its prevCheckpointHash MUST be null.  Epoch numbers
1391	   MUST be consecutive integers.  Adjacent windows MUST satisfy
1392	   next.startMs = prev.endMs (MUST-T11-8).

1394	   A later checkpoint that omits a prefix of earlier epochs (prefix
1395	   deletion) is detectable only if an external witness (transparency
1396	   log) has recorded the missing prefix (MUST-T11-9).  Without that
1397	   witness, T11 guarantees about suppression are *conditional*.

1399	   -01 stated that dependency and stopped there: nothing in its
1400	   verification algorithm read a witness receipt, so the witness had no
1401	   way to speak. -02 gave it one.  A verifier that holds witness
1402	   receipts for the period under audit compares what the witness
1403	   recorded against what the chain presented, and reports the difference
1404	   under its own name (Section 11.3).  A witness that holds a checkpoint
1405	   the presented chain omits is not the same condition as a chain that
1406	   leaves a gap in its own coverage, and the two MUST NOT be reported
1407	   under one identifier (MUST-T11-11).  The first says evidence is being
1408	   withheld; the second says the evidence shown is incomplete.  An
1409	   operator who cannot tell them apart cannot tell an incomplete record
1410	   from a concealed one.

1412	11.3.  The transparency witness

1414	   A checkpoint registered with a Transparency Service [RFC9943] is a
1415	   Signed Statement whose payload is the checkpoint COSE object and
1416	   whose content type is application/cedulon-checkpoint+cbor
1417	   (Section 14).  What comes back, and what it proves, is stated as two
1418	   named tiers, because -04 promised the mechanics of one tier while
1419	   describing the checks of the other.

1421	   *Tier 1 - the witness receipt.* The witness returns a co-signature
1422	   over the statement hash of what it recorded: a COSE_Sign1 whose
1423	   payload binds the statement hash, the entry index, and the witness's
1424	   tree head.  Verifying it establishes exactly one sentence - "the
1425	   witness signed for this hash" - and nothing more; in particular it
1426	   does not establish membership in an append-only log. -04 called this
1427	   object a transparency receipt and cited the verification mechanics of
1428	   [RFC9942] for it while describing a hash comparison; the admission in
1429	   Section 18, that the receipt was a signature over a statement rather
1430	   than a proof of log membership, was correct, and the protocol text
1431	   now says the same thing.  The [RFC9942] citation applies in tier 2,
1432	   where its mechanics are actually performed.

1434	   A verifier MAY be given witness receipts for the period under audit.
1435	   It is a distinct input from the presented checkpoint chain, and
1436	   supplying it is optional: a verifier given none performs the same
1437	   steps, and reports the same findings, that it would if this input did
1438	   not exist (MUST-T11-10).  Supplying an empty set is not the same as
1439	   supplying none.  An empty set says a witness is configured and
1440	   recorded nothing, which is itself reportable; absence says no witness
1441	   was consulted.

1443	   A receipt binds a statement hash, not a statement.  The body is not
1444	   carried by the receipt and a verifier will often not hold it.  Two
1445	   levels of checking follow from that, and they are not the same (MUST-
1446	   T11-10).

1448	   Every receipt MUST have its signature verified before it counts for
1449	   anything.  That is what establishes the hash as one the service
1450	   signed for, and it is all that comparing recorded hashes against
1451	   presented ones requires: the verifier computes the statement hash of
1452	   each presented checkpoint itself.

1454	   A receipt accompanied by the statement body carries more, and MUST be
1455	   checked further before that body is relied on.  The body's statement
1456	   hash MUST equal the hash the receipt binds, and the body MUST itself
1457	   verify as a checkpoint.  A body that fails either check proves
1458	   nothing and MUST be ignored rather than counted, while the receipt it
1459	   came with remains usable for the hash comparison.  Equivocation is
1460	   the case that needs a body, because it compares claims rather than
1461	   hashes.

1463	   *Tier 2 - log membership.* A verifier MAY additionally be given, for
1464	   one recorded statement, the registered Signed Statement bytes (the
1465	   candidate entry) and an inclusion proof.  When both are present, the
1466	   verifier MUST perform the verification of [RFC9942] Section 5.2.1
1467	   over them: hash the candidate entry bytes to obtain the leaf, apply
1468	   the proof to reproduce a root, and accept only when the leaf hash,
1469	   the proof's leaf index, and the reproduced root are all equal to the
1470	   statement hash, entry index, and tree head of one witness receipt
1471	   that verifies under the pinned witness key (MUST-T11-18).
1472	   Reproducing a root is deliberately not sufficient on its own: the
1473	   proof format below carries no domain separation between leaves and
1474	   interior nodes, and the exact match against a witness-signed receipt
1475	   is what closes the ambiguities that follow from that.  A pair that
1476	   fails this check, or a candidate entry presented without a proof,
1477	   MUST be reported as a failing finding; the identifier witness-
1478	   inclusion-invalid names it.

1480	   The inclusion proof is an audit path: the leaf index, and the sibling
1481	   hashes from the leaf's level up to the root, lowest level first.  The
1482	   tree is built over statement hashes as leaves; an interior node is
1483	   the SHA-256 of the concatenation of its two children's 32 raw bytes;
1484	   a level with an odd count pairs its last node with itself, so the
1485	   path needs no separate leaf count.

1487	   Where witness receipts are supplied and this pair is not, tier 2 was
1488	   not exercised, and the report MUST say so rather than letting the
1489	   tier pass silently (MUST-T11-19); the identifier witness-inclusion-
1490	   not-exercised names it, as a warning - the witness attested the
1491	   statement hash, and log membership was not proven.  A pair that
1492	   verifies is silent, like every other passing check in the algorithm.

1494	   What tier 1 establishes, and what it does not, is worth stating
1495	   plainly.  It establishes that the service signed for that statement.
1496	   Whether the statement is a member of an append-only log is tier 2's
1497	   question, answered only where tier 2's inputs were supplied and
1498	   verified; whether the log has ever equivocated remains a property of
1499	   the service and its own proofs.  A verifier that treats a tier-1
1500	   receipt alone as proof of log membership is claiming more than the
1501	   receipt carries.

1503	11.4.  Verification algorithm

1505	   A verifier MUST perform all of these steps and MUST report every
1506	   finding they produce (MUST-T10-1, MUST-T11-2).  They are numbered for
1507	   reference, not to require an evaluation order: no step short-circuits
1508	   another, and an implementation may evaluate them in any order that
1509	   produces the same set of findings.

1511	   The data dependencies are named, because "any order" read naively
1512	   would break them.  Step 15 decides which witness receipts, and which
1513	   statement bodies, survive checking; steps 14 and 16 consume what
1514	   survives.  Step 7's index of refs is what steps 8 and 9 reconcile.
1515	   Step 11 decides which checkpoints verified, and step 14 compares only
1516	   those together with what step 15 admitted.  An implementation that
1517	   ran a consumer against an unchecked producer would not produce the
1518	   same set of findings, so those orders are not among the permitted
1519	   ones.

1521	   The second is the issuer pin.  The step that resolves it decides the
1522	   *attested set* - the receipts and checkpoints that verify under a
1523	   usable pinned issuer key, or the whole presented set when no usable
1524	   key is pinned - and every later step that walks receipts or
1525	   checkpoints consumes that set: the chain walk in step 6, the indexing
1526	   and reconciliation in steps 7 through 9, the checkpoint comparisons
1527	   in steps 11 through 13, and the MUST-T8-9 comparison.  A receipt the
1528	   pin rejects is reported once and then excluded, which is what keeps
1529	   the settlement it names visible as uncovered (MUST-T4-10); an
1530	   implementation that let it back into any of those steps would let a
1531	   forged receipt cover a settlement, satisfy a checkpoint count, or
1532	   invent a terms charge.  Two checks deliberately stay on the presented
1533	   set whatever any key says, and MUST NOT acquire the dependency: MUST-
1534	   T4-17, which asks whether a manifest was named at all, and the per-
1535	   receipt defect checks that ask what a receipt says about itself. -04
1536	   asserted that nothing else in the list fed another step, which the
1537	   dependencies above had already made false; the assertion is withdrawn
1538	   and the list above is the inventory, maintained rather than
1539	   summarised.

1541	   When a step names an identifier in backticks, that identifier SHOULD
1542	   be used for the condition in diagnostic output.  The normative
1543	   requirement is the behaviour: report the condition, identified by the
1544	   ref or other handle given in the step.  The identifiers are not an
1545	   interoperability surface.

1547	   1.   Establish the subject of the audit.  When an extract is
1548	        supplied, the settlement records it carries are the ones
1549	        reconciled; a settlement list from any other source MUST NOT be
1550	        substituted for them (MUST-T10-12).  If the caller supplies both
1551	        and they differ, the verifier MUST report that the caller-
1552	        supplied list disagrees with the extract, and MUST still
1553	        reconcile the extract.  The identifier extract-settlement-
1554	        mismatch SHOULD be used for this condition in diagnostic output.

1556	   2.   Verify the extract signature against the out-of-band rail key
1557	        (MUST-T10-8, MUST-T10-9).  If no key is pinned, the verifier
1558	        MUST treat the completeness guarantee as conditional (MUST-
1559	        T10-7).  The identifier unauthenticated-extract SHOULD be used
1560	        for this condition in diagnostic output.  If a key is pinned and
1561	        cannot be decoded, the verifier MUST report that the pinned key
1562	        is unreadable.  The identifier trust-key-unreadable SHOULD be
1563	        used for this condition.  If a key is pinned and the signature
1564	        does not verify against it, or verifies against a different key,
1565	        the verifier MUST report that the extract is not signed by the
1566	        pinned key.  The identifier extract-key-mismatch SHOULD be used
1567	        for this condition.  A finding that puts the extract itself in
1568	        doubt MUST prevent an unconditional guarantee.

1570	   3.   Check scope.  The verifier MUST report each settlement record
1571	        whose timestampMs falls outside the declared window, identified
1572	        by that record's ref (MUST-T10-10).  When the verifier states an
1573	        expected account, rail, or window, it MUST report an extract
1574	        that does not cover it (MUST-T10-11).  The identifier extract-
1575	        scope-mismatch SHOULD be used for both conditions.  If the
1576	        verifier stated no period, it MUST treat the guarantee as
1577	        conditional (MUST-T10-15).  The identifier unstated-audit-window
1578	        SHOULD be used for this condition.

1580	   4.   Resolve each Spend Receipt against the issuer root
1581	        (Section 10.1) in one pass.  Decode the COSE_Sign1; a content
1582	        type that is not the receipt type, a decoder bound, or a decoded
1583	        claim map that does not match the presented claims is a named
1584	        refusal, not a signature verdict (MUST-T4-2, MUST-T4-8).  Then
1585	        ask one question: does the signature verify under a pinned
1586	        issuer key. kid routes the check to a candidate key and carries
1587	        no authority of its own; the carried key is not consulted for
1588	        membership at all.  The resolution table in Section 10.1 reads
1589	        the answer out per cell.  Every cell there is a named condition
1590	        plus a membership decision; no cell is a silent removal, and the
1591	        word "reject" in earlier revisions meant nothing more than a
1592	        cell of that table.  Where a countersignature is present,
1593	        Section 10.2 governs what it establishes (MUST-T4-13, MUST-
1594	        T4-14).  Where a Trade Manifest is presented, Section 10.5
1595	        governs it (MUST-T4-15): with no publisher key pinned the
1596	        verifier reports unauthenticated-manifest and the guarantee is
1597	        conditional; with a pin that cannot be read, trust-key-
1598	        unreadable; with a pin the manifest does not answer to,
1599	        manifest-key-mismatch; and with a manifest that no presented
1600	        receipt references, manifest-covers-no-receipt (MUST-T4-17).  A
1601	        receipt that names the manifest but departs from its amount,
1602	        currency, expiry or, where the manifest names one, payee is
1603	        reported as manifest-terms-mismatch; with a usable issuer key
1604	        pinned the comparison runs over the attested receipts and the
1605	        departure fails the audit, and with no usable issuer key it is a
1606	        warning over the presented receipts and does not by itself fail
1607	        the audit (MUST-T8-9).  An audit presented with no Trade
1608	        Manifest is not made conditional by this step.

1610	   5.   Scope the receipts.  Membership follows the ref binding first: a
1611	        receipt whose ref appears on the extract is reconciled against
1612	        this extract even when its own timestampMs falls outside the
1613	        declared window - the rail has signed that the settlement
1614	        belongs to the window, and the receipt follows its settlement.
1615	        The timestampMs sieve applies only to receipts the extract does
1616	        not name: such a receipt outside the window is not a
1617	        completeness failure against this extract (MUST-T10-16);
1618	        auditing a longer period requires extracts that cover it.  At
1619	        the window's edges the declared allowance applies (Section 9):
1620	        an unmatched settled receipt within clockSkewMs of windowEndMs,
1621	        and an unmatched settlement record within clockSkewMs of
1622	        windowStartMs, are reported as boundary-deferred, a warning,
1623	        rather than as step 8's completeness findings - two honest
1624	        clocks can disagree by less than the allowance, and both
1625	        verifiers of an honest edge payment would otherwise reach the
1626	        same false accusation (MUST-T10-17).  Where the following
1627	        window's extract is presented and verifies, a deferred receipt
1628	        whose ref appears on it is resolved and not reported, and one
1629	        whose ref does not appear hardens into the step 8 finding; a
1630	        deferred settlement record near the opening edge resolves
1631	        through this step's ref binding, since the prior window's
1632	        receipt that names its ref is reconciled here regardless of
1633	        timestamp.  The following window's extract closes or hardens
1634	        closing-edge deferrals only; an opening-edge record stays
1635	        deferred until a receipt in the presented bag names its ref,
1636	        whether or not a following extract is presented.  In a single-
1637	        window audit a deferred record keeps the guarantee conditional.
1638	        Receipts remain subject to every other check regardless of
1639	        window.

1641	   6.   Walk the attested receipts in issuer order.  Issuer order is the
1642	        order induced by the prevReceiptHash chain: the verifier
1643	        rebuilds the chain from the links, and the order in which
1644	        receipts were presented carries no weight. timestampMs is
1645	        issuer-asserted and is not an ordering source.  The first
1646	        prevReceiptHash MUST be null.  Each later prevReceiptHash MUST
1647	        equal receiptHash of the previous receipt.  A miss, and a
1648	        receipt the links cannot place, MUST be reported as a break in
1649	        the receipt chain.  The identifier receipt-chain-break SHOULD be
1650	        used for this condition.

1652	   7.   Index the attested settled receipts and extract records by ref.
1653	        A ref that appears more than once on either side MUST be
1654	        reported as a repeated reference (MUST-T10-6).  The identifier
1655	        duplicate-ref SHOULD be used for this condition.

1657	   8.   For each ref that appears exactly once on each side, require a
1658	        one-to-one match on ref AND amount AND currency (MUST-T10-1),
1659	        compared as exact octets on the terms of MUST-T8-2; step 9's
1660	        aggregation is the only place this algorithm reads an amount as
1661	        a number.  Amount or currency mismatch MUST be reported as a
1662	        settlement that does not match its receipt, identified by that
1663	        ref.  The identifier settlement-mismatch SHOULD be used for this
1664	        condition.  A settlement with no receipt MUST be reported as
1665	        lacking a receipt, identified by its ref (MUST-T10-2).  The
1666	        identifier settlement-without-receipt SHOULD be used for this
1667	        condition.  A settled receipt with no extract row MUST be
1668	        reported as a completeness failure (MUST-T10-3).  The identifier
1669	        receipt-without-settlement SHOULD be used for this condition.  A
1670	        settled receipt with a null rail ref MUST be reported as settled
1671	        without a rail reference; this check asks what a receipt says
1672	        about itself and runs over the presented receipts, attested or
1673	        not.  The identifier settled-without-ref SHOULD be used for this
1674	        condition.  Where a settlement record declares a beneficiary
1675	        (Section 9), it MUST be compared against the matched receipt's
1676	        payee as exact octets; a difference is beneficiary-mismatch and
1677	        fails the audit.  Where neither the manifest names a payee nor
1678	        any settlement record declares a beneficiary, the report MUST
1679	        carry counterparty-unbound, a scope record: ref, amount and
1680	        currency closed against the payer's account extract, and the
1681	        counterparty's identity was not bound.  It is a statement of
1682	        what the evidence did not cover, not a doubt about what it did,
1683	        so it does not move the verdict and does not by itself make the
1684	        guarantee conditional.

1686	   9.   A ref already reported as repeating MUST still be reconciled by
1687	        amount rather than dropped from the comparison (MUST-T10-13).
1688	        For each currency under that ref, compare the total settled
1689	        against the total receipted.  A settled total that exceeds the
1690	        receipted total MUST be reported as a settlement lacking a
1691	        receipt, and the finding MUST state the unaccounted amount.  The
1692	        identifier settlement-without-receipt SHOULD be used for this
1693	        condition.  A settled total that is less than the receipted
1694	        total MUST be reported as a settlement that does not match its
1695	        receipt, identified by that ref.  The identifier settlement-
1696	        mismatch SHOULD be used for this condition.  An amount on that
1697	        repeating ref that cannot be parsed as an integer MUST be
1698	        reported without abandoning the audit; the identifier malformed-
1699	        amount SHOULD be used for this condition.  A verifier MUST still
1700	        report findings for the remaining records.

1702	   10.  Aborted receipts are not matched to extract rows and are not
1703	        added to totals.

1705	   11.  Decode each checkpoint.  Reject a failed signature, and reject a
1706	        kid that does not match the key obtained for the checkpoint
1707	        issuer, on the same terms as a receipt (MUST-T4-8).  Require
1708	        receiptCount, chainHeadHash, and totals to match the attested
1709	        receipts in [startMs, endMs) as defined above (MUST-T11-2); a
1710	        receipt step 4 rejected is not among them, or a forged receipt
1711	        could satisfy a checkpoint count.  The identifier checkpoint-
1712	        total-mismatch SHOULD be used for a failed signature, a wrong
1713	        receiptCount, or totals that disagree, and checkpoint-head-
1714	        mismatch for a chainHeadHash that is not the last link, in
1715	        issuer order (step 6), of the chain inside [startMs, endMs) -
1716	        "last receipt" binds to the chain, not to presentation or to
1717	        timestampMs.  If the signed totals is null, the verifier cannot
1718	        perform the totals comparison for that checkpoint.  It MUST
1719	        report that the comparison was skipped and MUST treat the
1720	        completeness guarantee as conditional; the absence of a
1721	        comparison is not a passed comparison (MUST-T11-12).  The
1722	        identifier checkpoint-totals-redacted SHOULD be used for this
1723	        condition. receiptCount and chainHeadHash MUST still be checked.
1724	        A checkpoint that fails verification MUST NOT be treated as
1725	        redacted, whatever it claims about its own totals.

1727	   12.  Every chained receipt MUST fall in exactly one checkpoint
1728	        window.  A gap or double count MUST be reported as a window
1729	        coverage failure (MUST-T11-7, MUST-T11-8).  The identifier
1730	        window-coverage SHOULD be used for this condition.  The check
1731	        runs against the presented checkpoint windows and it is fail-
1732	        closed: a receipt that falls under none of them is this failure
1733	        - including every receipt when no checkpoint was presented, and
1734	        receipts after the last closed checkpoint's endMs.  The text
1735	        names that last state an *open epoch*, and it is auditable only
1736	        when the checkpoint that closes it is issued; the name explains
1737	        why the finding fired, it does not soften it.  Nothing about an
1738	        absent checkpoint is silent: uncovered evidence surfaces as a
1739	        finding rather than as a gap in the report.

1741	   13.  Walk checkpoints in epoch order. prevCheckpointHash MUST equal
1742	        the SHA-256 of the previous checkpoint COSE bytes, or null for
1743	        genesis (MUST-T11-4).  The identifier checkpoint-total-mismatch
1744	        SHOULD be used for a broken chain, which is the fourth condition
1745	        its table row names.

1747	   14.  If two successfully verified checkpoints share an epoch number
1748	        and have different hashes, the verifier MUST report equivocation
1749	        (MUST-T11-3).  The identifier equivocation SHOULD be used for
1750	        this condition.  The checkpoints compared here are those
1751	        presented *together with* any carried by verified witness
1752	        receipts (step 15).  Comparing only the presented chain cannot
1753	        raise this finding: MUST-T11-8, applied in step 12, requires
1754	        that chain's epochs to be consecutive, so no two of its members
1755	        share an epoch.  A copy recorded by a witness is where the
1756	        second one is found.

1758	   15.  If witness receipts were supplied, verify them against the out-
1759	        of-band witness key (Section 10.3); receipts that cannot be
1760	        checked that way are not evidence in either direction and the
1761	        verifier reports that it left them out (MUST-T11-15); the
1762	        identifier unauthenticated-witness SHOULD be used for this
1763	        condition.  Discard any whose signature fails (MUST-T11-10).  A
1764	        surviving receipt whose statement body verifies against the
1765	        issuer root is a statement that issuer published; one that does
1766	        not is another party's, and is not this issuer equivocating
1767	        (MUST-T11-16).  The survivors are the recorded statement hashes
1768	        used in step 16.  Where a receipt also carries the statement
1769	        body, discard that body unless its statement hash equals the one
1770	        the receipt binds and it verifies as a checkpoint; the surviving
1771	        bodies are what step 14 compares.  Discarding a body does not
1772	        discard its receipt.  This is tier 1 of Section 11.3.  Where the
1773	        tier-2 pair - the registered Signed Statement bytes and an
1774	        inclusion proof - was also supplied, verify it as Section 11.3
1775	        states: apply the proof to the candidate entry's hash and accept
1776	        only on an exact match with a witness-signed receipt (MUST-
1777	        T11-18); a pair that fails, or a candidate without a proof, is
1778	        witness-inclusion-invalid and the audit MUST fail.  Where
1779	        witness receipts were supplied and no tier-2 pair was, report
1780	        witness-inclusion-not-exercised as a warning (MUST-T11-19): the
1781	        witness attested the statement hash, and log membership was not
1782	        proven.

1784	   16.  Compare the surviving witness records against the presented
1785	        chain (MUST-T11-11).  For each presented checkpoint with no
1786	        surviving record, report that it is not anchored; the identifier
1787	        checkpoint-not-anchored SHOULD be used.  This is a warning: a
1788	        witness may have been configured after the checkpoint was
1789	        issued, and an operator's own gap is not evidence of
1790	        concealment.  For each surviving record whose statement is
1791	        absent from the presented chain, report that a recorded
1792	        checkpoint was withheld; the identifier checkpoint-withheld
1793	        SHOULD be used.  This is a finding and the audit MUST fail.  A
1794	        record that carries no statement body cannot say whose statement
1795	        it binds, so it MUST NOT produce this finding; it is reported as
1796	        an entry that could not be attributed and makes the guarantee
1797	        conditional, because a real withholding must not be buried by
1798	        removing the body (MUST-T11-17).  Such a record still
1799	        establishes anchoring in the first half of this step: proving
1800	        that a checkpoint was logged needs only its hash.  The verifier
1801	        MUST NOT report a withheld checkpoint as a window coverage
1802	        failure (MUST-T11-11).

1804	   17.  If any finding remains that is not a warning (a warning is a
1805	        condition that only makes the completeness guarantee
1806	        conditional), the audit MUST fail (MUST-T10-4).

2143	   A finding that puts the extract itself in doubt (extract-key-
2144	   mismatch, trust-key-unreadable, extract-scope-mismatch, or extract-
2145	   settlement-mismatch) MUST also prevent an unconditional guarantee,
2146	   not merely fail the audit.  A finding that puts a presented Trade
2147	   Manifest in doubt (manifest-key-mismatch, or trust-key-unreadable on
2148	   the manifest pin) does the same.

2150	   An unconditional guarantee therefore requires all of: an extract, a
2151	   pinned rail key the extract's signature verifies against, a stated
2152	   period the extract covers, an issuer root for whatever receipts and
2153	   checkpoints are presented, a manifest root for whatever Trade
2154	   Manifest is presented, no finding that puts the extract in doubt, and
2155	   no warning that withholds part of the comparison.  A checkpoint whose
2156	   totals were signed as withheld (checkpoint-totals-redacted) removes a
2157	   comparison the guarantee rests on, and a presented checkpoint a
2158	   supplied witness does not hold (checkpoint-not-anchored) leaves part
2159	   of the chain unwitnessed.  Either one makes the result conditional.
2160	   Anything less than the whole list is conditional, and the report MUST
2161	   say so.

2163	   An implementation MUST make the guarantee and any warnings visible in
2164	   whatever human-readable audit report it produces under this document,
2165	   not only in a returned structure (MUST-T10-14).  A report that says
2166	   the books balance while withholding that the balance is conditional
2167	   invites the reader to take a conditional result for an unconditional
2168	   one.

2170	   Checkpoints SHOULD be registered with a Transparency Service (SHOULD-
2171	   T11-5).  A test deployment MAY use an in-process append-only log as
2172	   the witness (MAY-T11-6).  Cedulon still MUST NOT take custody.

2174	   The guarantee named above is about completeness against the extract,
2175	   which is the subject of T10.  It is not a claim that no checkpoint
2176	   was suppressed.  Suppression is the subject of T11, and a report MUST
2177	   NOT be read as settling it when no witness was consulted: with no

2185	12.  Lifecycle

2187	   1.  *Manifest.* Parties sign a Trade Manifest (optional for metered
2188	       API spend; required for goods with acceptance criteria).

2190	   2.  *Policy check.* The adapter submits a structured request to the
2191	       PDP.  Default is deny.  An allow is a Decision Token (MUST-T6-4).

2193	   3.  *Payment.* On allow, the adapter performs the x402 (or other
2194	       rail) exchange using exactly the decision fields (MUST-T6-1).
2195	       The Decision Token is consumed (MUST-T6-2).  A reused nonce is
2196	       denied (MUST-T3-1, MUST-T3-2).  A tampered or expired token is
2197	       denied (MUST-T6-5).

2199	   4.  *Receipt.* The Receipt Issuer signs a Spend Receipt.  Rail
2200	       credentials MUST NOT appear in the receipt, logs, or tool results
2201	       (MUST-T5-2, MUST-T7-1).

2203	   5.  *Dispute Evidence Bundle.* If delivery bytes do not match the
2204	       acceptance-criteria hash, an implementation MUST be able to emit
2205	       a bundle of manifest + receipt + delivery hash (MUST-T8-3).  The
2206	       bundle MUST NOT be described as an arbitral award or escrow
2207	       release (MUST-T8-4).

2209	13.  Policy Semantics

2211	   Policy is default deny.  The engine understands three families of
2212	   rule:

2214	   *  *Limit:* maximum amount per payment; maximum cumulative amount per
2215	      window (MUST-T2-2).

2217	   *  *Velocity:* maximum number of allowed payments per window (MUST-
2218	      T2-1).

2220	   *  *Scope:* optional allow-lists for payee, currency, and tool name.

2222	   Fail-closed: missing engine, crash, or exception yields deny (MUST-
2223	   T2-3).  Implementations SHOULD emit stable reason codes (SHOULD-
2224	   T2-5).  Decision tokens SHOULD expire after a short TTL (SHOULD-
2225	   T6-3).

2227	   The agent-facing spend interface MUST invoke the PDP and MUST NOT
2228	   expose a parallel ungated rail call to the model (MUST-T5-1).

2230	   One boundary is stated here rather than left to be inferred.  In the
2231	   retrospective audit, "allowed by policy" is the Receipt Issuer's
2232	   signed assertion: the spend passed the issuer's gate under the
2233	   policyHash the receipt names.  The Decision Token is consumed at the
2234	   gate, and the verification algorithm never sees it; the audit does
2235	   not independently re-verify the PDP's allow.  The completeness side
2236	   of the audit has an independent leg - the rail extract - and the
2237	   policy side deliberately does not: that is a trust boundary of this
2238	   profile, not an oversight.  A deployment that wants the policy answer
2239	   to be independently verifiable needs a receipt-to-token binding,
2240	   which this revision does not define; it is named as a possible
2241	   extension (Section 19), and adding it would change what a receipt
2242	   carries, so it is not smuggled in here.

2244	14.  SCITT Anchoring

2246	   A Receipt Issuer or relying party MAY construct a SCITT Signed
2247	   Statement whose payload is either the Spend Receipt COSE object or a
2248	   privacy profile (Section 15) and register it with a Transparency
2249	   Service [RFC9943].  The service returns a COSE receipt [RFC9942].
2250	   Embedding that receipt yields a Transparent Statement.  Cedulon does
2251	   not define a new transparency algorithm.

2253	   An epoch checkpoint MUST be registrable on the same terms (MUST-
2254	   T11-14).  Its Signed Statement carries the checkpoint COSE_Sign1
2255	   object as the payload and application/cedulon-checkpoint+cbor as the
2256	   content type, which is among the media types Section 17 asks to have
2257	   registered and which, until then, is a placeholder like the rest.
2258	   Nothing else about registration differs from a receipt.

2260	   This is a short section for a requirement -01 was missing, and the
2261	   omission mattered more than its length suggests. -01 asked for
2262	   checkpoints to be registered (SHOULD-T11-5) while profiling only the
2263	   receipt here, so the object carrying the suppression guarantee had no
2264	   stated form to be registered in.  Two implementations could follow
2265	   -01 to the letter and register incomparable things.

2267	15.  Privacy Considerations

2269	   A public transparency encoding MUST support omitting or hashing payer
2270	   and payee identifiers and MUST support amount redaction or bucket
2271	   encoding (MUST-T9-1).  Implementations MUST NOT write government-ID
2272	   numbers, payment-instrument PAN, or street address into a public
2273	   statement (MUST-T9-2).  Default public anchors SHOULD publish
2274	   policyHash, manifestHash, receiptHash, and timestampMs rather than
2275	   full claims (SHOULD-T9-3).  A private auditor MAY receive an
2276	   unredacted receipt out of band (MAY-T9-4).

2278	   The paragraph above counts receipt fields.  A checkpoint publishes
2279	   something a receipt does not: a per-currency total for a whole
2280	   window, which discloses trading volume even when every individual
2281	   receipt is redacted (MUST-T9-5). -01 gave no rule for it, so an
2282	   implementation could publish that total, or withhold it in a way no
2283	   verifier could recognise, and neither reading contradicted the text.

2285	   The rule is the one stated in Section 11: totals MAY be withheld by
2286	   signing it as null (MUST-T11-12), and only that form counts as a
2287	   redaction (MUST-T11-13).  The structural claims are not redactable,
2288	   because a verifier that cannot read the window or the chain head
2289	   cannot check anything at all, and a checkpoint that hid them would be
2290	   indistinguishable from a broken one.

2292	   Withholding is honest and it is also a cost: a verifier that cannot
2293	   recompute the totals says so, and the completeness guarantee for that
2294	   window is conditional.  A deployment that wants an unconditional
2295	   result publishes the totals; a deployment that wants the volume
2296	   private accepts a conditional one.  What a deployment MUST NOT do is
2297	   obtain the unconditional result while withholding the evidence for
2298	   it.

3076	16.14.  T12: Settlement without a recorded receipt

3078	   The threats above are about a counterparty, a rail or an attacker.
3079	   This one is about the issuer's own implementation, and it produces
3080	   exactly the condition the rest of this document exists to make
3081	   detectable.

3083	   An issuer that settles a payment, appends the receipt in memory and
3084	   then persists its state has three steps where it could have two
3085	   outcomes.  If the write fails, the rail holds a settlement and the
3086	   receipt exists nowhere durable.  The next start reads a state that
3087	   does not contain it, and the audit in Section 11 reports settlement-
3088	   without-receipt against an honest issuer that did everything its own
3089	   policy asked.  The evidence is missing because the issuer lost it,
3090	   not because anyone hid it, and nothing in the report can tell those
3091	   apart.

3093	     +============+==================================================+
3094	     | ID         | Requirement                                      |
3095	     +============+==================================================+
3096	     | MUST-T12-1 | An issuer MUST NOT complete a settlement whose   |
3097	     |            | receipt it cannot record durably.  The ability   |
3098	     |            | to record MUST be established before value       |
3099	     |            | moves, not after.                                |
3100	     +------------+--------------------------------------------------+
3101	     | MUST-T12-2 | Where a settlement has been made and its record  |
3102	     |            | cannot be completed, the issuer MUST undo the    |
3103	     |            | settlement in every place it still controls: in  |
3104	     |            | memory, on the rail ledger it controls, and in   |
3105	     |            | any later write it has not yet issued.  A        |
3106	     |            | refused payment MUST NOT consume the nonce or    |
3107	     |            | the payment allowance it never used.             |
3108	     +------------+--------------------------------------------------+
3109	     | MUST-T12-3 | Two issuers MUST NOT share one durable state.    |
3110	     |            | An implementation that permits it MUST fail      |
3111	     |            | loudly rather than let one writer overwrite the  |
3112	     |            | other's receipt, and the failure MUST name what  |
3113	     |            | an operator can act on.                          |
3114	     +------------+--------------------------------------------------+
3115	     | MUST-T12-4 | Where a settlement has entered a rail the issuer |
3116	     |            | does not control, and the local record cannot be |
3117	     |            | completed, the outcome is indeterminate.  The    |
3118	     |            | issuer MUST NOT treat the payment as reversed,   |
3119	     |            | and MUST NOT return the authority to spend,      |
3120	     |            | unless it holds authenticated evidence that the  |
3121	     |            | rail did not complete the settlement or that a   |
3122	     |            | reversing entry completed.                       |
3123	     +------------+--------------------------------------------------+

3125	                                  Table 23

3127	   Ordering:  Settle-then-record is the natural order to write and the
3128	      wrong one to ship.  The record is what makes the settlement
3129	      accountable, so the record is what has to be secured first (MUST-
3130	      T12-1), and a settlement that cannot be recorded has to be undone
3131	      everywhere the issuer still controls, including the nonce and the
3132	      allowance it never used (MUST-T12-2).  That undo is what the in-
3133	      process RailLedger and the session tests measure.  Once value has
3134	      entered a rail the issuer does not control, a local snapshot
3135	      cannot retract it.  Persistence failing after that point leaves
3136	      the outcome indeterminate; authority is not returned without
3137	      authenticated evidence that the rail did not complete the
3138	      settlement or that a reversing entry did (MUST-T12-4).

3140	   Recovery:  A durable-state conflict is not necessarily fatal, but it
3141	      MUST NOT be silent, and an implementation that refuses every
3142	      subsequent write without offering a way back has turned a
3143	      recoverable condition into an outage.  The reason reported has to
3144	      separate the cases an operator would act on differently (MUST-
3145	      T12-3): a write that conflicted with another writer, a write that
3146	      failed, and a state another process is holding.  A single opaque
3147	      failure leaves the operator to guess which of those happened.

3149	   Observability:  A violation of this threat is not visible in the
3150	      evidence a verifier receives.  The audit sees a settlement with no
3151	      receipt and reports settlement-without-receipt, which is the same
3152	      finding an adversary would produce, and nothing in the extract or
3153	      the receipt set distinguishes an issuer that lost the evidence
3154	      from one that hid it.  That is why the requirements here fall on
3155	      the issuer rather than on the verifier, and why an operator-facing
3156	      reason is required rather than optional.

3160	   This document requests the registration of five media types in the
3161	   "Media Types" registry [RFC6838], in the standards tree, each
3162	   carrying the +cbor structured syntax suffix that [RFC8949] registers.
3163	   Each names one of the COSE_Sign1 objects this document defines and is
3164	   carried as the COSE content type header parameter (label 3) of that
3165	   object (Section 6).  The value is a normative check inside a
3166	   protected header (MUST-T4-8, MUST-T6-5), which is why the names
3167	   cannot stay unregistered while that check stands.  Registration in
3168	   the standards tree requires IETF approval; until then, an
3169	   implementation outside a closed deployment should treat these names
3170	   as placeholders that a registration may change.  The provisional
3171	   registration procedure of [RFC6838] Section 5.2.1 is available to an
3172	   Internet-Draft, and a provisional entry, if one is made, is
3173	   superseded by the registration this section requests.

3175	   The claim labels this document assigns inside the CBOR claim sets,
3176	   -70001 through -70402 (Section 6.1, Section 5.1), lie in the Private
3177	   Use range of the "CBOR Web Token (CWT) Claims" registry [RFC8392],
3178	   integer values less than -65536, and this document requests no
3179	   assignment for them.  A later Standards Track revision that moves
3180	   them into the assigned range will request new labels then, without
3181	   reinterpreting these.

3183	   No other IANA action is requested.

3777	      edge cases are locked as tests.

3779	   *  The abstract no longer says that payment rails and mandate
3780	      protocols lack a fail-closed policy check; a mandate protocol has
3781	      one, and returns signed receipts.  It names the missing piece as
3782	      what a party that is neither payer nor rail operator can retrieve
3783	      and reconcile against an authenticated rail extract.  A reply on
3784	      the DISPATCH list narrowed the claim, and the narrowing is right.

3786	18.3.  Changes from -03

3788	   -04 had one subject: -03 could not be implemented from its own text.
3789	   Eighteen decision points were read out of it by someone working from
3790	   the words alone, and eight of them had two defensible answers.
3791	   Nothing here adds a capability.  Everything here closes a place where
3792	   two conforming implementations would produce different bytes or reach
3793	   different verdicts.

3795	   Five of those eight were the same defect wearing different clothes.
3796	   -03 hashed or signed a JSON document in four places and called the
3797	   encoding "canonical" without ever defining it, and named the digest
3798	   for some hash-valued fields and not others.  Section 7 defines the
3799	   encoding by reference to [RFC8785], and Section 7.1 states, for every
3800	   hash-valued field, exactly which octets go in.  The heaviest of those
3801	   was requestHash: -03 called it "the six-field hash" in the same
3802	   sentence that named SHA-256 for policyHash, and a reader was entitled
3803	   to conclude it was not a digest at all.

3805	   MUST-T8-9 changes in a way a diff will show, and the change is
3806	   deliberate. -03 said an unpinned departure from manifest terms fails
3807	   the audit, full stop.  An implementation showed what that permits: a
3808	   receipt signed by any key, carrying the right manifest hash and a
3809	   wrong amount, makes a verifier report a breach that never happened
3810	   against a payment reference the forger picks.  The requirement now
3811	   separates the two cases and says why it differs from MUST-T4-17,
3812	   which is a naming question rather than a charge and is answered from
3813	   the presented set whether or not anything vouches for it.

3815	   MUST-T4-18 and MUST-T4-19 are new: -03 bound the encoder to
3816	   deterministic CBOR and said nothing about the decoder, neither about
3817	   a duplicate key nor about what a decoder does when an input is larger
3818	   than it is willing to read.  The second fixes no numbers.  A bound is
3819	   deployment policy; refusing by name rather than by running out of
3820	   stack is not.

3822	   MUST-T3-3 and MUST-T8-2 gain the boundary and the comparison rule
3823	   they were missing: whether a settlement exactly at expiry is inside
3824	   the manifest, and whether a currency may be case-folded before it is
3825	   compared.  MUST-T10-1 says what an audit with nothing in it reports.

3827	   The acceptance-criteria hash had two readings and no way to signal
3828	   which one was used, so two implementations would have hashed the same
3829	   delivery differently.  This revision defines one of them and puts the
3830	   other out of scope until something can say which is meant.

3832	   The verification algorithm now names its data dependencies. -03 named
3833	   one and said nothing else fed another step, which stopped being true
3834	   once the issuer pin began deciding the attested set: the chain walk,
3835	   the reconciliation, the checkpoint comparisons and MUST-T8-9 all
3836	   consume it, and the algorithm now says so, along with the two checks
3837	   that deliberately stay on the presented set.  The two severities of
3838	   MUST-T8-9 are now stated in the algorithm step and the finding table
3839	   as well as in the requirement, after an early draft of this revision
3840	   changed the requirement and left the step and the table carrying the
3841	   old unconditional verdict.

3843	   Three encodings that could be read two ways are now stated once each:
3844	   kid is the SHA-256 of the SubjectPublicKeyInfo DER truncated to its
3845	   first 8 bytes, in that order; "the signed COSE_Sign1 octets" are the
3846	   untagged array, never tag 18; and the six-field request document is
3847	   given as an exact JSON shape, member by member, rather than as a list
3848	   of names.  The hash-input table now covers acceptanceCriteriaHash and
3849	   says whose digest ap2MandateHash is.  The Decision Token gains the
3850	   same expiry boundary MUST-T3-3 states for the manifest.

3852	   Appendix A no longer defers to the tests of an implementation; a
3853	   specification that points at code cannot be implemented from its own
3854	   text, which is the property this revision is trying to restore.

3856	   One change is metadata rather than text: the submission stream in the
3857	   document's header changes from independent to IETF.  The Note to
3858	   Readers has named an eventual Standards Track intent since -00, and
3859	   Section 17 asks for Standards Tree registration; neither belongs on
3860	   the Independent Stream.  The rendered pages are unchanged by it.

3862	   What has not changed: MUST-T12-4 remains specified and not executed,
3863	   and this document still has no independent implementation written
3864	   from its text alone.  The point of this revision is to make that
3865	   possible, not to claim it happened.

3867	18.4.  Changes from -02

3869	   -03 had two subjects.  The first is that -02 stated a rule for signed
3870	   objects and left the implementation and the verification algorithm
3871	   without a counterpart for every object the rule applied to.  The
3872	   second is T12, which no reader reported and which is not about an
3873	   adversary at all.

3875	   -02 Section 6.2 already required a verifier to obtain the public key
3876	   from an authenticated channel (a preconfigured issuer set, a
3877	   directory, or a transparency statement) and to reject a message whose
3878	   kid does not match that key (MUST-T4-8).  That rule was general.
3879	   What -02 did not carry was the verification algorithm that applies it
3880	   to each signed object, the separate root inputs a verifier supplies
3881	   out of band, and the error semantics that name a missing, unreadable,
3882	   or mismatched pin.  A companion implementation that followed the
3883	   algorithm it had, rather than that prose, still checked a Spend
3884	   Receipt, an epoch checkpoint, a Decision Token and a transparency
3885	   inclusion receipt against the key travelling inside them.  A receipt
3886	   like that silences the settlement-without-receipt finding for the
3887	   settlement it names.  The completeness property is then computed over
3888	   evidence that answers to nobody.  Section 10 states the missing
3889	   algorithm and the error semantics, and the verification algorithm now
3890	   applies them.

3892	   A reader raised the inclusion-receipt half of this against the posted
3893	   -02 after checking the archived text against the implementation
3894	   commit.  Following it into the code turned up the other three, along
3895	   with two conditions that were not about keys at all.

3897	   The first is that reporting a mismatch is not enough.  An
3898	   implementation that names a foreign key and then still lets the
3899	   receipt match its settlement has described the attack in its output
3900	   while concluding that the books balance.  MUST-T4-10 now requires the
3901	   settlement to stay reported.

3903	   The second is that an expectation which only fires when the evidence
3904	   is present can be cancelled by deleting the evidence.  A pinned payee
3905	   key with no countersignature to check was silence, so removing a
3906	   countersignature removed the question with it.  MUST-T4-14 closes
3907	   that, and MUST-T11-17 closes the same shape in the witness: an
3908	   inclusion receipt with its body stripped off can no longer bury a
3909	   withholding, while still not being allowed to accuse anyone.

3911	   T12 is new and is not about an adversary.  An issuer that settles,
3912	   appends the receipt in memory and then writes its state will, when
3913	   that write fails, leave the rail holding a settlement whose receipt
3914	   exists nowhere durable.  Restarted, it reports settlement-without-
3915	   receipt against itself.  The condition this document exists to make
3916	   detectable was reachable through the implementation's own ordering,
3917	   and no requirement in -02 said otherwise.

3919	   The same first subject carries five requirements this section has not
3920	   named so far, all of them stated in Section 10.  A pinned key that
3921	   cannot be decoded MUST NOT fall back to the keys the objects carry
3922	   (MUST-T4-11).  An issuer root may comprise more than one key, and a
3923	   verifier must accept one that does, so a rotation inside the audited
3924	   window does not force it to choose between findings against honest
3925	   receipts and abandoning the pin (MUST-T4-12); the same set-of-keys
3926	   acceptance applies to a publisher, witness, or rail pin.  The same
3927	   out-of-band rule reaches the payee countersignature (MUST-T4-13), the
3928	   transparency witness (MUST-T11-15 and MUST-T11-16), the Decision
3929	   Token, whose consumer issued it and therefore already holds the key
3930	   to check it with (MUST-T6-6), and a presented Trade Manifest (MUST-
3931	   T4-15).

3933	   Two requirements belong to neither subject.  MUST-T7-5 and MUST-T7-6
3934	   come from measuring the protection a stored signing key actually has
3935	   instead of deriving it from the platform.  A mount that ignores
3936	   filesystem permissions accepts the call and protects nothing, and a
3937	   writable directory or a symbolic link anywhere on the path makes the
3938	   file permission moot.  They are stated because the implementation
3939	   reported protection it did not have.

3941	   Two things are stated here that -02 got right and -03 kept unchanged:
3942	   the extract rule itself, and the treatment of a pinned key the
3943	   verifier cannot decode.  What changed is their reach, and the change
3944	   is not backward compatible.  Where a verifier pinned the rail key,
3945	   supplied no issuer key, and was presented with receipts or
3946	   checkpoints, -02 reported the guarantee as unconditional and -03
3947	   reports it as conditional.  Nothing about the evidence changed; what
3948	   changed is that the guarantee now says which questions were never
3949	   asked.  An audit presented with neither is unaffected, for the reason
3950	   given in Section 10.1.

3952	18.5.  Changes from -01

3954	   -02 had one subject: the checkpoint, which carries the T11 guarantee
3955	   against suppression and rollback, was not wired into anything that
3956	   could discharge it.

3958	   A reader of -01 set out the gap and a second reader confirmed it
3959	   independently.  Four things were wrong at once, and they were the
3960	   same thing seen from four sides.  The SCITT anchoring section
3961	   profiled the Spend Receipt and not the checkpoint, so SHOULD-T11-5
3962	   asked for checkpoints to be registered without saying in what form
3963	   (MUST-T11-14 now says).  None of the steps of the verification
3964	   algorithm read a witness receipt, so a deployment could follow
3965	   SHOULD-T11-5 to the letter and still have a verifier that never
3966	   consulted the witness (steps 15 and 16 now do).  MUST-T11-3,
3967	   equivocation, could not fire at all: the only checkpoints compared
3968	   were the presented ones, and MUST-T11-8 requires those to be
3969	   consecutive, so no two of them can share an epoch.  A second copy is
3970	   found in a witness, and nothing brought one in.  And the checkpoint
3971	   binds a per-currency total for a window while the privacy section
3972	   counted only receipt fields, so a window total could be published, or
3973	   withheld, with no stated rule either way.

3975	   The repairs are MUST-T11-10 through MUST-T11-14.  The transparency
3976	   receipt is a new optional input; supplying none leaves the verifier
3977	   behaving exactly as in -01, which is deliberate, because the point of
3978	   the witness is to add a claim that could not be made before, not to
3979	   withdraw one that could.

3981	   Two decisions inside those repairs are worth stating on their own,
3982	   because a reader might reasonably have expected the other choice.

3984	   *  A recorded checkpoint the presented chain omits gets its own
3985	      identifier, checkpoint-withheld, rather than being folded into
3986	      window-coverage (MUST-T11-11).  The reporter asked which way it
3987	      should go.  Coverage says the record shown is incomplete; a
3988	      withheld checkpoint says the party under audit is holding a record
3989	      it did not show.  An operator who sees one identifier for both
3990	      cannot tell an incomplete record from a concealed one, which is
3991	      the distinction the whole threat is about.

3993	   *  A checkpoint with no receipt in a supplied witness is a warning
3994	      rather than a failure.  A witness may be configured after
3995	      checkpoints have already been issued, and an operator's own gap in
3996	      anchoring is not evidence that anything was concealed.  The
3997	      asymmetry is deliberate: what the witness holds and the chain does
3998	      not is a finding, what the chain holds and the witness does not is
3999	      a warning.

4001	   One change is not a repair of -01 but of the first attempt at this
4002	   revision, and it is recorded because the failure is instructive.
4003	   Signed totals were first made redactable through a field carried
4004	   alongside the checkpoint rather than inside the COSE payload.
4005	   Anything outside the signature is chosen by whoever presents the
4006	   object, which here is the party under audit, so a checkpoint whose
4007	   signed totals disagreed with its receipts could be re-presented as
4008	   redacted and the mismatch went unreported.  Redaction is now inside
4009	   the signature and a redaction asserted anywhere else MUST be ignored
4010	   (MUST-T11-12, MUST-T11-13).  This was the same shape as the bypass
4011	   -01 was written to close: a check that a party under audit could
4012	   switch off.

4014	   Reversal, refund, partial settlement, and the escrow role remain out
4015	   of scope and are still expected later, with no date. -01 said the
4016	   same, and no revision since has improved on it.

4018	   The reporters are named in the Acknowledgments.

4020	19.  Evolution and Future Work (Informative)

4022	   This section is a direction, not a commitment.  The structures below
4023	   are reserved in name only.  Normative wire formats, tests, and
4024	   threat-model MUST lines for them belong in later revisions (-07 or
4025	   later), written with the same discipline as this -06.

4027	19.1.  Re-attestation profile

4029	   Algorithms retire.  A Spend Receipt or checkpoint signed under
4030	   Ed25519 today may need a later verifier that no longer accepts -19.
4031	   A companion seed [REATTEST] sketches re-attestation: register the
4032	   original COSE bytes as a SCITT Signed Statement and have a current
4033	   algorithm countersign or receipt them.  The principle is that
4034	   structures outlive ciphers.  The first concrete example is the
4035	   profile's own move from generic EdDSA (-8) to Ed25519 (-19) in
4036	   [RFC9864].

4038	19.2.  Streaming reconciliation

4040	   Epoch checkpoints in this document are batch windows.  A later
4041	   revision may define a continuous, second-scale profile [STREAMING] in
4042	   which the same completeness relation is evaluated as settlements
4043	   arrive, without waiting for an epoch close.  That work does not
4044	   change the matching rules in this document, and it did not arrive in
4045	   this revision either.

4047	19.3.  Generalization

4049	   Payment is the special case that this -06 implements.  The same
4050	   completeness calculus (an authenticated extract of consumed units
4051	   reconciled to signed receipts) can apply to other consumable
4052	   resources such as compute, data, or energy.  This document does not
4053	   specify those profiles.

4055	20.  Informative Notes on Adjacent Protocols

4057	   x402 [X402] uses HTTP 402 [RFC9110] to negotiate stablecoin payment.
4058	   AP2 [AP2] uses signed mandates as verifiable credentials.  Cedulon
4059	   does not replace either protocol.  Profiles built on HTTP Message
4060	   Signatures [RFC9421] authenticate bots; they are not a spend receipt.
4061	   draft-bates-atp [BATES-ATP] is a lineage neighbor.  It does not
4062	   define rail-extract completeness.

4064	   draft-vauban-x402-stark-receipts [VAUBAN] specifies complementary
4065	   x402 receipt-format variants that a Cedulon Spend Receipt MAY carry
4066	   as a rail proof; it does not define rail-extract completeness. draft-
4067	   schrock-ep-outcome-binding [SCHROCK] compares authorized action bytes
4068	   to independently observed effects; it does not define rail-extract
4069	   completeness. draft-marques-asqav-compliance-receipts [MARQUES]
4070	   profiles access-control action receipts (the broader Acta family
4071	   includes [ACTA]); it does not define rail-extract completeness.
4072	   draft-hopley-x402-compliance-receipt [HOPLEY] records an admission-
4073	   time compliance decision; it does not define rail-extract
4074	   completeness.

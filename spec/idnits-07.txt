7	          Cedulon: An Audit Layer for Agent-to-Agent Commerce
8	                         draft-dogru-cedulon-07

10	Abstract

12	   This document defines the Cedulon Protocol, an audit layer for agent-
13	   to-agent commerce.  Payment rails such as HTTP 402 flows (x402) and
14	   mandate protocols (AP2) already move value, and a mandate protocol
15	   can already refuse a spend before it happens and return signed
16	   receipts.  What they do not, by themselves, give a party that is
17	   neither payer nor rail operator is a retrievable record of that
18	   decision and a signed spend receipt that reconciles against an
19	   authenticated extract of the rail.  Cedulon specifies a Trade
20	   Manifest (signed offer before payment), a Policy Decision Point with
21	   default deny, a Spend Receipt (COSE/CWT claim set after a gated
22	   payment), epoch checkpoints, and rail-extract reconciliation.  The
23	   reconciliation shows that no settlement on the extract lacks a
24	   receipt and no settled receipt is absent from the extract.  That
25	   result is unconditional only when the verifier pins the rail key out
26	   of band and states the period under audit; otherwise the document
27	   requires it to be reported as conditional.  Checkpoints carry the
28	   suppression guarantee, so the document profiles the checkpoint as a
29	   Signed Statement, gives the verification algorithm a step that
30	   consumes the witness receipts returned for checkpoints, names what a
31	   witness holding a checkpoint the presented chain omits reports,
32	   brings equivocation within reach by comparing recorded copies against
33	   the presented chain, and states how checkpoint totals may be withheld
34	   without withholding the fact that they were.  No signed object is
35	   attested by a key it carries itself, and a presented Trade Manifest
36	   must be bound both to the receipts that name it and to the terms
37	   those receipts claim.  The document also names a threat no adversary
38	   causes, a settlement recorded on a rail with no receipt behind it,
39	   and defines a Dispute Evidence Bundle (evidence, not an award) and
40	   optional SCITT anchoring.  The encodings earlier revisions called
41	   canonical are defined, and the exact input to every hash-valued field
42	   is stated, so that an independent verifier can be written from the
43	   text alone.  This revision states one distinction in the seven places
44	   that had kept its older absolute form: a key an object carries is
45	   never the signer's identity and never a substitute for a key the
46	   verifier did not obtain, while a signature checked against it where
47	   no key is held establishes internal consistency and attests nothing.
48	   It also brings the account and the rail under audit into the declared
49	   scope on the terms the period already had, forbids reading a
50	   settlement finding out of an extract the pinned rail key refused, and
51	   states the wire form of the witness receipt, whose media type it
52	   registers beside the other five.  Cedulon is not a competitor to x402
53	   or AP2; it sits above them.

168	1.  Introduction

170	   _Note to Readers:_ This document is submitted as Informational.  The
171	   author's eventual intended track, if the work is taken up, is a
172	   Standards Track profile of COSE [RFC9052] and CWT [RFC8392] for
173	   agent-spend receipts.  This -07 does not claim IETF consensus.

175	   Agents can now pay.  Open HTTP 402 protocols [X402] attach stablecoin
176	   settlement to ordinary requests.  Card networks and processors issue
177	   agent-scoped tokens.  Google's Agent Payments Protocol (AP2) [AP2]
178	   binds user intent to signed mandates.

180	   What is missing is an interoperable *audit layer*: a machine-
181	   checkable answer to "was this spend allowed by policy, against which
182	   offer, and what bytes were delivered?"  The third question is
183	   answered to the extent the evidence carries it: machine-checkably
184	   where an attributable payee countersignature binds a deliveredHash
185	   (Section 5.1), and narrowed to "what bytes were promised, and what
186	   hash was presented beside them" where none does - a bundle must not
187	   claim more than its signatures cover.  Without this layer, a prompt-
188	   injected or looping agent can drain a rail that has already accepted
189	   a valid signature.  A counterparty can ship the wrong artifact.  A
190	   transparency log, if used at all, is proprietary.

192	   Cedulon fills that gap.  It does not clear funds, hold custody, or
193	   operate a payment facilitator.  An optional escrow actor is defined
194	   only as a third-party role interface (Section 16.13).
195	   Implementations of this specification MUST NOT take custody of funds
196	   or operate escrow (MUST-T8-custody).

198	   The control is an old one.  Reconciling an internal ledger against an
199	   external statement is what double-entry bookkeeping [PACIOLI] made
200	   routine, and signing the artifacts on both sides is Grigg's triple-
201	   entry idea [GRIGG].  Neither is claimed here.  What this document
202	   contributes is an open wire profile for that control in a setting
203	   where the parties are software: a COSE receipt shape, an extract
204	   shape, a checkpoint chain, and a verification algorithm precise
205	   enough that two implementations reach the same finding on the same
206	   evidence.  The novelty is interoperability, not the idea.

208	   Neighbor drafts are complementary, not substitutes. draft-bates-atp
209	   [BATES-ATP] covers tamper-evident causal lineage as a signed DAG.
210	   Cedulon is the completeness layer: a spend that never produced a
211	   receipt is visible when an authenticated rail extract is reconciled
212	   (MUST-T10-1).

214	2.  Terminology

216	   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT",
217	   "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and
218	   "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in
219	   BCP 14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all
220	   capitals, as shown here.

222	   The following terms are used:

224	   Trade Manifest:  A signed statement produced *before* payment.  It
225	      binds a description of goods or service, price, currency,
226	      acceptance-criteria hash, cancel condition, expiry, and an
227	      optional AP2 mandate reference.

229	   Policy Decision Point (PDP):  The function that evaluates a
230	      structured spend request against stored policy.  The default is
231	      deny.

233	   Spend Receipt:  A signed statement produced *after* a gated payment
234	      attempt.  It binds payer, payee, amount, currency, policy hash,
235	      manifestHash or an explicit noManifest flag, rail payment
236	      reference, timestampMs, nonce, prevReceiptHash, and outcome.

238	   Receipt Issuer:  The party that signs Spend Receipts.

240	   Anchor:  An optional SCITT Transparency Service [RFC9943] that
241	      registers a signed statement and returns a COSE receipt [RFC9942].

243	   Dispute Evidence Bundle:  A package of the Trade Manifest, the Spend
244	      Receipt, and a delivery hash.  It is evidence for a later human or
245	      legal process.  It is not an arbitral award and not an escrow
246	      release.

248	   Decision Token:  A portable, single-use PDP allow encoded as
249	      COSE_Sign1.  The claim set binds requestHash, policyHash,
250	      expiryMs, nonce, and singleUseId.  See Section 8.

252	   Rail Extract:  An authenticated list of settlement records for one
253	      account, one rail, and one time window.  See Section 9.

255	   Presented-unattested:  The state of a receipt or checkpoint the
256	      verifier holds no pinned issuer key for.  Its signature is checked
257	      against the key the object itself carries (Section 6.3), which
258	      establishes internal consistency only, so the object is neither
259	      attested nor rejected: every presented object is weighed as one
260	      set and the completeness guarantee is reported as conditional.  It
261	      is a state of the report, not a finding code.  See Section 10.1.

263	3.  Architecture

265	   Cedulon has three control-plane objects and one optional log:

267	     Principal --policy--> PDP --allow/deny--> x402/AP2 rail
268	                                 |
269	                                 v
270	                         Receipt Issuer --> Spend Receipt
271	                                 |
272	                                 v
273	                         Anchor / SCITT (optional)

275	   The payer agent never talks to the rail except through an adapter
276	   that calls the PDP first (MUST-T5-1).

278	3.1.  Policy Decision Point

280	   The PDP evaluates structured fields only (MUST-T1-1): amount,
281	   currency, payee, tool identifier, nonce, optional manifest hash, and
282	   evaluation time.  It applies limit, velocity, and scope checks (MUST-
283	   T2-1, MUST-T2-2).  If the PDP is unreachable, uninitialized, or
284	   throws, the result is deny (MUST-T2-3).  Denied attempts do not
285	   increment success counters (MUST-T2-4).

287	   An allow produces a Decision Token whose requestHash covers six
288	   fields: amount, currency, payee, tool, nonce, and manifestHash (MUST-
289	   T3-4, MUST-T6-1).  The token is a COSE_Sign1 object (MUST-T6-4), is
290	   single-use (MUST-T6-2), and MAY be carried to the adapter that
291	   performs settlement.

293	3.2.  Receipt Issuer

295	   After the adapter attempts settlement (success or a recorded deny
296	   that still needs an audit trail for an allowed-then-aborted path),
297	   the Receipt Issuer signs a Spend Receipt over the deterministic CBOR
298	   encoding of its claims (MUST-T4-1).  Verifiers reject bad signatures
299	   and byte mismatch (MUST-T4-2).

301	3.3.  Anchor / SCITT

303	   Parties MAY register the signed receipt (or a privacy-preserving hash
304	   encoding) as a SCITT Signed Statement [RFC9943] and attach the COSE
305	   receipt (MAY-T4-6).  This document does not operate a Transparency
306	   Service.

308	4.  Trade Manifest

310	   A Trade Manifest is the commerce analogue of a promise: it is issued
311	   *before* value moves.  It is conceptually symmetric to a later Spend
312	   Receipt (promise then proof), and it MAY carry an AP2 mandate hash so
313	   that user intent and the Cedulon offer stay linked (SHOULD-T8-5).

315	   A Trade Manifest MUST bind all of the following (MUST-T8-1):

317	   *  goods or service description

319	   *  price (integer minor units, encoded as a decimal string matching
320	      0|[1-9][0-9]*)

322	   *  currency (ISO 4217 alphabetic or a documented token identifier)

324	   *  acceptance-criteria hash (SHA-256 [RFC6234] of the exact delivery
325	      bytes, lowercase hexadecimal)

327	   *  cancel condition (opaque string agreed by the parties)

329	   *  expiry (POSIX milliseconds, expiresAtMs)

331	   -03 allowed this hash to be taken over "the exact delivery bytes or a
332	   declared schema instance" and gave a verifier no way to tell which
333	   one an issuer had used.  Two implementations reading the same
334	   manifest would then compute different digests over the same delivery
335	   and neither would be wrong.  This revision defines the first reading
336	   only.  Hashing a schema instance instead would need a marker in the
337	   manifest saying so, this document defines no such marker, and until
338	   one is defined that use is out of scope rather than an alternative a
339	   verifier is expected to guess at.

341	   It MAY include ap2MandateHash.  The corresponding CBOR label is
342	   always present; a missing mandate is encoded as CBOR null.

344	   It MAY name a payee.  An offer that is specific to one counterparty
345	   carries that party's payee identifier; when present, every receipt
346	   that names this manifest has its payee compared against it as exact
347	   octets under MUST-T8-9, on that requirement's two-branch severity.
348	   An open offer legitimately omits the member, and no comparison is
349	   made.  Unlike ap2MandateHash, the label is encoded only when the
350	   member is present, so a manifest signed before -05 carries the same
351	   bytes and keeps verifying; this is why the null convention above does
352	   not apply to it.

354	   The manifest is COSE_Sign1 [RFC9052] over a deterministic CBOR claim
355	   map (Section 6). manifestHash is the SHA-256 of the signed COSE bytes
356	   (MUST-T8-7).  A spend bound to a manifest MUST be denied if the
357	   requested amount or currency differs from the manifest (MUST-T8-2) or
358	   if the manifest is expired (MUST-T3-3).

360	   A spend that is not bound to a verified manifest MUST be marked
361	   noManifest on the receipt and MUST still pass limit, velocity, and
362	   scope checks (MUST-T1-2).  An implementation MAY refuse all
363	   noManifest spend (MAY-T1-4).

365	5.  Spend Receipt

367	   The Spend Receipt claim set is carried in COSE_Sign1 [RFC9052]
368	   wrapping a CWT-compatible map [RFC8392].  New receipts MUST use the
369	   COSE profile (Section 6).

371	   Claims (MUST-T4-3, MUST-T4-4, MUST-T4-7):

373	    +=================+===============================================+
374	    | Claim           | Description                                   |
375	    +=================+===============================================+
376	    | payer           | Payer agent identifier                        |
377	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
378	    | payee           | Payee identifier                              |
379	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
380	    | amount          | Minor units as a decimal string 0|[1-9][0-9]* |
381	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
382	    | currency        | Currency identifier                           |
383	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
384	    | policyHash      | SHA-256 of the canonical policy document      |
385	    |                 | (lowercase hex)                               |
386	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
387	    | manifestHash    | SHA-256 of the signed manifest COSE bytes, or |
388	    |                 | null when noManifest is true                  |
389	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
390	    | noManifest      | Boolean; MUST be true if and only if          |
391	    |                 | manifestHash is null                          |
392	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
393	    | x402PaymentRef  | Rail payment reference, or null               |
394	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
395	    | timestampMs     | POSIX milliseconds                            |
396	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
397	    | nonce           | Unique spend nonce; at least 128 bits of      |
398	    |                 | randomness; unique in the issuer scope        |
399	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
400	    | prevReceiptHash | Previous receipt hash, or null for the first  |
401	    |                 | receipt (SHOULD-T4-5)                         |
402	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
403	    | outcome         | settled or aborted                            |
404	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+

406	                                  Table 1

408	   A receipt with outcome = settled MUST have a non-null x402PaymentRef
409	   (MUST-T4-7).  An aborted receipt MUST NOT be added into checkpoint
410	   totals.

412	   All twelve labels in Section 6.1 are always present.  An empty
413	   optional value is encoded as CBOR null, never by omitting the label.

415	   receiptHash is the SHA-256 of the receipt's signed COSE bytes,
416	   encoded as lowercase hex.

418	   Verifiers MUST reject a receipt if the signature fails or if the
419	   decoded claim map does not match the presented claims (MUST-T4-2).

421	5.1.  Optional payee countersignature

423	   A payee MAY attach a countersignature over the issuer's signed Spend
424	   Receipt (MAY-T8-10).  The profile uses a *detached* COSE_Sign1
425	   [RFC9052] whose payload is a CBOR map with private-use labels:

427	     +========+===============+======================================+
428	     | Label  | Claim         | CBOR type                            |
429	     +========+===============+======================================+
430	     | -70401 | receiptCose   | bstr (exact issuer COSE_Sign1 bytes) |
431	     +--------+---------------+--------------------------------------+

438	   The countersignature uses the header profile in Section 6 and content
439	   type application/cedulon-countersign+cbor.

441	   This is a second Sign1 object, not RFC 9052 Countersignature0
442	   (unprotected-header label 11).  Countersignature0 would write into
443	   the issuer object and change receiptHash after issue, breaking the
444	   receipt chain.  A detached Sign1 keeps the issuer bytes stable,
445	   reuses kid and content-type, and is absent by simply omitting the
446	   sibling object.

448	   Absence of a countersignature MUST NOT invalidate the issuer receipt
449	   (MAY-T8-10).  A countersignature travels beside the issuer signature
450	   without being covered by it, so anyone holding an honest receipt can
451	   append one of their own.  Attribution is therefore the gate: a
452	   countersignature that cannot be attributed to the pinned payee key -
453	   the signature fails, kid or content type does not match, label -70401
454	   is not the issuer COSE bytes (MUST-T8-8), or the signature is valid
455	   under some other key - MUST be rejected as approval evidence.  What
456	   is rejected is the countersignature, not the receipt: the verdict on
457	   the untouched issuer receipt MUST NOT change because an
458	   unattributable object was attached.  An appendable object the issuer
459	   signature does not cover must not be able to manufacture a negative
460	   result; this is the lesson MUST-T8-9 already encodes for the manifest
461	   comparison, applied one object over.  The identifiers countersign-bad
462	   (unverifiable) and countersign-key-mismatch (verifiable under another
463	   key) name the discarded object as warnings, and where the verifier
464	   pinned a payee key and no attributable countersignature remains, the
465	   countersign-missing warning still applies: a discarded forgery is the
466	   absence of the payee's word, not a substitute for it.

468	   The optional deliveredHash claim binds delivery to the receipt under
469	   the payee's key.  A payee who countersigns MAY include the SHA-256 of
470	   the exact bytes it received in the same signed payload as the issuer
471	   receipt bytes.  When an attributable countersignature carries
472	   deliveredHash and the verifier also holds the Trade Manifest, the two
473	   digests are compared as exact octets: deliveredHash against
474	   acceptanceCriteriaHash.  A mismatch is delivery-mismatch, and it is a
475	   finding rather than a warning, because both ends of the comparison
476	   are signed.  A deliveredHash carried by an unattributable
477	   countersignature is discarded with it.  A countersigner MUST refuse
478	   to sign a deliveredHash that is not 32 octets; a verifier that meets
479	   one anyway treats the countersignature as carrying no deliveredHash,
480	   so the delivery question narrows as it does when the claim is absent,
481	   and the signature verdict does not move.  A countersignature without
482	   the claim is valid exactly as before -05, which introduced it.

484	   A Dispute Evidence Bundle that includes a verified countersignature
485	   has stronger evidence that the payee accepted those bytes; the bundle
486	   is still not an award (MUST-T8-4).

488	6.  COSE Profile

490	   This profile uses deterministic CBOR [RFC8949] Section 4.2.1
491	   (definite lengths, shortest integer form, map keys sorted in
492	   *bytewise lexicographic* order of their encoded keys).
493	   Implementations MUST encode only the types used by Cedulon claim
494	   maps: null, bool, unsigned and negative integers, UTF-8 text, byte
495	   strings, arrays, and maps (MUST-T4-1).

497	   -03 bound the encoder and said nothing about the decoder, which left
498	   the reading side free where the writing side was not.  A decoder MUST
499	   refuse a CBOR map that carries a duplicate encoded key (MUST-T4-18).
500	   The encoding rules already forbid producing one, so a decoder that
501	   accepts it accepts a document no conforming encoder can produce, and
502	   two decoders may disagree on which of the two values wins, which is a
503	   disagreement about what was signed.

505	   A decoder MUST also impose a bound on what it will attempt: on
506	   encoded size, on nesting depth, and on the number of elements it will
507	   decode from an audit input.  It MUST refuse an input that exceeds a
508	   bound with a named refusal rather than by exhausting memory or the
509	   stack, and it SHOULD document the bounds it applies (MUST-T4-19).
510	   This document fixes no numbers.  A bound that is right for a desktop
511	   verifier is wrong for a service, and a number written here would be
512	   wrong for one of them within a year.  What a reader is entitled to is
513	   that the refusal is a refusal, named and reported, rather than a
514	   crash that an operator has to interpret.

516	6.1.  Claim labels

630	6.2.  COSE_Sign1 headers

632	   The protected header MUST be a deterministic CBOR map containing
633	   (MUST-T4-1, MUST-T4-8):

635	   *  1 (alg) = -19 (Ed25519, [RFC9864]; the generic EdDSA value -8 from
636	      [RFC9053] is deprecated for this profile)

638	   *  3 (content type) = a tstr that distinguishes the payload:
639	      application/cedulon-receipt+cbor, application/cedulon-
640	      checkpoint+cbor, application/cedulon-manifest+cbor, application/
641	      cedulon-decision+cbor, application/cedulon-countersign+cbor, or
642	      application/cedulon-inclusion+cbor

644	   *  4 (kid) = bstr, mandatory.  The profile computes kid as the first
645	      eight bytes of SHA-256 over the issuer's SubjectPublicKeyInfo DER,
646	      in the Ed25519 SubjectPublicKeyInfo encoding of [RFC8410].  A
647	      verifier MUST obtain the public key from an authenticated channel
648	      (preconfigured issuer set, directory, or transparency statement)
649	      and MUST reject a message whose kid does not match that key.

651	   The unprotected header MUST be empty, and a decoder MUST refuse a
652	   message whose unprotected header is not an empty map, by the name
653	   cose-sign1-unprotected, rather than verify the signature and ignore
654	   the header (MUST-T4-21).  The reason is in Section 7.1: every digest
655	   over a signed object in this profile is computed over the COSE_Sign1
656	   octets, which include the unprotected header, while the signature
657	   does not cover it.  A decoder that ignored a stuffed header would
658	   verify the signature and compute a receiptHash the issuer never
659	   produced, so one honestly signed receipt could carry as many digests
660	   as a stranger cared to give it, and a chain built on prevReceiptHash
661	   would follow whichever copy it was handed.  The posted -05 stated the
662	   header empty and said nothing about the decoder; the companion
663	   decoder ignored the header until the question of what the digest
664	   covers was measured.  The payload MUST be the CBOR encoding of the
665	   claim map.  The signature is Ed25519 [RFC8032] over the COSE
666	   Sig_structure ["Signature1", protected, h'', payload].

668	6.3.  How a signed object is presented

670	   The signed octets above are what this profile defines and what every
671	   digest in Section 7.1 is taken over.  An object handed to a verifier
672	   travels with a little more than that, and the previous revisions used
673	   one of those members - the key an object carries - without ever
674	   saying where it came from.

676	   A presented Spend Receipt, epoch checkpoint, Trade Manifest, or
677	   Decision Token carries, beside the signed octets, its claim set or
678	   body in decoded form and *the signer's public key as a
679	   SubjectPublicKeyInfo PEM*; a countersigned receipt carries the
680	   payee's key the same way, and an object presented in the JSON
681	   encoding of Section 7 repeats its signature there as base64.  None of
682	   these is inside the signed octets, which is the whole point of naming
683	   them here: the signature covers the COSE message and nothing else, so
684	   every one of these members is a surface anyone holding the object can
685	   rewrite.  This is the same shape the Rail Extract states in
686	   Section 9, and it is stated once here for the COSE objects rather
687	   than left to be inferred from an implementation.

689	   A carried key is not an identity source and MUST NOT be used as one
690	   (MUST-T4-11).  Under a pin it has exactly one effect: an object that
691	   verifies under the pinned key while carrying a different key is
692	   reported as carried-key-mismatch, a warning, and stays attested
693	   (Section 10.1).  With no pin held it is the only key present, so the
694	   signature check that runs against it says the object is internally
695	   consistent and says nothing about who signed it; two issuers cannot
696	   be told apart in that state.

698	7.  Canonical JSON encoding

700	   Not everything this document hashes or signs is CBOR.  The policy
701	   document, the six request fields bound by a Decision Token, and the
702	   scoped body of a Rail Extract are JSON, and -03 called each of them
703	   "canonical" without saying what that meant.  Two implementations
704	   could therefore agree on every requirement in this document and still
705	   produce different bytes, which makes an independent verifier
706	   impossible to write from the text.  This section closes that.

708	   Where this document says "the canonical encoding" of a JSON document,
709	   it means the encoding defined by [RFC8785], and the octets hashed or
710	   signed are the UTF-8 octets of that encoding.

712	   Three notes on the boundary of that reference:

714	   *  [RFC8785] Section 3.1 takes I-JSON [RFC7493] as its input, and an
715	      I-JSON object carries no duplicate member names.  This document
716	      makes that precondition a rule at every place a verifier receives
717	      a JSON document as text - a rail extract body, a policy document,
718	      a stored receipt file: a text in which any object, at any depth,
719	      repeats a member name MUST be refused by the name json-duplicate-
720	      key, before the text is parsed (MUST-T4-20).  The rule is measured
721	      on the text because a parser that keeps either value has already
722	      discarded the evidence of the other, and two verifiers parsing one
723	      text could then canonicalize two different documents and sign or
724	      check two different octet strings, which is the JSON form of the
725	      disagreement MUST-T4-18 forbids for CBOR.  A verifier handed an
726	      object rather than text, as a tool behind a JSON-RPC boundary is,
727	      cannot apply the rule and MUST NOT report that it did; whether the
728	      text was checked is then the transport's to state.  The posted -05
729	      said nothing about this.  The companion implementation refused
730	      such texts before the sentence existed, and its conformance runner
731	      carried the difference as a recorded split against -05 rather than
732	      as a pass.

734	   *  [RFC8785] Section 3.2.2.2 requires a serializer to terminate on a
735	      lone surrogate, and so does this document: a producer MUST refuse
736	      to encode a document containing one, by name, and MUST NOT sign
737	      what it could not encode.  A verifier reading bytes it cannot
738	      canonicalize for this reason reports the input as failing
739	      verification with the refusal named beside the verdict; it does
740	      not crash.  No field defined by this document may contain a lone
741	      surrogate, so a conforming document never reaches this rule. -04
742	      permitted emitting the escaped form instead, which contradicted
743	      the RFC it cited; that permission is removed.

745	   *  [RFC8785] defines no encoding for an integer outside the IEEE 754
746	      double range.  No document defined here carries one: every amount
747	      and cumulative limit is already a decimal string before it is
748	      encoded, and every hash is lowercase hexadecimal.  A document that
749	      would need such an integer is outside this specification.

751	7.1.  Which octets are hashed

753	   Every hash-valued field in this document is SHA-256 [RFC6234] of the
754	   input named below.  All but three are rendered as lowercase
755	   hexadecimal. kid differs only in its rendering: the digest is
756	   computed over the same stated input and then truncated to its first 8
757	   bytes, carried as a byte string rather than as hex (Section 6 states
758	   the same rule where the header is defined). ap2MandateHash differs in
759	   whose digest it is: AP2 defines the mandate and its octets, and this
760	   document carries the result opaquely rather than restating a rule it
761	   does not own. deliveredHash differs in its carrier: it is a claim in
762	   a CBOR map and is carried as the raw 32 digest bytes (bstr), not as
763	   hex; comparisons against acceptanceCriteriaHash are made over the
764	   digest value. -03 named the digest for some of these fields and not
765	   for others; the omissions were not a deliberate degree of freedom.

767	        +========================+===============================+
768	        | Field                  | Input to SHA-256              |
769	        +========================+===============================+
770	        | receiptHash            | the signed COSE_Sign1 octets  |
771	        |                        | of the receipt                |
772	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
773	        | manifestHash           | the signed COSE_Sign1 octets  |
774	        |                        | of the Trade Manifest         |
775	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
776	        | checkpointHash         | the signed COSE_Sign1 octets  |
777	        |                        | of the checkpoint             |
778	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
779	        | statementHash          | the signed COSE_Sign1 octets  |
780	        |                        | of the statement              |
781	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
782	        | acceptanceCriteriaHash | the exact delivery bytes, as  |
783	        |                        | defined where the Trade       |
784	        |                        | Manifest is                   |
785	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
786	        | deliveredHash          | the exact bytes the payee     |
787	        |                        | received; the same input rule |
788	        |                        | as acceptanceCriteriaHash,    |
789	        |                        | computed by the other party   |
790	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
791	        | policyHash             | the UTF-8 octets of the       |
792	        |                        | canonical policy document     |
793	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
794	        | requestHash            | the UTF-8 octets of the       |
795	        |                        | canonical six-field request   |
796	        |                        | document                      |
797	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
798	        | kid                    | the SubjectPublicKeyInfo DER; |
799	        |                        | the digest is then truncated  |
800	        |                        | to its first 8 bytes          |
801	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
802	        | ap2MandateHash         | the octets AP2 defines for    |
803	        |                        | its mandate; not profiled by  |
804	        |                        | this document                 |
805	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+

807	                                 Table 7

809	   Wherever this table, or any other sentence in this document, says
810	   "the signed COSE_Sign1 octets", those are the octets of the
811	   *untagged* four-element COSE_Sign1 array of [RFC9052].  This profile
812	   never wraps a message in CBOR tag 18, and the vectors in Appendix A
813	   carry the untagged form.  A verifier that hashed a tagged copy would
814	   compute a different digest for every object in this profile, so the
815	   choice is stated here once rather than left to be inferred from the
816	   vectors.

818	   The six fields of the request document are the ones MUST-T6-1 names:
819	   amount, currency, payee, tool, nonce, and manifestHash. -03 described
820	   requestHash as "the six-field hash" while naming SHA-256 for
821	   policyHash in the same sentence, which left a reader free to conclude
822	   that the request binding was not a digest at all.  It is one.

824	   Naming the members is not stating the document, so the document is
825	   stated here.  The request document is a JSON object carrying exactly
826	   those six members and no others, every member always present. amount
827	   is the decimal string of the request, in the amount syntax the
828	   receipt claims table states, never a JSON number: [RFC8785] encodes
829	   the number 1 and the string "1" differently, and an implementation
830	   free to pick either would produce two digests for one request.
831	   currency, payee, and nonce are the request's text strings. tool is
832	   the request's text string, or JSON null where the deployment names
833	   none. manifestHash is the lowercase hexadecimal string, or JSON null
834	   for a spend bound to no manifest; an absent value is null, never an
835	   omitted member.  A document with a seventh member, a missing member,
836	   or another type for one of these is not the request document this
837	   section defines.

839	   The policy document is different on purpose, and the difference is
840	   scope rather than an oversight.  Its member set is the deployment's
841	   own: this document defines how the bytes of whatever policy document
842	   a PDP evaluates are encoded (Section 7) and digested, not what its
843	   members are. policyHash binds a spend to the exact bytes its PDP
844	   evaluated; it is not a value two deployments are expected to compute
845	   from a shared schema, and nothing in the verification algorithm
846	   compares one deployment's policyHash to another's.

848	8.  Decision Token

850	   A Decision Token is the portable encoding of a PDP allow.  It is
851	   COSE_Sign1 with the header profile in Section 6 and the labels in
852	   Section 6.1.  All five labels are always present (MUST-T6-4).

854	   requestHash MUST be the SHA-256 of the canonical encoding of the six
855	   fields the PDP evaluated (MUST-T6-1), rendered as lowercase
856	   hexadecimal; Section 7 defines that encoding and Section 7.1 states
857	   the octets. -03 called this "the six-field hash" in the same sentence
858	   that named SHA-256 for policyHash, which left the digest for one of
859	   them unstated. policyHash MUST be the SHA-256 of the canonical policy
860	   document the PDP evaluated. expiryMs is a Unix time in milliseconds
861	   after which the token MUST be treated as expired (SHOULD-T6-3):
862	   expired when the evaluation time is strictly greater than expiryMs,
863	   not yet expired at exactly expiryMs, on the same boundary discipline
864	   MUST-T3-3 states for the manifest. nonce is the request nonce.
865	   singleUseId is the identifier consumed on the first settlement
866	   attempt (MUST-T6-2).

868	   A party that accepts a Decision Token MUST reject it if the signature
869	   fails, if kid does not match a configured PDP key, if the content
870	   type is not application/cedulon-decision+cbor, if the decoded claim
871	   map does not match the presented claims, or if expiryMs is in the
872	   past (MUST-T6-5).

874	9.  Rail Extract Profile

876	   A verifier checks completeness against a *rail extract*, not against

884	         +=============+=========================================+
885	         | Member      | JSON type                               |
886	         +=============+=========================================+
887	         | ref         | string (rail payment reference)         |

890	         +-------------+-----------------------------------------+
891	         | currency    | string                                  |

893	         | timestampMs | number (POSIX milliseconds, an integer) |

898	   These member names are normative.  A rail MAY add members of its own
899	   to a record; it MUST NOT rename the four above. -04 said the member
900	   names were the rail's to define, which contradicted this table and
901	   made the extract unconstructable from the text: a verifier reading
902	   reference where the table says ref has no rule telling it whether the
903	   two are the same member.  The table wins, and -04's sentence is
904	   withdrawn.  The table also used CBOR terms (tstr, uint) for what is a

907	   A record MAY carry a beneficiary member (string).  A rail that can

909	   when present, it is compared against the payee of the receipt that
910	   names the same ref, and a difference is reported (beneficiary-
911	   mismatch).  Resolving a reference to a beneficiary is a feature of
912	   the rail's own system: this profile does not assume it, and measures
913	   it only when the rail declares it.

915	9.2.  Scope

917	   An extract is scoped to one account identifier, one rail identifier,
918	   and one half-open time window [windowStartMs, windowEndMs).  The
919	   signed body is one JSON document with exactly this shape:

921	        +===============+=========================================+

923	        +===============+=========================================+
924	        | accountId     | string                                  |
925	        +---------------+-----------------------------------------+
926	        | railId        | string                                  |
927	        +---------------+-----------------------------------------+
928	        | windowStartMs | number (POSIX milliseconds, an integer) |
929	        +---------------+-----------------------------------------+
930	        | windowEndMs   | number (POSIX milliseconds, an integer) |

933	        |               | integer; optional; see Section 11)      |
934	        +---------------+-----------------------------------------+

936	        |               | above)                                  |
937	        +---------------+-----------------------------------------+

943	   name at both ends - by the signer before it signs and by the verifier
944	   before it checks a signature - so a malformed extract is the same
945	   refusal on both sides rather than a signature verdict.  Additional
946	   members beyond these are the rail's to add, as with records.

948	   The integer-valued members - windowStartMs, windowEndMs, each

951	   clockSkewMs MUST NOT be negative; a value outside those bounds, or a
952	   non-integer, is refused by name in the same way as a missing member.

954	   The body is read as text before it is read as an object.  A text in
955	   which any object repeats a member name is refused as json-duplicate-
956	   key at both ends, before parsing and before any signature is checked
957	   (MUST-T4-20, Section 7); a signer that parsed first would sign one of
958	   the two values and an honest verifier could check the other.

960	   clockSkewMs, when present, declares the boundary allowance the
961	   verifier applies at this window's edges during reconciliation
962	   (Section 11); when absent, the profile default of 300000 milliseconds
963	   (five minutes) applies.

965	9.3.  Authentication

967	   The rail signs Ed25519 [RFC8032] over the canonical encoding of the
968	   scoped body, which is a JSON document and therefore takes the
969	   encoding of Section 7: the signed octets are the UTF-8 octets of the
970	   [RFC8785] encoding of the body above.  The signature and the rail's
971	   public key travel beside the body, the signature as base64 and the
972	   key as a SubjectPublicKeyInfo PEM; neither is part of the signed
973	   octets.  A verifier MUST obtain the extract from the rail or from a
974	   signature the rail published (MUST-T10-7).  A deployment that cannot
975	   do so is running the reconciliation against evidence it did not
976	   obtain independently, and MUST report the guarantee as conditional.

978	   A signature on an extract proves internal consistency, not origin: a
979	   key generated by whoever produced the object verifies against itself.
980	   The verifier therefore MUST obtain the rail's public key out of band
981	   and MUST verify the extract signature against that key (MUST-T10-8).
982	   A key the extract carries is not that key: it is neither the rail's
983	   identity nor a fallback for one the verifier did not obtain.  Where
984	   no such key is held, the carried key is the only key present, so the
985	   signature check that runs against it says the extract is internally
986	   consistent and says nothing about who produced it, and the verifier
987	   MUST treat the guarantee as conditional.

989	   Keys are compared as bytes.  A verifier MUST compare the pinned key
990	   and the key that signed the extract by their SubjectPublicKeyInfo DER
991	   encoding, not by any text encoding of it, so that the same key
992	   presented in a different envelope still compares equal (MUST-T10-9).
993	   A pinned key the verifier cannot decode is a fault in the verifier's
994	   own configuration, not evidence about the extract, and MUST be
995	   reported as trust-key-unreadable rather than as a key mismatch.

997	   What a verifier emits for an extract it cannot authenticate depends
998	   on whether it stated an expectation.  With no pinned key the verifier
999	   has asserted nothing, so the condition is unauthenticated-extract
1000	   whatever the extract carries - a signature that verifies against the
1001	   carried key establishes internal consistency and not origin, and one
1002	   that fails or is refused is not a verdict about a key either.  It is
1003	   a warning: completeness findings may still be computed, but the
1004	   guarantee is *conditional* on the extract being authentic.  With a
1005	   pinned key the verifier has asserted what it requires, and an extract
1006	   that fails to meet it is a failure rather than a caveat; see the
1007	   verification algorithm for which finding applies. -00 defined only
1008	   the first case, and readers of -00 should note that -02 made the
1009	   pinned case fail closed.  See Section 16.

1011	9.4.  Scope agreement

1013	   An extract declares a window and carries settlement records.  The two
1014	   MUST agree: a verifier MUST report every settlement record whose
1015	   timestampMs falls outside [windowStartMs, windowEndMs) as extract-
1016	   scope-mismatch, identified by that record's ref (MUST-T10-10).  This
1017	   check is about the extract's internal consistency and MUST be
1018	   performed whether or not a rail key is pinned.

1020	   A verifier that knows which account, rail, and window it is auditing
1021	   MUST also check the extract against that expectation and MUST fail
1022	   closed when the extract does not cover it (MUST-T10-11).  An extract
1023	   for another account or rail, or one whose window does not span the
1024	   period under audit, cannot support a completeness claim about that
1025	   period.

1027	   A verifier that states no period leaves the extract free to define
1028	   one, and an extract that reports on a millisecond balances as easily
1029	   as one that reports on a month.  Pinning a key establishes who
1030	   signed; only a stated period establishes what the signature had to
1031	   cover.  A verifier that has not stated the period under audit
1032	   therefore MUST emit unstated-audit-window and MUST treat the
1033	   guarantee as conditional (MUST-T10-15), whatever else verifies.

1035	   The period is one axis of that scope.  The account and the rail are
1036	   the other two, and they behave the same way: an extract names one of
1037	   each, so a verifier that has not stated them leaves the extract to
1038	   say whose settlements were accounted for and which way out was
1039	   watched.  A verifier that has not stated the account or the rail
1040	   under audit therefore MUST emit unstated-audit-scope and MUST treat
1041	   the guarantee as conditional (MUST-T10-18).  Where no rail key is
1042	   pinned at all, all three axes are equally unstated and
1043	   unauthenticated-extract is the condition reported.

1045	   Stating them does not widen the population; it names it.  One account
1046	   settling on two rails has two settlement paths, and an extract for
1047	   the first reports nothing about the second: a spend that left that
1048	   way is not an unmatched row, it is outside the population the extract
1049	   declared.  Because the strongest line this profile prints - a
1050	   balanced audit under an unconditional guarantee - is true of one
1051	   account, on one rail, over one window, a report that carries it MUST
1052	   also carry that account, rail and window (MUST-T10-19).  A
1053	   completeness claim about an account is the conjunction of one such
1054	   report per rail that account can settle on, and enumerating those
1055	   rails is the deployment's statement, not something an extract can be
1056	   asked to prove.

1058	10.  Trust roots

1060	   Section 9 states the rule for one object: a signature proves internal
1061	   consistency, not origin, so the verifier obtains the rail key out of
1062	   band and checks the extract against it, and the key the extract
1063	   carries is neither the rail's identity nor a fallback for one the
1064	   verifier did not obtain (MUST-T10-8). -02 already required a verifier
1065	   to obtain the public key from an authenticated channel and to reject
1066	   a kid that does not match that key (MUST-T4-8).  What -02 did not
1067	   carry was the verification algorithm, the separate root inputs, and
1068	   the error semantics that name a missing or mismatched pin.  This
1069	   section states those for every signed object in the profile.

1071	   The gap is not theoretical.  A verifier that checks a Spend Receipt
1072	   against the key the receipt carries accepts a receipt signed by any
1073	   key at all, including one an attacker minted for the occasion.  Such
1074	   a receipt matches a settlement the attacker was never authorised to
1075	   make, the settlement stops looking uncovered, and the audit reports
1076	   nothing.  The completeness property in Section 11 is then computed
1077	   over evidence that answers to nobody.

1079	10.1.  The issuer root

1081	   A verifier MUST obtain the issuer's public key out of band and MUST
1082	   verify Spend Receipt and epoch checkpoint signatures against that
1083	   key.  A key the object carries is not that key: it establishes no
1084	   signer identity and is not a fallback for a pin the verifier does not
1085	   hold (MUST-T4-9, MUST-T4-11).  Where no issuer key is held, a
1086	   signature checked against the key its own object carries establishes
1087	   internal consistency and nothing more, which is the state Section 6.3
1088	   and the last row of the table below describe.  A verifier that holds
1089	   no such key and is presented with any Spend Receipt or epoch
1090	   checkpoint MUST treat the completeness guarantee as conditional and
1091	   SHOULD report the condition; the identifier unauthenticated-issuer is
1092	   used for it in this implementation.

1094	   The condition names those two objects because an audit given no
1095	   receipts and no checkpoints rests on the extract alone.  There the
1096	   absent issuer root withholds nothing, and warning about a root the
1097	   audit never consulted would spend the warning where it carries no
1098	   information.

1100	   Reporting a mismatch is not sufficient on its own.  A receipt that
1101	   does not answer to the pinned issuer key MUST NOT count as coverage
1102	   for the settlement it names, and the settlement MUST still be
1103	   reported as uncovered (MUST-T4-10).  A verifier that reports the
1104	   mismatch and then lets the receipt match the settlement anyway has
1105	   described the attack in its output while still concluding that the
1106	   books balance.

1108	   Keys are compared as bytes, by their SubjectPublicKeyInfo DER
1109	   encoding, on the same terms as MUST-T10-9.  A pinned issuer key the
1110	   verifier cannot decode is a fault in its own configuration and MUST
1111	   be reported as trust-key-unreadable rather than as a mismatch against
1112	   the objects; where no pinned key can be decoded at all, nothing is
1113	   attested and the verifier MUST NOT fall back to accepting the keys
1114	   the objects carry (MUST-T4-11).  Falling back is how a mistyped
1115	   configuration becomes a bypass.

1117	   Membership in the attested set follows one rule: the signature
1118	   verifies under a pinned issuer key.  The kid header routes the check
1119	   to a candidate key; the key an object carries beside its signature is
1120	   not an identity source, because it travels outside the signed octets
1121	   and anyone can rewrite it.  Two consequences are stated so that
1122	   implementations do not diverge on them.  First, an honestly signed
1123	   object whose carried key was swapped stays attested: the swap is
1124	   reported (carried-key-mismatch, a warning) and MUST NOT move the
1125	   object out of the attested set or change the verdict, on the same
1126	   reasoning as Section 5.1 - a surface the signature does not cover
1127	   must not be able to manufacture a negative result.  Second, an object
1128	   that claims the pin, by its carried key or its kid, but does not
1129	   verify under it is excluded from the attested set and MUST still be
1130	   walked and named - for a receipt, receipt-chain-break with a
1131	   signature-failed detail - never silently dropped; an object that
1132	   neither verifies under the pin nor claims it is issuer-key-mismatch,
1133	   excluded, and the settlement it names stays uncovered.

1182	   A verifier MUST accept an issuer root that is a set of keys rather

1191	10.2.  The payee root

1193	   The optional countersignature in Section 5.1 travels beside the
1194	   issuer signature without being covered by it.  Anyone holding an
1195	   honest receipt can append a countersignature of their own, so a
1196	   verifier that checks it against the key carried next to it learns
1197	   only that some key signed something.

1199	   A countersignature MUST NOT be treated as evidence that the payee
1200	   approved the payment unless it verifies against a payee key the
1201	   verifier obtained out of band (MUST-T4-13).  Without such a key the
1202	   verifier SHOULD report the condition and MUST treat the guarantee as
1203	   conditional.

1205	   Naming a payee key states an expectation, and an expectation that
1206	   only fires when the evidence is present can be cancelled by deleting
1207	   the evidence.  Where a verifier has pinned a key for a payee, a
1208	   settled receipt naming that payee and carrying no *attributable*
1209	   countersignature MUST be reported (MUST-T4-14): a countersignature
1210	   that failed to verify, or verified under some other key, is discarded
1211	   under Section 5.1 and leaves the expectation open exactly as a
1212	   missing one does.  Otherwise an attacker removes their own failed
1213	   forgery - or appends one - and the report returns to unconditional.
1214	   The discarded object itself is a warning, never a failure of the
1215	   receipt it rode beside; Section 5.1 states the invariant.

1217	10.3.  The witness root

1219	   Section 11.3 describes what a transparency witness adds.  An
1220	   inclusion receipt checked against the key it carries says that some
1221	   log is internally consistent, and a log is cheap to invent.

1223	   A verifier MUST obtain the transparency service's public key out of
1224	   band and MUST verify inclusion receipts against it (MUST-T11-15).
1225	   Inclusion receipts that cannot be checked that way MUST NOT be used
1226	   as evidence, in either direction: they cannot establish that a
1227	   checkpoint was anchored, and they cannot establish that one was
1228	   withheld.  A verifier SHOULD report that it left them out.

1230	   A log holds statements from everyone who uses it.  A statement held
1231	   by a pinned log MUST additionally answer to the issuer root before it
1232	   counts as something that issuer published (MUST-T11-16); otherwise
1233	   another user's epoch, sitting in a shared log, reads as this issuer
1234	   publishing two checkpoints for one epoch.

1236	   Anchoring and withholding are different claims and need different
1237	   evidence.  Establishing that a checkpoint was logged needs only the
1238	   statement hash.  Establishing that an issuer withheld one needs to
1239	   know whose statement it is, which an inclusion receipt carrying no
1240	   statement body cannot say.  Such a receipt MUST NOT be used to report
1241	   a withheld checkpoint, and MUST NOT be silently discarded either: a
1242	   real withholding must not be buried by removing the body (MUST-
1243	   T11-17).

1245	10.4.  The decision root

1247	   A Decision Token is issued by the policy decision point and consumed
1248	   by the same deployment.  The consumer therefore holds the key it
1249	   signs with and has no reason to ask the token which key to check it
1250	   against.  A consumer MUST verify a Decision Token against its own
1251	   issuing key and MUST NOT accept one it cannot check that way (MUST-
1252	   T6-6).

1254	10.5.  The manifest root

1256	   A Trade Manifest is optional.  A deployment that presents none is not
1257	   missing a root, and this requirement does not make such an audit
1258	   conditional.  The forbidden case is the other one: a manifest is
1259	   presented, and the verifier accepts it because the key travelling
1260	   inside it verifies against itself.

1262	   A verifier that is presented with a Trade Manifest MUST obtain the
1263	   publisher's public key out of band and MUST verify the manifest
1264	   signature against that key, not against a key the manifest carries
1265	   (MUST-T4-15).  A verifier without such a key that is presented with a
1266	   Trade Manifest MUST report the completeness guarantee as conditional
1267	   and SHOULD report the condition; the identifier unauthenticated-
1268	   manifest is used for it in this implementation.  An audit presented
1269	   with no Trade Manifest is not made conditional by this requirement.

1271	   A pinned manifest key the verifier cannot decode is a fault in its
1272	   own configuration and MUST be reported as trust-key-unreadable rather
1273	   than as a mismatch, on the same terms as MUST-T4-11.  A manifest that
1274	   does not verify against a readable pin MUST be reported as manifest-
1275	   key-mismatch and MUST fail the audit.  Falling back to the key the
1276	   manifest carries is how a presented document becomes a bypass.

1278	   Attribution is one question and coverage is another, and a root that
1279	   answers only the first leaves the document doing work it was never
1280	   spent under.  A verifier presented with a Trade Manifest MUST compare
1281	   the manifest hash to the manifestHash of the receipts presented to
1282	   the audit and MUST report a manifest that no presented receipt
1283	   references (MUST-T4-17); the identifier manifest-covers-no-receipt is
1284	   used for it in this implementation, and the completeness guarantee is
1285	   conditional.  The comparison runs against those presented receipts,
1286	   including aborted ones, and is made before any extract window is
1287	   applied and before any issuer key is applied; a hash on an aborted
1288	   receipt, on a receipt outside the extract window, or on a receipt no
1289	   pinned key attests still counts as a reference.  This requirement
1290	   asks whether any receipt names the terms, not whether a settlement in
1291	   the window was made under them, and not whether the receipt that
1292	   names them is attributable.  A forged receipt can therefore silence
1293	   this warning.  That is accepted: what it silences is a statement that
1294	   terms were named, and the report it leaves behind is still marked
1295	   conditional and still carries the finding that the receipt answers to
1296	   no pinned key.  A correctly attributed manifest travelling beside a
1297	   set of receipts marked noManifest states terms nothing presented was
1298	   spent under, and a report that stays silent about it reads as terms-
1299	   backed when it is not.  This requirement does not reach the audit
1300	   presented with no Trade Manifest, which remains a deployment choice
1301	   under MUST-T1-2.

1303	   Naming a manifest is not obeying one, and that is the third place
1304	   this document has had to say the same thing twice.  A verifier
1305	   presented with a Trade Manifest MUST compare the amount, the currency
1306	   and the settlement time of every receipt that names it against the
1307	   manifest's amount, currency and expiry, and MUST report a receipt
1308	   that departs from them (MUST-T8-9); the identifier manifest-terms-
1309	   mismatch is used for it in this implementation.  Every receipt that
1310	   names the manifest is measured, aborted ones included: an aborted
1311	   receipt that carries the hash of terms and a departing amount
1312	   recorded an attempt against terms it misstates, and MUST-T4-17 next
1313	   door already counts it as a reference.  The time compared is the
1314	   receipt's timestampMs, against the boundary MUST-T3-3 states:
1315	   strictly after expiresAtMs departs, exactly at it does not.  Amount
1316	   and currency are compared on the exact-octet terms of MUST-T8-2 - the
1317	   audit asks whether the gate's own rules were kept, so it compares the
1318	   way the gate compares.

1320	   Where a usable issuer key is pinned, the comparison is made over the
1321	   receipts that verify under it and the audit fails.  Where none is
1322	   pinned, the departure is still reported and the audit does not fail
1323	   on it alone.  An issuer key is usable when the pinned issuer root
1324	   holds at least one key the verifier can decode.  A pinned root none
1325	   of whose keys decode is already trust-key-unreadable and attests
1326	   nothing, so the comparison takes the unpinned branch while that
1327	   finding stands; the audit has failed on the configuration fault, and
1328	   the departure is still said out loud without becoming a charge no
1329	   readable key backs. -03 stated the first case for both, and an
1330	   implementation showed why that is wrong: a receipt signed by any key
1331	   at all, carrying the right manifest hash and the wrong amount, made
1332	   the verifier report a breach that never happened, against a payment
1333	   reference the forger chose.  Reporting a departure costs nothing if
1334	   it is unattributable; failing an audit on it hands an attacker a way
1335	   to accuse an honest payer.

1337	   MUST-T4-17 and this requirement therefore differ, and the difference
1338	   is not an inconsistency.  Asking whether a manifest hash appears
1339	   anywhere is a question about a set of documents, and an
1340	   unattributable document is still in that set.  Saying that a named
1341	   party broke terms it signed is a charge, and a charge needs a key
1342	   behind it.

1344	   Only receipts that name the manifest are measured against it: reading
1345	   the terms onto a receipt that never claimed them would invent a
1346	   violation the payer did not commit.

1348	   The rules being enforced here are not new.  A gate already refuses a
1349	   bound spend whose amount or currency differs from the manifest (MUST-
1350	   T8-2) and one made against an expired manifest (MUST-T3-3).  Both
1351	   were written for the point where money moves, and an audit reads the
1352	   record after that point, where the gate is no longer present to be
1353	   asked.  Without a counterpart a receipt can carry the hash of terms
1354	   it breaks and the report still calls the books balanced.  This is a
1355	   finding rather than a condition on the guarantee: a verifier that
1356	   reports it held every root it needed, and the statement it is making
1357	   is unconditional.

1359	   The gate answers differently from the audit, and the difference is
1360	   deliberate.  A policy decision point presented with a Trade Manifest
1361	   it cannot attribute MUST refuse the payment rather than settle and
1362	   record the doubt (MUST-T4-16).  An audit describes what it found and
1363	   may say the result is conditional; a gate decides whether money
1364	   moves, and a settled payment carrying the hash of terms nobody
1365	   authorised cannot be withdrawn by reporting it afterwards.  The
1366	   receipt would record those terms as agreed.

1368	10.6.  What the roots do not cover

1370	   A verifier that supplies none of these roots is not making an error,
1371	   and this document does not require it to.  It is making a weaker
1372	   statement, and the guarantee it reports must say so.  With no issuer
1373	   key nothing distinguishes one submitted receipt from another, so
1374	   conditions computed across the submitted set - two receipts claiming
1375	   one settlement reference, for instance - cannot be attributed to
1376	   anyone and are reported as conditions of the submission rather than
1377	   as failures of a party.

1379	11.  Reconciliation and Epoch Checkpoints

1381	   Completeness is the property that, given an authenticated rail
1382	   extract, every settlement in the extract has a matching settled Spend
1383	   Receipt, every settled receipt has a matching settlement, receipt and
1384	   checkpoint hash chains verify, and checkpoint totals equal the sum of
1385	   *settled* receipts in the checkpoint window.  If a spend occurred
1386	   without a receipt, the missing receipt is itself the evidence (MUST-
1387	   T10-2).

1389	   The property is stated over a population, and the extract is what
1390	   declares it: one account, on one rail, over one window (Section 9).
1391	   "Every settlement" means every settlement that extract carried.  A
1392	   settlement path no presented extract covers is not reconciled and not
1393	   found missing - it is outside the population - so the report names
1394	   the account, rail and window it was computed over (MUST-T10-19), and
1395	   a verifier that stated none of them says so instead (MUST-T10-18).

1397	   A checkpoint published with its totals withheld (Section 11.1) cannot
1398	   contribute the last of those to the property.  It is not a violation
1399	   of completeness and it is not a demonstration of it either: the
1400	   comparison was not made, and a result that rests on a comparison
1401	   nobody made is conditional (MUST-T11-12).

1403	11.1.  Checkpoint claims

1405	   An epoch checkpoint MUST be COSE_Sign1-signed with the header profile
1406	   in Section 6 and MUST bind all of the following (MUST-T11-1):

1408	   epoch, startMs, endMs, receiptCount, chainHeadHash, totals, and
1409	   prevCheckpointHash.

1411	   The checkpoint window is half-open [startMs, endMs) (MUST-T11-7).
1412	   receiptCount MUST equal the number of receipts (settled and aborted)
1413	   whose timestampMs falls in that window. chainHeadHash MUST equal
1414	   receiptHash of the last receipt in that window - the last link, in
1415	   issuer order (the prevReceiptHash chain, as the verification
1416	   algorithm's step 6 defines it), of the chain inside the window, not
1417	   the last one presented or the latest timestampMs - or null if the
1418	   window is empty (MUST-T11-2).  Where totals is present it MUST sum
1419	   only receipts with outcome = settled; the one permitted absence is
1420	   the signed redaction below.

1422	   An issuer that publishes a checkpoint without its totals MUST encode
1423	   totals as null in the signed payload (MUST-T11-12).  An empty map is
1424	   an honest zero for an empty window and is not a redaction.  Because
1425	   the redaction is inside the signature, it cannot be added to, or
1426	   removed from, a checkpoint after signing.

1428	   A verifier MUST NOT accept a redaction asserted anywhere but the
1429	   signed payload (MUST-T11-13).  A presentation-layer flag alongside a
1430	   checkpoint is chosen by whoever presents it, which is the party under
1431	   audit; honouring such a flag would let that party switch off the
1432	   totals comparison for a checkpoint whose signed totals are wrong.
1433	   The structural claims (epoch, startMs, endMs, receiptCount,
1434	   chainHeadHash, prevCheckpointHash) MUST NOT be redacted: a checkpoint
1435	   missing any of them does not decode, and a verifier MUST treat it as
1436	   a failed checkpoint rather than as a redacted one.

1438	11.2.  Genesis and continuity

1440	   The first checkpoint in a presented chain is the genesis checkpoint
1441	   of that chain.  Its prevCheckpointHash MUST be null.  Epoch numbers
1442	   MUST be consecutive integers.  Adjacent windows MUST satisfy
1443	   next.startMs = prev.endMs (MUST-T11-8).

1445	   A later checkpoint that omits a prefix of earlier epochs (prefix
1446	   deletion) is detectable only if an external witness (transparency
1447	   log) has recorded the missing prefix (MUST-T11-9).  Without that
1448	   witness, T11 guarantees about suppression are *conditional*.

1450	   -01 stated that dependency and stopped there: nothing in its
1451	   verification algorithm read a witness receipt, so the witness had no
1452	   way to speak. -02 gave it one.  A verifier that holds witness
1453	   receipts for the period under audit compares what the witness
1454	   recorded against what the chain presented, and reports the difference
1455	   under its own name (Section 11.3).  A witness that holds a checkpoint
1456	   the presented chain omits is not the same condition as a chain that
1457	   leaves a gap in its own coverage, and the two MUST NOT be reported
1458	   under one identifier (MUST-T11-11).  The first says evidence is being
1459	   withheld; the second says the evidence shown is incomplete.  An
1460	   operator who cannot tell them apart cannot tell an incomplete record
1461	   from a concealed one.

1463	11.3.  The transparency witness

1465	   A checkpoint registered with a Transparency Service [RFC9943] is a
1466	   Signed Statement whose payload is the checkpoint COSE object and
1467	   whose content type is application/cedulon-checkpoint+cbor
1468	   (Section 14).  What comes back, and what it proves, is stated as two
1469	   named tiers, because -04 promised the mechanics of one tier while
1470	   describing the checks of the other.

1472	   *Tier 1 - the witness receipt.* The witness returns a co-signature
1473	   over the statement hash of what it recorded: a COSE_Sign1 whose
1474	   payload binds the statement hash, the entry index, and the witness's
1475	   tree head.  Verifying it establishes exactly one sentence - "the
1476	   witness signed for this hash" - and nothing more; in particular it
1477	   does not establish membership in an append-only log. -04 called this
1478	   object a transparency receipt and cited the verification mechanics of
1479	   [RFC9942] for it while describing a hash comparison; the admission in
1480	   Section 18, that the receipt was a signature over a statement rather
1481	   than a proof of log membership, was correct, and the protocol text
1482	   now says the same thing.  The [RFC9942] citation applies in tier 2,
1483	   where its mechanics are actually performed.

1485	   On the wire the witness receipt is a COSE_Sign1 under Section 6,
1486	   signed by the witness key, with content type application/cedulon-
1487	   inclusion+cbor (Section 17.6).  Its payload is a deterministic CBOR
1488	   map carrying three entries and is not a CWT claim set: label 1 is the
1489	   statement hash, label 2 the entry index, label 3 the tree head.  The
1490	   two hashes are text strings holding the lowercase hexadecimal of a
1491	   32-octet SHA-256, the statement hash taken over the octets of the
1492	   statement the witness recorded; the entry index is an unsigned
1493	   integer.  The hash strings are compared as issued and are not passed
1494	   through the hash-claim grammar the claim sets use; the revision that
1495	   moves them to byte strings will close that difference.  Entries under
1496	   other labels are not defined, and a verifier of this revision does
1497	   not refuse them.  The map carries no statement body: a receipt that
1498	   travels with the body carries it beside the COSE object, not inside
1499	   it (Section 10.3, MUST-T11-17).  A receipt whose content type is
1500	   another value, or whose payload lacks one of the three entries or
1501	   carries one of another type, does not verify: the content type is the
1502	   check Section 6 makes on every object of this profile, and the
1503	   payload map is the one this paragraph states.

1505	   A verifier MAY be given witness receipts for the period under audit.
1506	   It is a distinct input from the presented checkpoint chain, and
1507	   supplying it is optional: a verifier given none performs the same
1508	   steps, and reports the same findings, that it would if this input did
1509	   not exist (MUST-T11-10).  Supplying an empty set is not the same as
1510	   supplying none.  An empty set says a witness is configured and
1511	   recorded nothing, which is itself reportable; absence says no witness
1512	   was consulted.

1514	   A receipt binds a statement hash, not a statement.  The body is not
1515	   carried by the receipt and a verifier will often not hold it.  Two
1516	   levels of checking follow from that, and they are not the same (MUST-
1517	   T11-10).

1519	   Every receipt MUST have its signature verified before it counts for
1520	   anything.  That is what establishes the hash as one the service
1521	   signed for, and it is all that comparing recorded hashes against
1522	   presented ones requires: the verifier computes the statement hash of
1523	   each presented checkpoint itself.

1525	   A receipt accompanied by the statement body carries more, and MUST be
1526	   checked further before that body is relied on.  The body's statement
1527	   hash MUST equal the hash the receipt binds, and the body MUST itself
1528	   verify as a checkpoint.  A body that fails either check proves
1529	   nothing and MUST be ignored rather than counted, while the receipt it
1530	   came with remains usable for the hash comparison.  Equivocation is
1531	   the case that needs a body, because it compares claims rather than
1532	   hashes.

1534	   *Tier 2 - log membership.* A verifier MAY additionally be given, for
1535	   one recorded statement, the registered Signed Statement bytes (the
1536	   candidate entry) and an inclusion proof.  When both are present, the
1537	   verifier MUST perform the verification of [RFC9942] Section 5.2.1
1538	   over them: hash the candidate entry bytes to obtain the leaf, apply
1539	   the proof to reproduce a root, and accept only when the leaf hash,
1540	   the proof's leaf index, and the reproduced root are all equal to the
1541	   statement hash, entry index, and tree head of one witness receipt
1542	   that verifies under the pinned witness key (MUST-T11-18).
1543	   Reproducing a root is deliberately not sufficient on its own: the
1544	   proof format below carries no domain separation between leaves and
1545	   interior nodes, and the exact match against a witness-signed receipt
1546	   is what closes the ambiguities that follow from that.  A pair that
1547	   fails this check, or a candidate entry presented without a proof,
1548	   MUST be reported as a failing finding; the identifier witness-
1549	   inclusion-invalid names it.

1551	   The inclusion proof is an audit path: the leaf index, and the sibling
1552	   hashes from the leaf's level up to the root, lowest level first.  The
1553	   tree is built over statement hashes as leaves; an interior node is
1554	   the SHA-256 of the concatenation of its two children's 32 raw bytes;
1555	   a level with an odd count pairs its last node with itself, so the
1556	   path needs no separate leaf count.

1558	   Where witness receipts are supplied and this pair is not, tier 2 was
1559	   not exercised, and the report MUST say so rather than letting the
1560	   tier pass silently (MUST-T11-19); the identifier witness-inclusion-
1561	   not-exercised names it, as a warning - the witness attested the
1562	   statement hash, and log membership was not proven.  A pair that
1563	   verifies is silent, like every other passing check in the algorithm.

1565	   What tier 1 establishes, and what it does not, is worth stating
1566	   plainly.  It establishes that the service signed for that statement.
1567	   Whether the statement is a member of an append-only log is tier 2's
1568	   question, answered only where tier 2's inputs were supplied and
1569	   verified; whether the log has ever equivocated remains a property of
1570	   the service and its own proofs.  A verifier that treats a tier-1
1571	   receipt alone as proof of log membership is claiming more than the
1572	   receipt carries.

1574	11.4.  Verification algorithm

1576	   A verifier MUST perform all of these steps and MUST report every
1577	   finding they produce (MUST-T10-1, MUST-T11-2).  They are numbered for
1578	   reference, not to require an evaluation order: no step short-circuits
1579	   another, and an implementation may evaluate them in any order that
1580	   produces the same set of findings.

1582	   The data dependencies are named, because "any order" read naively
1583	   would break them.  Step 15 decides which witness receipts, and which
1584	   statement bodies, survive checking; steps 14 and 16 consume what
1585	   survives.  Step 7's index of refs is what steps 8 and 9 reconcile.
1586	   Step 11 decides which checkpoints verified, and step 14 compares only
1587	   those together with what step 15 admitted.  An implementation that
1588	   ran a consumer against an unchecked producer would not produce the
1589	   same set of findings, so those orders are not among the permitted
1590	   ones.

1592	   The second is the issuer pin.  The step that resolves it decides the
1593	   *attested set* - the receipts and checkpoints that verify under a
1594	   usable pinned issuer key, or the whole presented set when no usable
1595	   key is pinned - and every later step that walks receipts or
1596	   checkpoints consumes that set: the chain walk in step 6, the indexing
1597	   and reconciliation in steps 7 through 9, the checkpoint comparisons
1598	   in steps 11 through 13, and the MUST-T8-9 comparison.  A receipt the
1599	   pin rejects is reported once and then excluded, which is what keeps
1600	   the settlement it names visible as uncovered (MUST-T4-10); an
1601	   implementation that let it back into any of those steps would let a
1602	   forged receipt cover a settlement, satisfy a checkpoint count, or
1603	   invent a terms charge.  Two checks deliberately stay on the presented
1604	   set whatever any key says, and MUST NOT acquire the dependency: MUST-
1605	   T4-17, which asks whether a manifest was named at all, and the per-
1606	   receipt defect checks that ask what a receipt says about itself. -04
1607	   asserted that nothing else in the list fed another step, which the
1608	   dependencies above had already made false; the assertion is withdrawn
1609	   and the list above is the inventory, maintained rather than
1610	   summarised.

1612	   When a step names an identifier in backticks, that identifier SHOULD
1613	   be used for the condition in diagnostic output.  The normative
1614	   requirement is the behaviour: report the condition, identified by the
1615	   ref or other handle given in the step.  The identifiers are not an
1616	   interoperability surface.

1618	   1.   Establish the subject of the audit.  When an extract is
1619	        supplied, the settlement records it carries are the ones
1620	        reconciled; a settlement list from any other source MUST NOT be
1621	        substituted for them (MUST-T10-12).  If the caller supplies both
1622	        and they differ, the verifier MUST report that the caller-
1623	        supplied list disagrees with the extract, and MUST still
1624	        reconcile the extract.  The identifier extract-settlement-
1625	        mismatch SHOULD be used for this condition in diagnostic output.

1627	   2.   Verify the extract signature against the out-of-band rail key
1628	        (MUST-T10-8, MUST-T10-9).  If no key is pinned, the check that
1629	        runs is against the key the extract carries, which establishes
1630	        internal consistency and not origin; the verifier MUST still
1631	        compute it, MUST NOT read it as a statement about who produced
1632	        the extract, and MUST treat the completeness guarantee as
1633	        conditional (MUST-T10-7).  The identifier unauthenticated-
1634	        extract SHOULD be used for this condition in diagnostic output,
1635	        whatever the extract carries.  If a key is pinned and cannot be
1636	        decoded, the verifier MUST report that the pinned key is
1637	        unreadable.  The identifier trust-key-unreadable SHOULD be used
1638	        for this condition.  If a key is pinned and the signature does
1639	        not verify against it, or verifies against a different key, the
1640	        verifier MUST report that the extract is not signed by the
1641	        pinned key.  The identifier extract-key-mismatch SHOULD be used
1642	        for this condition.  The rows of an extract the pin refused are
1643	        not reconciled against the receipts, and no settlement finding
1644	        is read out of that document (MUST-T10-20); the identifier
1645	        settlement-comparison-skipped SHOULD be used to say that the
1646	        comparison did not run.  A finding that puts the extract itself
1647	        in doubt MUST prevent an unconditional guarantee.

1649	   3.   Check scope.  The verifier MUST report each settlement record
1650	        whose timestampMs falls outside the declared window, identified
1651	        by that record's ref (MUST-T10-10).  When the verifier states an
1652	        expected account, rail, or window, it MUST report an extract
1653	        that does not cover it (MUST-T10-11).  The identifier extract-
1654	        scope-mismatch SHOULD be used for both conditions.  If the
1655	        verifier stated no period, it MUST treat the guarantee as
1656	        conditional (MUST-T10-15).  The identifier unstated-audit-window
1657	        SHOULD be used for this condition.  If it stated no account or
1658	        no rail, it MUST treat the guarantee as conditional for the same
1659	        reason (MUST-T10-18); the identifier unstated-audit-scope SHOULD
1660	        be used.  Whatever the verdict, the report MUST name the
1661	        account, rail and window the extract declared (MUST-T10-19).

1663	   4.   Resolve each Spend Receipt against the issuer root
1664	        (Section 10.1) in one pass.  Decode the COSE_Sign1; a content
1665	        type that is not the receipt type, a decoder bound, or a decoded
1666	        claim map that does not match the presented claims is a named
1667	        refusal, not a signature verdict (MUST-T4-2, MUST-T4-8).  Then
1668	        ask one question: does the signature verify under a pinned
1669	        issuer key. kid routes the check to a candidate key and carries
1670	        no authority of its own; the carried key is not consulted for
1671	        membership at all.  The resolution table in Section 10.1 reads
1672	        the answer out per cell.  Every cell there is a named condition
1673	        plus a membership decision; no cell is a silent removal, and the
1674	        word "reject" in earlier revisions meant nothing more than a
1675	        cell of that table.  Where a countersignature is present,
1676	        Section 10.2 governs what it establishes (MUST-T4-13, MUST-
1677	        T4-14).  Where a Trade Manifest is presented, Section 10.5
1678	        governs it (MUST-T4-15): with no publisher key pinned the
1679	        verifier reports unauthenticated-manifest and the guarantee is
1680	        conditional; with a pin that cannot be read, trust-key-
1681	        unreadable; with a pin the manifest does not answer to,
1682	        manifest-key-mismatch; and with a manifest that no presented
1683	        receipt references, manifest-covers-no-receipt (MUST-T4-17).  A
1684	        receipt that names the manifest but departs from its amount,
1685	        currency, expiry or, where the manifest names one, payee is
1686	        reported as manifest-terms-mismatch; with a usable issuer key
1687	        pinned the comparison runs over the attested receipts and the
1688	        departure fails the audit, and with no usable issuer key it is a
1689	        warning over the presented receipts and does not by itself fail
1690	        the audit (MUST-T8-9).  An audit presented with no Trade
1691	        Manifest is not made conditional by this step.

1693	   5.   Scope the receipts.  Membership follows the ref binding first: a
1694	        receipt whose ref appears on the extract is reconciled against
1695	        this extract even when its own timestampMs falls outside the
1696	        declared window - the rail has signed that the settlement
1697	        belongs to the window, and the receipt follows its settlement.
1698	        The timestampMs sieve applies only to receipts the extract does
1699	        not name: such a receipt outside the window is not a
1700	        completeness failure against this extract (MUST-T10-16);
1701	        auditing a longer period requires extracts that cover it.  At
1702	        the window's edges the declared allowance applies (Section 9):
1703	        an unmatched settled receipt within clockSkewMs of windowEndMs,
1704	        and an unmatched settlement record within clockSkewMs of
1705	        windowStartMs, are reported as boundary-deferred, a warning,
1706	        rather than as step 8's completeness findings - two honest
1707	        clocks can disagree by less than the allowance, and both
1708	        verifiers of an honest edge payment would otherwise reach the
1709	        same false accusation (MUST-T10-17).  Where the following
1710	        window's extract is presented and verifies, a deferred receipt
1711	        whose ref appears on it is resolved and not reported, and one
1712	        whose ref does not appear hardens into the step 8 finding; a
1713	        deferred settlement record near the opening edge resolves
1714	        through this step's ref binding, since the prior window's
1715	        receipt that names its ref is reconciled here regardless of
1716	        timestamp.  The following window's extract closes or hardens
1717	        closing-edge deferrals only; an opening-edge record stays
1718	        deferred until a receipt in the presented bag names its ref,
1719	        whether or not a following extract is presented.  In a single-
1720	        window audit a deferred record keeps the guarantee conditional.
1721	        Receipts remain subject to every other check regardless of
1722	        window.

1724	   6.   Walk the attested receipts in issuer order.  Issuer order is the
1725	        order induced by the prevReceiptHash chain: the verifier
1726	        rebuilds the chain from the links, and the order in which
1727	        receipts were presented carries no weight. timestampMs is
1728	        issuer-asserted and is not an ordering source.  The first
1729	        prevReceiptHash MUST be null.  Each later prevReceiptHash MUST
1730	        equal receiptHash of the previous receipt.  A miss, and a
1731	        receipt the links cannot place, MUST be reported as a break in
1732	        the receipt chain.  The identifier receipt-chain-break SHOULD be
1733	        used for this condition.

1735	   7.   Index the attested settled receipts and extract records by ref.
1736	        A ref that appears more than once on either side MUST be
1737	        reported as a repeated reference (MUST-T10-6).  The identifier
1738	        duplicate-ref SHOULD be used for this condition.

1740	   8.   For each ref that appears exactly once on each side, require a
1741	        one-to-one match on ref AND amount AND currency (MUST-T10-1),
1742	        compared as exact octets on the terms of MUST-T8-2; step 9's
1743	        aggregation is the only place this algorithm reads an amount as
1744	        a number.  Amount or currency mismatch MUST be reported as a
1745	        settlement that does not match its receipt, identified by that
1746	        ref.  The identifier settlement-mismatch SHOULD be used for this
1747	        condition.  A settlement with no receipt MUST be reported as
1748	        lacking a receipt, identified by its ref (MUST-T10-2).  The
1749	        identifier settlement-without-receipt SHOULD be used for this
1750	        condition.  A settled receipt with no extract row MUST be
1751	        reported as a completeness failure (MUST-T10-3).  The identifier
1752	        receipt-without-settlement SHOULD be used for this condition.  A
1753	        settled receipt with a null rail ref MUST be reported as settled
1754	        without a rail reference; this check asks what a receipt says
1755	        about itself and runs over the presented receipts, attested or
1756	        not.  The identifier settled-without-ref SHOULD be used for this
1757	        condition.  Where a settlement record declares a beneficiary
1758	        (Section 9), it MUST be compared against the matched receipt's
1759	        payee as exact octets; a difference is beneficiary-mismatch and
1760	        fails the audit.  Where neither the manifest names a payee nor
1761	        any settlement record declares a beneficiary, the report MUST
1762	        carry counterparty-unbound, a scope record: ref, amount and
1763	        currency closed against the payer's account extract, and the
1764	        counterparty's identity was not bound.  It is a statement of
1765	        what the evidence did not cover, not a doubt about what it did,
1766	        so it does not move the verdict and does not by itself make the
1767	        guarantee conditional.

1769	   9.   A ref already reported as repeating MUST still be reconciled by
1770	        amount rather than dropped from the comparison (MUST-T10-13).
1771	        For each currency under that ref, compare the total settled
1772	        against the total receipted.  A settled total that exceeds the
1773	        receipted total MUST be reported as a settlement lacking a
1774	        receipt, and the finding MUST state the unaccounted amount.  The
1775	        identifier settlement-without-receipt SHOULD be used for this
1776	        condition.  A settled total that is less than the receipted
1777	        total MUST be reported as a settlement that does not match its
1778	        receipt, identified by that ref.  The identifier settlement-
1779	        mismatch SHOULD be used for this condition.  An amount on that
1780	        repeating ref that cannot be parsed as an integer MUST be
1781	        reported without abandoning the audit; the identifier malformed-
1782	        amount SHOULD be used for this condition.  A verifier MUST still
1783	        report findings for the remaining records.

1785	   10.  Aborted receipts are not matched to extract rows and are not
1786	        added to totals.

1788	   11.  Decode each checkpoint.  Reject a failed signature, and reject a
1789	        kid that does not match the key obtained for the checkpoint
1790	        issuer, on the same terms as a receipt (MUST-T4-8).  Require
1791	        receiptCount, chainHeadHash, and totals to match the attested
1792	        receipts in [startMs, endMs) as defined above (MUST-T11-2); a
1793	        receipt step 4 rejected is not among them, or a forged receipt
1794	        could satisfy a checkpoint count.  The identifier checkpoint-
1795	        total-mismatch SHOULD be used for a failed signature, a wrong
1796	        receiptCount, or totals that disagree, and checkpoint-head-
1797	        mismatch for a chainHeadHash that is not the last link, in
1798	        issuer order (step 6), of the chain inside [startMs, endMs) -
1799	        "last receipt" binds to the chain, not to presentation or to
1800	        timestampMs.  If the signed totals is null, the verifier cannot
1801	        perform the totals comparison for that checkpoint.  It MUST
1802	        report that the comparison was skipped and MUST treat the
1803	        completeness guarantee as conditional; the absence of a
1804	        comparison is not a passed comparison (MUST-T11-12).  The
1805	        identifier checkpoint-totals-redacted SHOULD be used for this
1806	        condition. receiptCount and chainHeadHash MUST still be checked.
1807	        A checkpoint that fails verification MUST NOT be treated as
1808	        redacted, whatever it claims about its own totals.

1810	   12.  Every chained receipt MUST fall in exactly one checkpoint
1811	        window.  A gap or double count MUST be reported as a window
1812	        coverage failure (MUST-T11-7, MUST-T11-8).  The identifier
1813	        window-coverage SHOULD be used for this condition.  The check
1814	        runs against the presented checkpoint windows and it is fail-
1815	        closed: a receipt that falls under none of them is this failure
1816	        - including every receipt when no checkpoint was presented, and
1817	        receipts after the last closed checkpoint's endMs.  The text
1818	        names that last state an *open epoch*, and it is auditable only
1819	        when the checkpoint that closes it is issued; the name explains
1820	        why the finding fired, it does not soften it.  Nothing about an
1821	        absent checkpoint is silent: uncovered evidence surfaces as a
1822	        finding rather than as a gap in the report.

1824	   13.  Walk checkpoints in epoch order. prevCheckpointHash MUST equal
1825	        the SHA-256 of the previous checkpoint COSE bytes, or null for
1826	        genesis (MUST-T11-4).  The identifier checkpoint-total-mismatch
1827	        SHOULD be used for a broken chain, which is the fourth condition
1828	        its table row names.

1830	   14.  If two successfully verified checkpoints share an epoch number
1831	        and have different hashes, the verifier MUST report equivocation
1832	        (MUST-T11-3).  The identifier equivocation SHOULD be used for
1833	        this condition.  The checkpoints compared here are those
1834	        presented *together with* any carried by verified witness
1835	        receipts (step 15).  Comparing only the presented chain cannot
1836	        raise this finding: MUST-T11-8, applied in step 12, requires
1837	        that chain's epochs to be consecutive, so no two of its members
1838	        share an epoch.  A copy recorded by a witness is where the
1839	        second one is found.

1841	   15.  If witness receipts were supplied, verify them against the out-
1842	        of-band witness key (Section 10.3); receipts that cannot be
1843	        checked that way are not evidence in either direction and the
1844	        verifier reports that it left them out (MUST-T11-15); the
1845	        identifier unauthenticated-witness SHOULD be used for this
1846	        condition.  Discard any whose signature fails (MUST-T11-10).  A
1847	        surviving receipt whose statement body verifies against the
1848	        issuer root is a statement that issuer published; one that does
1849	        not is another party's, and is not this issuer equivocating
1850	        (MUST-T11-16).  The survivors are the recorded statement hashes
1851	        used in step 16.  Where a receipt also carries the statement
1852	        body, discard that body unless its statement hash equals the one
1853	        the receipt binds and it verifies as a checkpoint; the surviving
1854	        bodies are what step 14 compares.  Discarding a body does not
1855	        discard its receipt.  This is tier 1 of Section 11.3.  Where the
1856	        tier-2 pair - the registered Signed Statement bytes and an
1857	        inclusion proof - was also supplied, verify it as Section 11.3
1858	        states: apply the proof to the candidate entry's hash and accept
1859	        only on an exact match with a witness-signed receipt (MUST-
1860	        T11-18); a pair that fails, or a candidate without a proof, is
1861	        witness-inclusion-invalid and the audit MUST fail.  Where
1862	        witness receipts were supplied and no tier-2 pair was, report
1863	        witness-inclusion-not-exercised as a warning (MUST-T11-19): the
1864	        witness attested the statement hash, and log membership was not
1865	        proven.

1867	   16.  Compare the surviving witness records against the presented
1868	        chain (MUST-T11-11).  For each presented checkpoint with no
1869	        surviving record, report that it is not anchored; the identifier
1870	        checkpoint-not-anchored SHOULD be used.  This is a warning: a
1871	        witness may have been configured after the checkpoint was

2285	12.  Lifecycle

2287	   1.  *Manifest.* Parties sign a Trade Manifest (optional for metered
2288	       API spend; required for goods with acceptance criteria).

2290	   2.  *Policy check.* The adapter submits a structured request to the
2291	       PDP.  Default is deny.  An allow is a Decision Token (MUST-T6-4).

2293	   3.  *Payment.* On allow, the adapter performs the x402 (or other
2294	       rail) exchange using exactly the decision fields (MUST-T6-1).
2295	       The Decision Token is consumed (MUST-T6-2).  A reused nonce is
2296	       denied (MUST-T3-1, MUST-T3-2).  A tampered or expired token is
2297	       denied (MUST-T6-5).

2299	   4.  *Receipt.* The Receipt Issuer signs a Spend Receipt.  Rail
2300	       credentials MUST NOT appear in the receipt, logs, or tool results
2301	       (MUST-T5-2, MUST-T7-1).

2303	   5.  *Dispute Evidence Bundle.* If delivery bytes do not match the
2304	       acceptance-criteria hash, an implementation MUST be able to emit
2305	       a bundle of manifest + receipt + delivery hash (MUST-T8-3).  The
2306	       bundle MUST NOT be described as an arbitral award or escrow
2307	       release (MUST-T8-4).

2309	13.  Policy Semantics

2311	   Policy is default deny.  The engine understands three families of
2312	   rule:

2314	   *  *Limit:* maximum amount per payment; maximum cumulative amount per
2315	      window (MUST-T2-2).

2317	   *  *Velocity:* maximum number of allowed payments per window (MUST-
2318	      T2-1).

2320	   *  *Scope:* optional allow-lists for payee, currency, and tool name.

2322	   Fail-closed: missing engine, crash, or exception yields deny (MUST-
2323	   T2-3).  Implementations SHOULD emit stable reason codes (SHOULD-
2324	   T2-5).  Decision tokens SHOULD expire after a short TTL (SHOULD-
2325	   T6-3).

2327	   The agent-facing spend interface MUST invoke the PDP and MUST NOT
2328	   expose a parallel ungated rail call to the model (MUST-T5-1).

2330	   One boundary is stated here rather than left to be inferred.  In the
2331	   retrospective audit, "allowed by policy" is the Receipt Issuer's
2332	   signed assertion: the spend passed the issuer's gate under the
2333	   policyHash the receipt names.  The Decision Token is consumed at the
2334	   gate, and the verification algorithm never sees it; the audit does
2335	   not independently re-verify the PDP's allow.  The completeness side
2336	   of the audit has an independent leg - the rail extract - and the
2337	   policy side deliberately does not: that is a trust boundary of this
2338	   profile, not an oversight.  A deployment that wants the policy answer
2339	   to be independently verifiable needs a receipt-to-token binding,
2340	   which this revision does not define; it is named as a possible
2341	   extension (Section 19), and adding it would change what a receipt
2342	   carries, so it is not smuggled in here.

2344	14.  SCITT Anchoring

2346	   A Receipt Issuer or relying party MAY construct a SCITT Signed
2347	   Statement whose payload is either the Spend Receipt COSE object or a
2348	   privacy profile (Section 15) and register it with a Transparency
2349	   Service [RFC9943].  The service returns a COSE receipt [RFC9942].
2350	   Embedding that receipt yields a Transparent Statement.  Cedulon does
2351	   not define a new transparency algorithm.

2353	   An epoch checkpoint MUST be registrable on the same terms (MUST-
2354	   T11-14).  Its Signed Statement carries the checkpoint COSE_Sign1
2355	   object as the payload and application/cedulon-checkpoint+cbor as the
2356	   content type, which is among the media types Section 17 asks to have
2357	   registered and which, until then, is a placeholder like the rest.
2358	   Nothing else about registration differs from a receipt.

2360	   This is a short section for a requirement -01 was missing, and the
2361	   omission mattered more than its length suggests. -01 asked for
2362	   checkpoints to be registered (SHOULD-T11-5) while profiling only the
2363	   receipt here, so the object carrying the suppression guarantee had no
2364	   stated form to be registered in.  Two implementations could follow
2365	   -01 to the letter and register incomparable things.

2367	15.  Privacy Considerations

2369	   A public transparency encoding MUST support omitting or hashing payer
2370	   and payee identifiers and MUST support amount redaction or bucket
2371	   encoding (MUST-T9-1).  Implementations MUST NOT write government-ID
2372	   numbers, payment-instrument PAN, or street address into a public
2373	   statement (MUST-T9-2).  Default public anchors SHOULD publish
2374	   policyHash, manifestHash, receiptHash, and timestampMs rather than
2375	   full claims (SHOULD-T9-3).  A private auditor MAY receive an
2376	   unredacted receipt out of band (MAY-T9-4).

2378	   The paragraph above counts receipt fields.  A checkpoint publishes
2379	   something a receipt does not: a per-currency total for a whole
2380	   window, which discloses trading volume even when every individual
2381	   receipt is redacted (MUST-T9-5). -01 gave no rule for it, so an
2382	   implementation could publish that total, or withhold it in a way no
2383	   verifier could recognise, and neither reading contradicted the text.

2385	   The rule is the one stated in Section 11: totals MAY be withheld by
2386	   signing it as null (MUST-T11-12), and only that form counts as a
2387	   redaction (MUST-T11-13).  The structural claims are not redactable,
2388	   because a verifier that cannot read the window or the chain head
2389	   cannot check anything at all, and a checkpoint that hid them would be
2390	   indistinguishable from a broken one.

2392	   Withholding is honest and it is also a cost: a verifier that cannot
2393	   recompute the totals says so, and the completeness guarantee for that
2394	   window is conditional.  A deployment that wants an unconditional
2395	   result publishes the totals; a deployment that wants the volume
2396	   private accepts a conditional one.  What a deployment MUST NOT do is
2397	   obtain the unconditional result while withholding the evidence for
2398	   it.

3227	16.14.  T12: Settlement without a recorded receipt

3229	   The threats above are about a counterparty, a rail or an attacker.
3230	   This one is about the issuer's own implementation, and it produces
3231	   exactly the condition the rest of this document exists to make
3232	   detectable.

3234	   An issuer that settles a payment, appends the receipt in memory and
3235	   then persists its state has three steps where it could have two
3236	   outcomes.  If the write fails, the rail holds a settlement and the
3237	   receipt exists nowhere durable.  The next start reads a state that
3238	   does not contain it, and the audit in Section 11 reports settlement-
3239	   without-receipt against an honest issuer that did everything its own
3240	   policy asked.  The evidence is missing because the issuer lost it,
3241	   not because anyone hid it, and nothing in the report can tell those
3242	   apart.

3254	     |            | settlement in every place it still controls: in  |
3255	     |            | memory, on the rail ledger it controls, and in   |
3256	     |            | any later write it has not yet issued.  A        |
3257	     |            | refused payment MUST NOT consume the nonce or    |
3258	     |            | the payment allowance it never used.             |
3259	     +------------+--------------------------------------------------+
3260	     | MUST-T12-3 | Two issuers MUST NOT share one durable state.    |
3261	     |            | An implementation that permits it MUST fail      |
3262	     |            | loudly rather than let one writer overwrite the  |
3263	     |            | other's receipt, and the failure MUST name what  |
3264	     |            | an operator can act on.                          |
3265	     +------------+--------------------------------------------------+
3266	     | MUST-T12-4 | Where a settlement has entered a rail the issuer |
3267	     |            | does not control, and the local record cannot be |
3268	     |            | completed, the outcome is indeterminate.  The    |
3269	     |            | issuer MUST NOT treat the payment as reversed,   |
3270	     |            | and MUST NOT return the authority to spend,      |
3271	     |            | unless it holds authenticated evidence that the  |
3272	     |            | rail did not complete the settlement or that a   |
3273	     |            | reversing entry completed.                       |
3274	     +------------+--------------------------------------------------+

3276	                                  Table 23

3278	   Ordering:  Settle-then-record is the natural order to write and the
3279	      wrong one to ship.  The record is what makes the settlement
3280	      accountable, so the record is what has to be secured first (MUST-
3281	      T12-1), and a settlement that cannot be recorded has to be undone
3282	      everywhere the issuer still controls, including the nonce and the
3283	      allowance it never used (MUST-T12-2).  That undo is what the in-
3284	      process RailLedger and the session tests measure.  Once value has
3285	      entered a rail the issuer does not control, a local snapshot
3286	      cannot retract it.  Persistence failing after that point leaves
3287	      the outcome indeterminate; authority is not returned without
3288	      authenticated evidence that the rail did not complete the
3289	      settlement or that a reversing entry did (MUST-T12-4).

3291	   Recovery:  A durable-state conflict is not necessarily fatal, but it
3292	      MUST NOT be silent, and an implementation that refuses every
3293	      subsequent write without offering a way back has turned a
3294	      recoverable condition into an outage.  The reason reported has to
3295	      separate the cases an operator would act on differently (MUST-
3296	      T12-3): a write that conflicted with another writer, a write that
3297	      failed, and a state another process is holding.  A single opaque
3298	      failure leaves the operator to guess which of those happened.

3300	   Observability:  A violation of this threat is not visible in the
3301	      evidence a verifier receives.  The audit sees a settlement with no
3302	      receipt and reports settlement-without-receipt, which is the same
3303	      finding an adversary would produce, and nothing in the extract or
3304	      the receipt set distinguishes an issuer that lost the evidence
3305	      from one that hid it.  That is why the requirements here fall on
3306	      the issuer rather than on the verifier, and why an operator-facing
3307	      reason is required rather than optional.

4230	   inclusion receipt with its body stripped off can no longer bury a
4231	   withholding, while still not being allowed to accuse anyone.

4233	   T12 is new and is not about an adversary.  An issuer that settles,
4234	   appends the receipt in memory and then writes its state will, when
4235	   that write fails, leave the rail holding a settlement whose receipt
4236	   exists nowhere durable.  Restarted, it reports settlement-without-
4237	   receipt against itself.  The condition this document exists to make
4238	   detectable was reachable through the implementation's own ordering,
4239	   and no requirement in -02 said otherwise.

4241	   The same first subject carries five requirements this section has not
4242	   named so far, all of them stated in Section 10.  A pinned key that
4243	   cannot be decoded MUST NOT fall back to the keys the objects carry
4244	   (MUST-T4-11).  An issuer root may comprise more than one key, and a
4245	   verifier must accept one that does, so a rotation inside the audited
4246	   window does not force it to choose between findings against honest
4247	   receipts and abandoning the pin (MUST-T4-12); the same set-of-keys
4248	   acceptance applies to a publisher, witness, or rail pin.  The same
4249	   out-of-band rule reaches the payee countersignature (MUST-T4-13), the
4250	   transparency witness (MUST-T11-15 and MUST-T11-16), the Decision
4251	   Token, whose consumer issued it and therefore already holds the key
4252	   to check it with (MUST-T6-6), and a presented Trade Manifest (MUST-
4253	   T4-15).

4255	   Two requirements belong to neither subject.  MUST-T7-5 and MUST-T7-6
4256	   come from measuring the protection a stored signing key actually has
4257	   instead of deriving it from the platform.  A mount that ignores
4258	   filesystem permissions accepts the call and protects nothing, and a
4259	   writable directory or a symbolic link anywhere on the path makes the
4260	   file permission moot.  They are stated because the implementation
4261	   reported protection it did not have.

4263	   Two things are stated here that -02 got right and -03 kept unchanged:
4264	   the extract rule itself, and the treatment of a pinned key the
4265	   verifier cannot decode.  What changed is their reach, and the change
4266	   is not backward compatible.  Where a verifier pinned the rail key,
4267	   supplied no issuer key, and was presented with receipts or
4268	   checkpoints, -02 reported the guarantee as unconditional and -03
4269	   reports it as conditional.  Nothing about the evidence changed; what
4270	   changed is that the guarantee now says which questions were never
4271	   asked.  An audit presented with neither is unaffected, for the reason
4272	   given in Section 10.1.

4274	18.6.  Changes from -01

4276	   -02 had one subject: the checkpoint, which carries the T11 guarantee
4277	   against suppression and rollback, was not wired into anything that
4278	   could discharge it.

4280	   A reader of -01 set out the gap and a second reader confirmed it
4281	   independently.  Four things were wrong at once, and they were the
4282	   same thing seen from four sides.  The SCITT anchoring section
4283	   profiled the Spend Receipt and not the checkpoint, so SHOULD-T11-5
4284	   asked for checkpoints to be registered without saying in what form
4285	   (MUST-T11-14 now says).  None of the steps of the verification
4286	   algorithm read a witness receipt, so a deployment could follow
4287	   SHOULD-T11-5 to the letter and still have a verifier that never
4288	   consulted the witness (steps 15 and 16 now do).  MUST-T11-3,
4289	   equivocation, could not fire at all: the only checkpoints compared
4290	   were the presented ones, and MUST-T11-8 requires those to be
4291	   consecutive, so no two of them can share an epoch.  A second copy is
4292	   found in a witness, and nothing brought one in.  And the checkpoint
4293	   binds a per-currency total for a window while the privacy section
4294	   counted only receipt fields, so a window total could be published, or
4295	   withheld, with no stated rule either way.

4297	   The repairs are MUST-T11-10 through MUST-T11-14.  The transparency
4298	   receipt is a new optional input; supplying none leaves the verifier
4299	   behaving exactly as in -01, which is deliberate, because the point of
4300	   the witness is to add a claim that could not be made before, not to
4301	   withdraw one that could.

4303	   Two decisions inside those repairs are worth stating on their own,
4304	   because a reader might reasonably have expected the other choice.

4306	   *  A recorded checkpoint the presented chain omits gets its own
4307	      identifier, checkpoint-withheld, rather than being folded into
4308	      window-coverage (MUST-T11-11).  The reporter asked which way it
4309	      should go.  Coverage says the record shown is incomplete; a
4310	      withheld checkpoint says the party under audit is holding a record
4311	      it did not show.  An operator who sees one identifier for both
4312	      cannot tell an incomplete record from a concealed one, which is
4313	      the distinction the whole threat is about.

4315	   *  A checkpoint with no receipt in a supplied witness is a warning
4316	      rather than a failure.  A witness may be configured after
4317	      checkpoints have already been issued, and an operator's own gap in
4318	      anchoring is not evidence that anything was concealed.  The
4319	      asymmetry is deliberate: what the witness holds and the chain does
4320	      not is a finding, what the chain holds and the witness does not is
4321	      a warning.

4323	   One change is not a repair of -01 but of the first attempt at this
4324	   revision, and it is recorded because the failure is instructive.
4325	   Signed totals were first made redactable through a field carried
4326	   alongside the checkpoint rather than inside the COSE payload.
4327	   Anything outside the signature is chosen by whoever presents the
4328	   object, which here is the party under audit, so a checkpoint whose
4329	   signed totals disagreed with its receipts could be re-presented as
4330	   redacted and the mismatch went unreported.  Redaction is now inside
4331	   the signature and a redaction asserted anywhere else MUST be ignored
4332	   (MUST-T11-12, MUST-T11-13).  This was the same shape as the bypass
4333	   -01 was written to close: a check that a party under audit could
4334	   switch off.

4336	   Reversal, refund, partial settlement, and the escrow role remain out
4337	   of scope and are still expected later, with no date. -01 said the
4338	   same, and no revision since has improved on it.

4340	   The reporters are named in the Acknowledgments.

4342	19.  Evolution and Future Work (Informative)

4344	   This section is a direction, not a commitment.  The structures below
4345	   are reserved in name only.  Normative wire formats, tests, and
4346	   threat-model MUST lines for them belong in later revisions (-08 or
4347	   later), written with the same discipline as this -07.

4349	19.1.  Re-attestation profile

4351	   Algorithms retire.  A Spend Receipt or checkpoint signed under
4352	   Ed25519 today may need a later verifier that no longer accepts -19.
4353	   A companion seed [REATTEST] sketches re-attestation: register the
4354	   original COSE bytes as a SCITT Signed Statement and have a current
4355	   algorithm countersign or receipt them.  The principle is that
4356	   structures outlive ciphers.  The first concrete example is the
4357	   profile's own move from generic EdDSA (-8) to Ed25519 (-19) in
4358	   [RFC9864].

4360	19.2.  Streaming reconciliation

4362	   Epoch checkpoints in this document are batch windows.  A later
4363	   revision may define a continuous, second-scale profile [STREAMING] in
4364	   which the same completeness relation is evaluated as settlements
4365	   arrive, without waiting for an epoch close.  That work does not
4366	   change the matching rules in this document, and it did not arrive in
4367	   this revision either.

4369	19.3.  Generalization

4371	   Payment is the special case that this -07 implements.  The same
4372	   completeness calculus (an authenticated extract of consumed units
4373	   reconciled to signed receipts) can apply to other consumable
4374	   resources such as compute, data, or energy.  This document does not
4375	   specify those profiles.

4377	20.  Informative Notes on Adjacent Protocols

4379	   x402 [X402] uses HTTP 402 [RFC9110] to negotiate stablecoin payment.
4380	   AP2 [AP2] uses signed mandates as verifiable credentials.  Cedulon
4381	   does not replace either protocol.  Profiles built on HTTP Message
4382	   Signatures [RFC9421] authenticate bots; they are not a spend receipt.
4383	   draft-bates-atp [BATES-ATP] is a lineage neighbor.  It does not
4384	   define rail-extract completeness.

4386	   draft-vauban-x402-stark-receipts [VAUBAN] specifies complementary
4387	   x402 receipt-format variants that a Cedulon Spend Receipt MAY carry
4388	   as a rail proof; it does not define rail-extract completeness. draft-
4389	   schrock-ep-outcome-binding [SCHROCK] compares authorized action bytes
4390	   to independently observed effects; it does not define rail-extract
4391	   completeness. draft-marques-asqav-compliance-receipts [MARQUES]
4392	   profiles access-control action receipts (the broader Acta family
4393	   includes [ACTA]); it does not define rail-extract completeness.
4394	   draft-hopley-x402-compliance-receipt [HOPLEY] records an admission-
4395	   time compliance decision; it does not define rail-extract
4396	   completeness. draft-abak-agent-control-delivery-evidence [ABAK]
4397	   states evidence requirements for a governance control - stop,
4398	   suspend, revoke - travelling toward the component expected to
4399	   constrain a runtime, and keeps emission, receiver-side observation,
4400	   enforcement outcome and observed control effect as separate results.
4401	   Its object moves the other way from this one: Cedulon reconciles a
4402	   spend that already happened against what the rail reported, and
4403	   evidences neither the delivery of a control instruction nor its
4404	   enforcement.  A denied spend leaves no portable artifact here at all
4405	   - a Decision Token encodes an allow (Section 8) - so what a Cedulon
4406	   audit says about a refusal it says through the settlement that did
4407	   not appear on the extract, which is an effect observation over a
4408	   declared population and not an acknowledgement from an enforcement
4409	   point.  Its bounded-population rule and this document's MUST-T10-18
4410	   and MUST-T10-19 are the same kind of bound on two different objects.

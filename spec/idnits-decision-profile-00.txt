11	Abstract

13	   The Cedulon core document reconciles an issuer's signed Spend
14	   Receipts against an authenticated extract of a payment rail and
15	   reports, over a declared population, that no settlement lacks a
16	   receipt and no settled receipt is absent from the rail.  Money is the
17	   special case that document implements.  This document defines a
18	   second population on the same reconciler.  A Decision Record is
19	   signed by the party that decided whether an agent may act; an Effect
20	   Extract is an authenticated list of the effects that actually
21	   occurred on a channel.  An allow must be matched by exactly one
22	   effect whose content hash the record named; a refusal must be matched
23	   by none.  The Decision Record claim set, the Effect Extract shape,
24	   the points at which the reconciliation departs from the spend rules,
25	   the finding codes, and one media type are defined.  The text is
26	   provisional and the companion implementation carries the profile
27	   prepared and unpublished.

98	1.  Introduction

100	   The Cedulon core document [CEDULON] answers one question about an
101	   agent that spends: did every settlement on the rail have a receipt
102	   behind it, and did every settled receipt reach the rail?  It answers
103	   it by closing three signed objects over a declared population: the
104	   issuer's records, an authenticated extract of the counterparty
105	   system, and epoch checkpoints that total the records.  The verifier
106	   holds the keys out of band and the report names the population it
107	   covered.

109	   An agent that acts without spending raises the same question with
110	   different nouns.  A party decided whether the agent may reply, post,
111	   send, or call; a channel carried whatever the agent then did.  Did
112	   every effect on the channel have a decision behind it?  Did every
113	   allowed action occur, once, with the content that was allowed?  Did
114	   anything occur that was refused?  Section 19 of [CEDULON] reserves
115	   later profiles in name only, and its Section 19.3 sketches the same
116	   completeness calculus for other consumable resources: compute, data,
117	   energy.  This document is a different population on the same
118	   reconciler, decisions against effects rather than another unit of
119	   spend, and it is the first profile written out.

121	   The profile keeps the core's three roles and its verification
122	   algorithm.  What changes is the record, the row, the binding between
123	   them, and the words the report uses.  What does not change is
124	   measured: the companion implementation holds the spend behaviour byte
125	   for byte behind a golden file, and every rule in this document that
126	   departs from the spend rules is stated as a departure.

128	   This document is a companion to the core document, not a revision of
129	   it.  It is not an IETF working-group item.  Its requirement language
130	   is provisional in the sense Section 19 of [CEDULON] gives the
131	   structures it reserves: a direction written with the core's
132	   discipline, not a commitment, and a later revision may change it.
133	   The companion implementation carries the profile prepared and
134	   unpublished.

136	2.  Terminology

138	   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT",
139	   "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and
140	   "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in
141	   BCP 14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all
142	   capitals, as shown here.

144	   Terms defined in the core document keep their meaning here: Policy
145	   Decision Point, epoch checkpoint, trust root, population, finding,
146	   warning, guarantee.  The following are specific to this profile.

148	   Decider:  The party that decides, per request, whether the agent may
149	      act.  It signs Decision Records and epoch checkpoints over them.
150	      Its key is the issuer root of this profile (Section 7).

152	   Subject:  The party on whose request the decision was taken, named in
153	      the record as an opaque identifier.

155	   Decision Record:  A COSE_Sign1 object signed by the Decider stating
156	      one decision: allow, deny, or defer, with the request it answered,
157	      the policy it applied, and, for an allow, the reference and the
158	      content hash of the effect it allowed (Section 4).

160	   Effect:  One thing that happened on a channel as a result of, or in
161	      the absence of, a decision: a message sent, a post made, a call
162	      placed.  It is identified by a reference, classed by a short name,
163	      and bound by the SHA-256 of its content.

165	   Channel:  The system on which effects occur and from which an Effect
166	      Extract is taken.  It plays the role a rail plays in the core
167	      document.

169	   Effect Extract:  The authenticated list of effects on one channel,
170	      for one Decider, over one window (Section 5).  It plays the role a
171	      rail extract plays in the core document.

173	   Refusal:  A Decision Record whose decision is deny or defer.  A
174	      refusal expects no effect.

176	3.  The population

178	   The core document's reconciler closes an issuer record against a
179	   counterparty row over a declared population.  This profile fills the
180	   same three roles:

182	       +==============+===================+========================+
183	       | Role         | Spend (core)      | Decision (this         |
184	       |              |                   | document)              |
185	       +==============+===================+========================+
186	       | Issuer       | Spend Receipt     | Decision Record        |
187	       | record       |                   |                        |
188	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+
189	       | Counterparty | settlement record | effect row on an       |
190	       | row          | on a rail extract | Effect Extract         |
191	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+
192	       | Match key    | ref               | ref                    |
193	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+
194	       | Content      | amount and        | allow: a row exists    |
195	       | binding      | currency equal    | and effectHash is      |
196	       |              |                   | equal; refusal: no row |
197	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+
198	       | Record that  | outcome aborted   | decision deny or defer |
199	       | expects no   |                   |                        |
200	       | row          |                   |                        |
201	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+
202	       | Aggregate    | checkpoint totals | checkpoint totals per  |
203	       | witness      | per currency      | decision kind          |
204	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+
205	       | Declared     | account, rail,    | decider, channel,      |
206	       | population   | window            | window                 |
207	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+

209	                                  Table 1

211	   Which population a presented document belongs to is the verifier's
212	   call, made by the profile it applies, and never the document's.  A
213	   verifier applying this profile MUST read every presented record as a
214	   Decision Record and every presented extract as an Effect Extract, and
215	   MUST refuse by name a document that does not have that shape; it MUST
216	   NOT infer the population from members a body happens to carry (MUST-
217	   DP-1).  The companion found the alternative wrong in both directions:
218	   a rail extract that added a member named effects was re-routed away
219	   from the spend rules it was subject to, and an Effect Extract handed
220	   to the spend rules crashed before any report existed.  Under this
221	   profile a rail extract is the wrong document and is refused as one;
222	   under the spend rules an Effect Extract is refused the same way.

224	4.  Decision Record

226	   A Decision Record is COSE_Sign1 with the header profile of
227	   Section 6.2 of [CEDULON]: deterministic CBOR, alg -19 (Ed25519,
228	   [RFC9864]), kid mandatory and computed as the core states, an empty
229	   unprotected header refused by name if not empty, and the payload the
230	   CBOR encoding of the claim map below.  The content type header
231	   parameter is application/cedulon-decision-record+cbor (Section 10).

233	4.1.  Claim labels

235	   The labels lie in the Private Use range of the CWT Claims registry
236	   [RFC8392], below the block the core document uses for the Decision
237	   Token (-70301 to -70305) and the countersignature (-70401, -70402),
238	   so that no two Cedulon claim maps share a label.  Every claim
239	   annotated hash carries a SHA-256 [RFC6234] digest rendered as exactly
240	   64 lowercase hexadecimal characters, the grammar of Section 6.1 of
241	   [CEDULON], and a value outside that grammar is refused by name at
242	   signing and at verification.

244	         +========+================+=============================+
245	         | Label  | Claim          | CBOR type                   |
246	         +========+================+=============================+
247	         | -70501 | decider        | tstr                        |
248	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
249	         | -70502 | subject        | tstr                        |
250	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
251	         | -70503 | requestHash    | tstr (hash)                 |
252	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
253	         | -70504 | policyHash     | tstr (hash)                 |
254	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
255	         | -70505 | inputsHash     | tstr (hash) / null          |
256	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
257	         | -70506 | decision       | tstr (allow / deny / defer) |
258	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
259	         | -70507 | reasonCode     | tstr                        |
260	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
261	         | -70508 | ref            | tstr / null                 |
262	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
263	         | -70509 | effectHash     | tstr (hash) / null          |
264	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
265	         | -70510 | timestampMs    | uint                        |
266	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
267	         | -70511 | nonce          | tstr                        |
268	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
269	         | -70512 | prevRecordHash | tstr (hash) / null          |
270	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+

272	                                  Table 2

274	   All twelve labels are always present; a nullable claim carries CBOR
275	   null when it has no value.

277	   decider and subject are opaque identifiers chosen by the deployment.
278	   requestHash is the SHA-256 of the request the Decider evaluated, in
279	   the canonical encoding of Section 7 of [CEDULON] when the request is
280	   a JSON document and over its UTF-8 octets when it is text; this
281	   document does not fix the request's fields, and a deployment MUST
282	   state what it hashes. policyHash is the SHA-256 of the canonical
283	   policy document the Decider applied. inputsHash, when not null, is
284	   the SHA-256 of whatever further context the Decider consulted,
285	   encoded the same way, so that a later reader can tell two decisions
286	   on the same request apart by what else was on the table. reasonCode
287	   is a short token the deployment defines; it is carried, not
288	   interpreted.

290	   ref is the reference under which the allowed effect will appear on
291	   the channel, and the key on which the reconciliation matches.
292	   effectHash is the SHA-256 of the content of the effect the Decider
293	   allowed, over the octets the channel will carry: for a text reply,
294	   the UTF-8 octets of the text.  The Effect Extract computes the same
295	   digest over the same octets (Section 5), so equality of the two is
296	   equality of content.

298	   timestampMs is the decision time in POSIX milliseconds. nonce
299	   identifies the record. prevRecordHash links records into the
300	   Decider's chain: it is the SHA-256 of the previous record's
301	   COSE_Sign1 octets, the same input the core's receiptHash takes on the
302	   COSE path (Section 7.1 of [CEDULON]), or null for the first record of
303	   a chain.

305	4.2.  Claim rules

307	   A signer MUST refuse to sign, and a verifier MUST reject, a claim set
308	   that breaks any of the following, naming the rule in the refusal
309	   (MUST-DP-2):

311	   *  decision is one of allow, deny, defer.

313	   *  Every hash-annotated claim that is not null matches the hash
314	      grammar.

316	   *  timestampMs is a non-negative integer of magnitude at most 2^53 -
317	      1, the uint the label table states; a CBOR decoder hands back any
318	      number, and the rule is what makes the table true.

320	   *  An allow carries a non-empty ref and a non-null effectHash.  An
321	      allow that names no effect is a decision the reconciliation cannot
322	      close, and an allow that names no reference is one it cannot find.

324	   *  A refusal carries effectHash null.  A refusal binds to the absence
325	      of an effect, never to a content hash, so a hash on a refusal
326	      would be a claim the audit cannot measure and a second reading of
327	      whether the effect occurred.  A refusal MAY carry a ref: it names
328	      what was refused, and an effect appearing under that reference is
329	      the worst finding this profile has (Section 6.3).

331	   The verifier MUST apply these rules itself, on the claim map it
332	   decoded from the signed payload, and MUST NOT rely on the signer
333	   having applied them (MUST-DP-3).  The Decider is the party under
334	   audit.  A Decider that signed a well-formed COSE_Sign1 over a claim
335	   map that skips a rule has produced an object whose signature
336	   verifies, and a verifier that checked only the signature and the
337	   equality of the decoded map with the presented claims would attest
338	   it.  The companion implementation did exactly that until it was
339	   measured: an allow with no reference, signed below the signer's own
340	   rules under the pinned decider key, verified true, was attested, was
341	   counted as unmatched, and the audit still said the books balanced.
342	   The rules now run at both ends.

344	4.3.  Presentation and confusion

346	   A Decision Record is presented as Section 6.3 of [CEDULON] states for
347	   the core's COSE objects: the signed octets, the decoded claim set,
348	   and the Decider's public key as a SubjectPublicKeyInfo PEM beside
349	   them.  The carried key is not an identity source.  Under a pinned
350	   decider key a record that verifies under the pin while carrying
351	   another key is reported as carried-key-mismatch, a warning, and stays
352	   attested; with no pin held the signature check that runs against the
353	   carried key says the record is internally consistent and nothing
354	   about who signed it.

356	   A Decision Record is not a Decision Token.  The core's Decision Token
357	   (Section 8 of [CEDULON]) is the portable encoding of a PDP allow,
358	   carried by the party that will spend; a Decision Record is the
359	   Decider's own log of what it decided, kept for audit, and it exists
360	   for refusals as well.  The two carry different content types and
361	   different claim maps.  A verifier MUST reject a Decision Record whose
362	   content type is not application/cedulon-decision-record+cbor, and
363	   MUST reject a token presented as a record or a record presented as a
364	   token, on the content type, before the signature is checked and
365	   before any claim is read (MUST-DP-4).

367	4.4.  The Decider's chain and checkpoints

369	   Decision Records chain on prevRecordHash the way Spend Receipts chain
370	   on prevReceiptHash, and the Decider signs epoch checkpoints over them
371	   with the checkpoint claim set of Section 11.1 of [CEDULON] unchanged:
372	   receiptCount is the number of records in the window, chainHeadHash is
373	   the SHA-256 of the last record's COSE_Sign1 octets, and totals is a
374	   map from the three decision kinds to decimal counts, {"allow": n,
375	   "deny": n, "defer": n}, each rendered as a text string as the core
376	   renders its currency totals.  A verifier compares the totals it
377	   computes over the attested records in the window against the signed
378	   map, and a difference is checkpoint-total-mismatch as in the core.

380	   Two records that claim the same position in a chain cannot both link
381	   to it: the second record's prevRecordHash must be the first's hash,
382	   so a Decider that signs two decisions under one nonce, or presents
383	   one record twice, breaks its own chain and the walk names the break.
384	   The chain is the equivocation control of this profile, and a verifier
385	   MUST walk it over every presented record that carries the pinned
386	   decider key, not only over the records that verified, so that a
387	   record that claims the pin and fails the rules is named by the walk
388	   rather than dropped from the population without a word (MUST-DP-5).

390	5.  Effect Extract Profile

392	   A verifier checks completeness against an Effect Extract, not against
393	   the Decider's own records alone.  The extract is the channel's
394	   account of what happened, obtained independently of the Decider, and
395	   the profile is only as strong as that independence (Section 7).

397	5.1.  Body and row schema

399	   The extract body is one JSON document with exactly this shape:

401	      +===============+=============================================+
402	      | Member        | JSON type                                   |
403	      +===============+=============================================+
404	      | deciderId     | string (non-empty)                          |
405	      +---------------+---------------------------------------------+
406	      | channelId     | string (non-empty)                          |
407	      +---------------+---------------------------------------------+
408	      | windowStartMs | number (POSIX milliseconds, a safe integer) |
409	      +---------------+---------------------------------------------+
410	      | windowEndMs   | number (POSIX milliseconds, a safe integer, |
411	      |               | greater than windowStartMs)                 |
412	      +---------------+---------------------------------------------+
413	      | effects       | array of effect rows                        |
414	      +---------------+---------------------------------------------+

416	                                  Table 3

418	   Each effect row is a JSON object with exactly these members:

420	       +=============+=============================================+
421	       | Member      | JSON type                                   |
422	       +=============+=============================================+
423	       | ref         | string (non-empty; the reference the        |
424	       |             | Decision Record named)                      |
425	       +-------------+---------------------------------------------+
426	       | effectHash  | string (SHA-256 of the effect's content, 64 |
427	       |             | lowercase hex)                              |
428	       +-------------+---------------------------------------------+
429	       | effectClass | string (non-empty; a short class name the   |
430	       |             | channel defines, such as a reply or a post) |
431	       +-------------+---------------------------------------------+
432	       | timestampMs | number (POSIX milliseconds, a safe integer, |
433	       |             | inside the window)                          |
434	       +-------------+---------------------------------------------+
435	       | actor       | string (optional; the party the effect      |
436	       |             | reached)                                    |
437	       +-------------+---------------------------------------------+

439	                                  Table 4

441	   These member names are normative.  The body and its rows follow the
442	   core's rail extract (Section 9 of [CEDULON]) in every rule that
443	   document states for a JSON body: the text is read for a repeated
444	   member name before it is parsed and refused as json-duplicate-key;
445	   integers are safe integers; the window is half-open and MUST end
446	   after it starts; a missing member, a wrong type, an empty identifier,
447	   or a hash outside the grammar is refused by name at both ends, by the
448	   signer before it signs and by the verifier before it checks a
449	   signature.

451	   The profile departs from the rail extract at two points, and a reader
452	   who knows the core should note both (MUST-DP-6):

454	   *  A member this document does not name is refused, on the body and
455	      on a row.  The core lets a rail add members of its own because a
456	      rail is a system the profile does not control; an Effect Extract
457	      is produced by a process the deployment does control (Section 7),
458	      and the companion measured what a free member can do to a
459	      population (Section 3).  A later revision may open this once a
460	      channel that needs its own members is measured.

462	   *  A row whose timestampMs falls outside [windowStartMs, windowEndMs)
463	      makes the whole extract malformed, refused as effect-outside-
464	      window before any signature is checked.  The core accepts such a
465	      rail extract and names the row (extract-scope-mismatch).  Here the
466	      extract is the deployment's own document and a window it did not
467	      keep is a document it did not produce correctly: a signer applying
468	      this schema never produces such an extract, and a verifier refuses
469	      one that is presented as a document, whatever else it may also
470	      name about its rows.  The trade is stated so it can be reversed: a
471	      single row out of place fails the whole window closed.

473	   effectHash on a row is computed by the extract's signer over the same
474	   octets a Decider hashes for its effectHash claim: the content as the
475	   channel carried it.  A deployment MUST state those octets once for
476	   both sides; the companion's example channel hashes the UTF-8 octets
477	   of the message text.

479	5.2.  Authentication and scope

481	   The extract is signed the way a rail extract is signed: Ed25519
482	   [RFC8032] over the UTF-8 octets of the [RFC8785] encoding of the
483	   body, with the signature as base64 and the signer's public key as a
484	   SubjectPublicKeyInfo PEM beside the body, neither inside the signed
485	   octets.  It is a JSON document with a detached signature, not a COSE
486	   object, and like the rail extract it has no media type: the core
487	   registers names for the objects whose content type is checked inside
488	   a protected header, and an extract has no such header.  A first cut
489	   of the companion exported a +cbor name for it that nothing used; the
490	   name is withdrawn with this revision.  Section 9.3 of [CEDULON]
491	   applies unchanged: a signature proves internal consistency and not
492	   origin; the verifier MUST hold the extract signer's key out of band
493	   and MUST compare keys as SubjectPublicKeyInfo DER; with no key held
494	   the guarantee is conditional and unauthenticated-extract is reported;
495	   with a key held, an extract that does not verify under it is extract-
496	   key-mismatch and its rows are not reconciled (settlement-comparison-
497	   skipped, the core's name for the same condition).

499	   The extract is scoped to one Decider, one channel, and one window,
500	   and Section 9.4 of [CEDULON] applies with the nouns renamed: a
501	   verifier that knows which Decider, channel, and window it audits MUST
502	   check the extract against them and MUST fail closed on a mismatch
503	   (extract-scope-mismatch); one that has not stated the window MUST
504	   report unstated-audit-window, one that has not stated the Decider or
505	   the channel MUST report unstated-audit-scope, and in either case the
506	   guarantee is conditional.  The strongest line this profile can print,
507	   a balanced audit under an unconditional guarantee, is true of one
508	   Decider, on one channel, over one window, and a report that carries
509	   it MUST also carry those three (MUST-DP-7).

511	6.  Reconciliation

513	   The verification algorithm of Section 11.4 of [CEDULON] runs
514	   unchanged over this population: establish the subject, verify the
515	   extract, check scope, resolve records against the decider root, walk
516	   the chain, index both sides by ref, match, decode and walk the
517	   checkpoints, consult the witness if one is supplied, and decide.
518	   This section states only what the algorithm reads differently.

520	6.1.  What binds

522	   A Decision Record expects a row when its decision is allow, and
523	   expects none when it is a refusal.  For a ref that appears once on
524	   each side:

526	   *  an allow and a row bind when the row's effectHash equals the
527	      record's effectHash; a difference is effect-mismatch (the content
528	      that occurred is not the content that was allowed);

530	   *  an allow with no row is decision-without-effect;

532	   *  a row with no record is effect-without-decision;

534	   *  a row whose ref a refusal names is effect-against-refusal.

536	   The last is the finding this profile exists for.  A spend audit has
537	   no row that should not be there in the same sense: an aborted receipt
538	   that still carries its reference and a settlement under that
539	   reference is reported by the core as a settlement without a receipt,
540	   and it never told the two cases apart.  Here a refusal that was
541	   followed by the effect it refused is a different fact from an effect
542	   nobody decided on, and it has its own name.

544	   A ref that appears more than once on a side is duplicate-ref as in
545	   the core, and the repeating reference is then reconciled by count
546	   rather than by amount: there is nothing to sum.  More rows than
547	   records under one reference is effect-without-decision; more records
548	   than rows is decision-without-effect.

550	   There is no amount, no currency, no manifest, no terms, and no
551	   counterparty axis on this profile.  The core's counterparty-unbound
552	   scope record is not emitted: effectHash binds the content of the
553	   effect itself, which is more than a payee name ever bound on spend,
554	   and actor on a row is carried for the reader, not measured.  The
555	   core's boundary rule applies unchanged: an unmatched item inside the
556	   declared clock-skew allowance of a window edge is boundary-deferred,
557	   and a closing-edge allow whose ref the following extract names is
558	   carried, not a finding.

560	6.2.  Conservation

562	   With |R| the in-scope Decision Records and |E| the effect rows, the
563	   identities the core report publishes hold with the words changed:

565	   |R|      = refusals + allows
566	   refusals = deny + defer
567	   allows   = matched + deferred + carried
568	              + unmatched + repeated + unreconciled
569	   |E|      = matched + deferred
570	              + unmatched + repeated + unreconciled
571	   matched on |R| equals matched on |E|

573	   A report under this profile MUST publish these counts and MUST name
574	   the population they were computed over (MUST-DP-8), for the reason
575	   the core gives: a report whose counts do not close is a report that
576	   lost a record somewhere, and a reader is entitled to see that without
577	   re-running the audit.  The report publishes refusals as one count,
578	   the core's aborted; the split of that count into deny and defer is
579	   the checkpoint's totals (Section 4.4), not a counter of the report.

581	6.3.  Finding codes

583	   The identifiers below are for diagnostic output and are not an
584	   interoperability surface, as Section 11.5 of [CEDULON] states for the
585	   core's codes.  Four are new to this profile:

587	    +=========================+========+==============================+
588	    | Code                    | Effect | Meaning                      |
589	    +=========================+========+==============================+
590	    | decision-without-effect | audit  | An allow names a reference   |
591	    |                         | fails  | under which no effect        |
592	    |                         |        | occurred, or a reference had |
593	    |                         |        | more records than rows       |
594	    +-------------------------+--------+------------------------------+
595	    | effect-without-decision | audit  | An effect occurred under a   |
596	    |                         | fails  | reference no Decision Record |
597	    |                         |        | names, or a reference had    |
598	    |                         |        | more rows than records       |
599	    +-------------------------+--------+------------------------------+
600	    | effect-against-refusal  | audit  | An effect occurred under a   |
601	    |                         | fails  | reference a refusal names    |
602	    +-------------------------+--------+------------------------------+
603	    | effect-mismatch         | audit  | The effect that occurred     |
604	    |                         | fails  | does not carry the content   |
605	    |                         |        | hash the allow named         |
606	    +-------------------------+--------+------------------------------+

608	                                  Table 5

610	   The remaining codes a report under this profile can carry are the
611	   core's, with the same effect on the verdict and the guarantee:
612	   duplicate-ref, boundary-deferred, receipt-chain-break for a break in
613	   the Decider's chain (signature, rule, or link), checkpoint-total-
614	   mismatch, checkpoint-head-mismatch, window-coverage, equivocation,
615	   unauthenticated-extract, extract-key-mismatch, extract-scope-
616	   mismatch, extract-settlement-mismatch (a caller-supplied row list
617	   that disagrees with the extract), settlement-comparison-skipped,
618	   trust-key-unreadable, unauthenticated-issuer, issuer-key-mismatch,
619	   carried-key-mismatch, unstated-audit-window, unstated-audit-scope,
620	   the witness codes, and malformed-policy-hash; the other hash claims
621	   of a Decision Record are refused at verification (Section 4.2) and
622	   reach the chain walk rather than a malformed-hash code.  Two code
623	   names carry a spend noun onto this profile (receipt-chain-break,

626	   aliases.  The codes the core defines for a Trade Manifest, a payee
627	   countersignature, a beneficiary, or a counterparty are not reachable
628	   on this profile.

630	   The sentences a report prints beside those codes are another matter.
631	   An operator reading a decision report SHOULD NOT have to translate
632	   "settlement" as "effect" or "receipt" as "decision record"; an
633	   implementation SHOULD print the sentence in the population's own
634	   words, and the companion holds that under a test that runs every
635	   conformance case and refuses a spend noun in any decision sentence.
636	   Counter names in a returned structure are diagnostic and MAY keep the
637	   core's names.

639	7.  Trust roots

641	   This profile has two roots, filling the core's issuer root and rail
642	   root (Sections 10.1 and 9.3 of [CEDULON]):

644	   The decider root:  The key under which Decision Records and their
645	      checkpoints are attested.  Everything Section 10.1 of the core
646	      states for the issuer root applies: a pinned key attests by
647	      signature, a carried key is not an identity, a record under
648	      another key is issuer-key-mismatch and covers nothing, and with no
649	      pin unauthenticated-issuer makes the guarantee conditional.

651	   The effect-extract root:  The key under which the Effect Extract is
652	      attested.  Everything Section 9.3 of the core states for the rail
653	      key applies.

655	   The core's payee, witness, decision-token, and manifest roots are not
656	   used by this profile, except that a transparency witness MAY hold the
657	   Decider's checkpoints exactly as it holds a Receipt Issuer's, with
658	   the witness root and codes of the core unchanged.

660	   What the two roots do not cover is the relation between them.  The
661	   profile's claim is only as strong as the independence of the party
662	   that signs the Effect Extract from the party that signs the Decision
663	   Records.  Where the channel operator signs an export of its own log,
664	   the extract root is that operator's key and the independence is the
665	   operator's.  Where the channel operator signs nothing, which is the
666	   common case for a messaging platform, the extract is produced by a
667	   capture process the deployment runs, and the deployment is stating,
668	   by pinning that process's key, that the process is not the Decider
669	   and cannot be told what to omit.  A deployment MUST state which of
670	   the two it has, and a verifier MUST treat the guarantee as
671	   conditional where the extract root and the decider root are, or may
672	   be, the same party (MUST-DP-9).  The companion cannot measure that
673	   from the keys alone; two keys can be held by one hand.

675	8.  Security Considerations

677	   The core's threat analysis (Section 16 of [CEDULON]) applies where
678	   the nouns carry over: forgery and repudiation of a signed record
679	   (T4), key leakage (T7), and suppression of checkpoints (T11) are the
680	   same threats against a Decider that they are against a Receipt
681	   Issuer, and the controls are the same.  The threats below are the
682	   ones this population adds or sharpens.

684	8.1.  D1: An effect occurs against a refusal

686	   The agent, or something acting through its channel access, does what
687	   the Decider refused.  This is the threat the profile exists for.  A
688	   Decision Record for the refusal, with the reference it refused, and
689	   an authenticated extract that carries an effect under that reference
690	   make the event effect-against-refusal, and the audit fails.  A
691	   refusal that carried no reference cannot support this finding; the
692	   effect is then effect-without-decision, which also fails the audit
693	   but does not say it was refused.  A Decider SHOULD carry the
694	   reference on a refusal whenever the channel assigns one before the
695	   decision.

697	8.2.  D2: Effects without decisions

699	   Something acts on the channel that never asked.  Every such effect is
700	   effect-without-decision.  The control is the extract's completeness,
701	   which is the extract root's independence (Section 7); a capture
702	   process the Decider controls can leave the effect out.

704	8.3.  D3: Substitution of content

706	   The Decider allows one content and the channel carries another.  The
707	   allow's effectHash and the row's effectHash are computed over the
708	   same octets, and a difference is effect-mismatch.  The control fails
709	   open if the two sides hash different octets, which is why a
710	   deployment MUST state the octets once for both (Section 5.1).

712	8.4.  D4: The Decider signs below its own rules

714	   A Decider produces a well-formed signature over a claim map that
715	   breaks a rule this profile states: an allow with no reference or no
716	   content hash, a refusal with a content hash, a hash outside the
717	   grammar.  Every such record is a record the reconciliation cannot
718	   close or would close wrongly.  The verifier applies the rules on the
719	   decoded payload (MUST-DP-3) and walks the chain over every record
720	   that claims the pin (MUST-DP-5), so the record is refused and named
721	   rather than attested or dropped.

723	8.5.  D5: Equivocation on the record chain

725	   The Decider signs two decisions for one request, or presents a record
726	   twice, and offers each to a different reader.  The chain (MUST-DP-5)
727	   makes the second record unlinkable: it names the same predecessor as
728	   the first, or none, and the walk reports the break.  The epoch
729	   checkpoint totals, held by a witness where one is used, close the
730	   other route.

732	8.6.  D6: The class of the effect is not bound

734	   A Decision Record names a reference and a content hash and does not
735	   name the class of effect it allowed.  A row of a different class
736	   under the same reference and the same content hash matches.  This is
737	   a known gap of this revision: the record has no claim for the class,
738	   and adding one is a claim-set change.  A deployment whose channel
739	   assigns references per class closes it by construction; one whose
740	   references are shared across classes does not, and SHOULD say so in
741	   its statement of what it hashes.

743	8.7.  D7: Silent defaults in capture

745	   The process that turns a channel's log into Decision Records or
746	   Effect Extract rows fills a missing value with a default, and the
747	   default hashes to something.  An allow with no stated content that is
748	   hashed as the empty string produces a record that will match an empty
749	   effect and mismatch every real one, with no finding that says the
750	   content was never stated.  Such a process MUST refuse the line by
751	   name rather than fill it (MUST-DP-10).  The companion's example
752	   adapter did fill it until it was measured, and refuses it now.

754	8.8.  D8: The capture process is the Decider

756	   The party that produces the Effect Extract is, or answers to, the
757	   party that signed the Decision Records.  Every finding in D1 and D2
758	   can then be made to disappear by omission, and no signature check
759	   detects it.  This is the independence statement of Section 7 (MUST-
760	   DP-9), and it is a deployment fact the profile can name but not
761	   prove.  A verifier that holds both roots and cannot state their
762	   independence has a conditional result, and MUST say so.

764	9.  Privacy Considerations

766	   A Decision Record carries no request content and no effect content:
767	   hashes of both, an opaque subject identifier, and a reason code.  The
768	   Effect Extract carries a reference, a class, a content hash, a time,
769	   and optionally the identifier of the party the effect reached.  The
770	   core's Privacy Considerations (Section 15 of [CEDULON]) apply to what
771	   a transparency witness is given.

773	   Two points are specific to this population.  A content hash over a
774	   short text is a fingerprint of that text: a reader who can guess the
775	   message can confirm the guess.  This revision hashes the plain
776	   content octets, as the companion does, so that the two sides need no
777	   shared secret to agree; a keyed or salted construction that would
778	   defeat the guess is a claim-set change and is not defined here.  A
779	   deployment whose effects are short and guessable SHOULD treat the
780	   extract and the records as confidential to the audit.  The subject
781	   and actor identifiers are opaque to the profile but need not be
782	   opaque to a reader; a deployment SHOULD pseudonymize them before
783	   either object leaves its control.

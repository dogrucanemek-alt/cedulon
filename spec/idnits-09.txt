7	          Cedulon: An Audit Layer for Agent-to-Agent Commerce
8	                         draft-dogru-cedulon-09

10	Abstract

12	   This document defines the Cedulon Protocol, an audit layer for agent-
13	   to-agent commerce.  Payment rails such as HTTP 402 flows (x402) and
14	   mandate protocols (AP2) already move value, and a mandate protocol
15	   can already refuse a spend before it happens.  What they do not, by
16	   themselves, give a party that is neither payer nor rail operator is a
17	   retrievable record of that decision and a signed spend receipt that
18	   reconciles against an authenticated extract of the rail.  Cedulon
19	   specifies a Trade Manifest (a signed offer before payment), a Policy
20	   Decision Point with default deny, a Spend Receipt (a COSE/CWT claim
21	   set issued after a gated payment), epoch checkpoints, and rail-
22	   extract reconciliation.

24	   The reconciliation shows that no settlement on the extract lacks a
25	   receipt and no settled receipt is absent from the extract.  That
26	   result is unconditional only when the verifier pins the rail key out
27	   of band and names the account, rail and window under audit; otherwise
28	   it is reported as conditional.  Checkpoints are profiled as SCITT
29	   Signed Statements, and a checkpoint the presented chain omits is
30	   reported by a witness that holds it.  No signed object is attested by
31	   a key it carries itself, and the exact input to every hash-valued
32	   field is stated, so that an independent verifier can be written from
33	   the text alone.  Cedulon is not a competitor to x402 or AP2; it sits
34	   above them.

151	1.  Introduction

153	   Agents can now pay.  Open HTTP 402 protocols [X402] attach stablecoin
154	   settlement to ordinary requests.  Card networks and processors issue
155	   agent-scoped tokens.  Google's Agent Payments Protocol (AP2) [AP2]
156	   binds user intent to signed mandates.

158	   What is missing is an interoperable *audit layer*: a machine-
159	   checkable answer to "was this spend allowed by policy, against which
160	   offer, and what bytes were delivered?"  The third question is
161	   answered to the extent the evidence carries it: machine-checkably
162	   where an attributable payee countersignature binds a deliveredHash
163	   (Section 5.1), and narrowed to "what bytes were promised, and what
164	   hash was presented beside them" where none does - a bundle must not
165	   claim more than its signatures cover.  Without this layer, a prompt-
166	   injected or looping agent can drain a rail that has already accepted
167	   a valid signature.  A counterparty can ship the wrong artifact.  A
168	   transparency log, if used at all, is proprietary.

170	   Cedulon fills that gap.  It does not clear funds, hold custody, or
171	   operate a payment facilitator.  An optional escrow actor is defined
172	   only as a third-party role interface (Section 16.13).
173	   Implementations of this specification MUST NOT take custody of funds
174	   or operate escrow (MUST-T8-custody).

176	   The control is an old one.  Reconciling an internal ledger against an
177	   external statement is what double-entry bookkeeping [PACIOLI] made
178	   routine, and signing the artifacts on both sides is Grigg's triple-
179	   entry idea [GRIGG].  Neither is claimed here.  What this document
180	   contributes is an open wire profile for that control in a setting
181	   where the parties are software: a COSE receipt shape, an extract
182	   shape, a checkpoint chain, and a verification algorithm precise
183	   enough that two implementations reach the same finding on the same
184	   evidence.  The novelty is interoperability, not the idea.

186	   Neighbor drafts are complementary, not substitutes. draft-bates-atp
187	   [BATES-ATP] covers tamper-evident causal lineage as a signed DAG.
188	   Cedulon is the completeness layer: a spend that never produced a
189	   receipt is visible when an authenticated rail extract is reconciled
190	   (MUST-T10-1).

192	2.  Terminology

194	   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT",
195	   "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and
196	   "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in
197	   BCP 14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all
198	   capitals, as shown here.

200	   The following terms are used:

202	   Trade Manifest:  A signed statement produced *before* payment.  It
203	      binds a description of goods or service, price, currency,
204	      acceptance-criteria hash, cancel condition, expiry, and an
205	      optional AP2 mandate reference.

207	   Policy Decision Point (PDP):  The function that evaluates a
208	      structured spend request against stored policy.  The default is
209	      deny.

211	   Spend Receipt:  A signed statement produced *after* a gated payment
212	      attempt.  It binds payer, payee, amount, currency, policy hash,
213	      manifestHash or an explicit noManifest flag, rail payment
214	      reference, timestampMs, nonce, prevReceiptHash, and outcome.

216	   Receipt Issuer:  The party that signs Spend Receipts.

218	   Anchor:  An optional SCITT Transparency Service [RFC9943] that
219	      registers a signed statement and returns a COSE receipt [RFC9942].

221	   Dispute Evidence Bundle:  A package of the Trade Manifest, the Spend
222	      Receipt, and a delivery hash.  It is evidence for a later human or
223	      legal process.  It is not an arbitral award and not an escrow
224	      release.

226	   Decision Token:  A portable, single-use PDP allow encoded as
227	      COSE_Sign1.  The claim set binds requestHash, policyHash,
228	      expiryMs, nonce, and singleUseId.  See Section 8.

230	   Rail Extract:  An authenticated list of settlement records for one
231	      account, one rail, and one time window.  See Section 9.

233	   Presented-unattested:  The state of a receipt or checkpoint the
234	      verifier holds no pinned issuer key for.  Its signature is checked
235	      against the key the object itself carries (Section 6.3), which
236	      establishes internal consistency only, so the object is neither
237	      attested nor rejected: every presented object is weighed as one
238	      set and the completeness guarantee is reported as conditional.  It
239	      is a state of the report, not a finding code.  See Section 10.1.

241	3.  Architecture

243	   Cedulon has three control-plane objects and one optional log:

245	     Principal --policy--> PDP --allow/deny--> x402/AP2 rail
246	                                 |
247	                                 v
248	                         Receipt Issuer --> Spend Receipt
249	                                 |
250	                                 v
251	                         Anchor / SCITT (optional)

253	   The payer agent never talks to the rail except through an adapter
254	   that calls the PDP first (MUST-T5-1).

256	3.1.  Policy Decision Point

258	   The PDP evaluates structured fields only (MUST-T1-1): amount,
259	   currency, payee, tool identifier, nonce, optional manifest hash, and
260	   evaluation time.  It applies limit, velocity, and scope checks (MUST-
261	   T2-1, MUST-T2-2).  If the PDP is unreachable, uninitialized, or
262	   throws, the result is deny (MUST-T2-3).  Denied attempts do not
263	   increment success counters (MUST-T2-4).

265	   An allow produces a Decision Token whose requestHash covers six
266	   fields: amount, currency, payee, tool, nonce, and manifestHash (MUST-
267	   T3-4, MUST-T6-1).  The token is a COSE_Sign1 object (MUST-T6-4), is
268	   single-use (MUST-T6-2), and MAY be carried to the adapter that
269	   performs settlement.

271	3.2.  Receipt Issuer

273	   After the adapter attempts settlement (success or a recorded deny
274	   that still needs an audit trail for an allowed-then-aborted path),
275	   the Receipt Issuer signs a Spend Receipt over the deterministic CBOR
276	   encoding of its claims (MUST-T4-1).  Verifiers reject bad signatures
277	   and byte mismatch (MUST-T4-2).

279	3.3.  Anchor / SCITT

281	   Parties MAY register the signed receipt (or a privacy-preserving hash
282	   encoding) as a SCITT Signed Statement [RFC9943] and attach the COSE
283	   receipt (MAY-T4-6).  This document does not operate a Transparency
284	   Service.

286	4.  Trade Manifest

288	   A Trade Manifest is the commerce analogue of a promise: it is issued
289	   *before* value moves.  It is conceptually symmetric to a later Spend
290	   Receipt (promise then proof), and it MAY carry an AP2 mandate hash so
291	   that user intent and the Cedulon offer stay linked (SHOULD-T8-5).

293	   A Trade Manifest MUST bind all of the following (MUST-T8-1):

295	   *  goods or service description

297	   *  price (integer minor units, encoded as a decimal string matching
298	      0|[1-9][0-9]*)

300	   *  currency (ISO 4217 alphabetic or a documented token identifier)

302	   *  acceptance-criteria hash (SHA-256 [RFC6234] of the exact delivery
303	      bytes, lowercase hexadecimal)

305	   *  cancel condition (opaque string agreed by the parties)

307	   *  expiry (POSIX milliseconds, expiresAtMs)

309	   -03 allowed this hash to be taken over "the exact delivery bytes or a
310	   declared schema instance" and gave a verifier no way to tell which
311	   one an issuer had used.  Two implementations reading the same
312	   manifest would then compute different digests over the same delivery
313	   and neither would be wrong.  This revision defines the first reading
314	   only.  Hashing a schema instance instead would need a marker in the
315	   manifest saying so, this document defines no such marker, and until
316	   one is defined that use is out of scope rather than an alternative a
317	   verifier is expected to guess at.

319	   It MAY include ap2MandateHash.  The corresponding CBOR label is
320	   always present; a missing mandate is encoded as CBOR null.

322	   It MAY name a payee.  An offer that is specific to one counterparty
323	   carries that party's payee identifier; when present, every receipt
324	   that names this manifest has its payee compared against it as exact
325	   octets under MUST-T8-9, on that requirement's two-branch severity.
326	   An open offer legitimately omits the member, and no comparison is
327	   made.  Unlike ap2MandateHash, the label is encoded only when the
328	   member is present, so a manifest signed before -05 carries the same
329	   bytes and keeps verifying; this is why the null convention above does
330	   not apply to it.

332	   The manifest is COSE_Sign1 [RFC9052] over a deterministic CBOR claim
333	   map (Section 6). manifestHash is the SHA-256 of the signed COSE bytes
334	   (MUST-T8-7).  A spend bound to a manifest MUST be denied if the
335	   requested amount or currency differs from the manifest (MUST-T8-2) or
336	   if the manifest is expired (MUST-T3-3).

338	   A spend that is not bound to a verified manifest MUST be marked
339	   noManifest on the receipt and MUST still pass limit, velocity, and
340	   scope checks (MUST-T1-2).  An implementation MAY refuse all
341	   noManifest spend (MAY-T1-4).

343	5.  Spend Receipt

345	   The Spend Receipt claim set is carried in COSE_Sign1 [RFC9052]
346	   wrapping a CWT-compatible map [RFC8392].  New receipts MUST use the
347	   COSE profile (Section 6).

349	   Claims (MUST-T4-3, MUST-T4-4, MUST-T4-7):

351	    +=================+===============================================+
352	    | Claim           | Description                                   |
353	    +=================+===============================================+
354	    | payer           | Payer agent identifier                        |
355	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
356	    | payee           | Payee identifier                              |
357	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
358	    | amount          | Minor units as a decimal string 0|[1-9][0-9]* |
359	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
360	    | currency        | Currency identifier                           |
361	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
362	    | policyHash      | SHA-256 of the canonical policy document      |
363	    |                 | (lowercase hex)                               |
364	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
365	    | manifestHash    | SHA-256 of the signed manifest COSE bytes, or |
366	    |                 | null when noManifest is true                  |
367	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
368	    | noManifest      | Boolean; MUST be true if and only if          |
369	    |                 | manifestHash is null                          |
370	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
371	    | x402PaymentRef  | Rail payment reference, or null               |
372	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
373	    | timestampMs     | POSIX milliseconds                            |
374	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
375	    | nonce           | Unique spend nonce; at least 128 bits of      |
376	    |                 | randomness; unique in the issuer scope        |
377	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
378	    | prevReceiptHash | Previous receipt hash, or null for the first  |
379	    |                 | receipt (SHOULD-T4-5)                         |
380	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
381	    | outcome         | settled or aborted                            |
382	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+

384	                                  Table 1

386	   A receipt with outcome = settled MUST have a non-null x402PaymentRef
387	   (MUST-T4-7).  An aborted receipt MUST NOT be added into checkpoint
388	   totals.

390	   All twelve labels in Section 6.1 are always present.  An empty
391	   optional value is encoded as CBOR null, never by omitting the label.

393	   receiptHash is the SHA-256 of the receipt's signed COSE bytes,
394	   encoded as lowercase hex.

396	   Verifiers MUST reject a receipt if the signature fails or if the
397	   decoded claim map does not match the presented claims (MUST-T4-2).

399	5.1.  Optional payee countersignature

401	   A payee MAY attach a countersignature over the issuer's signed Spend
402	   Receipt (MAY-T8-10).  The profile uses a *detached* COSE_Sign1
403	   [RFC9052] whose payload is a CBOR map with private-use labels:

405	     +========+===============+======================================+
406	     | Label  | Claim         | CBOR type                            |
407	     +========+===============+======================================+
408	     | -70401 | receiptCose   | bstr (exact issuer COSE_Sign1 bytes) |
409	     +--------+---------------+--------------------------------------+
410	     | -70402 | deliveredHash | bstr (optional; SHA-256 of the exact |
411	     |        |               | delivered bytes)                     |
412	     +--------+---------------+--------------------------------------+

414	                                  Table 2

416	   The countersignature uses the header profile in Section 6 and content
417	   type application/cedulon-countersign+cbor.

419	   This is a second Sign1 object, not RFC 9052 Countersignature0
420	   (unprotected-header label 11).  Countersignature0 would write into
421	   the issuer object and change receiptHash after issue, breaking the
422	   receipt chain.  A detached Sign1 keeps the issuer bytes stable,
423	   reuses kid and content-type, and is absent by simply omitting the
424	   sibling object.

426	   Absence of a countersignature MUST NOT invalidate the issuer receipt
427	   (MAY-T8-10).  A countersignature travels beside the issuer signature
428	   without being covered by it, so anyone holding an honest receipt can
429	   append one of their own.  Attribution is therefore the gate: a
430	   countersignature that cannot be attributed to the pinned payee key -
431	   the signature fails, kid or content type does not match, label -70401
432	   is not the issuer COSE bytes (MUST-T8-8), or the signature is valid
433	   under some other key - MUST be rejected as approval evidence.  What
434	   is rejected is the countersignature, not the receipt: the verdict on
435	   the untouched issuer receipt MUST NOT change because an
436	   unattributable object was attached.  An appendable object the issuer
437	   signature does not cover must not be able to manufacture a negative
438	   result; this is the lesson MUST-T8-9 already encodes for the manifest
439	   comparison, applied one object over.  The identifiers countersign-bad
440	   (unverifiable) and countersign-key-mismatch (verifiable under another
441	   key) name the discarded object as warnings, and where the verifier
442	   pinned a payee key and no attributable countersignature remains, the
443	   countersign-missing warning still applies: a discarded forgery is the
444	   absence of the payee's word, not a substitute for it.

446	   The optional deliveredHash claim binds delivery to the receipt under
447	   the payee's key.  A payee who countersigns MAY include the SHA-256 of
448	   the exact bytes it received in the same signed payload as the issuer
449	   receipt bytes.  When an attributable countersignature carries
450	   deliveredHash and the verifier also holds the Trade Manifest, the two
451	   digests are compared as exact octets: deliveredHash against
452	   acceptanceCriteriaHash.  A mismatch is delivery-mismatch, and it is a
453	   finding rather than a warning, because both ends of the comparison
454	   are signed.  A deliveredHash carried by an unattributable
455	   countersignature is discarded with it.  A countersigner MUST refuse
456	   to sign a deliveredHash that is not 32 octets; a verifier that meets
457	   one anyway treats the countersignature as carrying no deliveredHash,
458	   so the delivery question narrows as it does when the claim is absent,
459	   and the signature verdict does not move.  A countersignature without
460	   the claim is valid exactly as before -05, which introduced it.

462	   A Dispute Evidence Bundle that includes a verified countersignature
463	   has stronger evidence that the payee accepted those bytes; the bundle
464	   is still not an award (MUST-T8-4).

466	6.  COSE Profile

468	   This profile uses deterministic CBOR [RFC8949] Section 4.2.1
469	   (definite lengths, shortest integer form, map keys sorted in
470	   *bytewise lexicographic* order of their encoded keys).
471	   Implementations MUST encode only the types used by Cedulon claim
472	   maps: null, bool, unsigned and negative integers, UTF-8 text, byte
473	   strings, arrays, and maps (MUST-T4-1).

475	   -03 bound the encoder and said nothing about the decoder, which left
476	   the reading side free where the writing side was not.  A decoder MUST
477	   refuse a CBOR map that carries a duplicate encoded key (MUST-T4-18).
478	   The encoding rules already forbid producing one, so a decoder that
479	   accepts it accepts a document no conforming encoder can produce, and
480	   two decoders may disagree on which of the two values wins, which is a
481	   disagreement about what was signed.

483	   A decoder MUST also impose a bound on what it will attempt: on
484	   encoded size, on nesting depth, and on the number of elements it will
485	   decode from an audit input.  It MUST refuse an input that exceeds a
486	   bound with a named refusal rather than by exhausting memory or the
487	   stack, and it SHOULD document the bounds it applies (MUST-T4-19).
488	   This document fixes no numbers.  A bound that is right for a desktop
489	   verifier is wrong for a service, and a number written here would be
490	   wrong for one of them within a year.  What a reader is entitled to is
491	   that the refusal is a refusal, named and reported, rather than a
492	   crash that an operator has to interpret.

494	6.1.  Claim labels

608	6.2.  COSE_Sign1 headers

610	   The protected header MUST be a deterministic CBOR map containing
611	   (MUST-T4-1, MUST-T4-8):

613	   *  1 (alg) = -19 (Ed25519, [RFC9864]; the generic EdDSA value -8 from
614	      [RFC9053] is deprecated for this profile)

616	   *  3 (content type) = a tstr that distinguishes the payload:
617	      application/cedulon-receipt+cbor, application/cedulon-
618	      checkpoint+cbor, application/cedulon-manifest+cbor, application/
619	      cedulon-decision+cbor, application/cedulon-countersign+cbor, or
620	      application/cedulon-inclusion+cbor

622	   *  4 (kid) = bstr, mandatory.  The profile computes kid as the first
623	      eight bytes of SHA-256 over the issuer's SubjectPublicKeyInfo DER,
624	      in the Ed25519 SubjectPublicKeyInfo encoding of [RFC8410].  A
625	      verifier MUST obtain the public key from an authenticated channel
626	      (preconfigured issuer set, directory, or transparency statement)
627	      and MUST reject a message whose kid does not match that key.

629	   The unprotected header MUST be empty, and a decoder MUST refuse a
630	   message whose unprotected header is not an empty map, by the name
631	   cose-sign1-unprotected, rather than verify the signature and ignore
632	   the header (MUST-T4-21).  The reason is in Section 7.1: every digest
633	   over a signed object in this profile is computed over the COSE_Sign1
634	   octets, which include the unprotected header, while the signature
635	   does not cover it.  A decoder that ignored a stuffed header would
636	   verify the signature and compute a receiptHash the issuer never
637	   produced, so one honestly signed receipt could carry as many digests
638	   as a stranger cared to give it, and a chain built on prevReceiptHash
639	   would follow whichever copy it was handed.  The posted -05 stated the
640	   header empty and said nothing about the decoder; the companion
641	   decoder ignored the header until the question of what the digest
642	   covers was measured.  The payload MUST be the CBOR encoding of the
643	   claim map.  The signature is Ed25519 [RFC8032] over the COSE
644	   Sig_structure ["Signature1", protected, h'', payload].

646	6.3.  How a signed object is presented

648	   The signed octets above are what this profile defines and what every
649	   digest in Section 7.1 is taken over.  An object handed to a verifier
650	   travels with a little more than that, and the previous revisions used
651	   one of those members - the key an object carries - without ever
652	   saying where it came from.

654	   A presented Spend Receipt, epoch checkpoint, Trade Manifest, or
655	   Decision Token carries, beside the signed octets, its claim set or
656	   body in decoded form and *the signer's public key as a
657	   SubjectPublicKeyInfo PEM*; a countersigned receipt carries the
658	   payee's key the same way, and an object presented in the JSON
659	   encoding of Section 7 repeats its signature there as base64.  None of
660	   these is inside the signed octets, which is the whole point of naming
661	   them here: the signature covers the COSE message and nothing else, so
662	   every one of these members is a surface anyone holding the object can
663	   rewrite.  This is the same shape the Rail Extract states in
664	   Section 9, and it is stated once here for the COSE objects rather
665	   than left to be inferred from an implementation.

667	   A carried key is not an identity source and MUST NOT be used as one
668	   (MUST-T4-11).  Under a pin it has exactly one effect: an object that
669	   verifies under the pinned key while carrying a different key is
670	   reported as carried-key-mismatch, a warning, and stays attested
671	   (Section 10.1).  With no pin held it is the only key present, so the
672	   signature check that runs against it says the object is internally
673	   consistent and says nothing about who signed it; two issuers cannot
674	   be told apart in that state.

676	7.  Canonical JSON encoding

678	   Not everything this document hashes or signs is CBOR.  The policy
679	   document, the six request fields bound by a Decision Token, and the
680	   scoped body of a Rail Extract are JSON, and -03 called each of them
681	   "canonical" without saying what that meant.  Two implementations
682	   could therefore agree on every requirement in this document and still
683	   produce different bytes, which makes an independent verifier
684	   impossible to write from the text.  This section closes that.

686	   Where this document says "the canonical encoding" of a JSON document,
687	   it means the encoding defined by [RFC8785], and the octets hashed or
688	   signed are the UTF-8 octets of that encoding.

690	   Three notes on the boundary of that reference:

692	   *  [RFC8785] Section 3.1 takes I-JSON [RFC7493] as its input, and an
693	      I-JSON object carries no duplicate member names.  This document
694	      makes that precondition a rule at every place a verifier receives
695	      a JSON document as text - a rail extract body, a policy document,
696	      a stored receipt file: a text in which any object, at any depth,
697	      repeats a member name MUST be refused by the name json-duplicate-
698	      key, before the text is parsed (MUST-T4-20).  The rule is measured
699	      on the text because a parser that keeps either value has already
700	      discarded the evidence of the other, and two verifiers parsing one
701	      text could then canonicalize two different documents and sign or
702	      check two different octet strings, which is the JSON form of the
703	      disagreement MUST-T4-18 forbids for CBOR.  A verifier handed an
704	      object rather than text, as a tool behind a JSON-RPC boundary is,
705	      cannot apply the rule and MUST NOT report that it did; whether the
706	      text was checked is then the transport's to state.  The posted -05
707	      said nothing about this.  The companion implementation refused
708	      such texts before the sentence existed, and its conformance runner
709	      carried the difference as a recorded split against -05 rather than
710	      as a pass.

712	   *  [RFC8785] Section 3.2.2.2 requires a serializer to terminate on a
713	      lone surrogate, and so does this document: a producer MUST refuse
714	      to encode a document containing one, by name, and MUST NOT sign
715	      what it could not encode.  A verifier reading bytes it cannot
716	      canonicalize for this reason reports the input as failing
717	      verification with the refusal named beside the verdict; it does
718	      not crash.  No field defined by this document may contain a lone
719	      surrogate, so a conforming document never reaches this rule. -04
720	      permitted emitting the escaped form instead, which contradicted
721	      the RFC it cited; that permission is removed.

723	   *  [RFC8785] defines no encoding for an integer outside the IEEE 754
724	      double range.  No document defined here carries one: every amount
725	      and cumulative limit is already a decimal string before it is
726	      encoded, and every hash is lowercase hexadecimal.  A document that
727	      would need such an integer is outside this specification.

729	7.1.  Which octets are hashed

731	   Every hash-valued field in this document is SHA-256 [RFC6234] of the
732	   input named below.  All but three are rendered as lowercase
733	   hexadecimal. kid differs only in its rendering: the digest is
734	   computed over the same stated input and then truncated to its first 8
735	   bytes, carried as a byte string rather than as hex (Section 6 states
736	   the same rule where the header is defined). ap2MandateHash differs in
737	   whose digest it is: AP2 defines the mandate and its octets, and this
738	   document carries the result opaquely rather than restating a rule it
739	   does not own. deliveredHash differs in its carrier: it is a claim in
740	   a CBOR map and is carried as the raw 32 digest bytes (bstr), not as
741	   hex; comparisons against acceptanceCriteriaHash are made over the
742	   digest value. -03 named the digest for some of these fields and not
743	   for others; the omissions were not a deliberate degree of freedom.

745	        +========================+===============================+
746	        | Field                  | Input to SHA-256              |
747	        +========================+===============================+
748	        | receiptHash            | the signed COSE_Sign1 octets  |
749	        |                        | of the receipt                |
750	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
751	        | manifestHash           | the signed COSE_Sign1 octets  |
752	        |                        | of the Trade Manifest         |
753	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
754	        | checkpointHash         | the signed COSE_Sign1 octets  |
755	        |                        | of the checkpoint             |
756	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
757	        | statementHash          | the signed COSE_Sign1 octets  |
758	        |                        | of the statement              |
759	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
760	        | acceptanceCriteriaHash | the exact delivery bytes, as  |
761	        |                        | defined where the Trade       |
762	        |                        | Manifest is                   |
763	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
764	        | deliveredHash          | the exact bytes the payee     |
765	        |                        | received; the same input rule |
766	        |                        | as acceptanceCriteriaHash,    |
767	        |                        | computed by the other party   |
768	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
769	        | policyHash             | the UTF-8 octets of the       |
770	        |                        | canonical policy document     |
771	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
772	        | requestHash            | the UTF-8 octets of the       |
773	        |                        | canonical six-field request   |
774	        |                        | document                      |
775	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
776	        | kid                    | the SubjectPublicKeyInfo DER; |
777	        |                        | the digest is then truncated  |
778	        |                        | to its first 8 bytes          |
779	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
780	        | ap2MandateHash         | the octets AP2 defines for    |
781	        |                        | its mandate; not profiled by  |
782	        |                        | this document                 |
783	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+

785	                                 Table 7

787	   Wherever this table, or any other sentence in this document, says
788	   "the signed COSE_Sign1 octets", those are the octets of the
789	   *untagged* four-element COSE_Sign1 array of [RFC9052].  This profile
790	   never wraps a message in CBOR tag 18, and the vectors in Appendix A
791	   carry the untagged form.  A verifier that hashed a tagged copy would
792	   compute a different digest for every object in this profile, so the
793	   choice is stated here once rather than left to be inferred from the
794	   vectors.

796	   The six fields of the request document are the ones MUST-T6-1 names:
797	   amount, currency, payee, tool, nonce, and manifestHash. -03 described
798	   requestHash as "the six-field hash" while naming SHA-256 for
799	   policyHash in the same sentence, which left a reader free to conclude
800	   that the request binding was not a digest at all.  It is one.

802	   Naming the members is not stating the document, so the document is
803	   stated here.  The request document is a JSON object carrying exactly
804	   those six members and no others, every member always present. amount
805	   is the decimal string of the request, in the amount syntax the
806	   receipt claims table states, never a JSON number: [RFC8785] encodes
807	   the number 1 and the string "1" differently, and an implementation
808	   free to pick either would produce two digests for one request.
809	   currency, payee, and nonce are the request's text strings. tool is
810	   the request's text string, or JSON null where the deployment names
811	   none. manifestHash is the lowercase hexadecimal string, or JSON null
812	   for a spend bound to no manifest; an absent value is null, never an
813	   omitted member.  A document with a seventh member, a missing member,
814	   or another type for one of these is not the request document this
815	   section defines.

817	   The policy document is different on purpose, and the difference is
818	   scope rather than an oversight.  Its member set is the deployment's
819	   own: this document defines how the bytes of whatever policy document
820	   a PDP evaluates are encoded (Section 7) and digested, not what its
821	   members are. policyHash binds a spend to the exact bytes its PDP
822	   evaluated; it is not a value two deployments are expected to compute
823	   from a shared schema, and nothing in the verification algorithm
824	   compares one deployment's policyHash to another's.

826	8.  Decision Token

828	   A Decision Token is the portable encoding of a PDP allow.  It is
829	   COSE_Sign1 with the header profile in Section 6 and the labels in
830	   Section 6.1.  All five labels are always present (MUST-T6-4).

832	   requestHash MUST be the SHA-256 of the canonical encoding of the six
833	   fields the PDP evaluated (MUST-T6-1), rendered as lowercase
834	   hexadecimal; Section 7 defines that encoding and Section 7.1 states
835	   the octets. -03 called this "the six-field hash" in the same sentence
836	   that named SHA-256 for policyHash, which left the digest for one of
837	   them unstated. policyHash MUST be the SHA-256 of the canonical policy
838	   document the PDP evaluated. expiryMs is a Unix time in milliseconds
839	   after which the token MUST be treated as expired (SHOULD-T6-3):
840	   expired when the evaluation time is strictly greater than expiryMs,
841	   not yet expired at exactly expiryMs, on the same boundary discipline
842	   MUST-T3-3 states for the manifest. nonce is the request nonce.
843	   singleUseId is the identifier consumed on the first settlement
844	   attempt (MUST-T6-2).

846	   A party that accepts a Decision Token MUST reject it if the signature
847	   fails, if kid does not match a configured PDP key, if the content
848	   type is not application/cedulon-decision+cbor, if the decoded claim
849	   map does not match the presented claims, or if expiryMs is in the
850	   past (MUST-T6-5).

852	9.  Rail Extract Profile

854	   A verifier checks completeness against a *rail extract*, not against
855	   the issuer's own receipts alone (MUST-T10-7).

857	9.1.  Record schema

859	   The extract body is a JSON document.  Each settlement record MUST
860	   contain the following members, under these names:

862	         +=============+=========================================+
863	         | Member      | JSON type                               |
864	         +=============+=========================================+
865	         | ref         | string (rail payment reference)         |
866	         +-------------+-----------------------------------------+
867	         | amount      | string matching 0|[1-9][0-9]*           |
868	         +-------------+-----------------------------------------+
869	         | currency    | string                                  |
870	         +-------------+-----------------------------------------+
871	         | timestampMs | number (POSIX milliseconds, an integer) |
872	         +-------------+-----------------------------------------+

874	                                  Table 8

876	   These member names are normative.  A rail MAY add members of its own
877	   to a record; it MUST NOT rename the four above. -04 said the member
878	   names were the rail's to define, which contradicted this table and
879	   made the extract unconstructable from the text: a verifier reading
880	   reference where the table says ref has no rule telling it whether the
881	   two are the same member.  The table wins, and -04's sentence is
882	   withdrawn.  The table also used CBOR terms (tstr, uint) for what is a
883	   JSON body; the types above are stated in JSON terms.

885	   A record MAY carry a beneficiary member (string).  A rail that can
886	   resolve a payment reference to the party credited declares it there;
887	   when present, it is compared against the payee of the receipt that
888	   names the same ref, and a difference is reported (beneficiary-
889	   mismatch).  Resolving a reference to a beneficiary is a feature of
890	   the rail's own system: this profile does not assume it, and measures
891	   it only when the rail declares it.

893	9.2.  Scope

895	   An extract is scoped to one account identifier, one rail identifier,
896	   and one half-open time window [windowStartMs, windowEndMs).  The
897	   signed body is one JSON document with exactly this shape:

899	        +===============+=========================================+
900	        | Member        | JSON type                               |
901	        +===============+=========================================+
902	        | accountId     | string                                  |
903	        +---------------+-----------------------------------------+
904	        | railId        | string                                  |
905	        +---------------+-----------------------------------------+
906	        | windowStartMs | number (POSIX milliseconds, an integer) |
907	        +---------------+-----------------------------------------+
908	        | windowEndMs   | number (POSIX milliseconds, an integer) |
909	        +---------------+-----------------------------------------+
910	        | clockSkewMs   | number (milliseconds, a non-negative    |
911	        |               | integer; optional; see Section 11)      |
912	        +---------------+-----------------------------------------+
913	        | settlements   | array of settlement records (schema     |
914	        |               | above)                                  |
915	        +---------------+-----------------------------------------+

917	                                  Table 9

919	   All six named members except clockSkewMs MUST be present; a body
920	   missing one, or a record renaming a core member, MUST be refused by
921	   name at both ends - by the signer before it signs and by the verifier
922	   before it checks a signature - so a malformed extract is the same
923	   refusal on both sides rather than a signature verdict.  Additional
924	   members beyond these are the rail's to add, as with records.

926	   The integer-valued members - windowStartMs, windowEndMs, each
927	   record's timestampMs, and clockSkewMs - MUST be integers of magnitude
928	   at most 2^53 - 1, the range a JSON number carries exactly, and
929	   clockSkewMs MUST NOT be negative; a value outside those bounds, or a
930	   non-integer, is refused by name in the same way as a missing member.
931	   windowEndMs MUST be greater than windowStartMs: the window is half-
932	   open, so one that does not end after it starts declares no
933	   population, and a body carrying one is refused by name (malformed-
934	   extract-window) in the same way, at both ends.

936	   The body is read as text before it is read as an object.  A text in
937	   which any object repeats a member name is refused as json-duplicate-
938	   key at both ends, before parsing and before any signature is checked
939	   (MUST-T4-20, Section 7); a signer that parsed first would sign one of
940	   the two values and an honest verifier could check the other.

942	   clockSkewMs, when present, declares the boundary allowance the
943	   verifier applies at this window's edges during reconciliation
944	   (Section 11); when absent, the profile default of 300000 milliseconds
945	   (five minutes) applies.

947	9.3.  Authentication

949	   The rail signs Ed25519 [RFC8032] over the canonical encoding of the
950	   scoped body, which is a JSON document and therefore takes the
951	   encoding of Section 7: the signed octets are the UTF-8 octets of the
952	   [RFC8785] encoding of the body above.  The signature and the rail's
953	   public key travel beside the body, the signature as base64 and the
954	   key as a SubjectPublicKeyInfo PEM; neither is part of the signed
955	   octets.  A verifier MUST obtain the extract from the rail or from a
956	   signature the rail published (MUST-T10-7).  A deployment that cannot
957	   do so is running the reconciliation against evidence it did not
958	   obtain independently, and MUST report the guarantee as conditional.

960	   A signature on an extract proves internal consistency, not origin: a
961	   key generated by whoever produced the object verifies against itself.
962	   The verifier therefore MUST obtain the rail's public key out of band
963	   and MUST verify the extract signature against that key (MUST-T10-8).
964	   A key the extract carries is not that key: it is neither the rail's
965	   identity nor a fallback for one the verifier did not obtain.  Where
966	   no such key is held, the carried key is the only key present, so the
967	   signature check that runs against it says the extract is internally
968	   consistent and says nothing about who produced it, and the verifier
969	   MUST treat the guarantee as conditional.

971	   Keys are compared as bytes.  A verifier MUST compare the pinned key
972	   and the key that signed the extract by their SubjectPublicKeyInfo DER
973	   encoding, not by any text encoding of it, so that the same key
974	   presented in a different envelope still compares equal (MUST-T10-9).
975	   A pinned key the verifier cannot decode is a fault in the verifier's
976	   own configuration, not evidence about the extract, and MUST be
977	   reported as trust-key-unreadable rather than as a key mismatch.

979	   What a verifier emits for an extract it cannot authenticate depends
980	   on whether it stated an expectation.  With no pinned key the verifier
981	   has asserted nothing, so the condition is unauthenticated-extract
982	   whatever the extract carries - a signature that verifies against the
983	   carried key establishes internal consistency and not origin, and one
984	   that fails or is refused is not a verdict about a key either.  It is
985	   a warning: completeness findings may still be computed, but the
986	   guarantee is *conditional* on the extract being authentic.  With a
987	   pinned key the verifier has asserted what it requires, and an extract
988	   that fails to meet it is a failure rather than a caveat; see the
989	   verification algorithm for which finding applies. -00 defined only
990	   the first case, and readers of -00 should note that -02 made the
991	   pinned case fail closed.  See Section 16.

993	9.4.  Scope agreement

995	   An extract declares a window and carries settlement records.  The two
996	   MUST agree: a verifier MUST report every settlement record whose
997	   timestampMs falls outside [windowStartMs, windowEndMs) as extract-
998	   scope-mismatch, identified by that record's ref (MUST-T10-10).  This
999	   check is about the extract's internal consistency and MUST be
1000	   performed whether or not a rail key is pinned.

1002	   A verifier that knows which account, rail, and window it is auditing
1003	   MUST also check the extract against that expectation and MUST fail
1004	   closed when the extract does not cover it (MUST-T10-11).  An extract
1005	   for another account or rail, or one whose window does not span the
1006	   period under audit, cannot support a completeness claim about that
1007	   period.

1009	   A verifier that states no period leaves the extract free to define
1010	   one, and an extract that reports on a millisecond balances as easily
1011	   as one that reports on a month.  Pinning a key establishes who
1012	   signed; only a stated period establishes what the signature had to
1013	   cover.  A verifier that has not stated the period under audit
1014	   therefore MUST emit unstated-audit-window and MUST treat the
1015	   guarantee as conditional (MUST-T10-15), whatever else verifies.

1017	   The period is one axis of that scope.  The account and the rail are
1018	   the other two, and they behave the same way: an extract names one of
1019	   each, so a verifier that has not stated them leaves the extract to
1020	   say whose settlements were accounted for and which way out was
1021	   watched.  A verifier that has not stated the account or the rail
1022	   under audit therefore MUST emit unstated-audit-scope and MUST treat
1023	   the guarantee as conditional (MUST-T10-18).  Where no rail key is
1024	   pinned at all, all three axes are equally unstated and
1025	   unauthenticated-extract is the condition reported.

1027	   Stating them does not widen the population; it names it.  One account
1028	   settling on two rails has two settlement paths, and an extract for
1029	   the first reports nothing about the second: a spend that left that
1030	   way is not an unmatched row, it is outside the population the extract
1031	   declared.  Because the strongest line this profile prints - a
1032	   balanced audit under an unconditional guarantee - is true of one
1033	   account, on one rail, over one window, a report that carries it MUST
1034	   also carry that account, rail and window (MUST-T10-19).  A
1035	   completeness claim about an account is the conjunction of one such
1036	   report per rail that account can settle on, and enumerating those
1037	   rails is the deployment's statement, not something an extract can be
1038	   asked to prove.

1040	10.  Trust roots

1042	   Section 9 states the rule for one object: a signature proves internal
1043	   consistency, not origin, so the verifier obtains the rail key out of
1044	   band and checks the extract against it, and the key the extract
1045	   carries is neither the rail's identity nor a fallback for one the
1046	   verifier did not obtain (MUST-T10-8). -02 already required a verifier
1047	   to obtain the public key from an authenticated channel and to reject
1048	   a kid that does not match that key (MUST-T4-8).  What -02 did not
1049	   carry was the verification algorithm, the separate root inputs, and
1050	   the error semantics that name a missing or mismatched pin.  This
1051	   section states those for every signed object in the profile.

1053	   The gap is not theoretical.  A verifier that checks a Spend Receipt
1054	   against the key the receipt carries accepts a receipt signed by any
1055	   key at all, including one an attacker minted for the occasion.  Such
1056	   a receipt matches a settlement the attacker was never authorised to
1057	   make, the settlement stops looking uncovered, and the audit reports
1058	   nothing.  The completeness property in Section 11 is then computed
1059	   over evidence that answers to nobody.

1061	10.1.  The issuer root

1063	   A verifier MUST obtain the issuer's public key out of band and MUST
1064	   verify Spend Receipt and epoch checkpoint signatures against that
1065	   key.  A key the object carries is not that key: it establishes no
1066	   signer identity and is not a fallback for a pin the verifier does not
1067	   hold (MUST-T4-9, MUST-T4-11).  Where no issuer key is held, a
1068	   signature checked against the key its own object carries establishes
1069	   internal consistency and nothing more, which is the state Section 6.3
1070	   and the last row of the table below describe.  A verifier that holds
1071	   no such key and is presented with any Spend Receipt or epoch
1072	   checkpoint MUST treat the completeness guarantee as conditional and
1073	   SHOULD report the condition; the identifier unauthenticated-issuer is
1074	   used for it in this implementation.

1076	   The condition names those two objects because an audit given no
1077	   receipts and no checkpoints rests on the extract alone.  There the
1078	   absent issuer root withholds nothing, and warning about a root the
1079	   audit never consulted would spend the warning where it carries no
1080	   information.

1082	   Reporting a mismatch is not sufficient on its own.  A receipt that
1083	   does not answer to the pinned issuer key MUST NOT count as coverage
1084	   for the settlement it names, and the settlement MUST still be
1085	   reported as uncovered (MUST-T4-10).  A verifier that reports the
1086	   mismatch and then lets the receipt match the settlement anyway has
1087	   described the attack in its output while still concluding that the
1088	   books balance.

1090	   Keys are compared as bytes, by their SubjectPublicKeyInfo DER
1091	   encoding, on the same terms as MUST-T10-9.  A pinned issuer key the
1092	   verifier cannot decode is a fault in its own configuration and MUST
1093	   be reported as trust-key-unreadable rather than as a mismatch against
1094	   the objects; where no pinned key can be decoded at all, nothing is
1095	   attested and the verifier MUST NOT fall back to accepting the keys
1096	   the objects carry (MUST-T4-11).  Falling back is how a mistyped
1097	   configuration becomes a bypass.

1099	   Membership in the attested set follows one rule: the signature
1100	   verifies under a pinned issuer key.  The kid header routes the check
1101	   to a candidate key; the key an object carries beside its signature is
1102	   not an identity source, because it travels outside the signed octets
1103	   and anyone can rewrite it.  Two consequences are stated so that
1104	   implementations do not diverge on them.  First, an honestly signed
1105	   object whose carried key was swapped stays attested: the swap is
1106	   reported (carried-key-mismatch, a warning) and MUST NOT move the
1107	   object out of the attested set or change the verdict, on the same
1108	   reasoning as Section 5.1 - a surface the signature does not cover
1109	   must not be able to manufacture a negative result.  Second, an object
1110	   that claims the pin, by its carried key or its kid, but does not
1111	   verify under it is excluded from the attested set and MUST still be
1112	   walked and named - for a receipt, receipt-chain-break with a
1113	   signature-failed detail - never silently dropped; an object that
1114	   neither verifies under the pin nor claims it is issuer-key-mismatch,
1115	   excluded, and the settlement it names stays uncovered.

1173	10.2.  The payee root

1175	   The optional countersignature in Section 5.1 travels beside the
1176	   issuer signature without being covered by it.  Anyone holding an
1177	   honest receipt can append a countersignature of their own, so a
1178	   verifier that checks it against the key carried next to it learns
1179	   only that some key signed something.

1181	   A countersignature MUST NOT be treated as evidence that the payee
1182	   approved the payment unless it verifies against a payee key the
1183	   verifier obtained out of band (MUST-T4-13).  Without such a key the
1184	   verifier SHOULD report the condition and MUST treat the guarantee as
1185	   conditional.

1187	   Naming a payee key states an expectation, and an expectation that
1188	   only fires when the evidence is present can be cancelled by deleting
1189	   the evidence.  Where a verifier has pinned a key for a payee, a
1190	   settled receipt naming that payee and carrying no *attributable*
1191	   countersignature MUST be reported (MUST-T4-14): a countersignature
1192	   that failed to verify, or verified under some other key, is discarded
1193	   under Section 5.1 and leaves the expectation open exactly as a
1194	   missing one does.  Otherwise an attacker removes their own failed
1195	   forgery - or appends one - and the report returns to unconditional.
1196	   The discarded object itself is a warning, never a failure of the
1197	   receipt it rode beside; Section 5.1 states the invariant.

1199	10.3.  The witness root

1201	   Section 11.3 describes what a transparency witness adds.  An
1202	   inclusion receipt checked against the key it carries says that some
1203	   log is internally consistent, and a log is cheap to invent.

1205	   A verifier MUST obtain the transparency service's public key out of
1206	   band and MUST verify inclusion receipts against it (MUST-T11-15).
1207	   Inclusion receipts that cannot be checked that way MUST NOT be used
1208	   as evidence, in either direction: they cannot establish that a
1209	   checkpoint was anchored, and they cannot establish that one was
1210	   withheld.  A verifier SHOULD report that it left them out.

1212	   A log holds statements from everyone who uses it.  A statement held
1213	   by a pinned log MUST additionally answer to the issuer root before it
1214	   counts as something that issuer published (MUST-T11-16); otherwise
1215	   another user's epoch, sitting in a shared log, reads as this issuer
1216	   publishing two checkpoints for one epoch.

1218	   Anchoring and withholding are different claims and need different
1219	   evidence.  Establishing that a checkpoint was logged needs only the
1220	   statement hash.  Establishing that an issuer withheld one needs to
1221	   know whose statement it is, which an inclusion receipt carrying no
1222	   statement body cannot say.  Such a receipt MUST NOT be used to report
1223	   a withheld checkpoint, and MUST NOT be silently discarded either: a
1224	   real withholding must not be buried by removing the body (MUST-
1225	   T11-17).

1227	10.4.  The decision root

1229	   A Decision Token is issued by the policy decision point and consumed
1230	   by the same deployment.  The consumer therefore holds the key it
1231	   signs with and has no reason to ask the token which key to check it
1232	   against.  A consumer MUST verify a Decision Token against its own
1233	   issuing key and MUST NOT accept one it cannot check that way (MUST-
1234	   T6-6).

1236	10.5.  The manifest root

1238	   A Trade Manifest is optional.  A deployment that presents none is not
1239	   missing a root, and this requirement does not make such an audit
1240	   conditional.  The forbidden case is the other one: a manifest is
1241	   presented, and the verifier accepts it because the key travelling
1242	   inside it verifies against itself.

1244	   A verifier that is presented with a Trade Manifest MUST obtain the
1245	   publisher's public key out of band and MUST verify the manifest
1246	   signature against that key, not against a key the manifest carries
1247	   (MUST-T4-15).  A verifier without such a key that is presented with a
1248	   Trade Manifest MUST report the completeness guarantee as conditional
1249	   and SHOULD report the condition; the identifier unauthenticated-
1250	   manifest is used for it in this implementation.  An audit presented
1251	   with no Trade Manifest is not made conditional by this requirement.

1253	   A pinned manifest key the verifier cannot decode is a fault in its
1254	   own configuration and MUST be reported as trust-key-unreadable rather
1255	   than as a mismatch, on the same terms as MUST-T4-11.  A manifest that
1256	   does not verify against a readable pin MUST be reported as manifest-
1257	   key-mismatch and MUST fail the audit.  Falling back to the key the
1258	   manifest carries is how a presented document becomes a bypass.

1260	   Attribution is one question and coverage is another, and a root that
1261	   answers only the first leaves the document doing work it was never
1262	   spent under.  A verifier presented with a Trade Manifest MUST compare
1263	   the manifest hash to the manifestHash of the receipts presented to
1264	   the audit and MUST report a manifest that no presented receipt
1265	   references (MUST-T4-17); the identifier manifest-covers-no-receipt is
1266	   used for it in this implementation, and the completeness guarantee is
1267	   conditional.  The comparison runs against those presented receipts,
1268	   including aborted ones, and is made before any extract window is
1269	   applied and before any issuer key is applied; a hash on an aborted
1270	   receipt, on a receipt outside the extract window, or on a receipt no
1271	   pinned key attests still counts as a reference.  This requirement
1272	   asks whether any receipt names the terms, not whether a settlement in
1273	   the window was made under them, and not whether the receipt that
1274	   names them is attributable.  A forged receipt can therefore silence
1275	   this warning.  That is accepted: what it silences is a statement that
1276	   terms were named, and the report it leaves behind is still marked
1277	   conditional and still carries the finding that the receipt answers to
1278	   no pinned key.  A correctly attributed manifest travelling beside a
1279	   set of receipts marked noManifest states terms nothing presented was
1280	   spent under, and a report that stays silent about it reads as terms-
1281	   backed when it is not.  This requirement does not reach the audit
1282	   presented with no Trade Manifest, which remains a deployment choice
1283	   under MUST-T1-2.

1285	   Naming a manifest is not obeying one, and that is the third place
1286	   this document has had to say the same thing twice.  A verifier
1287	   presented with a Trade Manifest MUST compare the amount, the currency
1288	   and the settlement time of every receipt that names it against the
1289	   manifest's amount, currency and expiry, and MUST report a receipt
1290	   that departs from them (MUST-T8-9); the identifier manifest-terms-
1291	   mismatch is used for it in this implementation.  Every receipt that
1292	   names the manifest is measured, aborted ones included: an aborted
1293	   receipt that carries the hash of terms and a departing amount
1294	   recorded an attempt against terms it misstates, and MUST-T4-17 next
1295	   door already counts it as a reference.  The time compared is the
1296	   receipt's timestampMs, against the boundary MUST-T3-3 states:
1297	   strictly after expiresAtMs departs, exactly at it does not.  Amount
1298	   and currency are compared on the exact-octet terms of MUST-T8-2 - the
1299	   audit asks whether the gate's own rules were kept, so it compares the
1300	   way the gate compares.

1302	   Where a usable issuer key is pinned, the comparison is made over the
1303	   receipts that verify under it and the audit fails.  Where none is
1304	   pinned, the departure is still reported and the audit does not fail
1305	   on it alone.  An issuer key is usable when the pinned issuer root
1306	   holds at least one key the verifier can decode.  A pinned root none
1307	   of whose keys decode is already trust-key-unreadable and attests
1308	   nothing, so the comparison takes the unpinned branch while that
1309	   finding stands; the audit has failed on the configuration fault, and
1310	   the departure is still said out loud without becoming a charge no
1311	   readable key backs. -03 stated the first case for both, and an
1312	   implementation showed why that is wrong: a receipt signed by any key
1313	   at all, carrying the right manifest hash and the wrong amount, made
1314	   the verifier report a breach that never happened, against a payment
1315	   reference the forger chose.  Reporting a departure costs nothing if
1316	   it is unattributable; failing an audit on it hands an attacker a way
1317	   to accuse an honest payer.

1319	   MUST-T4-17 and this requirement therefore differ, and the difference
1320	   is not an inconsistency.  Asking whether a manifest hash appears
1321	   anywhere is a question about a set of documents, and an
1322	   unattributable document is still in that set.  Saying that a named
1323	   party broke terms it signed is a charge, and a charge needs a key
1324	   behind it.

1326	   Only receipts that name the manifest are measured against it: reading
1327	   the terms onto a receipt that never claimed them would invent a
1328	   violation the payer did not commit.

1330	   The rules being enforced here are not new.  A gate already refuses a
1331	   bound spend whose amount or currency differs from the manifest (MUST-
1332	   T8-2) and one made against an expired manifest (MUST-T3-3).  Both
1333	   were written for the point where money moves, and an audit reads the
1334	   record after that point, where the gate is no longer present to be
1335	   asked.  Without a counterpart a receipt can carry the hash of terms
1336	   it breaks and the report still calls the books balanced.  This is a
1337	   finding rather than a condition on the guarantee: a verifier that
1338	   reports it held every root it needed, and the statement it is making
1339	   is unconditional.

1341	   The gate answers differently from the audit, and the difference is
1342	   deliberate.  A policy decision point presented with a Trade Manifest
1343	   it cannot attribute MUST refuse the payment rather than settle and
1344	   record the doubt (MUST-T4-16).  An audit describes what it found and
1345	   may say the result is conditional; a gate decides whether money
1346	   moves, and a settled payment carrying the hash of terms nobody
1347	   authorised cannot be withdrawn by reporting it afterwards.  The
1348	   receipt would record those terms as agreed.

1350	10.6.  What the roots do not cover

1352	   A verifier that supplies none of these roots is not making an error,
1353	   and this document does not require it to.  It is making a weaker
1354	   statement, and the guarantee it reports must say so.  With no issuer
1355	   key nothing distinguishes one submitted receipt from another, so
1356	   conditions computed across the submitted set - two receipts claiming
1357	   one settlement reference, for instance - cannot be attributed to
1358	   anyone and are reported as conditions of the submission rather than
1359	   as failures of a party.

1361	11.  Reconciliation and Epoch Checkpoints

1363	   Completeness is the property that, given an authenticated rail
1364	   extract, every settlement in the extract has a matching settled Spend
1365	   Receipt, every settled receipt has a matching settlement, receipt and
1366	   checkpoint hash chains verify, and checkpoint totals equal the sum of
1367	   *settled* receipts in the checkpoint window.  If a spend occurred
1368	   without a receipt, the missing receipt is itself the evidence (MUST-
1369	   T10-2).

1371	   The property is stated over a population, and the extract is what
1372	   declares it: one account, on one rail, over one window (Section 9).
1373	   "Every settlement" means every settlement that extract carried.  A
1374	   settlement path no presented extract covers is not reconciled and not
1375	   found missing - it is outside the population - so the report names
1376	   the account, rail and window it was computed over (MUST-T10-19), and
1377	   a verifier that stated none of them says so instead (MUST-T10-18).

1379	   A checkpoint published with its totals withheld (Section 11.1) cannot
1380	   contribute the last of those to the property.  It is not a violation
1381	   of completeness and it is not a demonstration of it either: the
1382	   comparison was not made, and a result that rests on a comparison
1383	   nobody made is conditional (MUST-T11-12).

1385	11.1.  Checkpoint claims

1387	   An epoch checkpoint MUST be COSE_Sign1-signed with the header profile
1388	   in Section 6 and MUST bind all of the following (MUST-T11-1):

1390	   epoch, startMs, endMs, receiptCount, chainHeadHash, totals, and
1391	   prevCheckpointHash.

1393	   The checkpoint window is half-open [startMs, endMs) (MUST-T11-7).
1394	   receiptCount MUST equal the number of receipts (settled and aborted)
1395	   whose timestampMs falls in that window. chainHeadHash MUST equal
1396	   receiptHash of the last receipt in that window - the last link, in
1397	   issuer order (the prevReceiptHash chain, as the verification
1398	   algorithm's step 6 defines it), of the chain inside the window, not
1399	   the last one presented or the latest timestampMs - or null if the
1400	   window is empty (MUST-T11-2).  Where totals is present it MUST sum
1401	   only receipts with outcome = settled; the one permitted absence is
1402	   the signed redaction below.

1404	   An issuer that publishes a checkpoint without its totals MUST encode
1405	   totals as null in the signed payload (MUST-T11-12).  An empty map is
1406	   an honest zero for an empty window and is not a redaction.  Because
1407	   the redaction is inside the signature, it cannot be added to, or
1408	   removed from, a checkpoint after signing.

1410	   A verifier MUST NOT accept a redaction asserted anywhere but the
1411	   signed payload (MUST-T11-13).  A presentation-layer flag alongside a
1412	   checkpoint is chosen by whoever presents it, which is the party under
1413	   audit; honouring such a flag would let that party switch off the
1414	   totals comparison for a checkpoint whose signed totals are wrong.
1415	   The structural claims (epoch, startMs, endMs, receiptCount,
1416	   chainHeadHash, prevCheckpointHash) MUST NOT be redacted: a checkpoint
1417	   missing any of them does not decode, and a verifier MUST treat it as
1418	   a failed checkpoint rather than as a redacted one.

1420	11.2.  Genesis and continuity

1422	   The first checkpoint in a presented chain is the genesis checkpoint
1423	   of that chain.  Its prevCheckpointHash MUST be null.  Epoch numbers
1424	   MUST be consecutive integers.  Adjacent windows MUST satisfy
1425	   next.startMs = prev.endMs (MUST-T11-8).

1427	   A later checkpoint that omits a prefix of earlier epochs (prefix
1428	   deletion) is detectable only if an external witness (transparency
1429	   log) has recorded the missing prefix (MUST-T11-9).  Without that
1430	   witness, T11 guarantees about suppression are *conditional*.

1432	   -01 stated that dependency and stopped there: nothing in its
1433	   verification algorithm read a witness receipt, so the witness had no
1434	   way to speak. -02 gave it one.  A verifier that holds witness
1435	   receipts for the period under audit compares what the witness
1436	   recorded against what the chain presented, and reports the difference
1437	   under its own name (Section 11.3).  A witness that holds a checkpoint
1438	   the presented chain omits is not the same condition as a chain that
1439	   leaves a gap in its own coverage, and the two MUST NOT be reported
1440	   under one identifier (MUST-T11-11).  The first says evidence is being
1441	   withheld; the second says the evidence shown is incomplete.  An
1442	   operator who cannot tell them apart cannot tell an incomplete record
1443	   from a concealed one.

1445	11.3.  The transparency witness

1447	   A checkpoint registered with a Transparency Service [RFC9943] is a
1448	   Signed Statement whose payload is the checkpoint COSE object and
1449	   whose content type is application/cedulon-checkpoint+cbor
1450	   (Section 14).  What comes back, and what it proves, is stated as two
1451	   named tiers, because -04 promised the mechanics of one tier while
1452	   describing the checks of the other.

1454	   *Tier 1 - the witness receipt.* The witness returns a co-signature
1455	   over the statement hash of what it recorded: a COSE_Sign1 whose
1456	   payload binds the statement hash, the entry index, and the witness's
1457	   tree head.  Verifying it establishes exactly one sentence - "the
1458	   witness signed for this hash" - and nothing more; in particular it
1459	   does not establish membership in an append-only log. -04 called this
1460	   object a transparency receipt and cited the verification mechanics of
1461	   [RFC9942] for it while describing a hash comparison; the admission in
1462	   Section 18, that the receipt was a signature over a statement rather
1463	   than a proof of log membership, was correct, and the protocol text
1464	   now says the same thing.  The [RFC9942] citation applies in tier 2,
1465	   where its mechanics are actually performed.

1467	   On the wire the witness receipt is a COSE_Sign1 under Section 6,
1468	   signed by the witness key, with content type application/cedulon-
1469	   inclusion+cbor (Section 17.6).  Its payload is a deterministic CBOR
1470	   map carrying three entries and is not a CWT claim set: label 1 is the
1471	   statement hash, label 2 the entry index, label 3 the tree head.  The
1472	   two hashes are text strings holding the lowercase hexadecimal of a
1473	   32-octet SHA-256, the statement hash taken over the octets of the
1474	   statement the witness recorded; the entry index is an unsigned
1475	   integer.  The hash strings are compared as issued and are not passed
1476	   through the hash-claim grammar the claim sets use; the revision that
1477	   moves them to byte strings will close that difference.  Entries under
1478	   other labels are not defined, and a verifier of this revision does
1479	   not refuse them.  The map carries no statement body: a receipt that
1480	   travels with the body carries it beside the COSE object, not inside
1481	   it (Section 10.3, MUST-T11-17).  A receipt whose content type is
1482	   another value, or whose payload lacks one of the three entries or
1483	   carries one of another type, does not verify: the content type is the
1484	   check Section 6 makes on every object of this profile, and the
1485	   payload map is the one this paragraph states.

1487	   A verifier MAY be given witness receipts for the period under audit.
1488	   It is a distinct input from the presented checkpoint chain, and
1489	   supplying it is optional: a verifier given none performs the same
1490	   steps, and reports the same findings, that it would if this input did
1491	   not exist (MUST-T11-10).  Supplying an empty set is not the same as
1492	   supplying none.  An empty set says a witness is configured and
1493	   recorded nothing, which is itself reportable; absence says no witness
1494	   was consulted.

1496	   A receipt binds a statement hash, not a statement.  The body is not
1497	   carried by the receipt and a verifier will often not hold it.  Two
1498	   levels of checking follow from that, and they are not the same (MUST-
1499	   T11-10).

1501	   Every receipt MUST have its signature verified before it counts for
1502	   anything.  That is what establishes the hash as one the service
1503	   signed for, and it is all that comparing recorded hashes against
1504	   presented ones requires: the verifier computes the statement hash of
1505	   each presented checkpoint itself.

1507	   A receipt accompanied by the statement body carries more, and MUST be
1508	   checked further before that body is relied on.  The body's statement
1509	   hash MUST equal the hash the receipt binds, and the body MUST itself
1510	   verify as a checkpoint.  A body that fails either check proves
1511	   nothing and MUST be ignored rather than counted, while the receipt it
1512	   came with remains usable for the hash comparison.  Equivocation is
1513	   the case that needs a body, because it compares claims rather than
1514	   hashes.

1516	   *Tier 2 - log membership.* A verifier MAY additionally be given, for
1517	   one recorded statement, the registered Signed Statement bytes (the
1518	   candidate entry) and an inclusion proof.  When both are present, the
1519	   verifier MUST perform the verification of [RFC9942] Section 5.2.1
1520	   over them: hash the candidate entry bytes to obtain the leaf, apply
1521	   the proof to reproduce a root, and accept only when the leaf hash,
1522	   the proof's leaf index, and the reproduced root are all equal to the
1523	   statement hash, entry index, and tree head of one witness receipt
1524	   that verifies under the pinned witness key (MUST-T11-18).
1525	   Reproducing a root is deliberately not sufficient on its own: the
1526	   proof format below carries no domain separation between leaves and
1527	   interior nodes, and the exact match against a witness-signed receipt
1528	   is what closes the ambiguities that follow from that.  A pair that
1529	   fails this check, or a candidate entry presented without a proof,
1530	   MUST be reported as a failing finding; the identifier witness-
1531	   inclusion-invalid names it.

1533	   The inclusion proof is an audit path: the leaf index, and the sibling
1534	   hashes from the leaf's level up to the root, lowest level first.  The
1535	   tree is built over statement hashes as leaves; an interior node is
1536	   the SHA-256 of the concatenation of its two children's 32 raw bytes;
1537	   a level with an odd count pairs its last node with itself, so the
1538	   path needs no separate leaf count.

1540	   Where witness receipts are supplied and this pair is not, tier 2 was
1541	   not exercised, and the report MUST say so rather than letting the
1542	   tier pass silently (MUST-T11-19); the identifier witness-inclusion-
1543	   not-exercised names it, as a warning - the witness attested the
1544	   statement hash, and log membership was not proven.  A pair that
1545	   verifies is silent, like every other passing check in the algorithm.

1547	   What tier 1 establishes, and what it does not, is worth stating
1548	   plainly.  It establishes that the service signed for that statement.
1549	   Whether the statement is a member of an append-only log is tier 2's
1550	   question, answered only where tier 2's inputs were supplied and
1551	   verified; whether the log has ever equivocated remains a property of
1552	   the service and its own proofs.  A verifier that treats a tier-1
1553	   receipt alone as proof of log membership is claiming more than the
1554	   receipt carries.

1556	11.4.  Verification algorithm

1558	   A verifier MUST perform all of these steps and MUST report every
1559	   finding they produce (MUST-T10-1, MUST-T11-2).  They are numbered for
1560	   reference, not to require an evaluation order: no step short-circuits
1561	   another, and an implementation may evaluate them in any order that
1562	   produces the same set of findings.

1564	   The data dependencies are named, because "any order" read naively
1565	   would break them.  Step 15 decides which witness receipts, and which
1566	   statement bodies, survive checking; steps 14 and 16 consume what
1567	   survives.  Step 7's index of refs is what steps 8 and 9 reconcile.
1568	   Step 11 decides which checkpoints verified, and step 14 compares only
1569	   those together with what step 15 admitted.  An implementation that
1570	   ran a consumer against an unchecked producer would not produce the
1571	   same set of findings, so those orders are not among the permitted
1572	   ones.

1574	   The second is the issuer pin.  The step that resolves it decides the
1575	   *attested set* - the receipts and checkpoints that verify under a
1576	   usable pinned issuer key, or the whole presented set when no usable
1577	   key is pinned - and every later step that walks receipts or
1578	   checkpoints consumes that set: the chain walk in step 6, the indexing
1579	   and reconciliation in steps 7 through 9, the checkpoint comparisons
1580	   in steps 11 through 13, and the MUST-T8-9 comparison.  A receipt the
1581	   pin rejects is reported once and then excluded, which is what keeps
1582	   the settlement it names visible as uncovered (MUST-T4-10); an
1583	   implementation that let it back into any of those steps would let a
1584	   forged receipt cover a settlement, satisfy a checkpoint count, or
1585	   invent a terms charge.  Two checks deliberately stay on the presented
1586	   set whatever any key says, and MUST NOT acquire the dependency: MUST-
1587	   T4-17, which asks whether a manifest was named at all, and the per-
1588	   receipt defect checks that ask what a receipt says about itself. -04
1589	   asserted that nothing else in the list fed another step, which the
1590	   dependencies above had already made false; the assertion is withdrawn
1591	   and the list above is the inventory, maintained rather than
1592	   summarised.

1594	   When a step names an identifier in backticks, that identifier SHOULD
1595	   be used for the condition in diagnostic output.  The normative
1596	   requirement is the behaviour: report the condition, identified by the
1597	   ref or other handle given in the step.  The identifiers are not an
1598	   interoperability surface.

1600	   1.   Establish the subject of the audit.  When an extract is
1601	        supplied, the settlement records it carries are the ones
1602	        reconciled; a settlement list from any other source MUST NOT be
1603	        substituted for them (MUST-T10-12).  If the caller supplies both
1604	        and they differ, the verifier MUST report that the caller-
1605	        supplied list disagrees with the extract, and MUST still
1606	        reconcile the extract.  The identifier extract-settlement-
1607	        mismatch SHOULD be used for this condition in diagnostic output.

1609	   2.   Verify the extract signature against the out-of-band rail key
1610	        (MUST-T10-8, MUST-T10-9).  If no key is pinned, the check that
1611	        runs is against the key the extract carries, which establishes
1612	        internal consistency and not origin; the verifier MUST still
1613	        compute it, MUST NOT read it as a statement about who produced
1614	        the extract, and MUST treat the completeness guarantee as
1615	        conditional (MUST-T10-7).  The identifier unauthenticated-
1616	        extract SHOULD be used for this condition in diagnostic output,
1617	        whatever the extract carries.  If a key is pinned and cannot be
1618	        decoded, the verifier MUST report that the pinned key is
1619	        unreadable.  The identifier trust-key-unreadable SHOULD be used
1620	        for this condition.  If a key is pinned and the signature does
1621	        not verify against it, or verifies against a different key, the
1622	        verifier MUST report that the extract is not signed by the
1623	        pinned key.  The identifier extract-key-mismatch SHOULD be used
1624	        for this condition.  The rows of an extract the pin refused are
1625	        not reconciled against the receipts, and no settlement finding
1626	        is read out of that document (MUST-T10-20); the identifier
1627	        settlement-comparison-skipped SHOULD be used to say that the
1628	        comparison did not run.  A finding that puts the extract itself
1629	        in doubt MUST prevent an unconditional guarantee.

1631	   3.   Check scope.  The verifier MUST report each settlement record
1632	        whose timestampMs falls outside the declared window, identified
1633	        by that record's ref (MUST-T10-10).  When the verifier states an
1634	        expected account, rail, or window, it MUST report an extract
1635	        that does not cover it (MUST-T10-11).  The identifier extract-
1636	        scope-mismatch SHOULD be used for both conditions.  If the
1637	        verifier stated no period, it MUST treat the guarantee as
1638	        conditional (MUST-T10-15).  The identifier unstated-audit-window
1639	        SHOULD be used for this condition.  If it stated no account or
1640	        no rail, it MUST treat the guarantee as conditional for the same
1641	        reason (MUST-T10-18); the identifier unstated-audit-scope SHOULD
1642	        be used.  Whatever the verdict, the report MUST name the
1643	        account, rail and window the extract declared, in every
1644	        structure it returns for the audit (MUST-T10-19).

1646	   4.   Resolve each Spend Receipt against the issuer root
1647	        (Section 10.1) in one pass.  Decode the COSE_Sign1; a content
1648	        type that is not the receipt type, a decoder bound, or a decoded
1649	        claim map that does not match the presented claims is a named
1650	        refusal, not a signature verdict (MUST-T4-2, MUST-T4-8).  Then
1651	        ask one question: does the signature verify under a pinned
1652	        issuer key. kid routes the check to a candidate key and carries
1653	        no authority of its own; the carried key is not consulted for
1654	        membership at all.  The resolution table in Section 10.1 reads
1655	        the answer out per cell.  Every cell there is a named condition
1656	        plus a membership decision; no cell is a silent removal, and the
1657	        word "reject" in earlier revisions meant nothing more than a
1658	        cell of that table.  Where a countersignature is present,
1659	        Section 10.2 governs what it establishes (MUST-T4-13, MUST-
1660	        T4-14).  Where a Trade Manifest is presented, Section 10.5
1661	        governs it (MUST-T4-15): with no publisher key pinned the
1662	        verifier reports unauthenticated-manifest and the guarantee is
1663	        conditional; with a pin that cannot be read, trust-key-
1664	        unreadable; with a pin the manifest does not answer to,
1665	        manifest-key-mismatch; and with a manifest that no presented
1666	        receipt references, manifest-covers-no-receipt (MUST-T4-17).  A
1667	        receipt that names the manifest but departs from its amount,
1668	        currency, expiry or, where the manifest names one, payee is
1669	        reported as manifest-terms-mismatch; with a usable issuer key
1670	        pinned the comparison runs over the attested receipts and the
1671	        departure fails the audit, and with no usable issuer key it is a
1672	        warning over the presented receipts and does not by itself fail
1673	        the audit (MUST-T8-9).  An audit presented with no Trade
1674	        Manifest is not made conditional by this step.

1676	   5.   Scope the receipts.  Membership follows the ref binding first: a
1677	        receipt whose ref appears on the extract is reconciled against
1678	        this extract even when its own timestampMs falls outside the
1679	        declared window - the rail has signed that the settlement
1680	        belongs to the window, and the receipt follows its settlement.
1681	        The timestampMs sieve applies only to receipts the extract does
1682	        not name: such a receipt outside the window is not a
1683	        completeness failure against this extract (MUST-T10-16);
1684	        auditing a longer period requires extracts that cover it.  At
1685	        the window's edges the declared allowance applies (Section 9):
1686	        an unmatched settled receipt within clockSkewMs of windowEndMs,
1687	        and an unmatched settlement record within clockSkewMs of
1688	        windowStartMs, are reported as boundary-deferred, a warning,
1689	        rather than as step 8's completeness findings - two honest
1690	        clocks can disagree by less than the allowance, and both
1691	        verifiers of an honest edge payment would otherwise reach the
1692	        same false accusation (MUST-T10-17).  Where the following
1693	        window's extract is presented and verifies, a deferred receipt
1694	        whose ref appears on it is resolved and not reported, and one
1695	        whose ref does not appear hardens into the step 8 finding; a
1696	        deferred settlement record near the opening edge resolves
1697	        through this step's ref binding, since the prior window's
1698	        receipt that names its ref is reconciled here regardless of
1699	        timestamp.  The following window's extract closes or hardens
1700	        closing-edge deferrals only; an opening-edge record stays
1701	        deferred until a receipt in the presented bag names its ref,
1702	        whether or not a following extract is presented.  In a single-
1703	        window audit a deferred record keeps the guarantee conditional.
1704	        Receipts remain subject to every other check regardless of
1705	        window.

1707	   6.   Walk the attested receipts in issuer order.  Issuer order is the
1708	        order induced by the prevReceiptHash chain: the verifier
1709	        rebuilds the chain from the links, and the order in which
1710	        receipts were presented carries no weight. timestampMs is
1711	        issuer-asserted and is not an ordering source.  The first
1712	        prevReceiptHash MUST be null.  Each later prevReceiptHash MUST
1713	        equal receiptHash of the previous receipt.  A miss, and a
1714	        receipt the links cannot place, MUST be reported as a break in
1715	        the receipt chain.  The identifier receipt-chain-break SHOULD be
1716	        used for this condition.

1718	   7.   Index the attested settled receipts and extract records by ref.
1719	        A ref that appears more than once on either side MUST be
1720	        reported as a repeated reference (MUST-T10-6).  The identifier
1721	        duplicate-ref SHOULD be used for this condition.

1723	   8.   For each ref that appears exactly once on each side, require a
1724	        one-to-one match on ref AND amount AND currency (MUST-T10-1),
1725	        compared as exact octets on the terms of MUST-T8-2; step 9's
1726	        aggregation is the only place this algorithm reads an amount as
1727	        a number.  Amount or currency mismatch MUST be reported as a
1728	        settlement that does not match its receipt, identified by that
1729	        ref.  The identifier settlement-mismatch SHOULD be used for this
1730	        condition.  A settlement with no receipt MUST be reported as
1731	        lacking a receipt, identified by its ref (MUST-T10-2).  The
1732	        identifier settlement-without-receipt SHOULD be used for this
1733	        condition.  A settled receipt with no extract row MUST be
1734	        reported as a completeness failure (MUST-T10-3).  The identifier
1735	        receipt-without-settlement SHOULD be used for this condition.  A
1736	        settled receipt with a null rail ref MUST be reported as settled
1737	        without a rail reference; this check asks what a receipt says
1738	        about itself and runs over the presented receipts, attested or
1739	        not.  The identifier settled-without-ref SHOULD be used for this
1740	        condition.  Where a settlement record declares a beneficiary
1741	        (Section 9), it MUST be compared against the matched receipt's
1742	        payee as exact octets; a difference is beneficiary-mismatch and
1743	        fails the audit.  Where neither the manifest names a payee nor
1744	        any settlement record declares a beneficiary, the report MUST
1745	        carry counterparty-unbound, a scope record: ref, amount and
1746	        currency closed against the payer's account extract, and the
1747	        counterparty's identity was not bound.  It is a statement of
1748	        what the evidence did not cover, not a doubt about what it did,
1749	        so it does not move the verdict and does not by itself make the
1750	        guarantee conditional.

1752	   9.   A ref already reported as repeating MUST still be reconciled by
1753	        amount rather than dropped from the comparison (MUST-T10-13).
1754	        For each currency under that ref, compare the total settled
1755	        against the total receipted.  A settled total that exceeds the
1756	        receipted total MUST be reported as a settlement lacking a
1757	        receipt, and the finding MUST state the unaccounted amount.  The
1758	        identifier settlement-without-receipt SHOULD be used for this
1759	        condition.  A settled total that is less than the receipted
1760	        total MUST be reported as a settlement that does not match its
1761	        receipt, identified by that ref.  The identifier settlement-
1762	        mismatch SHOULD be used for this condition.  An amount on that
1763	        repeating ref that cannot be parsed as an integer MUST be
1764	        reported without abandoning the audit; the identifier malformed-
1765	        amount SHOULD be used for this condition.  A verifier MUST still
1766	        report findings for the remaining records.

1768	   10.  Aborted receipts are not matched to extract rows and are not
1769	        added to totals.

1771	   11.  Decode each checkpoint.  Reject a failed signature, and reject a
1772	        kid that does not match the key obtained for the checkpoint
1773	        issuer, on the same terms as a receipt (MUST-T4-8).  Require
1774	        receiptCount, chainHeadHash, and totals to match the attested
1775	        receipts in [startMs, endMs) as defined above (MUST-T11-2); a
1776	        receipt step 4 rejected is not among them, or a forged receipt
1777	        could satisfy a checkpoint count.  The identifier checkpoint-
1778	        total-mismatch SHOULD be used for a failed signature, a wrong
1779	        receiptCount, or totals that disagree, and checkpoint-head-
1780	        mismatch for a chainHeadHash that is not the last link, in
1781	        issuer order (step 6), of the chain inside [startMs, endMs) -
1782	        "last receipt" binds to the chain, not to presentation or to
1783	        timestampMs.  If the signed totals is null, the verifier cannot
1784	        perform the totals comparison for that checkpoint.  It MUST
1785	        report that the comparison was skipped and MUST treat the
1786	        completeness guarantee as conditional; the absence of a
1787	        comparison is not a passed comparison (MUST-T11-12).  The
1788	        identifier checkpoint-totals-redacted SHOULD be used for this
1789	        condition. receiptCount and chainHeadHash MUST still be checked.
1790	        A checkpoint that fails verification MUST NOT be treated as
1791	        redacted, whatever it claims about its own totals.

1793	   12.  Every chained receipt MUST fall in exactly one checkpoint
1794	        window.  A gap or double count MUST be reported as a window
1795	        coverage failure (MUST-T11-7, MUST-T11-8).  The identifier
1796	        window-coverage SHOULD be used for this condition.  The check
1797	        runs against the presented checkpoint windows and it is fail-
1798	        closed: a receipt that falls under none of them is this failure
1799	        - including every receipt when no checkpoint was presented, and
1800	        receipts after the last closed checkpoint's endMs.  The text
1801	        names that last state an *open epoch*, and it is auditable only
1802	        when the checkpoint that closes it is issued; the name explains
1803	        why the finding fired, it does not soften it.  Nothing about an
1804	        absent checkpoint is silent: uncovered evidence surfaces as a
1805	        finding rather than as a gap in the report.

1807	   13.  Walk checkpoints in epoch order. prevCheckpointHash MUST equal
1808	        the SHA-256 of the previous checkpoint COSE bytes, or null for
1809	        genesis (MUST-T11-4).  The identifier checkpoint-total-mismatch
1810	        SHOULD be used for a broken chain, which is the fourth condition
1811	        its table row names.

1813	   14.  If two successfully verified checkpoints share an epoch number
1814	        and have different hashes, the verifier MUST report equivocation
1815	        (MUST-T11-3).  The identifier equivocation SHOULD be used for
1816	        this condition.  The checkpoints compared here are those
1817	        presented *together with* any carried by verified witness
1818	        receipts (step 15).  Comparing only the presented chain cannot
1819	        raise this finding: MUST-T11-8, applied in step 12, requires
1820	        that chain's epochs to be consecutive, so no two of its members
1821	        share an epoch.  A copy recorded by a witness is where the
1822	        second one is found.

1824	   15.  If witness receipts were supplied, verify them against the out-
1825	        of-band witness key (Section 10.3); receipts that cannot be
1826	        checked that way are not evidence in either direction and the
1827	        verifier reports that it left them out (MUST-T11-15); the
1828	        identifier unauthenticated-witness SHOULD be used for this
1829	        condition.  Discard any whose signature fails (MUST-T11-10).  A
1830	        surviving receipt whose statement body verifies against the
1831	        issuer root is a statement that issuer published; one that does
1832	        not is another party's, and is not this issuer equivocating
1833	        (MUST-T11-16).  The survivors are the recorded statement hashes
1834	        used in step 16.  Where a receipt also carries the statement
1835	        body, discard that body unless its statement hash equals the one
1836	        the receipt binds and it verifies as a checkpoint; the surviving
1837	        bodies are what step 14 compares.  Discarding a body does not
1838	        discard its receipt.  This is tier 1 of Section 11.3.  Where the
1839	        tier-2 pair - the registered Signed Statement bytes and an
1840	        inclusion proof - was also supplied, verify it as Section 11.3
1841	        states: apply the proof to the candidate entry's hash and accept
1842	        only on an exact match with a witness-signed receipt (MUST-
1843	        T11-18); a pair that fails, or a candidate without a proof, is
1844	        witness-inclusion-invalid and the audit MUST fail.  Where
1845	        witness receipts were supplied and no tier-2 pair was, report
1846	        witness-inclusion-not-exercised as a warning (MUST-T11-19): the
1847	        witness attested the statement hash, and log membership was not
1848	        proven.

1850	   16.  Compare the surviving witness records against the presented
1851	        chain (MUST-T11-11).  For each presented checkpoint with no
1852	        surviving record, report that it is not anchored; the identifier
1853	        checkpoint-not-anchored SHOULD be used.  This is a warning: a
1854	        witness may have been configured after the checkpoint was
1855	        issued, and an operator's own gap is not evidence of
1856	        concealment.  For each surviving record whose statement is
1857	        absent from the presented chain, report that a recorded
1858	        checkpoint was withheld; the identifier checkpoint-withheld
1859	        SHOULD be used.  This is a finding and the audit MUST fail.  A
1860	        record that carries no statement body cannot say whose statement
1861	        it binds, so it MUST NOT produce this finding; it is reported as
1862	        an entry that could not be attributed and makes the guarantee
1863	        conditional, because a real withholding must not be buried by
1864	        removing the body (MUST-T11-17).  Such a record still
1865	        establishes anchoring in the first half of this step: proving
1866	        that a checkpoint was logged needs only its hash.  The verifier
1867	        MUST NOT report a withheld checkpoint as a window coverage
1868	        failure (MUST-T11-11).

1870	   17.  If any finding remains that is not a warning (a warning is a
1871	        condition that only makes the completeness guarantee

2233	   An unconditional guarantee therefore requires all of: an extract, a

2247	   whatever human-readable audit report it produces under this document,

2249	   the books balance while withholding that the balance is conditional
2250	   invites the reader to take a conditional result for an unconditional

2268	12.  Lifecycle

2270	   1.  *Manifest.* Parties sign a Trade Manifest (optional for metered
2271	       API spend; required for goods with acceptance criteria).

2273	   2.  *Policy check.* The adapter submits a structured request to the
2274	       PDP.  Default is deny.  An allow is a Decision Token (MUST-T6-4).

2276	   3.  *Payment.* On allow, the adapter performs the x402 (or other
2277	       rail) exchange using exactly the decision fields (MUST-T6-1).
2278	       The Decision Token is consumed (MUST-T6-2).  A reused nonce is
2279	       denied (MUST-T3-1, MUST-T3-2).  A tampered or expired token is
2280	       denied (MUST-T6-5).

2282	   4.  *Receipt.* The Receipt Issuer signs a Spend Receipt.  Rail
2283	       credentials MUST NOT appear in the receipt, logs, or tool results
2284	       (MUST-T5-2, MUST-T7-1).

2286	   5.  *Dispute Evidence Bundle.* If delivery bytes do not match the
2287	       acceptance-criteria hash, an implementation MUST be able to emit
2288	       a bundle of manifest + receipt + delivery hash (MUST-T8-3).  The
2289	       bundle MUST NOT be described as an arbitral award or escrow
2290	       release (MUST-T8-4).

2292	13.  Policy Semantics

2294	   Policy is default deny.  The engine understands three families of
2295	   rule:

2297	   *  *Limit:* maximum amount per payment; maximum cumulative amount per
2298	      window (MUST-T2-2).

2300	   *  *Velocity:* maximum number of allowed payments per window (MUST-
2301	      T2-1).

2303	   *  *Scope:* optional allow-lists for payee, currency, and tool name.

2305	   Fail-closed: missing engine, crash, or exception yields deny (MUST-
2306	   T2-3).  Implementations SHOULD emit stable reason codes (SHOULD-
2307	   T2-5).  Decision tokens SHOULD expire after a short TTL (SHOULD-
2308	   T6-3).

2310	   The agent-facing spend interface MUST invoke the PDP and MUST NOT
2311	   expose a parallel ungated rail call to the model (MUST-T5-1).

2313	   One boundary is stated here rather than left to be inferred.  In the
2314	   retrospective audit, "allowed by policy" is the Receipt Issuer's
2315	   signed assertion: the spend passed the issuer's gate under the
2316	   policyHash the receipt names.  The Decision Token is consumed at the
2317	   gate, and the verification algorithm never sees it; the audit does
2318	   not independently re-verify the PDP's allow.  The completeness side
2319	   of the audit has an independent leg - the rail extract - and the
2320	   policy side deliberately does not: that is a trust boundary of this
2321	   profile, not an oversight.  A deployment that wants the policy answer
2322	   to be independently verifiable needs a receipt-to-token binding,
2323	   which this revision does not define; it is named as a possible
2324	   extension (Section 19), and adding it would change what a receipt
2325	   carries, so it is not smuggled in here.

2327	14.  SCITT Anchoring

2329	   A Receipt Issuer or relying party MAY construct a SCITT Signed
2330	   Statement whose payload is either the Spend Receipt COSE object or a
2331	   privacy profile (Section 15) and register it with a Transparency
2332	   Service [RFC9943].  The service returns a COSE receipt [RFC9942].
2333	   Embedding that receipt yields a Transparent Statement.  Cedulon does
2334	   not define a new transparency algorithm.

2336	   An epoch checkpoint MUST be registrable on the same terms (MUST-
2337	   T11-14).  Its Signed Statement carries the checkpoint COSE_Sign1
2338	   object as the payload and application/cedulon-checkpoint+cbor as the
2339	   content type, which is among the media types Section 17 asks to have
2340	   registered and which, until then, is a placeholder like the rest.
2341	   Nothing else about registration differs from a receipt.

2343	   This is a short section for a requirement -01 was missing, and the
2344	   omission mattered more than its length suggests. -01 asked for
2345	   checkpoints to be registered (SHOULD-T11-5) while profiling only the
2346	   receipt here, so the object carrying the suppression guarantee had no
2347	   stated form to be registered in.  Two implementations could follow
2348	   -01 to the letter and register incomparable things.

2350	15.  Privacy Considerations

2352	   A public transparency encoding MUST support omitting or hashing payer
2353	   and payee identifiers and MUST support amount redaction or bucket
2354	   encoding (MUST-T9-1).  Implementations MUST NOT write government-ID
2355	   numbers, payment-instrument PAN, or street address into a public
2356	   statement (MUST-T9-2).  Default public anchors SHOULD publish
2357	   policyHash, manifestHash, receiptHash, and timestampMs rather than
2358	   full claims (SHOULD-T9-3).  A private auditor MAY receive an
2359	   unredacted receipt out of band (MAY-T9-4).

2361	   The paragraph above counts receipt fields.  A checkpoint publishes
2362	   something a receipt does not: a per-currency total for a whole
2363	   window, which discloses trading volume even when every individual
2364	   receipt is redacted (MUST-T9-5). -01 gave no rule for it, so an
2365	   implementation could publish that total, or withhold it in a way no
2366	   verifier could recognise, and neither reading contradicted the text.

2368	   The rule is the one stated in Section 11: totals MAY be withheld by
2369	   signing it as null (MUST-T11-12), and only that form counts as a
2370	   redaction (MUST-T11-13).  The structural claims are not redactable,
2371	   because a verifier that cannot read the window or the chain head
2372	   cannot check anything at all, and a checkpoint that hid them would be
2373	   indistinguishable from a broken one.

2375	   Withholding is honest and it is also a cost: a verifier that cannot
2376	   recompute the totals says so, and the completeness guarantee for that
2377	   window is conditional.  A deployment that wants an unconditional
2378	   result publishes the totals; a deployment that wants the volume
2379	   private accepts a conditional one.  What a deployment MUST NOT do is
2380	   obtain the unconditional result while withholding the evidence for
2381	   it.

3214	16.14.  T12: Settlement without a recorded receipt

3216	   The threats above are about a counterparty, a rail or an attacker.
3217	   This one is about the issuer's own implementation, and it produces
3218	   exactly the condition the rest of this document exists to make
3219	   detectable.

3221	   An issuer that settles a payment, appends the receipt in memory and
3222	   then persists its state has three steps where it could have two
3223	   outcomes.  If the write fails, the rail holds a settlement and the
3224	   receipt exists nowhere durable.  The next start reads a state that
3225	   does not contain it, and the audit in Section 11 reports settlement-
3226	   without-receipt against an honest issuer that did everything its own
3227	   policy asked.  The evidence is missing because the issuer lost it,
3228	   not because anyone hid it, and nothing in the report can tell those
3229	   apart.

3243	     |            | any later write it has not yet issued.  A        |

3251	     |            | an operator can act on.                          |
3252	     +------------+--------------------------------------------------+
3253	     | MUST-T12-4 | Where a settlement has entered a rail the issuer |
3254	     |            | does not control, and the local record cannot be |
3255	     |            | completed, the outcome is indeterminate.  The    |
3256	     |            | issuer MUST NOT treat the payment as reversed,   |
3257	     |            | and MUST NOT return the authority to spend,      |
3258	     |            | unless it holds authenticated evidence that the  |
3259	     |            | rail did not complete the settlement or that a   |
3260	     |            | reversing entry completed.                       |
3261	     +------------+--------------------------------------------------+

3263	                                  Table 23

3265	   Ordering:  Settle-then-record is the natural order to write and the
3266	      wrong one to ship.  The record is what makes the settlement
3267	      accountable, so the record is what has to be secured first (MUST-
3268	      T12-1), and a settlement that cannot be recorded has to be undone
3269	      everywhere the issuer still controls, including the nonce and the
3270	      allowance it never used (MUST-T12-2).  That undo is what the in-
3271	      process RailLedger and the session tests measure.  Once value has
3272	      entered a rail the issuer does not control, a local snapshot
3273	      cannot retract it.  Persistence failing after that point leaves
3274	      the outcome indeterminate; authority is not returned without
3275	      authenticated evidence that the rail did not complete the
3276	      settlement or that a reversing entry did (MUST-T12-4).

3278	   Recovery:  A durable-state conflict is not necessarily fatal, but it
3279	      MUST NOT be silent, and an implementation that refuses every
3280	      subsequent write without offering a way back has turned a
3281	      recoverable condition into an outage.  The reason reported has to
3282	      separate the cases an operator would act on differently (MUST-
3283	      T12-3): a write that conflicted with another writer, a write that
3284	      failed, and a state another process is holding.  A single opaque
3285	      failure leaves the operator to guess which of those happened.

3287	   Observability:  A violation of this threat is not visible in the
3288	      evidence a verifier receives.  The audit sees a settlement with no
3289	      receipt and reports settlement-without-receipt, which is the same
3290	      finding an adversary would produce, and nothing in the extract or
3291	      the receipt set distinguishes an issuer that lost the evidence
3292	      from one that hid it.  That is why the requirements here fall on
3293	      the issuer rather than on the verifier, and why an operator-facing
3294	      reason is required rather than optional.

4223	   says whose digest ap2MandateHash is.  The Decision Token gains the
4224	   same expiry boundary MUST-T3-3 states for the manifest.

4226	   Appendix A no longer defers to the tests of an implementation; a
4227	   specification that points at code cannot be implemented from its own
4228	   text, which is the property this revision is trying to restore.

4230	   One change is metadata rather than text: the submission stream in the
4231	   document's header changes from independent to IETF.  The Note to
4232	   Readers has named an eventual Standards Track intent since -00, and
4233	   Section 17 asks for Standards Tree registration; neither belongs on
4234	   the Independent Stream.  The rendered pages are unchanged by it.

4236	   What has not changed: MUST-T12-4 remains specified and not executed,
4237	   and this document still has no independent implementation written
4238	   from its text alone.  The point of this revision is to make that
4239	   possible, not to claim it happened.

4241	18.7.  Changes from -02

4243	   -03 had two subjects.  The first is that -02 stated a rule for signed
4244	   objects and left the implementation and the verification algorithm
4245	   without a counterpart for every object the rule applied to.  The
4246	   second is T12, which no reader reported and which is not about an
4247	   adversary at all.

4249	   -02 Section 6.2 already required a verifier to obtain the public key
4250	   from an authenticated channel (a preconfigured issuer set, a
4251	   directory, or a transparency statement) and to reject a message whose
4252	   kid does not match that key (MUST-T4-8).  That rule was general.
4253	   What -02 did not carry was the verification algorithm that applies it
4254	   to each signed object, the separate root inputs a verifier supplies
4255	   out of band, and the error semantics that name a missing, unreadable,
4256	   or mismatched pin.  A companion implementation that followed the
4257	   algorithm it had, rather than that prose, still checked a Spend
4258	   Receipt, an epoch checkpoint, a Decision Token and a transparency
4259	   inclusion receipt against the key travelling inside them.  A receipt
4260	   like that silences the settlement-without-receipt finding for the
4261	   settlement it names.  The completeness property is then computed over
4262	   evidence that answers to nobody.  Section 10 states the missing
4263	   algorithm and the error semantics, and the verification algorithm now
4264	   applies them.

4266	   A reader raised the inclusion-receipt half of this against the posted
4267	   -02 after checking the archived text against the implementation
4268	   commit.  Following it into the code turned up the other three, along
4269	   with two conditions that were not about keys at all.

4271	   The first is that reporting a mismatch is not enough.  An
4272	   implementation that names a foreign key and then still lets the
4273	   receipt match its settlement has described the attack in its output
4274	   while concluding that the books balance.  MUST-T4-10 now requires the
4275	   settlement to stay reported.

4277	   The second is that an expectation which only fires when the evidence
4278	   is present can be cancelled by deleting the evidence.  A pinned payee
4279	   key with no countersignature to check was silence, so removing a
4280	   countersignature removed the question with it.  MUST-T4-14 closes
4281	   that, and MUST-T11-17 closes the same shape in the witness: an
4282	   inclusion receipt with its body stripped off can no longer bury a
4283	   withholding, while still not being allowed to accuse anyone.

4285	   T12 is new and is not about an adversary.  An issuer that settles,
4286	   appends the receipt in memory and then writes its state will, when
4287	   that write fails, leave the rail holding a settlement whose receipt
4288	   exists nowhere durable.  Restarted, it reports settlement-without-
4289	   receipt against itself.  The condition this document exists to make
4290	   detectable was reachable through the implementation's own ordering,
4291	   and no requirement in -02 said otherwise.

4293	   The same first subject carries five requirements this section has not
4294	   named so far, all of them stated in Section 10.  A pinned key that
4295	   cannot be decoded MUST NOT fall back to the keys the objects carry
4296	   (MUST-T4-11).  An issuer root may comprise more than one key, and a
4297	   verifier must accept one that does, so a rotation inside the audited
4298	   window does not force it to choose between findings against honest
4299	   receipts and abandoning the pin (MUST-T4-12); the same set-of-keys
4300	   acceptance applies to a publisher, witness, or rail pin.  The same
4301	   out-of-band rule reaches the payee countersignature (MUST-T4-13), the
4302	   transparency witness (MUST-T11-15 and MUST-T11-16), the Decision
4303	   Token, whose consumer issued it and therefore already holds the key
4304	   to check it with (MUST-T6-6), and a presented Trade Manifest (MUST-
4305	   T4-15).

4307	   Two requirements belong to neither subject.  MUST-T7-5 and MUST-T7-6
4308	   come from measuring the protection a stored signing key actually has
4309	   instead of deriving it from the platform.  A mount that ignores
4310	   filesystem permissions accepts the call and protects nothing, and a
4311	   writable directory or a symbolic link anywhere on the path makes the
4312	   file permission moot.  They are stated because the implementation
4313	   reported protection it did not have.

4315	   Two things are stated here that -02 got right and -03 kept unchanged:
4316	   the extract rule itself, and the treatment of a pinned key the
4317	   verifier cannot decode.  What changed is their reach, and the change
4318	   is not backward compatible.  Where a verifier pinned the rail key,
4319	   supplied no issuer key, and was presented with receipts or
4320	   checkpoints, -02 reported the guarantee as unconditional and -03
4321	   reports it as conditional.  Nothing about the evidence changed; what
4322	   changed is that the guarantee now says which questions were never
4323	   asked.  An audit presented with neither is unaffected, for the reason
4324	   given in Section 10.1.

4326	18.8.  Changes from -01

4328	   -02 had one subject: the checkpoint, which carries the T11 guarantee
4329	   against suppression and rollback, was not wired into anything that
4330	   could discharge it.

4332	   A reader of -01 set out the gap and a second reader confirmed it
4333	   independently.  Four things were wrong at once, and they were the
4334	   same thing seen from four sides.  The SCITT anchoring section
4335	   profiled the Spend Receipt and not the checkpoint, so SHOULD-T11-5
4336	   asked for checkpoints to be registered without saying in what form
4337	   (MUST-T11-14 now says).  None of the steps of the verification
4338	   algorithm read a witness receipt, so a deployment could follow
4339	   SHOULD-T11-5 to the letter and still have a verifier that never
4340	   consulted the witness (steps 15 and 16 now do).  MUST-T11-3,
4341	   equivocation, could not fire at all: the only checkpoints compared
4342	   were the presented ones, and MUST-T11-8 requires those to be
4343	   consecutive, so no two of them can share an epoch.  A second copy is
4344	   found in a witness, and nothing brought one in.  And the checkpoint
4345	   binds a per-currency total for a window while the privacy section
4346	   counted only receipt fields, so a window total could be published, or
4347	   withheld, with no stated rule either way.

4349	   The repairs are MUST-T11-10 through MUST-T11-14.  The transparency
4350	   receipt is a new optional input; supplying none leaves the verifier
4351	   behaving exactly as in -01, which is deliberate, because the point of
4352	   the witness is to add a claim that could not be made before, not to
4353	   withdraw one that could.

4355	   Two decisions inside those repairs are worth stating on their own,
4356	   because a reader might reasonably have expected the other choice.

4358	   *  A recorded checkpoint the presented chain omits gets its own
4359	      identifier, checkpoint-withheld, rather than being folded into
4360	      window-coverage (MUST-T11-11).  The reporter asked which way it
4361	      should go.  Coverage says the record shown is incomplete; a
4362	      withheld checkpoint says the party under audit is holding a record
4363	      it did not show.  An operator who sees one identifier for both
4364	      cannot tell an incomplete record from a concealed one, which is
4365	      the distinction the whole threat is about.

4367	   *  A checkpoint with no receipt in a supplied witness is a warning
4368	      rather than a failure.  A witness may be configured after
4369	      checkpoints have already been issued, and an operator's own gap in
4370	      anchoring is not evidence that anything was concealed.  The
4371	      asymmetry is deliberate: what the witness holds and the chain does
4372	      not is a finding, what the chain holds and the witness does not is
4373	      a warning.

4375	   One change is not a repair of -01 but of the first attempt at this
4376	   revision, and it is recorded because the failure is instructive.
4377	   Signed totals were first made redactable through a field carried
4378	   alongside the checkpoint rather than inside the COSE payload.
4379	   Anything outside the signature is chosen by whoever presents the
4380	   object, which here is the party under audit, so a checkpoint whose
4381	   signed totals disagreed with its receipts could be re-presented as
4382	   redacted and the mismatch went unreported.  Redaction is now inside
4383	   the signature and a redaction asserted anywhere else MUST be ignored
4384	   (MUST-T11-12, MUST-T11-13).  This was the same shape as the bypass
4385	   -01 was written to close: a check that a party under audit could
4386	   switch off.

4388	   Reversal, refund, partial settlement, and the escrow role remain out
4389	   of scope and are still expected later, with no date. -01 said the
4390	   same, and no revision since has improved on it.

4392	   The reporters are named in the Acknowledgments.

4394	19.  Evolution and Future Work (Informative)

4396	   This document is submitted as Informational.  The author's intended
4397	   eventual track, if the work is taken up, is a Standards Track profile
4398	   of COSE [RFC9052] and CWT [RFC8392] for agent-spend receipts.

4400	   This section is a direction, not a commitment.  The structures below
4401	   are reserved in name only.  Normative wire formats, tests, and
4402	   threat-model MUST lines for them belong in later revisions (-10 or
4403	   later), written with the same discipline as this -09.

4405	19.1.  Re-attestation profile

4407	   Algorithms retire.  A Spend Receipt or checkpoint signed under
4408	   Ed25519 today may need a later verifier that no longer accepts -19.
4409	   A companion seed [REATTEST] sketches re-attestation: register the
4410	   original COSE bytes as a SCITT Signed Statement and have a current
4411	   algorithm countersign or receipt them.  The principle is that
4412	   structures outlive ciphers.  The first concrete example is the
4413	   profile's own move from generic EdDSA (-8) to Ed25519 (-19) in
4414	   [RFC9864].

4416	19.2.  Streaming reconciliation

4418	   Epoch checkpoints in this document are batch windows.  A later
4419	   revision may define a continuous, second-scale profile [STREAMING] in
4420	   which the same completeness relation is evaluated as settlements
4421	   arrive, without waiting for an epoch close.  That work does not
4422	   change the matching rules in this document, and it did not arrive in
4423	   this revision either.

4425	19.3.  Generalization

4427	   Payment is the special case that this -09 implements.  The same
4428	   completeness calculus (an authenticated extract of consumed units
4429	   reconciled to signed receipts) can apply to other consumable
4430	   resources such as compute, data, or energy.  This document does not
4431	   specify those profiles.

4433	20.  Informative Notes on Adjacent Protocols

4435	   x402 [X402] uses HTTP 402 [RFC9110] to negotiate stablecoin payment.
4436	   AP2 [AP2] uses signed mandates as verifiable credentials.  Cedulon
4437	   does not replace either protocol.  Profiles built on HTTP Message
4438	   Signatures [RFC9421] authenticate bots; they are not a spend receipt.
4439	   draft-bates-atp [BATES-ATP] is a lineage neighbor.  It does not
4440	   define rail-extract completeness.

4442	   draft-vauban-x402-stark-receipts [VAUBAN] specifies complementary
4443	   x402 receipt-format variants that a Cedulon Spend Receipt MAY carry
4444	   as a rail proof; it does not define rail-extract completeness. draft-
4445	   schrock-ep-outcome-binding [SCHROCK] compares authorized action bytes
4446	   to independently observed effects; it does not define rail-extract
4447	   completeness. draft-marques-asqav-compliance-receipts [MARQUES]
4448	   profiles access-control action receipts (the broader Acta family
4449	   includes [ACTA]); it does not define rail-extract completeness.
4450	   draft-hopley-x402-compliance-receipt [HOPLEY] records an admission-
4451	   time compliance decision; it does not define rail-extract
4452	   completeness. draft-abak-agent-control-delivery-evidence [ABAK]
4453	   states evidence requirements for a governance control - stop,
4454	   suspend, revoke - travelling toward the component expected to
4455	   constrain a runtime, and keeps emission, receiver-side observation,
4456	   enforcement outcome and observed control effect as separate results.
4457	   Its object moves the other way from this one: Cedulon reconciles a
4458	   spend that already happened against what the rail reported, and
4459	   evidences neither the delivery of a control instruction nor its
4460	   enforcement.  A denied spend leaves no portable artifact here at all
4461	   - a Decision Token encodes an allow (Section 8) - so what a Cedulon
4462	   audit says about a refusal it says through the settlement that did
4463	   not appear on the extract, which is an effect observation over a
4464	   declared population and not an acknowledgement from an enforcement
4465	   point.  Its bounded-population rule and this document's MUST-T10-18
4466	   and MUST-T10-19 are the same kind of bound on two different objects.

4468	   draft-kuehlewind-audit-architecture [KUEHLEWIND-AUDIT] describes an
4469	   architecture for auditing agent-driven interactions: distributed
4470	   audit records that link user intent, delegation and authorization to
4471	   an execution, propagation of audit context across domains, optional
4472	   attestation under RATS, and registration of the records with a SCITT
4473	   Transparency Service, with financial transactions by agents among its
4474	   motivating cases.  It is the trail a Cedulon audit would be read
4475	   within.  What this document adds for the case of a spend is the
4476	   completeness result over a declared population of an authenticated
4477	   rail extract; the architecture does not define rail-extract
4478	   completeness. draft-birkholz-verifiable-agent-conversations
4479	   [BIRKHOLZ-VAC] defines a COSE-signed record of an agent's
4480	   conversation - session metadata, messages, tool invocations,
4481	   reasoning traces - for the same Transparency Services.  A Spend
4482	   Receipt is the kind of artifact such a record would name for a
4483	   payment step; neither document profiles the other, and the
4484	   conversation record does not define rail-extract completeness.

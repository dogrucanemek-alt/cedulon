11	Abstract

13	   The Cedulon core document reconciles an issuer's signed Spend
14	   Receipts against an authenticated extract of a payment rail and
15	   reports, over a declared population, that no settlement lacks a
16	   receipt and no settled receipt is absent from the rail.  Money is the
17	   special case that document implements.  This document defines a
18	   second population on the same reconciler.  A Decision Record is
19	   signed by the party that decided whether an agent may act; an Effect
20	   Extract is an authenticated list of the effects that actually
21	   occurred on a channel.  An allow must be matched by exactly one
22	   effect whose content hash the record named; a refusal must be matched
23	   by none.  The Decision Record claim set, the Effect Extract shape,
24	   the points at which the reconciliation departs from the spend rules,
25	   the finding codes, and one media type are defined.  This revision
26	   states that the binding compares content and reference and not the
27	   order of two clocks, corrects the boundary to two adjacent documents,
28	   and records the first reading of one frozen fixture by a second,
29	   independently written reader.  The text is provisional; the companion
30	   implementation carrying this profile is published.

104	1.  Introduction

106	   The Cedulon core document [CEDULON] answers one question about an
107	   agent that spends: did every settlement on the rail have a receipt
108	   behind it, and did every settled receipt reach the rail?  It answers
109	   it by closing three signed objects over a declared population: the
110	   issuer's records, an authenticated extract of the counterparty
111	   system, and epoch checkpoints that total the records.  The verifier
112	   holds the keys out of band and the report names the population it
113	   covered.

115	   An agent that acts without spending raises the same question with
116	   different nouns.  A party decided whether the agent may reply, post,
117	   send, or call; a channel carried whatever the agent then did.  Did
118	   every effect on the channel have a decision behind it?  Did every
119	   allowed action occur, once, with the content that was allowed?  Did
120	   anything occur that was refused?  Section 19 of [CEDULON] reserves
121	   later profiles in name only, and its Section 19.3 sketches the same
122	   completeness calculus for other consumable resources: compute, data,
123	   energy.  This document is a different population on the same
124	   reconciler, decisions against effects rather than another unit of
125	   spend, and it is the first profile written out.

127	   The profile keeps the core's three roles and its verification
128	   algorithm.  What changes is the record, the row, the binding between
129	   them, and the words the report uses.  What does not change is
130	   measured: the companion implementation holds the spend behaviour byte
131	   for byte behind a golden file, and every rule in this document that
132	   departs from the spend rules is stated as a departure.

134	   Three documents written at the same time ask adjacent questions about
135	   the same agent, and the boundary between them is worth stating so
136	   that a reader does not take one for another.  This profile reconciles
137	   signed decisions against the effects a channel carried.  [AEB] runs
138	   from the invocation of an action, through its classification, to an
139	   authenticated reconciliation of the effect it produced;
140	   authorization, reservation, and the provider's entry are stages on
141	   that path, not the whole of it.  [OUTCOME] reconciles the exact
142	   action and its source against an observation of the effect whose
143	   source the relying party selects, so the observation is independent
144	   where that party chose an independent source and not otherwise, and
145	   it keeps missing evidence indeterminate rather than resolving it
146	   either way.  [ABAK] covers the delivery and enforcement of a
147	   governance control on its way to one or more enforcement points.  The
148	   finding this profile exists for, an effect that occurred against a
149	   refusal (Section 6.1), establishes that the decision and effect
150	   populations did not reconcile; it does not by itself establish
151	   whether the failure lay in control delivery, enforcement, another
152	   path, or elsewhere.  Where a deployment realizes a refusal through a
153	   downstream control path, the evidence for that path is the question
154	   [ABAK] addresses; where no such path exists, this profile does not
155	   imply one.

157	   This document is a companion to the core document, not a revision of
158	   it.  It is not an IETF working-group item.  Its requirement language
159	   is provisional in the sense Section 19 of [CEDULON] gives the
160	   structures it reserves: a direction written with the core's
161	   discipline, not a commitment, and a later revision may change it.
162	   The companion implementation carrying this profile is published
163	   (Section 11).

165	1.1.  The boundary rule

167	   One rule runs under several choices in this document, and it is
168	   stated here once so that a reader meets the rule before meeting the
169	   places where it was learned.  A state that exists in an
170	   implementation but never crosses the boundary to the party who has to
171	   rely on it does not exist for that party.  A property that a text
172	   does not state is a property the text does not have, whatever the
173	   code underneath does.  In both forms the thing is present in the
174	   model and absent at the boundary, and only the boundary is what
175	   somebody else can rely on.

177	   The rule was learned three times, in three shapes.  A consistency
178	   checker written against nothing of this document's returned a third
179	   verdict, could-not-compare, that its caller still read as a boolean,
180	   so the third value fell into the same bucket as inconsistent until an
181	   ordering rule and a reason string of its own made it observable.  The
182	   companion implementation carried two refusals that it never
183	   delivered: a lock it could not take surfaced as an uncaught exception
184	   rather than as the named refusal the core describes, and a state path
185	   replaced by a link was reported as another writer because the
186	   fingerprint was read before the path was checked.  And this document
187	   did not say whether the binding orders the two clocks until a reader
188	   measured the companion and found the question unanswered
189	   (Section 6.1).  The first two are the state form of the rule; the
190	   third is the text form.

192	   The consequence for this text is that a silence is stated as a
193	   silence.  Where the profile does not order, compare, or enforce
194	   something a reader might reasonably expect it to, the sentence that
195	   says so is written, so that a later reader against a shipped
196	   implementation finds a design note rather than a finding.

198	2.  Terminology

200	   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT",
201	   "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and
202	   "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in
203	   BCP 14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all
204	   capitals, as shown here.

206	   Terms defined in the core document keep their meaning here: Policy
207	   Decision Point, epoch checkpoint, trust root, population, finding,
208	   warning, guarantee.  The following are specific to this profile.

210	   Decider:  The party that decides, per request, whether the agent may
211	      act.  It signs Decision Records and epoch checkpoints over them.
212	      Its key is the issuer root of this profile (Section 7).

214	   Subject:  The party on whose request the decision was taken, named in
215	      the record as an opaque identifier.

217	   Decision Record:  A COSE_Sign1 object signed by the Decider stating
218	      one decision: allow, deny, or defer, with the request it answered,
219	      the policy it applied, and, for an allow, the reference and the
220	      content hash of the effect it allowed (Section 4).

222	   Effect:  One thing that happened on a channel as a result of, or in
223	      the absence of, a decision: a message sent, a post made, a call
224	      placed.  It is identified by a reference, classed by a short name,
225	      and bound by the SHA-256 of its content.

227	   Channel:  The system on which effects occur and from which an Effect
228	      Extract is taken.  It plays the role a rail plays in the core
229	      document.

231	   Effect Extract:  The authenticated list of effects on one channel,
232	      for one Decider, over one window (Section 5).  It plays the role a
233	      rail extract plays in the core document.

235	   Refusal:  A Decision Record whose decision is deny or defer.  A
236	      refusal expects no effect.

238	3.  The population

240	   The core document's reconciler closes an issuer record against a
241	   counterparty row over a declared population.  This profile fills the
242	   same three roles:

244	       +==============+===================+========================+
245	       | Role         | Spend (core)      | Decision (this         |
246	       |              |                   | document)              |
247	       +==============+===================+========================+
248	       | Issuer       | Spend Receipt     | Decision Record        |
249	       | record       |                   |                        |
250	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+
251	       | Counterparty | settlement record | effect row on an       |
252	       | row          | on a rail extract | Effect Extract         |
253	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+
254	       | Match key    | ref               | ref                    |
255	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+
256	       | Content      | amount and        | allow: a row exists    |
257	       | binding      | currency equal    | and effectHash is      |
258	       |              |                   | equal; refusal: no row |
259	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+
260	       | Record that  | outcome aborted   | decision deny or defer |
261	       | expects no   |                   |                        |
262	       | row          |                   |                        |
263	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+
264	       | Aggregate    | checkpoint totals | checkpoint totals per  |
265	       | witness      | per currency      | decision kind          |
266	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+
267	       | Declared     | account, rail,    | decider, channel,      |
268	       | population   | window            | window                 |
269	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+

271	                                  Table 1

273	   Which population a presented document belongs to is the verifier's
274	   call, made by the profile it applies, and never the document's.  A
275	   verifier applying this profile MUST read every presented record as a
276	   Decision Record and every presented extract as an Effect Extract, and
277	   MUST refuse by name a document that does not have that shape; it MUST
278	   NOT infer the population from members a body happens to carry (MUST-
279	   DP-1).  The companion found the alternative wrong in both directions:
280	   a rail extract that added a member named effects was re-routed away
281	   from the spend rules it was subject to, and an Effect Extract handed
282	   to the spend rules crashed before any report existed.  Under this
283	   profile a rail extract is the wrong document and is refused as one;
284	   under the spend rules an Effect Extract is refused the same way.

286	4.  Decision Record

288	   A Decision Record is COSE_Sign1 with the header profile of
289	   Section 6.2 of [CEDULON]: deterministic CBOR, alg -19 (Ed25519,
290	   [RFC9864]), kid mandatory and computed as the core states, an empty
291	   unprotected header refused by name if not empty, and the payload the
292	   CBOR encoding of the claim map below.  The content type header
293	   parameter is application/cedulon-decision-record+cbor (Section 10).

295	4.1.  Claim labels

297	   The labels lie in the Private Use range of the CWT Claims registry
298	   [RFC8392], below the block the core document uses for the Decision
299	   Token (-70301 to -70305) and the countersignature (-70401, -70402),
300	   so that no two Cedulon claim maps share a label.  Every claim
301	   annotated hash carries a SHA-256 [RFC6234] digest rendered as exactly
302	   64 lowercase hexadecimal characters, the grammar of Section 6.1 of
303	   [CEDULON], and a value outside that grammar is refused by name at
304	   signing and at verification.

306	         +========+================+=============================+
307	         | Label  | Claim          | CBOR type                   |
308	         +========+================+=============================+
309	         | -70501 | decider        | tstr                        |
310	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
311	         | -70502 | subject        | tstr                        |
312	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
313	         | -70503 | requestHash    | tstr (hash)                 |
314	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
315	         | -70504 | policyHash     | tstr (hash)                 |
316	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
317	         | -70505 | inputsHash     | tstr (hash) / null          |
318	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
319	         | -70506 | decision       | tstr (allow / deny / defer) |
320	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
321	         | -70507 | reasonCode     | tstr                        |
322	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
323	         | -70508 | ref            | tstr / null                 |
324	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
325	         | -70509 | effectHash     | tstr (hash) / null          |
326	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
327	         | -70510 | timestampMs    | uint                        |
328	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
329	         | -70511 | nonce          | tstr                        |
330	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
331	         | -70512 | prevRecordHash | tstr (hash) / null          |
332	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
333	         | -70513 | effectClass    | tstr / null                 |
334	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+

336	                                  Table 2

338	   All thirteen labels are always present; a nullable claim carries CBOR
339	   null when it has no value.  The thirteenth label is new in this
340	   revision; a record with twelve is refused at verification as a claim
341	   set that does not have this shape, and the companion carries no
342	   records signed under the earlier set outside its own fixtures.

344	   decider and subject are opaque identifiers chosen by the deployment.
345	   requestHash is the SHA-256 of the request the Decider evaluated.  The
346	   encoding is fixed: the canonical encoding of Section 7 of [CEDULON]
347	   when the request is a JSON document, and its UTF-8 octets when it is
348	   text.  The request's fields are not fixed by this document, and a
349	   deployment MUST state what it hashes. policyHash is the SHA-256 of
350	   the canonical policy document the Decider applied. inputsHash, when
351	   not null, is the SHA-256 of whatever further context the Decider
352	   consulted, encoded the same way, so that a later reader can tell two
353	   decisions on the same request apart by what else was on the table.
354	   reasonCode is a short token the deployment defines; it is carried,
355	   not interpreted.

357	   ref is the reference under which the allowed effect will appear on
358	   the channel, and the key on which the reconciliation matches.
359	   effectHash is the SHA-256 of the content of the effect the Decider
360	   allowed, over the octets the channel will carry: for a text reply,
361	   the UTF-8 octets of the text.  The Effect Extract computes the same
362	   digest over the same octets (Section 5), so equality of the two is
363	   equality of content.

365	   effectClass is the class of the effect the Decider allowed, a short
366	   name in the vocabulary the channel defines, such as a reply or a
367	   post.  An Effect Extract row carries the same claim in the same
368	   vocabulary (Section 5.1), so equality of the two is equality of
369	   class.  The class is under the Decider's signature so that what the
370	   Decider allowed cannot be read as one class by one reader and another
371	   by the next without changing what was signed; the earlier revision
372	   carried it on the row only and named the gap.

374	   timestampMs is the decision time in POSIX milliseconds. nonce
375	   identifies the record. prevRecordHash links records into the
376	   Decider's chain: it is the SHA-256 of the previous record's
377	   COSE_Sign1 octets, the same input the core's receiptHash takes on the
378	   COSE path (Section 7.1 of [CEDULON]), or null for the first record of
379	   a chain.

381	4.2.  Claim rules

383	   A signer MUST refuse to sign, and a verifier MUST reject, a claim set
384	   that breaks any of the following, naming the rule in the refusal
385	   (MUST-DP-2):

387	   *  decision is one of allow, deny, defer.

389	   *  Every hash-annotated claim that is not null matches the hash
390	      grammar.

392	   *  timestampMs is a non-negative integer of magnitude at most 2^53 -
393	      1, the uint the label table states; a CBOR decoder hands back any
394	      number, and the rule is what makes the table true.

396	   *  An allow carries a non-empty ref and a non-null effectHash.  An
397	      allow that names no effect is a decision the reconciliation cannot
398	      close, and an allow that names no reference is one it cannot find.

400	   *  An allow carries a non-empty effectClass.  An allow that names no
401	      class is one whose effect could be matched by a row of any class
402	      under the same reference and content, which is the substitution
403	      Section 8 names.

405	   *  A refusal MAY carry an effectClass: it names the class of what was
406	      refused, and it is carried, not measured.  A refusal binds to the
407	      absence of a row of any class.

409	   *  A refusal carries effectHash null.  A refusal binds to the absence
410	      of an effect, never to a content hash, so a hash on a refusal
411	      would be a claim the audit cannot measure and a second reading of
412	      whether the effect occurred.  A refusal MAY carry a ref: it names
413	      what was refused, and an effect appearing under that reference is
414	      the worst finding this profile has (Section 6.3).

416	   The verifier MUST apply these rules itself, on the claim map it
417	   decoded from the signed payload, and MUST NOT rely on the signer
418	   having applied them (MUST-DP-3).  The Decider is the party under
419	   audit.  A Decider that signed a well-formed COSE_Sign1 over a claim
420	   map that skips a rule has produced an object whose signature
421	   verifies, and a verifier that checked only the signature and the
422	   equality of the decoded map with the presented claims would attest
423	   it.  The companion implementation did exactly that until it was
424	   measured: an allow with no reference, signed below the signer's own
425	   rules under the pinned decider key, verified true, was attested, was
426	   counted as unmatched, and the audit still said the books balanced.
427	   The rules now run at both ends.

429	4.3.  Presentation and confusion

431	   A Decision Record is presented as Section 6.3 of [CEDULON] states for
432	   the core's COSE objects: the signed octets, the decoded claim set,
433	   and the Decider's public key as a SubjectPublicKeyInfo PEM beside
434	   them.  The carried key is not an identity source.  Under a pinned
435	   decider key a record that verifies under the pin while carrying
436	   another key is reported as carried-key-mismatch, a warning, and stays
437	   attested; with no pin held the signature check that runs against the
438	   carried key says the record is internally consistent and nothing
439	   about who signed it.

441	   A Decision Record is not a Decision Token.  The core's Decision Token
442	   (Section 8 of [CEDULON]) is the portable encoding of a PDP allow,
443	   carried by the party that will spend; a Decision Record is the
444	   Decider's own log of what it decided, kept for audit, and it exists
445	   for refusals as well.  The two carry different content types and
446	   different claim maps.  A verifier MUST reject a Decision Record whose
447	   content type is not application/cedulon-decision-record+cbor, and
448	   MUST reject a token presented as a record or a record presented as a
449	   token, on the content type, before the signature is checked and
450	   before any claim is read (MUST-DP-4).

452	4.4.  The Decider's chain and checkpoints

454	   Decision Records chain on prevRecordHash the way Spend Receipts chain
455	   on prevReceiptHash, and the Decider signs epoch checkpoints over them
456	   with the checkpoint claim set of Section 11.1 of [CEDULON] unchanged:
457	   receiptCount is the number of records in the window, chainHeadHash is
458	   the SHA-256 of the last record's COSE_Sign1 octets, and totals is a
459	   map from the three decision kinds to decimal counts, {"allow": n,
460	   "deny": n, "defer": n}, each rendered as a text string as the core
461	   renders its currency totals.  A verifier compares the totals it
462	   computes over the attested records in the window against the signed
463	   map, and a difference is checkpoint-total-mismatch as in the core.

465	   Two records that claim the same position in a chain cannot both link
466	   to it: the second record's prevRecordHash must be the first's hash,
467	   so a Decider that signs two decisions under one nonce, or presents
468	   one record twice to the same reader, breaks its own chain and the
469	   walk names the break.  A verifier MUST walk the chain over every
470	   presented record that carries the pinned decider key, not only over
471	   the records that verified, so that a record that claims the pin and
472	   fails the rules is named by the walk rather than dropped from the
473	   population without a word (MUST-DP-5).

475	   What the walk establishes is bounded by what one reader holds.  A
476	   Decider can sign two successors to the same predecessor and show one
477	   branch to one reader and the other branch to another; each reader
478	   walks a linear chain that verifies, and neither walk names a break.
479	   The earlier revision called the chain the equivocation control of
480	   this profile, which overstated it and contradicted the core:
481	   Section 11 of [CEDULON] states that the presented chain alone cannot
482	   satisfy the equivocation requirement, because its epochs are
483	   consecutive by construction, and that the comparison which reaches a
484	   fork is between the presented checkpoints and the copies a witness
485	   recorded (MUST-T11-3).  That rule applies to this profile unchanged.
486	   The Decider's epoch checkpoints are the Signed Statements the witness
487	   records, the verifier compares the witness's copies against the
488	   presented chain, and two verified checkpoints for one epoch with
489	   different hashes are equivocation.  Where no witness was consulted,
490	   the chain controls equivocation within the population one reader
491	   holds and nothing beyond it, and a report MUST NOT present the chain
492	   as settling more than that (the core's MUST-T11-9, with the nouns
493	   renamed).

495	5.  Effect Extract Profile

497	   A verifier checks completeness against an Effect Extract, not against
498	   the Decider's own records alone.  The extract is the channel's
499	   account of what happened, obtained independently of the Decider, and
500	   the profile is only as strong as that independence (Section 7).

502	5.1.  Body and row schema

504	   The extract body is one JSON document with exactly this shape:

506	      +===============+=============================================+
507	      | Member        | JSON type                                   |
508	      +===============+=============================================+
509	      | deciderId     | string (non-empty)                          |
510	      +---------------+---------------------------------------------+
511	      | channelId     | string (non-empty)                          |
512	      +---------------+---------------------------------------------+
513	      | windowStartMs | number (POSIX milliseconds, a safe integer) |
514	      +---------------+---------------------------------------------+
515	      | windowEndMs   | number (POSIX milliseconds, a safe integer, |
516	      |               | greater than windowStartMs)                 |
517	      +---------------+---------------------------------------------+
518	      | effects       | array of effect rows                        |
519	      +---------------+---------------------------------------------+

521	                                  Table 3

523	   Each effect row is a JSON object with exactly these members:

525	   +=============+====================================================+
526	   | Member      | JSON type                                          |
527	   +=============+====================================================+
528	   | ref         | string (non-empty; the reference the Decision      |
529	   |             | Record named)                                      |
530	   +-------------+----------------------------------------------------+
531	   | effectHash  | string (SHA-256 of the effect's content, 64        |
532	   |             | lowercase hex)                                     |
533	   +-------------+----------------------------------------------------+
534	   | effectClass | string (non-empty; a short class name the channel  |
535	   |             | defines, such as a reply or a post, in the         |
536	   |             | vocabulary the Decision Record's effectClass uses) |
537	   +-------------+----------------------------------------------------+
538	   | timestampMs | number (POSIX milliseconds, a safe integer, inside |
539	   |             | the window)                                        |
540	   +-------------+----------------------------------------------------+
541	   | actor       | string (optional; the party the effect reached)    |
542	   +-------------+----------------------------------------------------+

544	                                 Table 4

546	   These member names are normative.  The body and its rows follow the
547	   core's rail extract (Section 9 of [CEDULON]) in every rule that
548	   document states for a JSON body: the text is read for a repeated
549	   member name before it is parsed and refused as json-duplicate-key;
550	   integers are safe integers; the window is half-open and MUST end
551	   after it starts; a missing member, a wrong type, an empty identifier,
552	   or a hash outside the grammar is refused by name at both ends, by the
553	   signer before it signs and by the verifier before it checks a
554	   signature.

556	   The profile departs from the rail extract at two points, and a reader
557	   who knows the core should note both (MUST-DP-6):

559	   *  A member this document does not name is refused, on the body and
560	      on a row.  The core lets a rail add members of its own because a
561	      rail is a system the profile does not control; an Effect Extract
562	      is produced by a process the deployment does control (Section 7),
563	      and the companion measured what a free member can do to a
564	      population (Section 3).  A later revision may open this once a
565	      channel that needs its own members is measured.

567	   *  A row whose timestampMs falls outside [windowStartMs, windowEndMs)
568	      makes the whole extract malformed, refused as effect-outside-
569	      window before any signature is checked.  The core accepts such a
570	      rail extract and names the row (extract-scope-mismatch).  Here the
571	      extract is the deployment's own document and a window it did not
572	      keep is a document it did not produce correctly: a signer applying
573	      this schema never produces such an extract, and a verifier refuses
574	      one that is presented as a document, whatever else it may also
575	      name about its rows.  The trade is stated so it can be reversed: a
576	      single row out of place fails the whole window closed.

578	   effectHash on a row is computed by the extract's signer over the same
579	   octets a Decider hashes for its effectHash claim: the content as the
580	   channel carried it.  A deployment MUST state those octets once for
581	   both sides; the companion's example channel hashes the UTF-8 octets
582	   of the message text.

584	5.2.  Authentication and scope

586	   The extract is signed the way a rail extract is signed: Ed25519
587	   [RFC8032] over the UTF-8 octets of the [RFC8785] encoding of the
588	   body, with the signature as base64 and the signer's public key as a
589	   SubjectPublicKeyInfo PEM beside the body, neither inside the signed
590	   octets.  It is a JSON document with a detached signature, not a COSE
591	   object, and like the rail extract it has no media type.  The earlier
592	   revision gave as the reason that the core registers names only for
593	   objects whose content type is checked inside a protected header; that
594	   test decides what a name must be bound to, not whether a
595	   representation needs one, and it is withdrawn as the reason.  The
596	   reason is Section 3: which population a presented document belongs to
597	   is the verifier's call, made by the profile it applies and by the
598	   decider, channel, and window it declares (MUST-DP-1, MUST-DP-7), and
599	   never the document's.  That declaration is the typed outer context a
600	   media type would otherwise supply.  A name on the extract would be a
601	   self-description the verifier is told not to select on, and this
602	   document does not register one that its own rule forbids relying on.
603	   The day an extract is itself wrapped as a Signed Statement and needs
604	   a content type in a protected header, an application/cedulon-effect-
605	   extract+json registration is the name to make, without changing the
606	   verifier's rule; the extract is not recorded with a witness today.
607	   Section 9.3 of [CEDULON] applies unchanged: a signature proves
608	   internal consistency and not origin; the verifier MUST hold the
609	   extract signer's key out of band and MUST compare keys as
610	   SubjectPublicKeyInfo DER; with no key held the guarantee is
611	   conditional and unauthenticated-extract is reported; with a key held,
612	   an extract that does not verify under it is extract-key-mismatch and
613	   its rows are not reconciled (settlement-comparison-skipped, the
614	   core's name for the same condition).

616	   The extract is scoped to one Decider, one channel, and one window,
617	   and Section 9.4 of [CEDULON] applies with the nouns renamed: a
618	   verifier that knows which Decider, channel, and window it audits MUST
619	   check the extract against them and MUST fail closed on a mismatch
620	   (extract-scope-mismatch); one that has not stated the window MUST
621	   report unstated-audit-window, one that has not stated the Decider or
622	   the channel MUST report unstated-audit-scope, and in either case the
623	   guarantee is conditional.  The strongest line this profile can print,
624	   a balanced audit under an unconditional guarantee, is true of one
625	   Decider, on one channel, over one window, and a report that carries
626	   it MUST also carry those three (MUST-DP-7).

628	6.  Reconciliation

630	   The verification algorithm of Section 11.4 of [CEDULON] runs
631	   unchanged over this population: establish the subject, verify the
632	   extract, check scope, resolve records against the decider root, walk
633	   the chain, index both sides by ref, match, decode and walk the
634	   checkpoints, consult the witness if one is supplied, and decide.
635	   This section states only what the algorithm reads differently.

637	6.1.  What binds

639	   A Decision Record expects a row when its decision is allow, and
640	   expects none when it is a refusal.  For a ref that appears once on
641	   each side:

643	   *  an allow and a row bind when the row's effectHash equals the
644	      record's effectHash and the row's effectClass equals the record's
645	      effectClass; a difference in the hash is effect-mismatch (the
646	      content that occurred is not the content that was allowed), and a
647	      difference in the class with the hash equal is effect-class-
648	      mismatch (the content that was allowed occurred as something
649	      else).  The hash is compared first; a row that differs in both is
650	      reported for its content;

652	   *  an allow with no row is decision-without-effect;

654	   *  a row with no record is effect-without-decision;

656	   *  a row whose ref a refusal names is effect-against-refusal.

658	   The last is the finding this profile exists for.  A spend audit has
659	   no row that should not be there in the same sense: an aborted receipt
660	   that still carries its reference and a settlement under that
661	   reference is reported by the core as a settlement without a receipt,
662	   and it never told the two cases apart.  Here a refusal that was
663	   followed by the effect it refused is a different fact from an effect
664	   nobody decided on, and it has its own name.

666	   A ref that appears more than once on a side is duplicate-ref as in
667	   the core, and the repeating reference is then reconciled by count
668	   rather than by amount: there is nothing to sum.  More rows than
669	   records under one reference is effect-without-decision; more records
670	   than rows is decision-without-effect.

672	   There is no amount, no currency, no manifest, no terms, and no
673	   counterparty axis on this profile.  The core's counterparty-unbound
674	   scope record is not emitted: effectHash binds the content of the
675	   effect itself, which is more than a payee name ever bound on spend,
676	   and actor on a row is carried for the reader, not measured.  The
677	   core's boundary rule applies unchanged: an unmatched item inside the
678	   clock-skew allowance of a window edge is boundary-deferred, and a
679	   closing-edge allow whose ref the following extract names is carried,
680	   not a finding.  The Effect Extract declares no allowance of its own
681	   (Section 5.1); the verifier applies the core's default, which the
682	   companion holds at five minutes.

684	   The binding does not order the two clocks.  A row's timestampMs is
685	   checked against the window it sits in and not against the timestampMs
686	   of the record it answers, so a row dated before the record it binds
687	   to binds as if it had followed it: the profile compares content and
688	   reference, not sequence.  The core's spend binding is the same, and
689	   neither document has claimed otherwise; the gap is named here because
690	   a reader of the companion measured it and found it unstated.  A
691	   deployment that needs the order can compare the two stamps within the
692	   same allowance; this revision names no finding for that comparison,
693	   and a later revision may.  The paragraph is the text form of the rule
694	   in Section 1.1: the silence costs a sentence here, and would cost a
695	   finding later.

697	6.2.  Conservation

699	   With |R| the in-scope Decision Records and |E| the effect rows, the
700	   identities the core report publishes hold with the words changed:

702	   |R|      = refusals + allows
703	   refusals = deny + defer
704	   allows   = matched + deferred + carried
705	              + unmatched + repeated + unreconciled
706	   |E|      = matched + deferred
707	              + unmatched + repeated + unreconciled
708	   matched on |R| equals matched on |E|

710	   A report under this profile MUST publish these counts and MUST name
711	   the population they were computed over (MUST-DP-8), for the reason
712	   the core gives: a report whose counts do not close is a report that
713	   lost a record somewhere, and a reader is entitled to see that without
714	   re-running the audit.  The report publishes refusals as one count,
715	   the core's aborted; the split of that count into deny and defer is
716	   the checkpoint's totals (Section 4.4), not a counter of the report.

718	6.3.  Finding codes

722	   core's codes.  Five are new to this profile:

778	   Counter names in a returned structure are diagnostic and MAY keep the
779	   core's names.

781	7.  Trust roots

783	   This profile has two roots, filling the core's issuer root and rail
784	   root (Sections 10.1 and 9.3 of [CEDULON]):

786	   The decider root:  The key under which Decision Records and their
787	      checkpoints are attested.  Everything Section 10.1 of the core
788	      states for the issuer root applies: a pinned key attests by
789	      signature, a carried key is not an identity, a record under
790	      another key is issuer-key-mismatch and covers nothing, and with no
791	      pin unauthenticated-issuer makes the guarantee conditional.

793	   The effect-extract root:  The key under which the Effect Extract is
794	      attested.  Everything Section 9.3 of the core states for the rail
795	      key applies.

797	   The core's payee, witness, decision-token, and manifest roots are not
798	   used by this profile, except that a transparency witness MAY hold the
799	   Decider's checkpoints exactly as it holds a Receipt Issuer's, with
800	   the witness root and codes of the core unchanged.

802	   What the two roots do not cover is the relation between them.  The
803	   profile's claim is only as strong as the independence of the party
804	   that signs the Effect Extract from the party that signs the Decision
805	   Records.  Where the channel operator signs an export of its own log,
806	   the extract root is that operator's key and the independence is the
807	   operator's.  Where the channel operator signs nothing, which is the
808	   common case for a messaging platform, the extract is produced by a
809	   capture process the deployment runs, and the deployment is stating,
810	   by pinning that process's key, that the process is not the Decider
811	   and cannot be told what to omit.  The rule is not that the two roots
812	   be independent; it is that the deployment say which case it is in,
813	   and that the guarantee fall when independence is absent or unknown.
814	   A deployment MUST state which of the two it has, and a verifier MUST
815	   treat the guarantee as conditional where the extract root and the
816	   decider root are, or may be, the same party (MUST-DP-9).  The
817	   companion cannot measure that from the keys alone; two keys can be
818	   held by one hand.

820	8.  Security Considerations

822	   The core's threat analysis (Section 16 of [CEDULON]) applies where
823	   the nouns carry over: forgery and repudiation of a signed record
824	   (T4), key leakage (T7), and suppression of checkpoints (T11) are the
825	   same threats against a Decider that they are against a Receipt
826	   Issuer, and the controls are the same.  The threats below are the
827	   ones this population adds or sharpens.

829	8.1.  D1: An effect occurs against a refusal

831	   The agent, or something acting through its channel access, does what
832	   the Decider refused.  This is the threat the profile exists for.  A
833	   Decision Record for the refusal, with the reference it refused, and
834	   an authenticated extract that carries an effect under that reference
835	   make the event effect-against-refusal, and the audit fails.  A
836	   refusal that carried no reference cannot support this finding; the
837	   effect is then effect-without-decision, which also fails the audit
838	   but does not say it was refused.  A Decider SHOULD carry the
839	   reference on a refusal whenever the channel assigns one before the
840	   decision.

842	   The shape of the threat is not particular to this profile.  A reader
843	   who mapped this document's objects onto a second evidence format,
844	   with a signed decision predicate and a signed outcome predicate of
845	   its own, measured that format's verifier on 4 September 2026: a
846	   decision carrying a refusal and an outcome recording execution,
847	   citing that decision by content root, verified clean with no warning,
848	   because the verdict is not read on the outcome path [B7N0DE].  That
849	   is D1 as a verifying pair rather than a finding, recorded by the
850	   reader as a measurement of that artefact and cited here as one.

852	8.2.  D2: Effects without decisions

854	   Something acts on the channel that never asked.  Every such effect is
855	   effect-without-decision.  The control is the extract's completeness,
856	   which is the extract root's independence (Section 7); a capture
857	   process the Decider controls can leave the effect out.

859	8.3.  D3: Substitution of content

861	   The Decider allows one content and the channel carries another.  The
862	   allow's effectHash and the row's effectHash are computed over the
863	   same octets, and a difference is effect-mismatch.  The control fails
864	   open if the two sides hash different octets, which is why a
865	   deployment MUST state the octets once for both (Section 5.1).

867	8.4.  D4: The Decider signs below its own rules

869	   A Decider produces a well-formed signature over a claim map that
870	   breaks a rule this profile states: an allow with no reference or no
871	   content hash, a refusal with a content hash, a hash outside the
872	   grammar.  Every such record is a record the reconciliation cannot
873	   close or would close wrongly.  The verifier applies the rules on the
874	   decoded payload (MUST-DP-3) and walks the chain over every record
875	   that claims the pin (MUST-DP-5), so the record is refused and named
876	   rather than attested or dropped.

878	8.5.  D5: Equivocation on the record chain

880	   The Decider signs two decisions for one request, or two successors to
881	   one predecessor, and offers each to a different reader.  Within one
882	   reader's population the chain (MUST-DP-5) makes the second record
883	   unlinkable: it names the same predecessor as the first, or none, and
884	   the walk reports the break.  Across readers the chain sees nothing:
885	   each holds a linear chain that verifies.  The control for that case
886	   is the core's witness (Section 4.4): the Decider's epoch checkpoints
887	   are recorded with a witness the verifier has pinned, a Transparency
888	   Service [RFC9943] being one, and the copies the witness holds are
889	   compared against the presented chain.  A verifier that consulted no
890	   witness has not measured cross-reader equivocation.  The guarantee
891	   its report prints is the core's, which is defined over the extract,
892	   the pins, and the window and does not cover suppression or
893	   equivocation beyond the presented chain when no witness was consulted
894	   (Section 11 of [CEDULON], MUST-T11-9); measured on the companion,
895	   such a report prints unconditional with no warning and no finding,
896	   and is silent about the witness it was not given.  The silence is
897	   this revision's known gap: the claim is narrowed here, in the text,
898	   and a report line that names an unconsulted witness is a change to
899	   the reconciler both populations share, not made in this revision.  A
900	   deployment that needs the property records its checkpoints with a
901	   witness and gives its readers the witness key.

903	8.6.  D6: The class of the effect is substituted

905	   A row of a different class under the same reference and the same
906	   content hash: the same text allowed as a reply and carried as a post.
907	   The earlier revision named this as a gap, because the record carried
908	   no claim for the class and a row of any class matched.  The record
909	   now carries effectClass under the Decider's signature (Section 4.1),
910	   an allow without one is refused (Section 4.2), and a row whose class
911	   differs from the allow's with the content hash equal is effect-class-
912	   mismatch (Section 6.1).  What remains open is the vocabulary: the
913	   class names are the channel's, this document does not fix them, and a
914	   deployment SHOULD state them beside its statement of what it hashes
915	   so that two readers compare the same words.

917	8.7.  D7: Silent defaults in capture

919	   The process that turns a channel's log into Decision Records or
920	   Effect Extract rows fills a missing value with a default, and the
921	   default hashes to something.  An allow with no stated content that is
922	   hashed as the empty string produces a record that will match an empty
923	   effect and mismatch every real one, with no finding that says the
924	   content was never stated.  Such a process MUST refuse the line by
925	   name rather than fill it (MUST-DP-10).  The companion's example
926	   adapter did fill it until it was measured, and refuses it now.

928	8.8.  D8: The capture process is the Decider

930	   The party that produces the Effect Extract is, or answers to, the
931	   party that signed the Decision Records.  Every finding in D1 and D2
932	   can then be made to disappear by omission, and no signature check
933	   detects it.  This is the independence statement of Section 7 (MUST-
934	   DP-9), and it is a deployment fact the profile can name but not
935	   prove.  A verifier that holds both roots and cannot state their
936	   independence has a conditional result, and MUST say so.

938	9.  Privacy Considerations

940	   A Decision Record carries no request content and no effect content:
941	   hashes of both, an opaque subject identifier, and a reason code.  The
942	   Effect Extract carries a reference, a class, a content hash, a time,
943	   and optionally the identifier of the party the effect reached.  The
944	   core's Privacy Considerations (Section 15 of [CEDULON]) apply to what
945	   a transparency witness is given.

947	   Two points are specific to this population.  A content hash over a
948	   short text is a fingerprint of that text: a reader who can guess the
949	   message can confirm the guess.  This revision hashes the plain
950	   content octets, as the companion does, so that the two sides need no
951	   shared secret to agree; a keyed or salted construction that would
952	   defeat the guess is a claim-set change and is not defined here.  A
953	   deployment whose effects are short and guessable SHOULD treat the
954	   extract and the records as confidential to the audit.  The subject
955	   and actor identifiers are opaque to the profile but need not be
956	   opaque to a reader; a deployment SHOULD pseudonymize them before
957	   either object leaves its control.

1011	      every Cedulon object.  A Decision Record and a Decision Token are
1012	      distinct objects with distinct content types and are never
1013	      accepted for one another.

11	Abstract

13	   The Cedulon core document reconciles an issuer's signed Spend
14	   Receipts against an authenticated extract of a payment rail and
15	   reports, over a declared population, that no settlement lacks a
16	   receipt and no settled receipt is absent from the rail.  Money is the
17	   special case that document implements.  This document defines a
18	   second population on the same reconciler.  A Decision Record is
19	   signed by the party that decided whether an agent may act; an Effect
20	   Extract is an authenticated list of the effects that actually
21	   occurred on a channel.  An allow must be matched by exactly one
22	   effect whose content hash the record named; a refusal must be matched
23	   by none.  The Decision Record claim set, the Effect Extract shape,
24	   the points at which the reconciliation departs from the spend rules,
25	   the finding codes, and one media type are defined.  This revision
26	   states that the binding compares content and reference and not the
27	   order of two clocks, corrects the boundary to two adjacent documents,
28	   and records the first reading of one frozen fixture by a second,
29	   independently written reader.  The text is provisional; the companion
30	   implementation carrying this profile is published.

105	1.  Introduction

107	   The Cedulon core document [CEDULON] answers one question about an
108	   agent that spends: did every settlement on the rail have a receipt
109	   behind it, and did every settled receipt reach the rail?  It answers
110	   it by closing three signed objects over a declared population: the
111	   issuer's records, an authenticated extract of the counterparty
112	   system, and epoch checkpoints that total the records.  The verifier
113	   holds the keys out of band and the report names the population it
114	   covered.

116	   An agent that acts without spending raises the same question with
117	   different nouns.  A party decided whether the agent may reply, post,
118	   send, or call; a channel carried whatever the agent then did.  Did
119	   every effect on the channel have a decision behind it?  Did every
120	   allowed action occur, once, with the content that was allowed?  Did
121	   anything occur that was refused?  Section 19 of [CEDULON] reserves
122	   later profiles in name only, and its Section 19.3 sketches the same
123	   completeness calculus for other consumable resources: compute, data,
124	   energy.  This document is a different population on the same
125	   reconciler, decisions against effects rather than another unit of
126	   spend, and it is the first profile written out.

128	   The profile keeps the core's three roles and its verification
129	   algorithm.  What changes is the record, the row, the binding between
130	   them, and the words the report uses.  What does not change is
131	   measured: the companion implementation holds the spend behaviour byte
132	   for byte behind a golden file, and every rule in this document that
133	   departs from the spend rules is stated as a departure.

135	   Three documents written at the same time ask adjacent questions about
136	   the same agent, and the boundary between them is worth stating so
137	   that a reader does not take one for another.  This profile reconciles
138	   signed decisions against the effects a channel carried.  [AEB] runs
139	   from the invocation of an action, through its classification, to an
140	   authenticated reconciliation of the effect it produced;
141	   authorization, reservation, and the provider's entry are stages on
142	   that path, not the whole of it.  [OUTCOME] reconciles the exact
143	   action and its source against an observation of the effect whose
144	   source the relying party selects, so the observation is independent
145	   where that party chose an independent source and not otherwise, and
146	   it keeps missing evidence indeterminate rather than resolving it
147	   either way.  [ABAK] covers the delivery and enforcement of a
148	   governance control on its way to one or more enforcement points.  The
149	   finding this profile exists for, an effect that occurred against a
150	   refusal (Section 6.1), establishes that the decision and effect
151	   populations did not reconcile; it does not by itself establish
152	   whether the failure lay in control delivery, enforcement, another
153	   path, or elsewhere.  Where a deployment realizes a refusal through a
154	   downstream control path, the evidence for that path is the question
155	   [ABAK] addresses; where no such path exists, this profile does not
156	   imply one.

158	   This document is a companion to the core document, not a revision of
159	   it.  It is not an IETF working-group item.  Its requirement language
160	   is provisional in the sense Section 19 of [CEDULON] gives the
161	   structures it reserves: a direction written with the core's
162	   discipline, not a commitment, and a later revision may change it.
163	   The companion implementation carrying this profile is published
164	   (Section 11).

166	1.1.  The boundary rule

168	   One rule runs under several choices in this document, and it is
169	   stated here once so that a reader meets the rule before meeting the
170	   places where it was learned.  A state that exists in an
171	   implementation but never crosses the boundary to the party who has to
172	   rely on it does not exist for that party.  A property that a text
173	   does not state is a property the text does not have, whatever the
174	   code underneath does.  In both forms the thing is present in the
175	   model and absent at the boundary, and only the boundary is what
176	   somebody else can rely on.

178	   The rule was learned three times, in three shapes.  A consistency
179	   checker written against nothing of this document's returned a third
180	   verdict, could-not-compare, that its caller still read as a boolean,
181	   so the third value fell into the same bucket as inconsistent until an
182	   ordering rule and a reason string of its own made it observable.  The
183	   companion implementation carried two refusals that it never
184	   delivered: a lock it could not take surfaced as an uncaught exception
185	   rather than as the named refusal the core describes, and a state path
186	   replaced by a link was reported as another writer because the
187	   fingerprint was read before the path was checked.  And this document
188	   did not say whether the binding orders the two clocks until a reader
189	   measured the companion and found the question unanswered
190	   (Section 6.1).  The first two are the state form of the rule; the
191	   third is the text form.

193	   The consequence for this text is that a silence is stated as a
194	   silence.  Where the profile does not order, compare, or enforce
195	   something a reader might reasonably expect it to, the sentence that
196	   says so is written, so that a later reader against a shipped
197	   implementation finds a design note rather than a finding.

199	2.  Terminology

201	   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT",
202	   "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and
203	   "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in
204	   BCP 14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all
205	   capitals, as shown here.

207	   Terms defined in the core document keep their meaning here: Policy
208	   Decision Point, epoch checkpoint, trust root, population, finding,
209	   warning, guarantee.  The following are specific to this profile.

211	   Decider:  The party that decides, per request, whether the agent may
212	      act.  It signs Decision Records, and epoch checkpoints over them
213	      unless the deployment names a separate checkpoint key
214	      (Section 4.4).  Its key or keys form the issuer root of this
215	      profile (Section 7).

217	   Subject:  The party on whose request the decision was taken, named in
218	      the record as an opaque identifier.

220	   Decision Record:  A COSE_Sign1 object signed by the Decider stating
221	      one decision: allow, deny, or defer, with the request it answered,
222	      the policy it applied, and, for an allow, the reference and the
223	      content hash of the effect it allowed (Section 4).

225	   Effect:  One thing that happened on a channel as a result of, or in
226	      the absence of, a decision: a message sent, a post made, a call
227	      placed.  It is identified by a reference, classed by a short name,
228	      and bound by the SHA-256 of its content.

230	   Channel:  The system on which effects occur and from which an Effect
231	      Extract is taken.  It plays the role a rail plays in the core
232	      document.

234	   Effect Extract:  The authenticated list of effects on one channel,
235	      for one Decider, over one window (Section 5).  It plays the role a
236	      rail extract plays in the core document.

238	   Refusal:  A Decision Record whose decision is deny or defer.  A
239	      refusal expects no effect.

241	3.  The population

243	   The core document's reconciler closes an issuer record against a
244	   counterparty row over a declared population.  This profile fills the
245	   same three roles:

248	       | Role         | Spend (core)      | Decision (this         |
249	       |              |                   | document)              |
250	       +==============+===================+========================+
251	       | Issuer       | Spend Receipt     | Decision Record        |
252	       | record       |                   |                        |
253	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+
254	       | Counterparty | settlement record | effect row on an       |
255	       | row          | on a rail extract | Effect Extract         |
256	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+

259	       | Content      | amount and        | allow: a row exists    |
260	       | binding      | currency equal    | and effectHash is      |
261	       |              |                   | equal; refusal: no row |
262	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+
263	       | Record that  | outcome aborted   | decision deny or defer |
264	       | expects no   |                   |                        |
265	       | row          |                   |                        |
266	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+
267	       | Aggregate    | checkpoint totals | checkpoint totals per  |

270	       | Declared     | account, rail,    | decider, channel,      |
271	       | population   | window            | window                 |
272	       +--------------+-------------------+------------------------+

274	                                  Table 1

276	   Which population a presented document belongs to is the verifier's
277	   call, made by the profile it applies, and never the document's.  A
278	   verifier applying this profile MUST read every presented record as a
279	   Decision Record and every presented extract as an Effect Extract, and
280	   MUST refuse by name a document that does not have that shape; it MUST
281	   NOT infer the population from members a body happens to carry (MUST-
282	   DP-1).  The companion found the alternative wrong in both directions:
283	   a rail extract that added a member named effects was re-routed away
284	   from the spend rules it was subject to, and an Effect Extract handed
285	   to the spend rules crashed before any report existed.  Under this
286	   profile a rail extract is the wrong document and is refused as one;
287	   under the spend rules an Effect Extract is refused the same way.

289	4.  Decision Record

291	   A Decision Record is COSE_Sign1 with the header profile of
292	   Section 6.2 of [CEDULON]: deterministic CBOR, alg -19 (Ed25519,
293	   [RFC9864]), kid mandatory and computed as the core states, an empty
294	   unprotected header refused by name if not empty, and the payload the
295	   CBOR encoding of the claim map below.  The content type header
296	   parameter is application/cedulon-decision-record+cbor (Section 10).

298	4.1.  Claim labels

300	   The labels lie in the Private Use range of the CWT Claims registry
301	   [RFC8392], below the block the core document uses for the Decision
302	   Token (-70301 to -70305) and the countersignature (-70401, -70402),
303	   so that no two Cedulon claim maps share a label.  Every claim
304	   annotated hash carries a SHA-256 [RFC6234] digest rendered as exactly
305	   64 lowercase hexadecimal characters, the grammar of Section 6.1 of
306	   [CEDULON], and a value outside that grammar is refused by name at
307	   signing and at verification.

309	         +========+================+=============================+
310	         | Label  | Claim          | CBOR type                   |
311	         +========+================+=============================+
312	         | -70501 | decider        | tstr                        |
313	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
314	         | -70502 | subject        | tstr                        |
315	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
316	         | -70503 | requestHash    | tstr (hash)                 |
317	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
318	         | -70504 | policyHash     | tstr (hash)                 |
319	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
320	         | -70505 | inputsHash     | tstr (hash) / null          |
321	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
322	         | -70506 | decision       | tstr (allow / deny / defer) |
323	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
324	         | -70507 | reasonCode     | tstr                        |
325	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
326	         | -70508 | ref            | tstr / null                 |
327	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
328	         | -70509 | effectHash     | tstr (hash) / null          |
329	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
330	         | -70510 | timestampMs    | uint                        |
331	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
332	         | -70511 | nonce          | tstr                        |
333	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
334	         | -70512 | prevRecordHash | tstr (hash) / null          |
335	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+
336	         | -70513 | effectClass    | tstr / null                 |
337	         +--------+----------------+-----------------------------+

339	                                  Table 2

341	   All thirteen labels are always present; a nullable claim carries CBOR
342	   null when it has no value.  The thirteenth label is new in this
343	   revision; a record with twelve is refused at verification as a claim
344	   set that does not have this shape, and the companion carries no
345	   records signed under the earlier set outside its own fixtures.

347	   decider and subject are opaque identifiers chosen by the deployment.
348	   requestHash is the SHA-256 of the request the Decider evaluated.  The
349	   encoding is fixed: the canonical encoding of Section 7 of [CEDULON]
350	   when the request is a JSON document, and its UTF-8 octets when it is
351	   text.  The request's fields are not fixed by this document, and a
352	   deployment MUST state what it hashes. policyHash is the SHA-256 of
353	   the canonical policy document the Decider applied. inputsHash, when
354	   not null, is the SHA-256 of whatever further context the Decider
355	   consulted, encoded the same way, so that a later reader can tell two
356	   decisions on the same request apart by what else was on the table.

358	   As for the request, the fields of that context are not fixed by this
359	   document, and a deployment MUST state what it hashes and how it
360	   presents the hashed context beside the record. reasonCode is a short
361	   token the deployment defines; it is carried, not interpreted.

363	   ref is the reference under which the allowed effect will appear on
364	   the channel, and the key on which the reconciliation matches.
365	   effectHash is the SHA-256 of the content of the effect the Decider
366	   allowed, over the octets the channel will carry: for a text reply,
367	   the UTF-8 octets of the text.  The Effect Extract computes the same
368	   digest over the same octets (Section 5), so equality of the two is
369	   equality of content.

371	   effectClass is the class of the effect the Decider allowed, a short
372	   name in the vocabulary the channel defines, such as a reply or a
373	   post.  An Effect Extract row carries the same claim in the same
374	   vocabulary (Section 5.1), so equality of the two is equality of
375	   class.  The class is under the Decider's signature so that what the
376	   Decider allowed cannot be read as one class by one reader and another
377	   by the next without changing what was signed; the earlier revision
378	   carried it on the row only and named the gap.

380	   timestampMs is the decision time in POSIX milliseconds. nonce
381	   identifies the record. prevRecordHash links records into the
382	   Decider's chain: it is the SHA-256 of the previous record's
383	   COSE_Sign1 octets, the same input the core's receiptHash takes on the
384	   COSE path (Section 7.1 of [CEDULON]), or null for the first record of
385	   a chain.

387	4.2.  Claim rules

389	   A signer MUST refuse to sign, and a verifier MUST reject, a claim set
390	   that breaks any of the following, naming the rule in the refusal
391	   (MUST-DP-2):

393	   *  decision is one of allow, deny, defer.

395	   *  Every hash-annotated claim that is not null matches the hash
396	      grammar.

398	   *  timestampMs is a non-negative integer of magnitude at most 2^53 -
399	      1, the uint the label table states; a CBOR decoder hands back any
400	      number, and the rule is what makes the table true.

402	   *  An allow carries a non-empty ref and a non-null effectHash.  An
403	      allow that names no effect is a decision the reconciliation cannot
404	      close, and an allow that names no reference is one it cannot find.

406	   *  An allow carries a non-empty effectClass.  An allow that names no
407	      class is one whose effect could be matched by a row of any class
408	      under the same reference and content, which is the substitution
409	      Section 8 names.

411	   *  A refusal MAY carry an effectClass: it names the class of what was
412	      refused, and it is carried, not measured.  A refusal binds to the
413	      absence of a row of any class.

415	   *  A refusal carries effectHash null.  A refusal binds to the absence
416	      of an effect, never to a content hash, so a hash on a refusal
417	      would be a claim the audit cannot measure and a second reading of
418	      whether the effect occurred.  A refusal MAY carry a ref: it names
419	      what was refused, and an effect appearing under that reference is
420	      the worst finding this profile has (Section 6.3).

422	   The verifier MUST apply these rules itself, on the claim map it
423	   decoded from the signed payload, and MUST NOT rely on the signer
424	   having applied them (MUST-DP-3).  The Decider is the party under
425	   audit.  A Decider that signed a well-formed COSE_Sign1 over a claim
426	   map that skips a rule has produced an object whose signature
427	   verifies, and a verifier that checked only the signature and the
428	   equality of the decoded map with the presented claims would attest
429	   it.  The companion implementation did exactly that until it was
430	   measured: an allow with no reference, signed below the signer's own
431	   rules under the pinned decider key, verified true, was attested, was
432	   counted as unmatched, and the audit still said the books balanced.
433	   The rules now run at both ends.

435	4.3.  Presentation and confusion

437	   A Decision Record is presented as Section 6.3 of [CEDULON] states for
438	   the core's COSE objects: the signed octets, the decoded claim set,
439	   and the Decider's public key as a SubjectPublicKeyInfo PEM beside
440	   them.  The carried key is not an identity source.  Under a pinned
441	   decider key a record that verifies under the pin while carrying
442	   another key is reported as carried-key-mismatch, a warning, and stays
443	   attested; with no pin held the signature check that runs against the
444	   carried key says the record is internally consistent and nothing
445	   about who signed it.

447	   A Decision Record is not a Decision Token.  The core's Decision Token
448	   (Section 8 of [CEDULON]) is the portable encoding of a PDP allow,
449	   carried by the party that will spend; a Decision Record is the
450	   Decider's own log of what it decided, kept for audit, and it exists
451	   for refusals as well.  The two carry different content types and
452	   different claim maps.  A verifier MUST reject a Decision Record whose
453	   content type is not application/cedulon-decision-record+cbor, and
454	   MUST reject a token presented as a record or a record presented as a
455	   token, on the content type, before the signature is checked and
456	   before any claim is read (MUST-DP-4).

458	4.4.  The Decider's chain and checkpoints

460	   Decision Records chain on prevRecordHash the way Spend Receipts chain
461	   on prevReceiptHash, and epoch checkpoints over them, signed under the
462	   decider root as set out below, carry the checkpoint claim set of
463	   Section 11.1 of [CEDULON] unchanged: receiptCount is the number of
464	   records in the window, chainHeadHash is the SHA-256 of the last
465	   record's COSE_Sign1 octets, and totals is a map from the three
466	   decision kinds to decimal counts, {"allow": n, "deny": n, "defer":
467	   n}, each rendered as a text string as the core renders its currency
468	   totals.  A verifier compares the totals it computes over the attested
469	   records in the window against the signed map, and a difference is
470	   checkpoint-total-mismatch as in the core.

472	   The key that signs a checkpoint is the key the core's verification
473	   procedure obtains for the checkpoint issuer (Section 11.4 of
474	   [CEDULON], step 11), and Section 10.1 of the core has the verifier
475	   check checkpoints against the issuer root.  The core admits a root of
476	   several keys so that a key can be rotated (MUST-T4-12); this profile
477	   adds a split by role.  The decider root holds the key under which
478	   Decision Records are attested and, where the deployment names one, a
479	   separate key under which, of the Decider's objects, only checkpoints
480	   are attested, such as that of a process beside the Decider that signs
481	   no Decision Record.  Where that key also signs effect extracts, the
482	   extract root and the decider root share a key, and MUST-DP-9 makes
483	   the guarantee conditional.  A deployment MUST state which key signs
484	   its checkpoints; a verifier MUST hold that key out of band as part of
485	   the decider root and verify every checkpoint under it, and a Decision
486	   Record that verifies only under the checkpoint key is issuer-key-
487	   mismatch (MUST-DP-11).  A separate checkpoint key held by the same
488	   operator as the decider root is a second key, not a second trust
489	   domain, and a report MUST NOT present it as independent of the
490	   Decider.  A checkpoint signer is not the witness of Section 11 of
491	   [CEDULON], which records presented checkpoints so that a fork can be
492	   reached; naming one does not change that role.

494	   Two records that claim the same position in a chain cannot both link
495	   to it: the second record's prevRecordHash must be the first's hash,
496	   so a Decider that signs two decisions under one nonce, or presents
497	   one record twice to the same reader, breaks its own chain and the
498	   walk names the break.  A verifier MUST walk the chain over every
499	   presented record that carries the pinned decider key, not only over
500	   the records that verified, so that a record that claims the pin and
501	   fails the rules is named by the walk rather than dropped from the
502	   population without a word (MUST-DP-5).

504	   What the walk establishes is bounded by what one reader holds.  A
505	   Decider can sign two successors to the same predecessor and show one
506	   branch to one reader and the other branch to another; each reader
507	   walks a linear chain that verifies, and neither walk names a break.
508	   The earlier revision called the chain the equivocation control of
509	   this profile, which overstated it and contradicted the core:
510	   Section 11 of [CEDULON] states that the presented chain alone cannot
511	   satisfy the equivocation requirement, because its epochs are
512	   consecutive by construction, and that the comparison which reaches a
513	   fork is between the presented checkpoints and the copies a witness
514	   recorded (MUST-T11-3).  That rule applies to this profile unchanged.
515	   The Decider's epoch checkpoints are the Signed Statements the witness
516	   records, the verifier compares the witness's copies against the
517	   presented chain, and two verified checkpoints for one epoch with
518	   different hashes are equivocation.  Where no witness was consulted,
519	   the chain controls equivocation within the population one reader
520	   holds and nothing beyond it, and a report MUST NOT present the chain
521	   as settling more than that (the core's MUST-T11-9, with the nouns
522	   renamed).

524	5.  Effect Extract Profile

526	   A verifier checks completeness against an Effect Extract, not against
527	   the Decider's own records alone.  The extract is the channel's
528	   account of what happened, obtained independently of the Decider, and
529	   the profile is only as strong as that independence (Section 7).

531	5.1.  Body and row schema

533	   The extract body is one JSON document with exactly this shape:

535	      +===============+=============================================+
536	      | Member        | JSON type                                   |
537	      +===============+=============================================+
538	      | deciderId     | string (non-empty)                          |
539	      +---------------+---------------------------------------------+
540	      | channelId     | string (non-empty)                          |
541	      +---------------+---------------------------------------------+
542	      | windowStartMs | number (POSIX milliseconds, a safe integer) |
543	      +---------------+---------------------------------------------+
544	      | windowEndMs   | number (POSIX milliseconds, a safe integer, |
545	      |               | greater than windowStartMs)                 |
546	      +---------------+---------------------------------------------+
547	      | effects       | array of effect rows                        |
548	      +---------------+---------------------------------------------+

550	                                  Table 3

552	   Each effect row is a JSON object with exactly these members:

554	   +=============+====================================================+
555	   | Member      | JSON type                                          |
556	   +=============+====================================================+
557	   | ref         | string (non-empty; the reference the Decision      |
558	   |             | Record named)                                      |
559	   +-------------+----------------------------------------------------+
560	   | effectHash  | string (SHA-256 of the effect's content, 64        |
561	   |             | lowercase hex)                                     |
562	   +-------------+----------------------------------------------------+
563	   | effectClass | string (non-empty; a short class name the channel  |
564	   |             | defines, such as a reply or a post, in the         |
565	   |             | vocabulary the Decision Record's effectClass uses) |
566	   +-------------+----------------------------------------------------+
567	   | timestampMs | number (POSIX milliseconds, a safe integer, inside |
568	   |             | the window)                                        |
569	   +-------------+----------------------------------------------------+
570	   | actor       | string (optional; the party the effect reached)    |
571	   +-------------+----------------------------------------------------+

573	                                 Table 4

575	   These member names are normative.  The body and its rows follow the
576	   core's rail extract (Section 9 of [CEDULON]) in every rule that
577	   document states for a JSON body: the text is read for a repeated
578	   member name before it is parsed and refused as json-duplicate-key;
579	   integers are safe integers; the window is half-open and MUST end
580	   after it starts; a missing member, a wrong type, an empty identifier,
581	   or a hash outside the grammar is refused by name at both ends, by the
582	   signer before it signs and by the verifier before it checks a
583	   signature.

585	   The profile departs from the rail extract at two points, and a reader
586	   who knows the core should note both (MUST-DP-6):

588	   *  A member this document does not name is refused, on the body and
589	      on a row.  The core lets a rail add members of its own because a
590	      rail is a system the profile does not control; an Effect Extract
591	      is produced by a process the deployment does control (Section 7),
592	      and the companion measured what a free member can do to a
593	      population (Section 3).  A later revision may open this once a
594	      channel that needs its own members is measured.

596	   *  A row whose timestampMs falls outside [windowStartMs, windowEndMs)
597	      makes the whole extract malformed, refused as effect-outside-
598	      window before any signature is checked.  The core accepts such a
599	      rail extract and names the row (extract-scope-mismatch).  Here the
600	      extract is the deployment's own document and a window it did not
601	      keep is a document it did not produce correctly: a signer applying
602	      this schema never produces such an extract, and a verifier refuses
603	      one that is presented as a document, whatever else it may also
604	      name about its rows.  The trade is stated so it can be reversed: a
605	      single row out of place fails the whole window closed.

607	   effectHash on a row is computed by the extract's signer over the same
608	   octets a Decider hashes for its effectHash claim: the content as the
609	   channel carried it.  A deployment MUST state those octets once for
610	   both sides; the companion's example channel hashes the UTF-8 octets
611	   of the message text.

613	5.2.  Authentication and scope

615	   The extract is signed the way a rail extract is signed: Ed25519
616	   [RFC8032] over the UTF-8 octets of the [RFC8785] encoding of the
617	   body, with the signature as base64 and the signer's public key as a
618	   SubjectPublicKeyInfo PEM beside the body, neither inside the signed
619	   octets.  It is a JSON document with a detached signature, not a COSE
620	   object, and like the rail extract it has no media type.  The earlier
621	   revision gave as the reason that the core registers names only for
622	   objects whose content type is checked inside a protected header; that
623	   test decides what a name must be bound to, not whether a
624	   representation needs one, and it is withdrawn as the reason.  The
625	   reason is Section 3: which population a presented document belongs to
626	   is the verifier's call, made by the profile it applies and by the
627	   decider, channel, and window it declares (MUST-DP-1, MUST-DP-7), and
628	   never the document's.  That declaration is the typed outer context a
629	   media type would otherwise supply.  A name on the extract would be a
630	   self-description the verifier is told not to select on, and this
631	   document does not register one that its own rule forbids relying on.
632	   The day an extract is itself wrapped as a Signed Statement and needs
633	   a content type in a protected header, an application/cedulon-effect-
634	   extract+json registration is the name to make, without changing the
635	   verifier's rule; the extract is not recorded with a witness today.
636	   Section 9.3 of [CEDULON] applies unchanged: a signature proves
637	   internal consistency and not origin; the verifier MUST hold the
638	   extract signer's key out of band and MUST compare keys as
639	   SubjectPublicKeyInfo DER; with no key held the guarantee is
640	   conditional and unauthenticated-extract is reported; with a key held,
641	   an extract that does not verify under it is extract-key-mismatch and
642	   its rows are not reconciled (settlement-comparison-skipped, the
643	   core's name for the same condition).

645	   The extract is scoped to one Decider, one channel, and one window,
646	   and Section 9.4 of [CEDULON] applies with the nouns renamed: a
647	   verifier that knows which Decider, channel, and window it audits MUST
648	   check the extract against them and MUST fail closed on a mismatch
649	   (extract-scope-mismatch); one that has not stated the window MUST
650	   report unstated-audit-window, one that has not stated the Decider or
651	   the channel MUST report unstated-audit-scope, and in either case the
652	   guarantee is conditional.  The strongest line this profile can print,
653	   a balanced audit under an unconditional guarantee, is true of one
654	   Decider, on one channel, over one window, and a report that carries
655	   it MUST also carry those three (MUST-DP-7).

657	6.  Reconciliation

659	   The verification algorithm of Section 11.4 of [CEDULON] runs
660	   unchanged over this population: establish the subject, verify the
661	   extract, check scope, resolve records against the decider root, walk
662	   the chain, index both sides by ref, match, decode and walk the
663	   checkpoints, consult the witness if one is supplied, and decide.
664	   This section states only what the algorithm reads differently.

666	6.1.  What binds

668	   A Decision Record expects a row when its decision is allow, and
669	   expects none when it is a refusal.  For a ref that appears once on
670	   each side:

672	   *  an allow and a row bind when the row's effectHash equals the
673	      record's effectHash and the row's effectClass equals the record's
674	      effectClass; a difference in the hash is effect-mismatch (the
675	      content that occurred is not the content that was allowed), and a
676	      difference in the class with the hash equal is effect-class-
677	      mismatch (the content that was allowed occurred as something
678	      else).  The hash is compared first; a row that differs in both is
679	      reported for its content;

681	   *  an allow with no row is decision-without-effect;

683	   *  a row with no record is effect-without-decision;

685	   *  a row whose ref a refusal names is effect-against-refusal.

687	   The last is the finding this profile exists for.  A spend audit has
688	   no row that should not be there in the same sense: an aborted receipt
689	   that still carries its reference and a settlement under that
690	   reference is reported by the core as a settlement without a receipt,
691	   and it never told the two cases apart.  Here a refusal that was
692	   followed by the effect it refused is a different fact from an effect
693	   nobody decided on, and it has its own name.

695	   A ref that appears more than once on a side is duplicate-ref as in
696	   the core, and the repeating reference is then reconciled by count
697	   rather than by amount: there is nothing to sum.  More rows than
698	   records under one reference is effect-without-decision; more records
699	   than rows is decision-without-effect.

701	   There is no amount, no currency, no manifest, no terms, and no
702	   counterparty axis on this profile.  The core's counterparty-unbound
703	   scope record is not emitted: effectHash binds the content of the
704	   effect itself, which is more than a payee name ever bound on spend,
705	   and actor on a row is carried for the reader, not measured.  The
706	   core's boundary rule applies unchanged: an unmatched item inside the
707	   clock-skew allowance of a window edge is boundary-deferred, and a
708	   closing-edge allow whose ref the following extract names is carried,
709	   not a finding.  The Effect Extract declares no allowance of its own
710	   (Section 5.1); the verifier applies the core's default, which the
711	   companion holds at five minutes.

713	   Some deployments sign one extract per effect row, with a window that
714	   holds that row alone.  Such an extract is a receipt for its row: it
715	   states nothing about any other reference, and its window need not
716	   adjoin the next one.  For the boundary rule, the following extract of
717	   an item deferred at the closing edge of such a window is one whose
718	   window contains the item's timestampMs; a later single-row extract
719	   that does not contain it is not the following extract and does not
720	   harden the item.  Under single-row extracts alone, an allow with no
721	   row hardens into decision-without-effect only when another row's
722	   extract happens to cover its time; otherwise it stays boundary-
723	   deferred near a window edge and is outside the extracts' scope
724	   elsewhere, so a reader of single-row extracts alone cannot establish
725	   that every allow had its effect.  A deployment that signs single-row
726	   extracts MUST state so, and MUST state how a reader learns which
727	   allows should have a row, for example a window extract over the
728	   audited period or a signed list of the references that produced an
729	   effect (MUST-DP-12).  An unsigned index is not such a statement.

731	   The binding does not order the two clocks.  A row's timestampMs is
732	   checked against the window it sits in and not against the timestampMs
733	   of the record it answers, so a row dated before the record it binds
734	   to binds as if it had followed it: the profile compares content and
735	   reference, not sequence.  The core's spend binding is the same, and
736	   neither document has claimed otherwise; the gap is named here because
737	   a reader of the companion measured it and found it unstated.  A
738	   deployment that needs the order can compare the two stamps within the
739	   same allowance; this revision names no finding for that comparison,
740	   and a later revision may.  The paragraph is the text form of the rule
741	   in Section 1.1: the silence costs a sentence here, and would cost a
742	   finding later.

744	6.2.  Conservation

746	   With |R| the in-scope Decision Records and |E| the effect rows, the
747	   identities the core report publishes hold with the words changed:

749	   |R|      = refusals + allows
750	   refusals = deny + defer
751	   allows   = matched + deferred + carried
752	              + unmatched + repeated + unreconciled
753	   |E|      = matched + deferred
754	              + unmatched + repeated + unreconciled
755	   matched on |R| equals matched on |E|

757	   A report under this profile MUST publish these counts and MUST name
758	   the population they were computed over (MUST-DP-8), for the reason
759	   the core gives: a report whose counts do not close is a report that
760	   lost a record somewhere, and a reader is entitled to see that without
761	   re-running the audit.  The report publishes refusals as one count,
762	   the core's aborted; the split of that count into deny and defer is
763	   the checkpoint's totals (Section 4.4), not a counter of the report.

825	   Counter names in a returned structure are diagnostic and MAY keep the

828	7.  Trust roots

830	   This profile has two roots, filling the core's issuer root and rail
831	   root (Sections 10.1 and 9.3 of [CEDULON]):

833	   The decider root:  The key, or set of keys, under which Decision
834	      Records and their checkpoints are attested.  A separate checkpoint
835	      key, where the deployment names one, belongs to this root and
836	      attests no Decision Record (Section 4.4).  Everything Section 10.1
837	      of the core states for the issuer root applies, with the one
838	      change Section 4.4 makes: a pinned key attests by signature, a
839	      carried key is not an identity, a record under another key, the
840	      checkpoint key included, is issuer-key-mismatch and covers
841	      nothing, and with no pin unauthenticated-issuer makes the
842	      guarantee conditional.

844	   The effect-extract root:  The key under which the Effect Extract is
845	      attested.  Everything Section 9.3 of the core states for the rail
846	      key applies.

848	   The core's payee, witness, decision-token, and manifest roots are not
849	   used by this profile, except that a transparency witness MAY hold the
850	   Decider's checkpoints exactly as it holds a Receipt Issuer's, with
851	   the witness root and codes of the core unchanged.

853	   What the two roots do not cover is the relation between them.  The
854	   profile's claim is only as strong as the independence of the party
855	   that signs the Effect Extract from the party that signs the Decision
856	   Records.  Where the channel operator signs an export of its own log,
857	   the extract root is that operator's key and the independence is the
858	   operator's.  Where the channel operator signs nothing, which is the
859	   common case for a messaging platform, the extract is produced by a
860	   capture process the deployment runs, and the deployment is stating,
861	   by pinning that process's key, that the process is not the Decider
862	   and cannot be told what to omit.  The rule is not that the two roots
863	   be independent; it is that the deployment say which case it is in,
864	   and that the guarantee fall when independence is absent or unknown.
865	   A deployment MUST state which of the two it has, and a verifier MUST
866	   treat the guarantee as conditional where the extract root and the
867	   decider root are, or may be, the same party (MUST-DP-9).  The
868	   companion cannot measure that from the keys alone; two keys can be
869	   held by one hand.

871	8.  Security Considerations

873	   The core's threat analysis (Section 16 of [CEDULON]) applies where
874	   the nouns carry over: forgery and repudiation of a signed record
875	   (T4), key leakage (T7), and suppression of checkpoints (T11) are the
876	   same threats against a Decider that they are against a Receipt
877	   Issuer, and the controls are the same.  The threats below are the
878	   ones this population adds or sharpens.

880	8.1.  D1: An effect occurs against a refusal

882	   The agent, or something acting through its channel access, does what
883	   the Decider refused.  This is the threat the profile exists for.  A
884	   Decision Record for the refusal, with the reference it refused, and
885	   an authenticated extract that carries an effect under that reference
886	   make the event effect-against-refusal, and the audit fails.  A
887	   refusal that carried no reference cannot support this finding; the
888	   effect is then effect-without-decision, which also fails the audit
889	   but does not say it was refused.  A Decider SHOULD carry the
890	   reference on a refusal whenever the channel assigns one before the
891	   decision.

893	   The shape of the threat is not particular to this profile.  A reader
894	   who mapped this document's objects onto a second evidence format,
895	   with a signed decision predicate and a signed outcome predicate of
896	   its own, measured that format's verifier on 4 September 2026: a
897	   decision carrying a refusal and an outcome recording execution,
898	   citing that decision by content root, verified clean with no warning,
899	   because the verdict is not read on the outcome path [B7N0DE].  That
900	   is D1 as a verifying pair rather than a finding, recorded by the
901	   reader as a measurement of that artefact and cited here as one.

903	8.2.  D2: Effects without decisions

905	   Something acts on the channel that never asked.  Every such effect is
906	   effect-without-decision.  The control is the extract's completeness,
907	   which is the extract root's independence (Section 7); a capture
908	   process the Decider controls can leave the effect out.

910	8.3.  D3: Substitution of content

912	   The Decider allows one content and the channel carries another.  The
913	   allow's effectHash and the row's effectHash are computed over the
914	   same octets, and a difference is effect-mismatch.  The control fails
915	   open if the two sides hash different octets, which is why a
916	   deployment MUST state the octets once for both (Section 5.1).

918	8.4.  D4: The Decider signs below its own rules

920	   A Decider produces a well-formed signature over a claim map that
921	   breaks a rule this profile states: an allow with no reference or no
922	   content hash, a refusal with a content hash, a hash outside the
923	   grammar.  Every such record is a record the reconciliation cannot
924	   close or would close wrongly.  The verifier applies the rules on the
925	   decoded payload (MUST-DP-3) and walks the chain over every record
926	   that claims the pin (MUST-DP-5), so the record is refused and named
927	   rather than attested or dropped.

929	8.5.  D5: Equivocation on the record chain

931	   The Decider signs two decisions for one request, or two successors to
932	   one predecessor, and offers each to a different reader.  Within one
933	   reader's population the chain (MUST-DP-5) makes the second record
934	   unlinkable: it names the same predecessor as the first, or none, and
935	   the walk reports the break.  Across readers the chain sees nothing:
936	   each holds a linear chain that verifies.  The control for that case
937	   is the core's witness (Section 4.4): the Decider's epoch checkpoints
938	   are recorded with a witness the verifier has pinned, a Transparency
939	   Service [RFC9943] being one, and the copies the witness holds are
940	   compared against the presented chain.  A verifier that consulted no
941	   witness has not measured cross-reader equivocation.  The guarantee
942	   its report prints is the core's, which is defined over the extract,
943	   the pins, and the window and does not cover suppression or
944	   equivocation beyond the presented chain when no witness was consulted
945	   (Section 11 of [CEDULON], MUST-T11-9); measured on the companion,
946	   such a report prints unconditional with no warning and no finding,
947	   and is silent about the witness it was not given.  The silence is
948	   this revision's known gap: the claim is narrowed here, in the text,
949	   and a report line that names an unconsulted witness is a change to
950	   the reconciler both populations share, not made in this revision.  A
951	   deployment that needs the property records its checkpoints with a
952	   witness and gives its readers the witness key.

954	8.6.  D6: The class of the effect is substituted

956	   A row of a different class under the same reference and the same
957	   content hash: the same text allowed as a reply and carried as a post.
958	   The earlier revision named this as a gap, because the record carried
959	   no claim for the class and a row of any class matched.  The record
960	   now carries effectClass under the Decider's signature (Section 4.1),
961	   an allow without one is refused (Section 4.2), and a row whose class
962	   differs from the allow's with the content hash equal is effect-class-
963	   mismatch (Section 6.1).  What remains open is the vocabulary: the
964	   class names are the channel's, this document does not fix them, and a
965	   deployment SHOULD state them beside its statement of what it hashes
966	   so that two readers compare the same words.

968	8.7.  D7: Silent defaults in capture

970	   The process that turns a channel's log into Decision Records or
971	   Effect Extract rows fills a missing value with a default, and the
972	   default hashes to something.  An allow with no stated content that is
973	   hashed as the empty string produces a record that will match an empty
974	   effect and mismatch every real one, with no finding that says the
975	   content was never stated.  Such a process MUST refuse the line by
976	   name rather than fill it (MUST-DP-10).  The companion's example
977	   adapter did fill it until it was measured, and refuses it now.

979	8.8.  D8: The capture process is the Decider

981	   The party that produces the Effect Extract is, or answers to, the
982	   party that signed the Decision Records.  Every finding in D1 and D2
983	   can then be made to disappear by omission, and no signature check
984	   detects it.  This is the independence statement of Section 7 (MUST-
985	   DP-9), and it is a deployment fact the profile can name but not
986	   prove.  A verifier that holds both roots and cannot state their
987	   independence has a conditional result, and MUST say so.

989	9.  Privacy Considerations

991	   A Decision Record carries no request content and no effect content:
992	   hashes of both, an opaque subject identifier, and a reason code.  The
993	   Effect Extract carries a reference, a class, a content hash, a time,
994	   and optionally the identifier of the party the effect reached.  The
995	   core's Privacy Considerations (Section 15 of [CEDULON]) apply to what
996	   a transparency witness is given.

998	   Two points are specific to this population.  A content hash over a
999	   short text is a fingerprint of that text: a reader who can guess the
1000	   message can confirm the guess.  This revision hashes the plain
1001	   content octets, as the companion does, so that the two sides need no
1002	   shared secret to agree; a keyed or salted construction that would
1003	   defeat the guess is a claim-set change and is not defined here.  A
1004	   deployment whose effects are short and guessable SHOULD treat the
1005	   extract and the records as confidential to the audit.  The subject
1006	   and actor identifiers are opaque to the profile but need not be
1007	   opaque to a reader; a deployment SHOULD pseudonymize them before
1008	   either object leaves its control.

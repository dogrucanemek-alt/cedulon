7	          Cedulon: An Audit Layer for Agent-to-Agent Commerce
8	                         draft-dogru-cedulon-08

10	Abstract

12	   This document defines the Cedulon Protocol, an audit layer for agent-
13	   to-agent commerce.  Payment rails such as HTTP 402 flows (x402) and
14	   mandate protocols (AP2) already move value, and a mandate protocol
15	   can already refuse a spend before it happens and return signed
16	   receipts.  What they do not, by themselves, give a party that is
17	   neither payer nor rail operator is a retrievable record of that
18	   decision and a signed spend receipt that reconciles against an
19	   authenticated extract of the rail.  Cedulon specifies a Trade
20	   Manifest (signed offer before payment), a Policy Decision Point with
21	   default deny, a Spend Receipt (COSE/CWT claim set after a gated
22	   payment), epoch checkpoints, and rail-extract reconciliation.  The
23	   reconciliation shows that no settlement on the extract lacks a
24	   receipt and no settled receipt is absent from the extract.  That
25	   result is unconditional only when the verifier pins the rail key out
26	   of band and states the period under audit; otherwise the document
27	   requires it to be reported as conditional.  Checkpoints carry the
28	   suppression guarantee, so the document profiles the checkpoint as a
29	   Signed Statement, gives the verification algorithm a step that
30	   consumes the witness receipts returned for checkpoints, names what a
31	   witness holding a checkpoint the presented chain omits reports,
32	   brings equivocation within reach by comparing recorded copies against
33	   the presented chain, and states how checkpoint totals may be withheld
34	   without withholding the fact that they were.  No signed object is
35	   attested by a key it carries itself, a signature checked against such
36	   a key where no key is held establishes internal consistency and
37	   attests nothing, and a presented Trade Manifest must be bound both to
38	   the receipts that name it and to the terms those receipts claim.  The
39	   document also names a threat no adversary causes, a settlement
40	   recorded on a rail with no receipt behind it, and defines a Dispute
41	   Evidence Bundle (evidence, not an award) and optional SCITT
42	   anchoring.  The encodings earlier revisions called canonical are
43	   defined, and the exact input to every hash-valued field is stated, so
44	   that an independent verifier can be written from the text alone.  The
45	   account and the rail under audit are part of the declared scope on
46	   the terms the period already had, no settlement finding is read out
47	   of an extract the pinned rail key refused, and the witness receipt
48	   has a stated wire form and a registered media type.  This revision
49	   widens one requirement: a report names the account, rail and window
50	   it was computed over in every structure an implementation returns for
51	   that audit, not only in the printed report and the finding object, so
52	   that no returned result can be read as a statement about settlement
53	   paths it never looked at.  Cedulon is not a competitor to x402 or
54	   AP2; it sits above them.

169	1.  Introduction

171	   _Note to Readers:_ This document is submitted as Informational.  The
172	   author's eventual intended track, if the work is taken up, is a
173	   Standards Track profile of COSE [RFC9052] and CWT [RFC8392] for
174	   agent-spend receipts.  This -08 does not claim IETF consensus.

176	   Agents can now pay.  Open HTTP 402 protocols [X402] attach stablecoin
177	   settlement to ordinary requests.  Card networks and processors issue
178	   agent-scoped tokens.  Google's Agent Payments Protocol (AP2) [AP2]
179	   binds user intent to signed mandates.

181	   What is missing is an interoperable *audit layer*: a machine-
182	   checkable answer to "was this spend allowed by policy, against which
183	   offer, and what bytes were delivered?"  The third question is
184	   answered to the extent the evidence carries it: machine-checkably
185	   where an attributable payee countersignature binds a deliveredHash
186	   (Section 5.1), and narrowed to "what bytes were promised, and what
187	   hash was presented beside them" where none does - a bundle must not
188	   claim more than its signatures cover.  Without this layer, a prompt-
189	   injected or looping agent can drain a rail that has already accepted
190	   a valid signature.  A counterparty can ship the wrong artifact.  A
191	   transparency log, if used at all, is proprietary.

193	   Cedulon fills that gap.  It does not clear funds, hold custody, or
194	   operate a payment facilitator.  An optional escrow actor is defined
195	   only as a third-party role interface (Section 16.13).
196	   Implementations of this specification MUST NOT take custody of funds
197	   or operate escrow (MUST-T8-custody).

199	   The control is an old one.  Reconciling an internal ledger against an
200	   external statement is what double-entry bookkeeping [PACIOLI] made
201	   routine, and signing the artifacts on both sides is Grigg's triple-
202	   entry idea [GRIGG].  Neither is claimed here.  What this document
203	   contributes is an open wire profile for that control in a setting
204	   where the parties are software: a COSE receipt shape, an extract
205	   shape, a checkpoint chain, and a verification algorithm precise
206	   enough that two implementations reach the same finding on the same
207	   evidence.  The novelty is interoperability, not the idea.

209	   Neighbor drafts are complementary, not substitutes. draft-bates-atp
210	   [BATES-ATP] covers tamper-evident causal lineage as a signed DAG.
211	   Cedulon is the completeness layer: a spend that never produced a
212	   receipt is visible when an authenticated rail extract is reconciled
213	   (MUST-T10-1).

215	2.  Terminology

217	   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT",
218	   "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and
219	   "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in
220	   BCP 14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all
221	   capitals, as shown here.

223	   The following terms are used:

225	   Trade Manifest:  A signed statement produced *before* payment.  It
226	      binds a description of goods or service, price, currency,
227	      acceptance-criteria hash, cancel condition, expiry, and an
228	      optional AP2 mandate reference.

230	   Policy Decision Point (PDP):  The function that evaluates a
231	      structured spend request against stored policy.  The default is
232	      deny.

234	   Spend Receipt:  A signed statement produced *after* a gated payment
235	      attempt.  It binds payer, payee, amount, currency, policy hash,
236	      manifestHash or an explicit noManifest flag, rail payment
237	      reference, timestampMs, nonce, prevReceiptHash, and outcome.

239	   Receipt Issuer:  The party that signs Spend Receipts.

241	   Anchor:  An optional SCITT Transparency Service [RFC9943] that
242	      registers a signed statement and returns a COSE receipt [RFC9942].

244	   Dispute Evidence Bundle:  A package of the Trade Manifest, the Spend
245	      Receipt, and a delivery hash.  It is evidence for a later human or
246	      legal process.  It is not an arbitral award and not an escrow
247	      release.

249	   Decision Token:  A portable, single-use PDP allow encoded as
250	      COSE_Sign1.  The claim set binds requestHash, policyHash,
251	      expiryMs, nonce, and singleUseId.  See Section 8.

253	   Rail Extract:  An authenticated list of settlement records for one
254	      account, one rail, and one time window.  See Section 9.

256	   Presented-unattested:  The state of a receipt or checkpoint the
257	      verifier holds no pinned issuer key for.  Its signature is checked
258	      against the key the object itself carries (Section 6.3), which
259	      establishes internal consistency only, so the object is neither
260	      attested nor rejected: every presented object is weighed as one
261	      set and the completeness guarantee is reported as conditional.  It
262	      is a state of the report, not a finding code.  See Section 10.1.

264	3.  Architecture

266	   Cedulon has three control-plane objects and one optional log:

268	     Principal --policy--> PDP --allow/deny--> x402/AP2 rail
269	                                 |
270	                                 v
271	                         Receipt Issuer --> Spend Receipt
272	                                 |
273	                                 v
274	                         Anchor / SCITT (optional)

276	   The payer agent never talks to the rail except through an adapter
277	   that calls the PDP first (MUST-T5-1).

279	3.1.  Policy Decision Point

281	   The PDP evaluates structured fields only (MUST-T1-1): amount,
282	   currency, payee, tool identifier, nonce, optional manifest hash, and
283	   evaluation time.  It applies limit, velocity, and scope checks (MUST-
284	   T2-1, MUST-T2-2).  If the PDP is unreachable, uninitialized, or
285	   throws, the result is deny (MUST-T2-3).  Denied attempts do not
286	   increment success counters (MUST-T2-4).

288	   An allow produces a Decision Token whose requestHash covers six
289	   fields: amount, currency, payee, tool, nonce, and manifestHash (MUST-
290	   T3-4, MUST-T6-1).  The token is a COSE_Sign1 object (MUST-T6-4), is
291	   single-use (MUST-T6-2), and MAY be carried to the adapter that
292	   performs settlement.

294	3.2.  Receipt Issuer

296	   After the adapter attempts settlement (success or a recorded deny
297	   that still needs an audit trail for an allowed-then-aborted path),
298	   the Receipt Issuer signs a Spend Receipt over the deterministic CBOR
299	   encoding of its claims (MUST-T4-1).  Verifiers reject bad signatures
300	   and byte mismatch (MUST-T4-2).

302	3.3.  Anchor / SCITT

304	   Parties MAY register the signed receipt (or a privacy-preserving hash
305	   encoding) as a SCITT Signed Statement [RFC9943] and attach the COSE
306	   receipt (MAY-T4-6).  This document does not operate a Transparency
307	   Service.

309	4.  Trade Manifest

311	   A Trade Manifest is the commerce analogue of a promise: it is issued
312	   *before* value moves.  It is conceptually symmetric to a later Spend
313	   Receipt (promise then proof), and it MAY carry an AP2 mandate hash so
314	   that user intent and the Cedulon offer stay linked (SHOULD-T8-5).

316	   A Trade Manifest MUST bind all of the following (MUST-T8-1):

318	   *  goods or service description

320	   *  price (integer minor units, encoded as a decimal string matching
321	      0|[1-9][0-9]*)

323	   *  currency (ISO 4217 alphabetic or a documented token identifier)

325	   *  acceptance-criteria hash (SHA-256 [RFC6234] of the exact delivery
326	      bytes, lowercase hexadecimal)

328	   *  cancel condition (opaque string agreed by the parties)

330	   *  expiry (POSIX milliseconds, expiresAtMs)

332	   -03 allowed this hash to be taken over "the exact delivery bytes or a
333	   declared schema instance" and gave a verifier no way to tell which
334	   one an issuer had used.  Two implementations reading the same
335	   manifest would then compute different digests over the same delivery
336	   and neither would be wrong.  This revision defines the first reading
337	   only.  Hashing a schema instance instead would need a marker in the
338	   manifest saying so, this document defines no such marker, and until
339	   one is defined that use is out of scope rather than an alternative a
340	   verifier is expected to guess at.

342	   It MAY include ap2MandateHash.  The corresponding CBOR label is
343	   always present; a missing mandate is encoded as CBOR null.

345	   It MAY name a payee.  An offer that is specific to one counterparty
346	   carries that party's payee identifier; when present, every receipt
347	   that names this manifest has its payee compared against it as exact
348	   octets under MUST-T8-9, on that requirement's two-branch severity.
349	   An open offer legitimately omits the member, and no comparison is
350	   made.  Unlike ap2MandateHash, the label is encoded only when the
351	   member is present, so a manifest signed before -05 carries the same
352	   bytes and keeps verifying; this is why the null convention above does
353	   not apply to it.

355	   The manifest is COSE_Sign1 [RFC9052] over a deterministic CBOR claim
356	   map (Section 6). manifestHash is the SHA-256 of the signed COSE bytes
357	   (MUST-T8-7).  A spend bound to a manifest MUST be denied if the
358	   requested amount or currency differs from the manifest (MUST-T8-2) or
359	   if the manifest is expired (MUST-T3-3).

361	   A spend that is not bound to a verified manifest MUST be marked
362	   noManifest on the receipt and MUST still pass limit, velocity, and
363	   scope checks (MUST-T1-2).  An implementation MAY refuse all
364	   noManifest spend (MAY-T1-4).

366	5.  Spend Receipt

368	   The Spend Receipt claim set is carried in COSE_Sign1 [RFC9052]
369	   wrapping a CWT-compatible map [RFC8392].  New receipts MUST use the
370	   COSE profile (Section 6).

372	   Claims (MUST-T4-3, MUST-T4-4, MUST-T4-7):

374	    +=================+===============================================+
375	    | Claim           | Description                                   |
376	    +=================+===============================================+
377	    | payer           | Payer agent identifier                        |
378	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
379	    | payee           | Payee identifier                              |
380	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
381	    | amount          | Minor units as a decimal string 0|[1-9][0-9]* |
382	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
383	    | currency        | Currency identifier                           |
384	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
385	    | policyHash      | SHA-256 of the canonical policy document      |
386	    |                 | (lowercase hex)                               |
387	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
388	    | manifestHash    | SHA-256 of the signed manifest COSE bytes, or |
389	    |                 | null when noManifest is true                  |
390	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
391	    | noManifest      | Boolean; MUST be true if and only if          |
392	    |                 | manifestHash is null                          |
393	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
394	    | x402PaymentRef  | Rail payment reference, or null               |
395	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
396	    | timestampMs     | POSIX milliseconds                            |
397	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
398	    | nonce           | Unique spend nonce; at least 128 bits of      |
399	    |                 | randomness; unique in the issuer scope        |
400	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
401	    | prevReceiptHash | Previous receipt hash, or null for the first  |
402	    |                 | receipt (SHOULD-T4-5)                         |
403	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
404	    | outcome         | settled or aborted                            |
405	    +-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+

407	                                  Table 1

409	   A receipt with outcome = settled MUST have a non-null x402PaymentRef
410	   (MUST-T4-7).  An aborted receipt MUST NOT be added into checkpoint
411	   totals.

413	   All twelve labels in Section 6.1 are always present.  An empty
414	   optional value is encoded as CBOR null, never by omitting the label.

416	   receiptHash is the SHA-256 of the receipt's signed COSE bytes,
417	   encoded as lowercase hex.

419	   Verifiers MUST reject a receipt if the signature fails or if the
420	   decoded claim map does not match the presented claims (MUST-T4-2).

422	5.1.  Optional payee countersignature

424	   A payee MAY attach a countersignature over the issuer's signed Spend
425	   Receipt (MAY-T8-10).  The profile uses a *detached* COSE_Sign1
426	   [RFC9052] whose payload is a CBOR map with private-use labels:

428	     +========+===============+======================================+
429	     | Label  | Claim         | CBOR type                            |
430	     +========+===============+======================================+
431	     | -70401 | receiptCose   | bstr (exact issuer COSE_Sign1 bytes) |
432	     +--------+---------------+--------------------------------------+
433	     | -70402 | deliveredHash | bstr (optional; SHA-256 of the exact |
434	     |        |               | delivered bytes)                     |

437	                                  Table 2

439	   The countersignature uses the header profile in Section 6 and content
440	   type application/cedulon-countersign+cbor.

442	   This is a second Sign1 object, not RFC 9052 Countersignature0
443	   (unprotected-header label 11).  Countersignature0 would write into
444	   the issuer object and change receiptHash after issue, breaking the
445	   receipt chain.  A detached Sign1 keeps the issuer bytes stable,
446	   reuses kid and content-type, and is absent by simply omitting the
447	   sibling object.

449	   Absence of a countersignature MUST NOT invalidate the issuer receipt
450	   (MAY-T8-10).  A countersignature travels beside the issuer signature
451	   without being covered by it, so anyone holding an honest receipt can
452	   append one of their own.  Attribution is therefore the gate: a
453	   countersignature that cannot be attributed to the pinned payee key -
454	   the signature fails, kid or content type does not match, label -70401
455	   is not the issuer COSE bytes (MUST-T8-8), or the signature is valid
456	   under some other key - MUST be rejected as approval evidence.  What
457	   is rejected is the countersignature, not the receipt: the verdict on
458	   the untouched issuer receipt MUST NOT change because an
459	   unattributable object was attached.  An appendable object the issuer
460	   signature does not cover must not be able to manufacture a negative
461	   result; this is the lesson MUST-T8-9 already encodes for the manifest
462	   comparison, applied one object over.  The identifiers countersign-bad
463	   (unverifiable) and countersign-key-mismatch (verifiable under another
464	   key) name the discarded object as warnings, and where the verifier
465	   pinned a payee key and no attributable countersignature remains, the
466	   countersign-missing warning still applies: a discarded forgery is the
467	   absence of the payee's word, not a substitute for it.

469	   The optional deliveredHash claim binds delivery to the receipt under
470	   the payee's key.  A payee who countersigns MAY include the SHA-256 of
471	   the exact bytes it received in the same signed payload as the issuer
472	   receipt bytes.  When an attributable countersignature carries
473	   deliveredHash and the verifier also holds the Trade Manifest, the two
474	   digests are compared as exact octets: deliveredHash against
475	   acceptanceCriteriaHash.  A mismatch is delivery-mismatch, and it is a
476	   finding rather than a warning, because both ends of the comparison
477	   are signed.  A deliveredHash carried by an unattributable
478	   countersignature is discarded with it.  A countersigner MUST refuse
479	   to sign a deliveredHash that is not 32 octets; a verifier that meets
480	   one anyway treats the countersignature as carrying no deliveredHash,
481	   so the delivery question narrows as it does when the claim is absent,
482	   and the signature verdict does not move.  A countersignature without
483	   the claim is valid exactly as before -05, which introduced it.

485	   A Dispute Evidence Bundle that includes a verified countersignature
486	   has stronger evidence that the payee accepted those bytes; the bundle
487	   is still not an award (MUST-T8-4).

489	6.  COSE Profile

491	   This profile uses deterministic CBOR [RFC8949] Section 4.2.1
492	   (definite lengths, shortest integer form, map keys sorted in
493	   *bytewise lexicographic* order of their encoded keys).
494	   Implementations MUST encode only the types used by Cedulon claim
495	   maps: null, bool, unsigned and negative integers, UTF-8 text, byte
496	   strings, arrays, and maps (MUST-T4-1).

498	   -03 bound the encoder and said nothing about the decoder, which left
499	   the reading side free where the writing side was not.  A decoder MUST
500	   refuse a CBOR map that carries a duplicate encoded key (MUST-T4-18).
501	   The encoding rules already forbid producing one, so a decoder that
502	   accepts it accepts a document no conforming encoder can produce, and
503	   two decoders may disagree on which of the two values wins, which is a
504	   disagreement about what was signed.

506	   A decoder MUST also impose a bound on what it will attempt: on
507	   encoded size, on nesting depth, and on the number of elements it will
508	   decode from an audit input.  It MUST refuse an input that exceeds a
509	   bound with a named refusal rather than by exhausting memory or the
510	   stack, and it SHOULD document the bounds it applies (MUST-T4-19).
511	   This document fixes no numbers.  A bound that is right for a desktop
512	   verifier is wrong for a service, and a number written here would be
513	   wrong for one of them within a year.  What a reader is entitled to is
514	   that the refusal is a refusal, named and reported, rather than a
515	   crash that an operator has to interpret.

517	6.1.  Claim labels

631	6.2.  COSE_Sign1 headers

633	   The protected header MUST be a deterministic CBOR map containing
634	   (MUST-T4-1, MUST-T4-8):

636	   *  1 (alg) = -19 (Ed25519, [RFC9864]; the generic EdDSA value -8 from
637	      [RFC9053] is deprecated for this profile)

639	   *  3 (content type) = a tstr that distinguishes the payload:
640	      application/cedulon-receipt+cbor, application/cedulon-
641	      checkpoint+cbor, application/cedulon-manifest+cbor, application/
642	      cedulon-decision+cbor, application/cedulon-countersign+cbor, or
643	      application/cedulon-inclusion+cbor

645	   *  4 (kid) = bstr, mandatory.  The profile computes kid as the first
646	      eight bytes of SHA-256 over the issuer's SubjectPublicKeyInfo DER,
647	      in the Ed25519 SubjectPublicKeyInfo encoding of [RFC8410].  A
648	      verifier MUST obtain the public key from an authenticated channel
649	      (preconfigured issuer set, directory, or transparency statement)
650	      and MUST reject a message whose kid does not match that key.

652	   The unprotected header MUST be empty, and a decoder MUST refuse a
653	   message whose unprotected header is not an empty map, by the name
654	   cose-sign1-unprotected, rather than verify the signature and ignore
655	   the header (MUST-T4-21).  The reason is in Section 7.1: every digest
656	   over a signed object in this profile is computed over the COSE_Sign1
657	   octets, which include the unprotected header, while the signature
658	   does not cover it.  A decoder that ignored a stuffed header would
659	   verify the signature and compute a receiptHash the issuer never
660	   produced, so one honestly signed receipt could carry as many digests
661	   as a stranger cared to give it, and a chain built on prevReceiptHash
662	   would follow whichever copy it was handed.  The posted -05 stated the
663	   header empty and said nothing about the decoder; the companion
664	   decoder ignored the header until the question of what the digest
665	   covers was measured.  The payload MUST be the CBOR encoding of the
666	   claim map.  The signature is Ed25519 [RFC8032] over the COSE
667	   Sig_structure ["Signature1", protected, h'', payload].

669	6.3.  How a signed object is presented

671	   The signed octets above are what this profile defines and what every
672	   digest in Section 7.1 is taken over.  An object handed to a verifier
673	   travels with a little more than that, and the previous revisions used
674	   one of those members - the key an object carries - without ever
675	   saying where it came from.

677	   A presented Spend Receipt, epoch checkpoint, Trade Manifest, or
678	   Decision Token carries, beside the signed octets, its claim set or
679	   body in decoded form and *the signer's public key as a
680	   SubjectPublicKeyInfo PEM*; a countersigned receipt carries the
681	   payee's key the same way, and an object presented in the JSON
682	   encoding of Section 7 repeats its signature there as base64.  None of
683	   these is inside the signed octets, which is the whole point of naming
684	   them here: the signature covers the COSE message and nothing else, so
685	   every one of these members is a surface anyone holding the object can
686	   rewrite.  This is the same shape the Rail Extract states in
687	   Section 9, and it is stated once here for the COSE objects rather
688	   than left to be inferred from an implementation.

690	   A carried key is not an identity source and MUST NOT be used as one
691	   (MUST-T4-11).  Under a pin it has exactly one effect: an object that
692	   verifies under the pinned key while carrying a different key is
693	   reported as carried-key-mismatch, a warning, and stays attested
694	   (Section 10.1).  With no pin held it is the only key present, so the
695	   signature check that runs against it says the object is internally
696	   consistent and says nothing about who signed it; two issuers cannot
697	   be told apart in that state.

699	7.  Canonical JSON encoding

701	   Not everything this document hashes or signs is CBOR.  The policy
702	   document, the six request fields bound by a Decision Token, and the
703	   scoped body of a Rail Extract are JSON, and -03 called each of them
704	   "canonical" without saying what that meant.  Two implementations
705	   could therefore agree on every requirement in this document and still
706	   produce different bytes, which makes an independent verifier
707	   impossible to write from the text.  This section closes that.

709	   Where this document says "the canonical encoding" of a JSON document,
710	   it means the encoding defined by [RFC8785], and the octets hashed or
711	   signed are the UTF-8 octets of that encoding.

713	   Three notes on the boundary of that reference:

715	   *  [RFC8785] Section 3.1 takes I-JSON [RFC7493] as its input, and an
716	      I-JSON object carries no duplicate member names.  This document
717	      makes that precondition a rule at every place a verifier receives
718	      a JSON document as text - a rail extract body, a policy document,
719	      a stored receipt file: a text in which any object, at any depth,
720	      repeats a member name MUST be refused by the name json-duplicate-
721	      key, before the text is parsed (MUST-T4-20).  The rule is measured
722	      on the text because a parser that keeps either value has already
723	      discarded the evidence of the other, and two verifiers parsing one
724	      text could then canonicalize two different documents and sign or
725	      check two different octet strings, which is the JSON form of the
726	      disagreement MUST-T4-18 forbids for CBOR.  A verifier handed an
727	      object rather than text, as a tool behind a JSON-RPC boundary is,
728	      cannot apply the rule and MUST NOT report that it did; whether the
729	      text was checked is then the transport's to state.  The posted -05
730	      said nothing about this.  The companion implementation refused
731	      such texts before the sentence existed, and its conformance runner
732	      carried the difference as a recorded split against -05 rather than
733	      as a pass.

735	   *  [RFC8785] Section 3.2.2.2 requires a serializer to terminate on a
736	      lone surrogate, and so does this document: a producer MUST refuse
737	      to encode a document containing one, by name, and MUST NOT sign
738	      what it could not encode.  A verifier reading bytes it cannot
739	      canonicalize for this reason reports the input as failing
740	      verification with the refusal named beside the verdict; it does
741	      not crash.  No field defined by this document may contain a lone
742	      surrogate, so a conforming document never reaches this rule. -04
743	      permitted emitting the escaped form instead, which contradicted
744	      the RFC it cited; that permission is removed.

746	   *  [RFC8785] defines no encoding for an integer outside the IEEE 754
747	      double range.  No document defined here carries one: every amount
748	      and cumulative limit is already a decimal string before it is
749	      encoded, and every hash is lowercase hexadecimal.  A document that
750	      would need such an integer is outside this specification.

752	7.1.  Which octets are hashed

754	   Every hash-valued field in this document is SHA-256 [RFC6234] of the
755	   input named below.  All but three are rendered as lowercase
756	   hexadecimal. kid differs only in its rendering: the digest is
757	   computed over the same stated input and then truncated to its first 8
758	   bytes, carried as a byte string rather than as hex (Section 6 states
759	   the same rule where the header is defined). ap2MandateHash differs in
760	   whose digest it is: AP2 defines the mandate and its octets, and this
761	   document carries the result opaquely rather than restating a rule it
762	   does not own. deliveredHash differs in its carrier: it is a claim in
763	   a CBOR map and is carried as the raw 32 digest bytes (bstr), not as
764	   hex; comparisons against acceptanceCriteriaHash are made over the
765	   digest value. -03 named the digest for some of these fields and not
766	   for others; the omissions were not a deliberate degree of freedom.

768	        +========================+===============================+
769	        | Field                  | Input to SHA-256              |
770	        +========================+===============================+
771	        | receiptHash            | the signed COSE_Sign1 octets  |
772	        |                        | of the receipt                |
773	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
774	        | manifestHash           | the signed COSE_Sign1 octets  |
775	        |                        | of the Trade Manifest         |
776	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
777	        | checkpointHash         | the signed COSE_Sign1 octets  |
778	        |                        | of the checkpoint             |
779	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
780	        | statementHash          | the signed COSE_Sign1 octets  |
781	        |                        | of the statement              |
782	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
783	        | acceptanceCriteriaHash | the exact delivery bytes, as  |
784	        |                        | defined where the Trade       |
785	        |                        | Manifest is                   |
786	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
787	        | deliveredHash          | the exact bytes the payee     |
788	        |                        | received; the same input rule |
789	        |                        | as acceptanceCriteriaHash,    |
790	        |                        | computed by the other party   |
791	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
792	        | policyHash             | the UTF-8 octets of the       |
793	        |                        | canonical policy document     |
794	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
795	        | requestHash            | the UTF-8 octets of the       |
796	        |                        | canonical six-field request   |
797	        |                        | document                      |
798	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
799	        | kid                    | the SubjectPublicKeyInfo DER; |
800	        |                        | the digest is then truncated  |
801	        |                        | to its first 8 bytes          |
802	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+
803	        | ap2MandateHash         | the octets AP2 defines for    |
804	        |                        | its mandate; not profiled by  |
805	        |                        | this document                 |
806	        +------------------------+-------------------------------+

808	                                 Table 7

810	   Wherever this table, or any other sentence in this document, says
811	   "the signed COSE_Sign1 octets", those are the octets of the
812	   *untagged* four-element COSE_Sign1 array of [RFC9052].  This profile
813	   never wraps a message in CBOR tag 18, and the vectors in Appendix A
814	   carry the untagged form.  A verifier that hashed a tagged copy would
815	   compute a different digest for every object in this profile, so the
816	   choice is stated here once rather than left to be inferred from the
817	   vectors.

819	   The six fields of the request document are the ones MUST-T6-1 names:
820	   amount, currency, payee, tool, nonce, and manifestHash. -03 described
821	   requestHash as "the six-field hash" while naming SHA-256 for
822	   policyHash in the same sentence, which left a reader free to conclude
823	   that the request binding was not a digest at all.  It is one.

825	   Naming the members is not stating the document, so the document is
826	   stated here.  The request document is a JSON object carrying exactly
827	   those six members and no others, every member always present. amount
828	   is the decimal string of the request, in the amount syntax the
829	   receipt claims table states, never a JSON number: [RFC8785] encodes
830	   the number 1 and the string "1" differently, and an implementation
831	   free to pick either would produce two digests for one request.
832	   currency, payee, and nonce are the request's text strings. tool is
833	   the request's text string, or JSON null where the deployment names
834	   none. manifestHash is the lowercase hexadecimal string, or JSON null
835	   for a spend bound to no manifest; an absent value is null, never an
836	   omitted member.  A document with a seventh member, a missing member,
837	   or another type for one of these is not the request document this
838	   section defines.

840	   The policy document is different on purpose, and the difference is
841	   scope rather than an oversight.  Its member set is the deployment's
842	   own: this document defines how the bytes of whatever policy document
843	   a PDP evaluates are encoded (Section 7) and digested, not what its
844	   members are. policyHash binds a spend to the exact bytes its PDP
845	   evaluated; it is not a value two deployments are expected to compute
846	   from a shared schema, and nothing in the verification algorithm
847	   compares one deployment's policyHash to another's.

849	8.  Decision Token

851	   A Decision Token is the portable encoding of a PDP allow.  It is
852	   COSE_Sign1 with the header profile in Section 6 and the labels in
853	   Section 6.1.  All five labels are always present (MUST-T6-4).

855	   requestHash MUST be the SHA-256 of the canonical encoding of the six
856	   fields the PDP evaluated (MUST-T6-1), rendered as lowercase
857	   hexadecimal; Section 7 defines that encoding and Section 7.1 states
858	   the octets. -03 called this "the six-field hash" in the same sentence
859	   that named SHA-256 for policyHash, which left the digest for one of
860	   them unstated. policyHash MUST be the SHA-256 of the canonical policy
861	   document the PDP evaluated. expiryMs is a Unix time in milliseconds
862	   after which the token MUST be treated as expired (SHOULD-T6-3):
863	   expired when the evaluation time is strictly greater than expiryMs,
864	   not yet expired at exactly expiryMs, on the same boundary discipline
865	   MUST-T3-3 states for the manifest. nonce is the request nonce.
866	   singleUseId is the identifier consumed on the first settlement
867	   attempt (MUST-T6-2).

869	   A party that accepts a Decision Token MUST reject it if the signature
870	   fails, if kid does not match a configured PDP key, if the content
871	   type is not application/cedulon-decision+cbor, if the decoded claim
872	   map does not match the presented claims, or if expiryMs is in the
873	   past (MUST-T6-5).

875	9.  Rail Extract Profile

877	   A verifier checks completeness against a *rail extract*, not against

892	         | currency    | string                                  |

899	   These member names are normative.  A rail MAY add members of its own

901	   names were the rail's to define, which contradicted this table and

912	   mismatch).  Resolving a reference to a beneficiary is a feature of
913	   the rail's own system: this profile does not assume it, and measures
914	   it only when the rail declares it.

916	9.2.  Scope

918	   An extract is scoped to one account identifier, one rail identifier,
919	   and one half-open time window [windowStartMs, windowEndMs).  The
920	   signed body is one JSON document with exactly this shape:

922	        +===============+=========================================+
923	        | Member        | JSON type                               |
924	        +===============+=========================================+
925	        | accountId     | string                                  |
926	        +---------------+-----------------------------------------+
927	        | railId        | string                                  |
928	        +---------------+-----------------------------------------+
929	        | windowStartMs | number (POSIX milliseconds, an integer) |
930	        +---------------+-----------------------------------------+
931	        | windowEndMs   | number (POSIX milliseconds, an integer) |
932	        +---------------+-----------------------------------------+
933	        | clockSkewMs   | number (milliseconds, a non-negative    |
934	        |               | integer; optional; see Section 11)      |
935	        +---------------+-----------------------------------------+
936	        | settlements   | array of settlement records (schema     |
937	        |               | above)                                  |
938	        +---------------+-----------------------------------------+

940	                                  Table 9

942	   All six named members except clockSkewMs MUST be present; a body
943	   missing one, or a record renaming a core member, MUST be refused by
944	   name at both ends - by the signer before it signs and by the verifier
945	   before it checks a signature - so a malformed extract is the same
946	   refusal on both sides rather than a signature verdict.  Additional
947	   members beyond these are the rail's to add, as with records.

949	   The integer-valued members - windowStartMs, windowEndMs, each
950	   record's timestampMs, and clockSkewMs - MUST be integers of magnitude
951	   at most 2^53 - 1, the range a JSON number carries exactly, and
952	   clockSkewMs MUST NOT be negative; a value outside those bounds, or a
953	   non-integer, is refused by name in the same way as a missing member.
954	   windowEndMs MUST be greater than windowStartMs: the window is half-
955	   open, so one that does not end after it starts declares no
956	   population, and a body carrying one is refused by name (malformed-
957	   extract-window) in the same way, at both ends.

959	   The body is read as text before it is read as an object.  A text in
960	   which any object repeats a member name is refused as json-duplicate-
961	   key at both ends, before parsing and before any signature is checked
962	   (MUST-T4-20, Section 7); a signer that parsed first would sign one of
963	   the two values and an honest verifier could check the other.

965	   clockSkewMs, when present, declares the boundary allowance the
966	   verifier applies at this window's edges during reconciliation
967	   (Section 11); when absent, the profile default of 300000 milliseconds
968	   (five minutes) applies.

970	9.3.  Authentication

972	   The rail signs Ed25519 [RFC8032] over the canonical encoding of the
973	   scoped body, which is a JSON document and therefore takes the
974	   encoding of Section 7: the signed octets are the UTF-8 octets of the
975	   [RFC8785] encoding of the body above.  The signature and the rail's
976	   public key travel beside the body, the signature as base64 and the
977	   key as a SubjectPublicKeyInfo PEM; neither is part of the signed
978	   octets.  A verifier MUST obtain the extract from the rail or from a
979	   signature the rail published (MUST-T10-7).  A deployment that cannot
980	   do so is running the reconciliation against evidence it did not
981	   obtain independently, and MUST report the guarantee as conditional.

983	   A signature on an extract proves internal consistency, not origin: a
984	   key generated by whoever produced the object verifies against itself.
985	   The verifier therefore MUST obtain the rail's public key out of band
986	   and MUST verify the extract signature against that key (MUST-T10-8).
987	   A key the extract carries is not that key: it is neither the rail's
988	   identity nor a fallback for one the verifier did not obtain.  Where
989	   no such key is held, the carried key is the only key present, so the
990	   signature check that runs against it says the extract is internally
991	   consistent and says nothing about who produced it, and the verifier
992	   MUST treat the guarantee as conditional.

994	   Keys are compared as bytes.  A verifier MUST compare the pinned key
995	   and the key that signed the extract by their SubjectPublicKeyInfo DER
996	   encoding, not by any text encoding of it, so that the same key
997	   presented in a different envelope still compares equal (MUST-T10-9).
998	   A pinned key the verifier cannot decode is a fault in the verifier's
999	   own configuration, not evidence about the extract, and MUST be
1000	   reported as trust-key-unreadable rather than as a key mismatch.

1002	   What a verifier emits for an extract it cannot authenticate depends
1003	   on whether it stated an expectation.  With no pinned key the verifier
1004	   has asserted nothing, so the condition is unauthenticated-extract
1005	   whatever the extract carries - a signature that verifies against the
1006	   carried key establishes internal consistency and not origin, and one
1007	   that fails or is refused is not a verdict about a key either.  It is
1008	   a warning: completeness findings may still be computed, but the
1009	   guarantee is *conditional* on the extract being authentic.  With a
1010	   pinned key the verifier has asserted what it requires, and an extract
1011	   that fails to meet it is a failure rather than a caveat; see the
1012	   verification algorithm for which finding applies. -00 defined only
1013	   the first case, and readers of -00 should note that -02 made the
1014	   pinned case fail closed.  See Section 16.

1016	9.4.  Scope agreement

1018	   An extract declares a window and carries settlement records.  The two
1019	   MUST agree: a verifier MUST report every settlement record whose
1020	   timestampMs falls outside [windowStartMs, windowEndMs) as extract-
1021	   scope-mismatch, identified by that record's ref (MUST-T10-10).  This
1022	   check is about the extract's internal consistency and MUST be
1023	   performed whether or not a rail key is pinned.

1025	   A verifier that knows which account, rail, and window it is auditing
1026	   MUST also check the extract against that expectation and MUST fail
1027	   closed when the extract does not cover it (MUST-T10-11).  An extract
1028	   for another account or rail, or one whose window does not span the
1029	   period under audit, cannot support a completeness claim about that
1030	   period.

1032	   A verifier that states no period leaves the extract free to define
1033	   one, and an extract that reports on a millisecond balances as easily
1034	   as one that reports on a month.  Pinning a key establishes who
1035	   signed; only a stated period establishes what the signature had to
1036	   cover.  A verifier that has not stated the period under audit
1037	   therefore MUST emit unstated-audit-window and MUST treat the
1038	   guarantee as conditional (MUST-T10-15), whatever else verifies.

1040	   The period is one axis of that scope.  The account and the rail are
1041	   the other two, and they behave the same way: an extract names one of
1042	   each, so a verifier that has not stated them leaves the extract to
1043	   say whose settlements were accounted for and which way out was
1044	   watched.  A verifier that has not stated the account or the rail
1045	   under audit therefore MUST emit unstated-audit-scope and MUST treat
1046	   the guarantee as conditional (MUST-T10-18).  Where no rail key is
1047	   pinned at all, all three axes are equally unstated and
1048	   unauthenticated-extract is the condition reported.

1050	   Stating them does not widen the population; it names it.  One account
1051	   settling on two rails has two settlement paths, and an extract for
1052	   the first reports nothing about the second: a spend that left that
1053	   way is not an unmatched row, it is outside the population the extract
1054	   declared.  Because the strongest line this profile prints - a
1055	   balanced audit under an unconditional guarantee - is true of one
1056	   account, on one rail, over one window, a report that carries it MUST
1057	   also carry that account, rail and window (MUST-T10-19).  A
1058	   completeness claim about an account is the conjunction of one such
1059	   report per rail that account can settle on, and enumerating those
1060	   rails is the deployment's statement, not something an extract can be
1061	   asked to prove.

1063	10.  Trust roots

1065	   Section 9 states the rule for one object: a signature proves internal
1066	   consistency, not origin, so the verifier obtains the rail key out of
1067	   band and checks the extract against it, and the key the extract
1068	   carries is neither the rail's identity nor a fallback for one the
1069	   verifier did not obtain (MUST-T10-8). -02 already required a verifier
1070	   to obtain the public key from an authenticated channel and to reject
1071	   a kid that does not match that key (MUST-T4-8).  What -02 did not
1072	   carry was the verification algorithm, the separate root inputs, and
1073	   the error semantics that name a missing or mismatched pin.  This
1074	   section states those for every signed object in the profile.

1076	   The gap is not theoretical.  A verifier that checks a Spend Receipt
1077	   against the key the receipt carries accepts a receipt signed by any
1078	   key at all, including one an attacker minted for the occasion.  Such
1079	   a receipt matches a settlement the attacker was never authorised to
1080	   make, the settlement stops looking uncovered, and the audit reports
1081	   nothing.  The completeness property in Section 11 is then computed
1082	   over evidence that answers to nobody.

1084	10.1.  The issuer root

1086	   A verifier MUST obtain the issuer's public key out of band and MUST
1087	   verify Spend Receipt and epoch checkpoint signatures against that
1088	   key.  A key the object carries is not that key: it establishes no
1089	   signer identity and is not a fallback for a pin the verifier does not
1090	   hold (MUST-T4-9, MUST-T4-11).  Where no issuer key is held, a
1091	   signature checked against the key its own object carries establishes
1092	   internal consistency and nothing more, which is the state Section 6.3
1093	   and the last row of the table below describe.  A verifier that holds
1094	   no such key and is presented with any Spend Receipt or epoch
1095	   checkpoint MUST treat the completeness guarantee as conditional and
1096	   SHOULD report the condition; the identifier unauthenticated-issuer is
1097	   used for it in this implementation.

1099	   The condition names those two objects because an audit given no
1100	   receipts and no checkpoints rests on the extract alone.  There the
1101	   absent issuer root withholds nothing, and warning about a root the
1102	   audit never consulted would spend the warning where it carries no
1103	   information.

1105	   Reporting a mismatch is not sufficient on its own.  A receipt that
1106	   does not answer to the pinned issuer key MUST NOT count as coverage
1107	   for the settlement it names, and the settlement MUST still be
1108	   reported as uncovered (MUST-T4-10).  A verifier that reports the
1109	   mismatch and then lets the receipt match the settlement anyway has
1110	   described the attack in its output while still concluding that the
1111	   books balance.

1113	   Keys are compared as bytes, by their SubjectPublicKeyInfo DER
1114	   encoding, on the same terms as MUST-T10-9.  A pinned issuer key the
1115	   verifier cannot decode is a fault in its own configuration and MUST
1116	   be reported as trust-key-unreadable rather than as a mismatch against
1117	   the objects; where no pinned key can be decoded at all, nothing is
1118	   attested and the verifier MUST NOT fall back to accepting the keys
1119	   the objects carry (MUST-T4-11).  Falling back is how a mistyped
1120	   configuration becomes a bypass.

1122	   Membership in the attested set follows one rule: the signature
1123	   verifies under a pinned issuer key.  The kid header routes the check
1124	   to a candidate key; the key an object carries beside its signature is
1125	   not an identity source, because it travels outside the signed octets
1126	   and anyone can rewrite it.  Two consequences are stated so that
1127	   implementations do not diverge on them.  First, an honestly signed
1128	   object whose carried key was swapped stays attested: the swap is
1129	   reported (carried-key-mismatch, a warning) and MUST NOT move the
1130	   object out of the attested set or change the verdict, on the same
1131	   reasoning as Section 5.1 - a surface the signature does not cover
1132	   must not be able to manufacture a negative result.  Second, an object
1133	   that claims the pin, by its carried key or its kid, but does not
1134	   verify under it is excluded from the attested set and MUST still be
1135	   walked and named - for a receipt, receipt-chain-break with a
1136	   signature-failed detail - never silently dropped; an object that
1137	   neither verifies under the pin nor claims it is issuer-key-mismatch,
1138	   excluded, and the settlement it names stays uncovered.

1187	   A verifier MUST accept an issuer root that is a set of keys rather

1196	10.2.  The payee root

1198	   The optional countersignature in Section 5.1 travels beside the
1199	   issuer signature without being covered by it.  Anyone holding an
1200	   honest receipt can append a countersignature of their own, so a
1201	   verifier that checks it against the key carried next to it learns
1202	   only that some key signed something.

1204	   A countersignature MUST NOT be treated as evidence that the payee
1205	   approved the payment unless it verifies against a payee key the
1206	   verifier obtained out of band (MUST-T4-13).  Without such a key the
1207	   verifier SHOULD report the condition and MUST treat the guarantee as
1208	   conditional.

1210	   Naming a payee key states an expectation, and an expectation that
1211	   only fires when the evidence is present can be cancelled by deleting
1212	   the evidence.  Where a verifier has pinned a key for a payee, a
1213	   settled receipt naming that payee and carrying no *attributable*
1214	   countersignature MUST be reported (MUST-T4-14): a countersignature
1215	   that failed to verify, or verified under some other key, is discarded
1216	   under Section 5.1 and leaves the expectation open exactly as a
1217	   missing one does.  Otherwise an attacker removes their own failed
1218	   forgery - or appends one - and the report returns to unconditional.
1219	   The discarded object itself is a warning, never a failure of the
1220	   receipt it rode beside; Section 5.1 states the invariant.

1222	10.3.  The witness root

1224	   Section 11.3 describes what a transparency witness adds.  An
1225	   inclusion receipt checked against the key it carries says that some
1226	   log is internally consistent, and a log is cheap to invent.

1228	   A verifier MUST obtain the transparency service's public key out of
1229	   band and MUST verify inclusion receipts against it (MUST-T11-15).
1230	   Inclusion receipts that cannot be checked that way MUST NOT be used
1231	   as evidence, in either direction: they cannot establish that a
1232	   checkpoint was anchored, and they cannot establish that one was
1233	   withheld.  A verifier SHOULD report that it left them out.

1235	   A log holds statements from everyone who uses it.  A statement held
1236	   by a pinned log MUST additionally answer to the issuer root before it
1237	   counts as something that issuer published (MUST-T11-16); otherwise
1238	   another user's epoch, sitting in a shared log, reads as this issuer
1239	   publishing two checkpoints for one epoch.

1241	   Anchoring and withholding are different claims and need different
1242	   evidence.  Establishing that a checkpoint was logged needs only the
1243	   statement hash.  Establishing that an issuer withheld one needs to
1244	   know whose statement it is, which an inclusion receipt carrying no
1245	   statement body cannot say.  Such a receipt MUST NOT be used to report
1246	   a withheld checkpoint, and MUST NOT be silently discarded either: a
1247	   real withholding must not be buried by removing the body (MUST-
1248	   T11-17).

1250	10.4.  The decision root

1252	   A Decision Token is issued by the policy decision point and consumed
1253	   by the same deployment.  The consumer therefore holds the key it
1254	   signs with and has no reason to ask the token which key to check it
1255	   against.  A consumer MUST verify a Decision Token against its own
1256	   issuing key and MUST NOT accept one it cannot check that way (MUST-
1257	   T6-6).

1259	10.5.  The manifest root

1261	   A Trade Manifest is optional.  A deployment that presents none is not
1262	   missing a root, and this requirement does not make such an audit
1263	   conditional.  The forbidden case is the other one: a manifest is
1264	   presented, and the verifier accepts it because the key travelling
1265	   inside it verifies against itself.

1267	   A verifier that is presented with a Trade Manifest MUST obtain the
1268	   publisher's public key out of band and MUST verify the manifest
1269	   signature against that key, not against a key the manifest carries
1270	   (MUST-T4-15).  A verifier without such a key that is presented with a
1271	   Trade Manifest MUST report the completeness guarantee as conditional
1272	   and SHOULD report the condition; the identifier unauthenticated-
1273	   manifest is used for it in this implementation.  An audit presented
1274	   with no Trade Manifest is not made conditional by this requirement.

1276	   A pinned manifest key the verifier cannot decode is a fault in its
1277	   own configuration and MUST be reported as trust-key-unreadable rather
1278	   than as a mismatch, on the same terms as MUST-T4-11.  A manifest that
1279	   does not verify against a readable pin MUST be reported as manifest-
1280	   key-mismatch and MUST fail the audit.  Falling back to the key the
1281	   manifest carries is how a presented document becomes a bypass.

1283	   Attribution is one question and coverage is another, and a root that
1284	   answers only the first leaves the document doing work it was never
1285	   spent under.  A verifier presented with a Trade Manifest MUST compare
1286	   the manifest hash to the manifestHash of the receipts presented to
1287	   the audit and MUST report a manifest that no presented receipt
1288	   references (MUST-T4-17); the identifier manifest-covers-no-receipt is
1289	   used for it in this implementation, and the completeness guarantee is
1290	   conditional.  The comparison runs against those presented receipts,
1291	   including aborted ones, and is made before any extract window is
1292	   applied and before any issuer key is applied; a hash on an aborted
1293	   receipt, on a receipt outside the extract window, or on a receipt no
1294	   pinned key attests still counts as a reference.  This requirement
1295	   asks whether any receipt names the terms, not whether a settlement in
1296	   the window was made under them, and not whether the receipt that
1297	   names them is attributable.  A forged receipt can therefore silence
1298	   this warning.  That is accepted: what it silences is a statement that
1299	   terms were named, and the report it leaves behind is still marked
1300	   conditional and still carries the finding that the receipt answers to
1301	   no pinned key.  A correctly attributed manifest travelling beside a
1302	   set of receipts marked noManifest states terms nothing presented was
1303	   spent under, and a report that stays silent about it reads as terms-
1304	   backed when it is not.  This requirement does not reach the audit
1305	   presented with no Trade Manifest, which remains a deployment choice
1306	   under MUST-T1-2.

1308	   Naming a manifest is not obeying one, and that is the third place
1309	   this document has had to say the same thing twice.  A verifier
1310	   presented with a Trade Manifest MUST compare the amount, the currency
1311	   and the settlement time of every receipt that names it against the
1312	   manifest's amount, currency and expiry, and MUST report a receipt
1313	   that departs from them (MUST-T8-9); the identifier manifest-terms-
1314	   mismatch is used for it in this implementation.  Every receipt that
1315	   names the manifest is measured, aborted ones included: an aborted
1316	   receipt that carries the hash of terms and a departing amount
1317	   recorded an attempt against terms it misstates, and MUST-T4-17 next
1318	   door already counts it as a reference.  The time compared is the
1319	   receipt's timestampMs, against the boundary MUST-T3-3 states:
1320	   strictly after expiresAtMs departs, exactly at it does not.  Amount
1321	   and currency are compared on the exact-octet terms of MUST-T8-2 - the
1322	   audit asks whether the gate's own rules were kept, so it compares the
1323	   way the gate compares.

1325	   Where a usable issuer key is pinned, the comparison is made over the
1326	   receipts that verify under it and the audit fails.  Where none is
1327	   pinned, the departure is still reported and the audit does not fail
1328	   on it alone.  An issuer key is usable when the pinned issuer root
1329	   holds at least one key the verifier can decode.  A pinned root none
1330	   of whose keys decode is already trust-key-unreadable and attests
1331	   nothing, so the comparison takes the unpinned branch while that
1332	   finding stands; the audit has failed on the configuration fault, and
1333	   the departure is still said out loud without becoming a charge no
1334	   readable key backs. -03 stated the first case for both, and an
1335	   implementation showed why that is wrong: a receipt signed by any key
1336	   at all, carrying the right manifest hash and the wrong amount, made
1337	   the verifier report a breach that never happened, against a payment
1338	   reference the forger chose.  Reporting a departure costs nothing if
1339	   it is unattributable; failing an audit on it hands an attacker a way
1340	   to accuse an honest payer.

1342	   MUST-T4-17 and this requirement therefore differ, and the difference
1343	   is not an inconsistency.  Asking whether a manifest hash appears
1344	   anywhere is a question about a set of documents, and an
1345	   unattributable document is still in that set.  Saying that a named
1346	   party broke terms it signed is a charge, and a charge needs a key
1347	   behind it.

1349	   Only receipts that name the manifest are measured against it: reading
1350	   the terms onto a receipt that never claimed them would invent a
1351	   violation the payer did not commit.

1353	   The rules being enforced here are not new.  A gate already refuses a
1354	   bound spend whose amount or currency differs from the manifest (MUST-
1355	   T8-2) and one made against an expired manifest (MUST-T3-3).  Both
1356	   were written for the point where money moves, and an audit reads the
1357	   record after that point, where the gate is no longer present to be
1358	   asked.  Without a counterpart a receipt can carry the hash of terms
1359	   it breaks and the report still calls the books balanced.  This is a
1360	   finding rather than a condition on the guarantee: a verifier that
1361	   reports it held every root it needed, and the statement it is making
1362	   is unconditional.

1364	   The gate answers differently from the audit, and the difference is
1365	   deliberate.  A policy decision point presented with a Trade Manifest
1366	   it cannot attribute MUST refuse the payment rather than settle and
1367	   record the doubt (MUST-T4-16).  An audit describes what it found and
1368	   may say the result is conditional; a gate decides whether money
1369	   moves, and a settled payment carrying the hash of terms nobody
1370	   authorised cannot be withdrawn by reporting it afterwards.  The
1371	   receipt would record those terms as agreed.

1373	10.6.  What the roots do not cover

1375	   A verifier that supplies none of these roots is not making an error,
1376	   and this document does not require it to.  It is making a weaker
1377	   statement, and the guarantee it reports must say so.  With no issuer
1378	   key nothing distinguishes one submitted receipt from another, so
1379	   conditions computed across the submitted set - two receipts claiming
1380	   one settlement reference, for instance - cannot be attributed to
1381	   anyone and are reported as conditions of the submission rather than
1382	   as failures of a party.

1384	11.  Reconciliation and Epoch Checkpoints

1386	   Completeness is the property that, given an authenticated rail
1387	   extract, every settlement in the extract has a matching settled Spend
1388	   Receipt, every settled receipt has a matching settlement, receipt and
1389	   checkpoint hash chains verify, and checkpoint totals equal the sum of
1390	   *settled* receipts in the checkpoint window.  If a spend occurred
1391	   without a receipt, the missing receipt is itself the evidence (MUST-
1392	   T10-2).

1394	   The property is stated over a population, and the extract is what
1395	   declares it: one account, on one rail, over one window (Section 9).
1396	   "Every settlement" means every settlement that extract carried.  A
1397	   settlement path no presented extract covers is not reconciled and not
1398	   found missing - it is outside the population - so the report names
1399	   the account, rail and window it was computed over (MUST-T10-19), and
1400	   a verifier that stated none of them says so instead (MUST-T10-18).

1402	   A checkpoint published with its totals withheld (Section 11.1) cannot
1403	   contribute the last of those to the property.  It is not a violation
1404	   of completeness and it is not a demonstration of it either: the
1405	   comparison was not made, and a result that rests on a comparison
1406	   nobody made is conditional (MUST-T11-12).

1408	11.1.  Checkpoint claims

1410	   An epoch checkpoint MUST be COSE_Sign1-signed with the header profile
1411	   in Section 6 and MUST bind all of the following (MUST-T11-1):

1413	   epoch, startMs, endMs, receiptCount, chainHeadHash, totals, and
1414	   prevCheckpointHash.

1416	   The checkpoint window is half-open [startMs, endMs) (MUST-T11-7).
1417	   receiptCount MUST equal the number of receipts (settled and aborted)
1418	   whose timestampMs falls in that window. chainHeadHash MUST equal
1419	   receiptHash of the last receipt in that window - the last link, in
1420	   issuer order (the prevReceiptHash chain, as the verification
1421	   algorithm's step 6 defines it), of the chain inside the window, not
1422	   the last one presented or the latest timestampMs - or null if the
1423	   window is empty (MUST-T11-2).  Where totals is present it MUST sum
1424	   only receipts with outcome = settled; the one permitted absence is
1425	   the signed redaction below.

1427	   An issuer that publishes a checkpoint without its totals MUST encode
1428	   totals as null in the signed payload (MUST-T11-12).  An empty map is
1429	   an honest zero for an empty window and is not a redaction.  Because
1430	   the redaction is inside the signature, it cannot be added to, or
1431	   removed from, a checkpoint after signing.

1433	   A verifier MUST NOT accept a redaction asserted anywhere but the
1434	   signed payload (MUST-T11-13).  A presentation-layer flag alongside a
1435	   checkpoint is chosen by whoever presents it, which is the party under
1436	   audit; honouring such a flag would let that party switch off the
1437	   totals comparison for a checkpoint whose signed totals are wrong.
1438	   The structural claims (epoch, startMs, endMs, receiptCount,
1439	   chainHeadHash, prevCheckpointHash) MUST NOT be redacted: a checkpoint
1440	   missing any of them does not decode, and a verifier MUST treat it as
1441	   a failed checkpoint rather than as a redacted one.

1443	11.2.  Genesis and continuity

1445	   The first checkpoint in a presented chain is the genesis checkpoint
1446	   of that chain.  Its prevCheckpointHash MUST be null.  Epoch numbers
1447	   MUST be consecutive integers.  Adjacent windows MUST satisfy
1448	   next.startMs = prev.endMs (MUST-T11-8).

1450	   A later checkpoint that omits a prefix of earlier epochs (prefix
1451	   deletion) is detectable only if an external witness (transparency
1452	   log) has recorded the missing prefix (MUST-T11-9).  Without that
1453	   witness, T11 guarantees about suppression are *conditional*.

1455	   -01 stated that dependency and stopped there: nothing in its
1456	   verification algorithm read a witness receipt, so the witness had no
1457	   way to speak. -02 gave it one.  A verifier that holds witness
1458	   receipts for the period under audit compares what the witness
1459	   recorded against what the chain presented, and reports the difference
1460	   under its own name (Section 11.3).  A witness that holds a checkpoint
1461	   the presented chain omits is not the same condition as a chain that
1462	   leaves a gap in its own coverage, and the two MUST NOT be reported
1463	   under one identifier (MUST-T11-11).  The first says evidence is being
1464	   withheld; the second says the evidence shown is incomplete.  An
1465	   operator who cannot tell them apart cannot tell an incomplete record
1466	   from a concealed one.

1468	11.3.  The transparency witness

1470	   A checkpoint registered with a Transparency Service [RFC9943] is a
1471	   Signed Statement whose payload is the checkpoint COSE object and
1472	   whose content type is application/cedulon-checkpoint+cbor
1473	   (Section 14).  What comes back, and what it proves, is stated as two
1474	   named tiers, because -04 promised the mechanics of one tier while
1475	   describing the checks of the other.

1477	   *Tier 1 - the witness receipt.* The witness returns a co-signature
1478	   over the statement hash of what it recorded: a COSE_Sign1 whose
1479	   payload binds the statement hash, the entry index, and the witness's
1480	   tree head.  Verifying it establishes exactly one sentence - "the
1481	   witness signed for this hash" - and nothing more; in particular it
1482	   does not establish membership in an append-only log. -04 called this
1483	   object a transparency receipt and cited the verification mechanics of
1484	   [RFC9942] for it while describing a hash comparison; the admission in
1485	   Section 18, that the receipt was a signature over a statement rather
1486	   than a proof of log membership, was correct, and the protocol text
1487	   now says the same thing.  The [RFC9942] citation applies in tier 2,
1488	   where its mechanics are actually performed.

1490	   On the wire the witness receipt is a COSE_Sign1 under Section 6,
1491	   signed by the witness key, with content type application/cedulon-
1492	   inclusion+cbor (Section 17.6).  Its payload is a deterministic CBOR
1493	   map carrying three entries and is not a CWT claim set: label 1 is the
1494	   statement hash, label 2 the entry index, label 3 the tree head.  The
1495	   two hashes are text strings holding the lowercase hexadecimal of a
1496	   32-octet SHA-256, the statement hash taken over the octets of the
1497	   statement the witness recorded; the entry index is an unsigned
1498	   integer.  The hash strings are compared as issued and are not passed
1499	   through the hash-claim grammar the claim sets use; the revision that
1500	   moves them to byte strings will close that difference.  Entries under
1501	   other labels are not defined, and a verifier of this revision does
1502	   not refuse them.  The map carries no statement body: a receipt that
1503	   travels with the body carries it beside the COSE object, not inside
1504	   it (Section 10.3, MUST-T11-17).  A receipt whose content type is
1505	   another value, or whose payload lacks one of the three entries or
1506	   carries one of another type, does not verify: the content type is the
1507	   check Section 6 makes on every object of this profile, and the
1508	   payload map is the one this paragraph states.

1510	   A verifier MAY be given witness receipts for the period under audit.
1511	   It is a distinct input from the presented checkpoint chain, and
1512	   supplying it is optional: a verifier given none performs the same
1513	   steps, and reports the same findings, that it would if this input did
1514	   not exist (MUST-T11-10).  Supplying an empty set is not the same as
1515	   supplying none.  An empty set says a witness is configured and
1516	   recorded nothing, which is itself reportable; absence says no witness
1517	   was consulted.

1519	   A receipt binds a statement hash, not a statement.  The body is not
1520	   carried by the receipt and a verifier will often not hold it.  Two
1521	   levels of checking follow from that, and they are not the same (MUST-
1522	   T11-10).

1524	   Every receipt MUST have its signature verified before it counts for
1525	   anything.  That is what establishes the hash as one the service
1526	   signed for, and it is all that comparing recorded hashes against
1527	   presented ones requires: the verifier computes the statement hash of
1528	   each presented checkpoint itself.

1530	   A receipt accompanied by the statement body carries more, and MUST be
1531	   checked further before that body is relied on.  The body's statement
1532	   hash MUST equal the hash the receipt binds, and the body MUST itself
1533	   verify as a checkpoint.  A body that fails either check proves
1534	   nothing and MUST be ignored rather than counted, while the receipt it
1535	   came with remains usable for the hash comparison.  Equivocation is
1536	   the case that needs a body, because it compares claims rather than
1537	   hashes.

1539	   *Tier 2 - log membership.* A verifier MAY additionally be given, for
1540	   one recorded statement, the registered Signed Statement bytes (the
1541	   candidate entry) and an inclusion proof.  When both are present, the
1542	   verifier MUST perform the verification of [RFC9942] Section 5.2.1
1543	   over them: hash the candidate entry bytes to obtain the leaf, apply
1544	   the proof to reproduce a root, and accept only when the leaf hash,
1545	   the proof's leaf index, and the reproduced root are all equal to the
1546	   statement hash, entry index, and tree head of one witness receipt
1547	   that verifies under the pinned witness key (MUST-T11-18).
1548	   Reproducing a root is deliberately not sufficient on its own: the
1549	   proof format below carries no domain separation between leaves and
1550	   interior nodes, and the exact match against a witness-signed receipt
1551	   is what closes the ambiguities that follow from that.  A pair that
1552	   fails this check, or a candidate entry presented without a proof,
1553	   MUST be reported as a failing finding; the identifier witness-
1554	   inclusion-invalid names it.

1556	   The inclusion proof is an audit path: the leaf index, and the sibling
1557	   hashes from the leaf's level up to the root, lowest level first.  The
1558	   tree is built over statement hashes as leaves; an interior node is
1559	   the SHA-256 of the concatenation of its two children's 32 raw bytes;
1560	   a level with an odd count pairs its last node with itself, so the
1561	   path needs no separate leaf count.

1563	   Where witness receipts are supplied and this pair is not, tier 2 was
1564	   not exercised, and the report MUST say so rather than letting the
1565	   tier pass silently (MUST-T11-19); the identifier witness-inclusion-
1566	   not-exercised names it, as a warning - the witness attested the
1567	   statement hash, and log membership was not proven.  A pair that
1568	   verifies is silent, like every other passing check in the algorithm.

1570	   What tier 1 establishes, and what it does not, is worth stating
1571	   plainly.  It establishes that the service signed for that statement.
1572	   Whether the statement is a member of an append-only log is tier 2's
1573	   question, answered only where tier 2's inputs were supplied and
1574	   verified; whether the log has ever equivocated remains a property of
1575	   the service and its own proofs.  A verifier that treats a tier-1
1576	   receipt alone as proof of log membership is claiming more than the
1577	   receipt carries.

1579	11.4.  Verification algorithm

1581	   A verifier MUST perform all of these steps and MUST report every
1582	   finding they produce (MUST-T10-1, MUST-T11-2).  They are numbered for
1583	   reference, not to require an evaluation order: no step short-circuits
1584	   another, and an implementation may evaluate them in any order that
1585	   produces the same set of findings.

1587	   The data dependencies are named, because "any order" read naively
1588	   would break them.  Step 15 decides which witness receipts, and which
1589	   statement bodies, survive checking; steps 14 and 16 consume what
1590	   survives.  Step 7's index of refs is what steps 8 and 9 reconcile.
1591	   Step 11 decides which checkpoints verified, and step 14 compares only
1592	   those together with what step 15 admitted.  An implementation that
1593	   ran a consumer against an unchecked producer would not produce the
1594	   same set of findings, so those orders are not among the permitted
1595	   ones.

1597	   The second is the issuer pin.  The step that resolves it decides the
1598	   *attested set* - the receipts and checkpoints that verify under a
1599	   usable pinned issuer key, or the whole presented set when no usable
1600	   key is pinned - and every later step that walks receipts or
1601	   checkpoints consumes that set: the chain walk in step 6, the indexing
1602	   and reconciliation in steps 7 through 9, the checkpoint comparisons
1603	   in steps 11 through 13, and the MUST-T8-9 comparison.  A receipt the
1604	   pin rejects is reported once and then excluded, which is what keeps
1605	   the settlement it names visible as uncovered (MUST-T4-10); an
1606	   implementation that let it back into any of those steps would let a
1607	   forged receipt cover a settlement, satisfy a checkpoint count, or
1608	   invent a terms charge.  Two checks deliberately stay on the presented
1609	   set whatever any key says, and MUST NOT acquire the dependency: MUST-
1610	   T4-17, which asks whether a manifest was named at all, and the per-
1611	   receipt defect checks that ask what a receipt says about itself. -04
1612	   asserted that nothing else in the list fed another step, which the
1613	   dependencies above had already made false; the assertion is withdrawn
1614	   and the list above is the inventory, maintained rather than
1615	   summarised.

1617	   When a step names an identifier in backticks, that identifier SHOULD
1618	   be used for the condition in diagnostic output.  The normative
1619	   requirement is the behaviour: report the condition, identified by the
1620	   ref or other handle given in the step.  The identifiers are not an
1621	   interoperability surface.

1623	   1.   Establish the subject of the audit.  When an extract is
1624	        supplied, the settlement records it carries are the ones
1625	        reconciled; a settlement list from any other source MUST NOT be
1626	        substituted for them (MUST-T10-12).  If the caller supplies both
1627	        and they differ, the verifier MUST report that the caller-
1628	        supplied list disagrees with the extract, and MUST still
1629	        reconcile the extract.  The identifier extract-settlement-
1630	        mismatch SHOULD be used for this condition in diagnostic output.

1632	   2.   Verify the extract signature against the out-of-band rail key
1633	        (MUST-T10-8, MUST-T10-9).  If no key is pinned, the check that
1634	        runs is against the key the extract carries, which establishes
1635	        internal consistency and not origin; the verifier MUST still
1636	        compute it, MUST NOT read it as a statement about who produced
1637	        the extract, and MUST treat the completeness guarantee as
1638	        conditional (MUST-T10-7).  The identifier unauthenticated-
1639	        extract SHOULD be used for this condition in diagnostic output,
1640	        whatever the extract carries.  If a key is pinned and cannot be
1641	        decoded, the verifier MUST report that the pinned key is
1642	        unreadable.  The identifier trust-key-unreadable SHOULD be used
1643	        for this condition.  If a key is pinned and the signature does
1644	        not verify against it, or verifies against a different key, the
1645	        verifier MUST report that the extract is not signed by the
1646	        pinned key.  The identifier extract-key-mismatch SHOULD be used
1647	        for this condition.  The rows of an extract the pin refused are
1648	        not reconciled against the receipts, and no settlement finding
1649	        is read out of that document (MUST-T10-20); the identifier
1650	        settlement-comparison-skipped SHOULD be used to say that the
1651	        comparison did not run.  A finding that puts the extract itself
1652	        in doubt MUST prevent an unconditional guarantee.

1654	   3.   Check scope.  The verifier MUST report each settlement record
1655	        whose timestampMs falls outside the declared window, identified
1656	        by that record's ref (MUST-T10-10).  When the verifier states an
1657	        expected account, rail, or window, it MUST report an extract
1658	        that does not cover it (MUST-T10-11).  The identifier extract-
1659	        scope-mismatch SHOULD be used for both conditions.  If the
1660	        verifier stated no period, it MUST treat the guarantee as
1661	        conditional (MUST-T10-15).  The identifier unstated-audit-window
1662	        SHOULD be used for this condition.  If it stated no account or
1663	        no rail, it MUST treat the guarantee as conditional for the same
1664	        reason (MUST-T10-18); the identifier unstated-audit-scope SHOULD
1665	        be used.  Whatever the verdict, the report MUST name the
1666	        account, rail and window the extract declared, in every
1667	        structure it returns for the audit (MUST-T10-19).

1669	   4.   Resolve each Spend Receipt against the issuer root
1670	        (Section 10.1) in one pass.  Decode the COSE_Sign1; a content
1671	        type that is not the receipt type, a decoder bound, or a decoded
1672	        claim map that does not match the presented claims is a named
1673	        refusal, not a signature verdict (MUST-T4-2, MUST-T4-8).  Then
1674	        ask one question: does the signature verify under a pinned
1675	        issuer key. kid routes the check to a candidate key and carries
1676	        no authority of its own; the carried key is not consulted for
1677	        membership at all.  The resolution table in Section 10.1 reads
1678	        the answer out per cell.  Every cell there is a named condition
1679	        plus a membership decision; no cell is a silent removal, and the
1680	        word "reject" in earlier revisions meant nothing more than a
1681	        cell of that table.  Where a countersignature is present,
1682	        Section 10.2 governs what it establishes (MUST-T4-13, MUST-
1683	        T4-14).  Where a Trade Manifest is presented, Section 10.5
1684	        governs it (MUST-T4-15): with no publisher key pinned the
1685	        verifier reports unauthenticated-manifest and the guarantee is
1686	        conditional; with a pin that cannot be read, trust-key-
1687	        unreadable; with a pin the manifest does not answer to,
1688	        manifest-key-mismatch; and with a manifest that no presented
1689	        receipt references, manifest-covers-no-receipt (MUST-T4-17).  A
1690	        receipt that names the manifest but departs from its amount,
1691	        currency, expiry or, where the manifest names one, payee is
1692	        reported as manifest-terms-mismatch; with a usable issuer key
1693	        pinned the comparison runs over the attested receipts and the
1694	        departure fails the audit, and with no usable issuer key it is a
1695	        warning over the presented receipts and does not by itself fail
1696	        the audit (MUST-T8-9).  An audit presented with no Trade
1697	        Manifest is not made conditional by this step.

1699	   5.   Scope the receipts.  Membership follows the ref binding first: a
1700	        receipt whose ref appears on the extract is reconciled against
1701	        this extract even when its own timestampMs falls outside the
1702	        declared window - the rail has signed that the settlement
1703	        belongs to the window, and the receipt follows its settlement.
1704	        The timestampMs sieve applies only to receipts the extract does
1705	        not name: such a receipt outside the window is not a
1706	        completeness failure against this extract (MUST-T10-16);
1707	        auditing a longer period requires extracts that cover it.  At
1708	        the window's edges the declared allowance applies (Section 9):
1709	        an unmatched settled receipt within clockSkewMs of windowEndMs,
1710	        and an unmatched settlement record within clockSkewMs of
1711	        windowStartMs, are reported as boundary-deferred, a warning,
1712	        rather than as step 8's completeness findings - two honest
1713	        clocks can disagree by less than the allowance, and both
1714	        verifiers of an honest edge payment would otherwise reach the
1715	        same false accusation (MUST-T10-17).  Where the following
1716	        window's extract is presented and verifies, a deferred receipt
1717	        whose ref appears on it is resolved and not reported, and one
1718	        whose ref does not appear hardens into the step 8 finding; a
1719	        deferred settlement record near the opening edge resolves
1720	        through this step's ref binding, since the prior window's
1721	        receipt that names its ref is reconciled here regardless of
1722	        timestamp.  The following window's extract closes or hardens
1723	        closing-edge deferrals only; an opening-edge record stays
1724	        deferred until a receipt in the presented bag names its ref,
1725	        whether or not a following extract is presented.  In a single-
1726	        window audit a deferred record keeps the guarantee conditional.
1727	        Receipts remain subject to every other check regardless of
1728	        window.

1730	   6.   Walk the attested receipts in issuer order.  Issuer order is the
1731	        order induced by the prevReceiptHash chain: the verifier
1732	        rebuilds the chain from the links, and the order in which
1733	        receipts were presented carries no weight. timestampMs is
1734	        issuer-asserted and is not an ordering source.  The first
1735	        prevReceiptHash MUST be null.  Each later prevReceiptHash MUST
1736	        equal receiptHash of the previous receipt.  A miss, and a
1737	        receipt the links cannot place, MUST be reported as a break in
1738	        the receipt chain.  The identifier receipt-chain-break SHOULD be
1739	        used for this condition.

1741	   7.   Index the attested settled receipts and extract records by ref.
1742	        A ref that appears more than once on either side MUST be
1743	        reported as a repeated reference (MUST-T10-6).  The identifier
1744	        duplicate-ref SHOULD be used for this condition.

1746	   8.   For each ref that appears exactly once on each side, require a
1747	        one-to-one match on ref AND amount AND currency (MUST-T10-1),
1748	        compared as exact octets on the terms of MUST-T8-2; step 9's
1749	        aggregation is the only place this algorithm reads an amount as
1750	        a number.  Amount or currency mismatch MUST be reported as a
1751	        settlement that does not match its receipt, identified by that
1752	        ref.  The identifier settlement-mismatch SHOULD be used for this
1753	        condition.  A settlement with no receipt MUST be reported as
1754	        lacking a receipt, identified by its ref (MUST-T10-2).  The
1755	        identifier settlement-without-receipt SHOULD be used for this
1756	        condition.  A settled receipt with no extract row MUST be
1757	        reported as a completeness failure (MUST-T10-3).  The identifier
1758	        receipt-without-settlement SHOULD be used for this condition.  A
1759	        settled receipt with a null rail ref MUST be reported as settled
1760	        without a rail reference; this check asks what a receipt says
1761	        about itself and runs over the presented receipts, attested or
1762	        not.  The identifier settled-without-ref SHOULD be used for this
1763	        condition.  Where a settlement record declares a beneficiary
1764	        (Section 9), it MUST be compared against the matched receipt's
1765	        payee as exact octets; a difference is beneficiary-mismatch and
1766	        fails the audit.  Where neither the manifest names a payee nor
1767	        any settlement record declares a beneficiary, the report MUST
1768	        carry counterparty-unbound, a scope record: ref, amount and
1769	        currency closed against the payer's account extract, and the
1770	        counterparty's identity was not bound.  It is a statement of
1771	        what the evidence did not cover, not a doubt about what it did,
1772	        so it does not move the verdict and does not by itself make the
1773	        guarantee conditional.

1775	   9.   A ref already reported as repeating MUST still be reconciled by
1776	        amount rather than dropped from the comparison (MUST-T10-13).
1777	        For each currency under that ref, compare the total settled
1778	        against the total receipted.  A settled total that exceeds the
1779	        receipted total MUST be reported as a settlement lacking a
1780	        receipt, and the finding MUST state the unaccounted amount.  The
1781	        identifier settlement-without-receipt SHOULD be used for this
1782	        condition.  A settled total that is less than the receipted
1783	        total MUST be reported as a settlement that does not match its
1784	        receipt, identified by that ref.  The identifier settlement-
1785	        mismatch SHOULD be used for this condition.  An amount on that
1786	        repeating ref that cannot be parsed as an integer MUST be
1787	        reported without abandoning the audit; the identifier malformed-
1788	        amount SHOULD be used for this condition.  A verifier MUST still
1789	        report findings for the remaining records.

1791	   10.  Aborted receipts are not matched to extract rows and are not
1792	        added to totals.

1794	   11.  Decode each checkpoint.  Reject a failed signature, and reject a
1795	        kid that does not match the key obtained for the checkpoint
1796	        issuer, on the same terms as a receipt (MUST-T4-8).  Require
1797	        receiptCount, chainHeadHash, and totals to match the attested
1798	        receipts in [startMs, endMs) as defined above (MUST-T11-2); a
1799	        receipt step 4 rejected is not among them, or a forged receipt
1800	        could satisfy a checkpoint count.  The identifier checkpoint-
1801	        total-mismatch SHOULD be used for a failed signature, a wrong
1802	        receiptCount, or totals that disagree, and checkpoint-head-
1803	        mismatch for a chainHeadHash that is not the last link, in
1804	        issuer order (step 6), of the chain inside [startMs, endMs) -
1805	        "last receipt" binds to the chain, not to presentation or to
1806	        timestampMs.  If the signed totals is null, the verifier cannot
1807	        perform the totals comparison for that checkpoint.  It MUST
1808	        report that the comparison was skipped and MUST treat the
1809	        completeness guarantee as conditional; the absence of a
1810	        comparison is not a passed comparison (MUST-T11-12).  The
1811	        identifier checkpoint-totals-redacted SHOULD be used for this
1812	        condition. receiptCount and chainHeadHash MUST still be checked.
1813	        A checkpoint that fails verification MUST NOT be treated as
1814	        redacted, whatever it claims about its own totals.

1816	   12.  Every chained receipt MUST fall in exactly one checkpoint
1817	        window.  A gap or double count MUST be reported as a window
1818	        coverage failure (MUST-T11-7, MUST-T11-8).  The identifier
1819	        window-coverage SHOULD be used for this condition.  The check
1820	        runs against the presented checkpoint windows and it is fail-
1821	        closed: a receipt that falls under none of them is this failure
1822	        - including every receipt when no checkpoint was presented, and
1823	        receipts after the last closed checkpoint's endMs.  The text
1824	        names that last state an *open epoch*, and it is auditable only
1825	        when the checkpoint that closes it is issued; the name explains
1826	        why the finding fired, it does not soften it.  Nothing about an
1827	        absent checkpoint is silent: uncovered evidence surfaces as a
1828	        finding rather than as a gap in the report.

1830	   13.  Walk checkpoints in epoch order. prevCheckpointHash MUST equal
1831	        the SHA-256 of the previous checkpoint COSE bytes, or null for
1832	        genesis (MUST-T11-4).  The identifier checkpoint-total-mismatch
1833	        SHOULD be used for a broken chain, which is the fourth condition
1834	        its table row names.

1836	   14.  If two successfully verified checkpoints share an epoch number
1837	        and have different hashes, the verifier MUST report equivocation
1838	        (MUST-T11-3).  The identifier equivocation SHOULD be used for
1839	        this condition.  The checkpoints compared here are those
1840	        presented *together with* any carried by verified witness
1841	        receipts (step 15).  Comparing only the presented chain cannot
1842	        raise this finding: MUST-T11-8, applied in step 12, requires
1843	        that chain's epochs to be consecutive, so no two of its members
1844	        share an epoch.  A copy recorded by a witness is where the
1845	        second one is found.

1847	   15.  If witness receipts were supplied, verify them against the out-
1848	        of-band witness key (Section 10.3); receipts that cannot be
1849	        checked that way are not evidence in either direction and the
1850	        verifier reports that it left them out (MUST-T11-15); the
1851	        identifier unauthenticated-witness SHOULD be used for this
1852	        condition.  Discard any whose signature fails (MUST-T11-10).  A
1853	        surviving receipt whose statement body verifies against the
1854	        issuer root is a statement that issuer published; one that does
1855	        not is another party's, and is not this issuer equivocating
1856	        (MUST-T11-16).  The survivors are the recorded statement hashes
1857	        used in step 16.  Where a receipt also carries the statement
1858	        body, discard that body unless its statement hash equals the one
1859	        the receipt binds and it verifies as a checkpoint; the surviving
1860	        bodies are what step 14 compares.  Discarding a body does not
1861	        discard its receipt.  This is tier 1 of Section 11.3.  Where the
1862	        tier-2 pair - the registered Signed Statement bytes and an
1863	        inclusion proof - was also supplied, verify it as Section 11.3
1864	        states: apply the proof to the candidate entry's hash and accept
1865	        only on an exact match with a witness-signed receipt (MUST-
1866	        T11-18); a pair that fails, or a candidate without a proof, is
1867	        witness-inclusion-invalid and the audit MUST fail.  Where
1868	        witness receipts were supplied and no tier-2 pair was, report
1869	        witness-inclusion-not-exercised as a warning (MUST-T11-19): the
1870	        witness attested the statement hash, and log membership was not
1871	        proven.

1873	   16.  Compare the surviving witness records against the presented
1874	        chain (MUST-T11-11).  For each presented checkpoint with no
1875	        surviving record, report that it is not anchored; the identifier
1876	        checkpoint-not-anchored SHOULD be used.  This is a warning: a

2291	12.  Lifecycle

2293	   1.  *Manifest.* Parties sign a Trade Manifest (optional for metered
2294	       API spend; required for goods with acceptance criteria).

2296	   2.  *Policy check.* The adapter submits a structured request to the
2297	       PDP.  Default is deny.  An allow is a Decision Token (MUST-T6-4).

2299	   3.  *Payment.* On allow, the adapter performs the x402 (or other
2300	       rail) exchange using exactly the decision fields (MUST-T6-1).
2301	       The Decision Token is consumed (MUST-T6-2).  A reused nonce is
2302	       denied (MUST-T3-1, MUST-T3-2).  A tampered or expired token is
2303	       denied (MUST-T6-5).

2305	   4.  *Receipt.* The Receipt Issuer signs a Spend Receipt.  Rail
2306	       credentials MUST NOT appear in the receipt, logs, or tool results
2307	       (MUST-T5-2, MUST-T7-1).

2309	   5.  *Dispute Evidence Bundle.* If delivery bytes do not match the
2310	       acceptance-criteria hash, an implementation MUST be able to emit
2311	       a bundle of manifest + receipt + delivery hash (MUST-T8-3).  The
2312	       bundle MUST NOT be described as an arbitral award or escrow
2313	       release (MUST-T8-4).

2315	13.  Policy Semantics

2317	   Policy is default deny.  The engine understands three families of
2318	   rule:

2320	   *  *Limit:* maximum amount per payment; maximum cumulative amount per
2321	      window (MUST-T2-2).

2323	   *  *Velocity:* maximum number of allowed payments per window (MUST-
2324	      T2-1).

2326	   *  *Scope:* optional allow-lists for payee, currency, and tool name.

2328	   Fail-closed: missing engine, crash, or exception yields deny (MUST-
2329	   T2-3).  Implementations SHOULD emit stable reason codes (SHOULD-
2330	   T2-5).  Decision tokens SHOULD expire after a short TTL (SHOULD-
2331	   T6-3).

2333	   The agent-facing spend interface MUST invoke the PDP and MUST NOT
2334	   expose a parallel ungated rail call to the model (MUST-T5-1).

2336	   One boundary is stated here rather than left to be inferred.  In the
2337	   retrospective audit, "allowed by policy" is the Receipt Issuer's
2338	   signed assertion: the spend passed the issuer's gate under the
2339	   policyHash the receipt names.  The Decision Token is consumed at the
2340	   gate, and the verification algorithm never sees it; the audit does
2341	   not independently re-verify the PDP's allow.  The completeness side
2342	   of the audit has an independent leg - the rail extract - and the
2343	   policy side deliberately does not: that is a trust boundary of this
2344	   profile, not an oversight.  A deployment that wants the policy answer
2345	   to be independently verifiable needs a receipt-to-token binding,
2346	   which this revision does not define; it is named as a possible
2347	   extension (Section 19), and adding it would change what a receipt
2348	   carries, so it is not smuggled in here.

2350	14.  SCITT Anchoring

2352	   A Receipt Issuer or relying party MAY construct a SCITT Signed
2353	   Statement whose payload is either the Spend Receipt COSE object or a
2354	   privacy profile (Section 15) and register it with a Transparency
2355	   Service [RFC9943].  The service returns a COSE receipt [RFC9942].
2356	   Embedding that receipt yields a Transparent Statement.  Cedulon does
2357	   not define a new transparency algorithm.

2359	   An epoch checkpoint MUST be registrable on the same terms (MUST-
2360	   T11-14).  Its Signed Statement carries the checkpoint COSE_Sign1
2361	   object as the payload and application/cedulon-checkpoint+cbor as the
2362	   content type, which is among the media types Section 17 asks to have
2363	   registered and which, until then, is a placeholder like the rest.
2364	   Nothing else about registration differs from a receipt.

2366	   This is a short section for a requirement -01 was missing, and the
2367	   omission mattered more than its length suggests. -01 asked for
2368	   checkpoints to be registered (SHOULD-T11-5) while profiling only the
2369	   receipt here, so the object carrying the suppression guarantee had no
2370	   stated form to be registered in.  Two implementations could follow
2371	   -01 to the letter and register incomparable things.

2373	15.  Privacy Considerations

2375	   A public transparency encoding MUST support omitting or hashing payer
2376	   and payee identifiers and MUST support amount redaction or bucket
2377	   encoding (MUST-T9-1).  Implementations MUST NOT write government-ID
2378	   numbers, payment-instrument PAN, or street address into a public
2379	   statement (MUST-T9-2).  Default public anchors SHOULD publish
2380	   policyHash, manifestHash, receiptHash, and timestampMs rather than
2381	   full claims (SHOULD-T9-3).  A private auditor MAY receive an
2382	   unredacted receipt out of band (MAY-T9-4).

2384	   The paragraph above counts receipt fields.  A checkpoint publishes
2385	   something a receipt does not: a per-currency total for a whole
2386	   window, which discloses trading volume even when every individual
2387	   receipt is redacted (MUST-T9-5). -01 gave no rule for it, so an
2388	   implementation could publish that total, or withhold it in a way no
2389	   verifier could recognise, and neither reading contradicted the text.

2391	   The rule is the one stated in Section 11: totals MAY be withheld by
2392	   signing it as null (MUST-T11-12), and only that form counts as a
2393	   redaction (MUST-T11-13).  The structural claims are not redactable,
2394	   because a verifier that cannot read the window or the chain head
2395	   cannot check anything at all, and a checkpoint that hid them would be
2396	   indistinguishable from a broken one.

2398	   Withholding is honest and it is also a cost: a verifier that cannot
2399	   recompute the totals says so, and the completeness guarantee for that
2400	   window is conditional.  A deployment that wants an unconditional
2401	   result publishes the totals; a deployment that wants the volume
2402	   private accepts a conditional one.  What a deployment MUST NOT do is
2403	   obtain the unconditional result while withholding the evidence for
2404	   it.

3237	16.14.  T12: Settlement without a recorded receipt

3239	   The threats above are about a counterparty, a rail or an attacker.
3240	   This one is about the issuer's own implementation, and it produces
3241	   exactly the condition the rest of this document exists to make
3242	   detectable.

3244	   An issuer that settles a payment, appends the receipt in memory and
3245	   then persists its state has three steps where it could have two
3246	   outcomes.  If the write fails, the rail holds a settlement and the
3247	   receipt exists nowhere durable.  The next start reads a state that
3248	   does not contain it, and the audit in Section 11 reports settlement-
3249	   without-receipt against an honest issuer that did everything its own
3250	   policy asked.  The evidence is missing because the issuer lost it,
3251	   not because anyone hid it, and nothing in the report can tell those
3252	   apart.

3259	     |            | to record MUST be established before value       |
3260	     |            | moves, not after.                                |

3262	     | MUST-T12-2 | Where a settlement has been made and its record  |
3263	     |            | cannot be completed, the issuer MUST undo the    |
3264	     |            | settlement in every place it still controls: in  |
3265	     |            | memory, on the rail ledger it controls, and in   |
3266	     |            | any later write it has not yet issued.  A        |
3267	     |            | refused payment MUST NOT consume the nonce or    |
3268	     |            | the payment allowance it never used.             |
3269	     +------------+--------------------------------------------------+
3270	     | MUST-T12-3 | Two issuers MUST NOT share one durable state.    |
3271	     |            | An implementation that permits it MUST fail      |
3272	     |            | loudly rather than let one writer overwrite the  |
3273	     |            | other's receipt, and the failure MUST name what  |
3274	     |            | an operator can act on.                          |
3275	     +------------+--------------------------------------------------+
3276	     | MUST-T12-4 | Where a settlement has entered a rail the issuer |
3277	     |            | does not control, and the local record cannot be |
3278	     |            | completed, the outcome is indeterminate.  The    |
3279	     |            | issuer MUST NOT treat the payment as reversed,   |
3280	     |            | and MUST NOT return the authority to spend,      |

3282	     |            | rail did not complete the settlement or that a   |
3283	     |            | reversing entry completed.                       |

3288	   Ordering:  Settle-then-record is the natural order to write and the
3289	      wrong one to ship.  The record is what makes the settlement
3290	      accountable, so the record is what has to be secured first (MUST-
3291	      T12-1), and a settlement that cannot be recorded has to be undone
3292	      everywhere the issuer still controls, including the nonce and the
3293	      allowance it never used (MUST-T12-2).  That undo is what the in-
3294	      process RailLedger and the session tests measure.  Once value has
3295	      entered a rail the issuer does not control, a local snapshot
3296	      cannot retract it.  Persistence failing after that point leaves
3297	      the outcome indeterminate; authority is not returned without
3298	      authenticated evidence that the rail did not complete the
3299	      settlement or that a reversing entry did (MUST-T12-4).

3301	   Recovery:  A durable-state conflict is not necessarily fatal, but it
3302	      MUST NOT be silent, and an implementation that refuses every
3303	      subsequent write without offering a way back has turned a
3304	      recoverable condition into an outage.  The reason reported has to
3305	      separate the cases an operator would act on differently (MUST-
3306	      T12-3): a write that conflicted with another writer, a write that
3307	      failed, and a state another process is holding.  A single opaque
3308	      failure leaves the operator to guess which of those happened.

3310	   Observability:  A violation of this threat is not visible in the
3311	      evidence a verifier receives.  The audit sees a settlement with no
3312	      receipt and reports settlement-without-receipt, which is the same
3313	      finding an adversary would produce, and nothing in the extract or
3314	      the receipt set distinguishes an issuer that lost the evidence
3315	      from one that hid it.  That is why the requirements here fall on
3316	      the issuer rather than on the verifier, and why an operator-facing
3317	      reason is required rather than optional.

4227	   says whose digest ap2MandateHash is.  The Decision Token gains the
4228	   same expiry boundary MUST-T3-3 states for the manifest.

4230	   Appendix A no longer defers to the tests of an implementation; a
4231	   specification that points at code cannot be implemented from its own
4232	   text, which is the property this revision is trying to restore.

4234	   One change is metadata rather than text: the submission stream in the
4235	   document's header changes from independent to IETF.  The Note to
4236	   Readers has named an eventual Standards Track intent since -00, and
4237	   Section 17 asks for Standards Tree registration; neither belongs on
4238	   the Independent Stream.  The rendered pages are unchanged by it.

4240	   What has not changed: MUST-T12-4 remains specified and not executed,
4241	   and this document still has no independent implementation written
4242	   from its text alone.  The point of this revision is to make that
4243	   possible, not to claim it happened.

4245	18.6.  Changes from -02

4247	   -03 had two subjects.  The first is that -02 stated a rule for signed
4248	   objects and left the implementation and the verification algorithm
4249	   without a counterpart for every object the rule applied to.  The
4250	   second is T12, which no reader reported and which is not about an
4251	   adversary at all.

4253	   -02 Section 6.2 already required a verifier to obtain the public key
4254	   from an authenticated channel (a preconfigured issuer set, a
4255	   directory, or a transparency statement) and to reject a message whose
4256	   kid does not match that key (MUST-T4-8).  That rule was general.
4257	   What -02 did not carry was the verification algorithm that applies it
4258	   to each signed object, the separate root inputs a verifier supplies
4259	   out of band, and the error semantics that name a missing, unreadable,
4260	   or mismatched pin.  A companion implementation that followed the
4261	   algorithm it had, rather than that prose, still checked a Spend
4262	   Receipt, an epoch checkpoint, a Decision Token and a transparency
4263	   inclusion receipt against the key travelling inside them.  A receipt
4264	   like that silences the settlement-without-receipt finding for the
4265	   settlement it names.  The completeness property is then computed over
4266	   evidence that answers to nobody.  Section 10 states the missing
4267	   algorithm and the error semantics, and the verification algorithm now
4268	   applies them.

4270	   A reader raised the inclusion-receipt half of this against the posted
4271	   -02 after checking the archived text against the implementation
4272	   commit.  Following it into the code turned up the other three, along
4273	   with two conditions that were not about keys at all.

4275	   The first is that reporting a mismatch is not enough.  An
4276	   implementation that names a foreign key and then still lets the
4277	   receipt match its settlement has described the attack in its output
4278	   while concluding that the books balance.  MUST-T4-10 now requires the
4279	   settlement to stay reported.

4281	   The second is that an expectation which only fires when the evidence
4282	   is present can be cancelled by deleting the evidence.  A pinned payee
4283	   key with no countersignature to check was silence, so removing a
4284	   countersignature removed the question with it.  MUST-T4-14 closes
4285	   that, and MUST-T11-17 closes the same shape in the witness: an
4286	   inclusion receipt with its body stripped off can no longer bury a
4287	   withholding, while still not being allowed to accuse anyone.

4289	   T12 is new and is not about an adversary.  An issuer that settles,
4290	   appends the receipt in memory and then writes its state will, when
4291	   that write fails, leave the rail holding a settlement whose receipt
4292	   exists nowhere durable.  Restarted, it reports settlement-without-
4293	   receipt against itself.  The condition this document exists to make
4294	   detectable was reachable through the implementation's own ordering,
4295	   and no requirement in -02 said otherwise.

4297	   The same first subject carries five requirements this section has not
4298	   named so far, all of them stated in Section 10.  A pinned key that
4299	   cannot be decoded MUST NOT fall back to the keys the objects carry
4300	   (MUST-T4-11).  An issuer root may comprise more than one key, and a
4301	   verifier must accept one that does, so a rotation inside the audited
4302	   window does not force it to choose between findings against honest
4303	   receipts and abandoning the pin (MUST-T4-12); the same set-of-keys
4304	   acceptance applies to a publisher, witness, or rail pin.  The same
4305	   out-of-band rule reaches the payee countersignature (MUST-T4-13), the
4306	   transparency witness (MUST-T11-15 and MUST-T11-16), the Decision
4307	   Token, whose consumer issued it and therefore already holds the key
4308	   to check it with (MUST-T6-6), and a presented Trade Manifest (MUST-
4309	   T4-15).

4311	   Two requirements belong to neither subject.  MUST-T7-5 and MUST-T7-6
4312	   come from measuring the protection a stored signing key actually has
4313	   instead of deriving it from the platform.  A mount that ignores
4314	   filesystem permissions accepts the call and protects nothing, and a
4315	   writable directory or a symbolic link anywhere on the path makes the
4316	   file permission moot.  They are stated because the implementation
4317	   reported protection it did not have.

4319	   Two things are stated here that -02 got right and -03 kept unchanged:
4320	   the extract rule itself, and the treatment of a pinned key the
4321	   verifier cannot decode.  What changed is their reach, and the change
4322	   is not backward compatible.  Where a verifier pinned the rail key,
4323	   supplied no issuer key, and was presented with receipts or
4324	   checkpoints, -02 reported the guarantee as unconditional and -03
4325	   reports it as conditional.  Nothing about the evidence changed; what
4326	   changed is that the guarantee now says which questions were never
4327	   asked.  An audit presented with neither is unaffected, for the reason
4328	   given in Section 10.1.

4330	18.7.  Changes from -01

4332	   -02 had one subject: the checkpoint, which carries the T11 guarantee
4333	   against suppression and rollback, was not wired into anything that
4334	   could discharge it.

4336	   A reader of -01 set out the gap and a second reader confirmed it
4337	   independently.  Four things were wrong at once, and they were the
4338	   same thing seen from four sides.  The SCITT anchoring section
4339	   profiled the Spend Receipt and not the checkpoint, so SHOULD-T11-5
4340	   asked for checkpoints to be registered without saying in what form
4341	   (MUST-T11-14 now says).  None of the steps of the verification
4342	   algorithm read a witness receipt, so a deployment could follow
4343	   SHOULD-T11-5 to the letter and still have a verifier that never
4344	   consulted the witness (steps 15 and 16 now do).  MUST-T11-3,
4345	   equivocation, could not fire at all: the only checkpoints compared
4346	   were the presented ones, and MUST-T11-8 requires those to be
4347	   consecutive, so no two of them can share an epoch.  A second copy is
4348	   found in a witness, and nothing brought one in.  And the checkpoint
4349	   binds a per-currency total for a window while the privacy section
4350	   counted only receipt fields, so a window total could be published, or
4351	   withheld, with no stated rule either way.

4353	   The repairs are MUST-T11-10 through MUST-T11-14.  The transparency
4354	   receipt is a new optional input; supplying none leaves the verifier
4355	   behaving exactly as in -01, which is deliberate, because the point of
4356	   the witness is to add a claim that could not be made before, not to
4357	   withdraw one that could.

4359	   Two decisions inside those repairs are worth stating on their own,
4360	   because a reader might reasonably have expected the other choice.

4362	   *  A recorded checkpoint the presented chain omits gets its own
4363	      identifier, checkpoint-withheld, rather than being folded into
4364	      window-coverage (MUST-T11-11).  The reporter asked which way it
4365	      should go.  Coverage says the record shown is incomplete; a
4366	      withheld checkpoint says the party under audit is holding a record
4367	      it did not show.  An operator who sees one identifier for both
4368	      cannot tell an incomplete record from a concealed one, which is
4369	      the distinction the whole threat is about.

4371	   *  A checkpoint with no receipt in a supplied witness is a warning
4372	      rather than a failure.  A witness may be configured after
4373	      checkpoints have already been issued, and an operator's own gap in
4374	      anchoring is not evidence that anything was concealed.  The
4375	      asymmetry is deliberate: what the witness holds and the chain does
4376	      not is a finding, what the chain holds and the witness does not is
4377	      a warning.

4379	   One change is not a repair of -01 but of the first attempt at this
4380	   revision, and it is recorded because the failure is instructive.
4381	   Signed totals were first made redactable through a field carried
4382	   alongside the checkpoint rather than inside the COSE payload.
4383	   Anything outside the signature is chosen by whoever presents the
4384	   object, which here is the party under audit, so a checkpoint whose
4385	   signed totals disagreed with its receipts could be re-presented as
4386	   redacted and the mismatch went unreported.  Redaction is now inside
4387	   the signature and a redaction asserted anywhere else MUST be ignored
4388	   (MUST-T11-12, MUST-T11-13).  This was the same shape as the bypass
4389	   -01 was written to close: a check that a party under audit could
4390	   switch off.

4392	   Reversal, refund, partial settlement, and the escrow role remain out
4393	   of scope and are still expected later, with no date. -01 said the
4394	   same, and no revision since has improved on it.

4396	   The reporters are named in the Acknowledgments.

4398	19.  Evolution and Future Work (Informative)

4400	   This section is a direction, not a commitment.  The structures below
4401	   are reserved in name only.  Normative wire formats, tests, and
4402	   threat-model MUST lines for them belong in later revisions (-09 or
4403	   later), written with the same discipline as this -08.

4405	19.1.  Re-attestation profile

4407	   Algorithms retire.  A Spend Receipt or checkpoint signed under
4408	   Ed25519 today may need a later verifier that no longer accepts -19.
4409	   A companion seed [REATTEST] sketches re-attestation: register the
4410	   original COSE bytes as a SCITT Signed Statement and have a current
4411	   algorithm countersign or receipt them.  The principle is that
4412	   structures outlive ciphers.  The first concrete example is the
4413	   profile's own move from generic EdDSA (-8) to Ed25519 (-19) in
4414	   [RFC9864].

4416	19.2.  Streaming reconciliation

4418	   Epoch checkpoints in this document are batch windows.  A later
4419	   revision may define a continuous, second-scale profile [STREAMING] in
4420	   which the same completeness relation is evaluated as settlements
4421	   arrive, without waiting for an epoch close.  That work does not
4422	   change the matching rules in this document, and it did not arrive in
4423	   this revision either.

4425	19.3.  Generalization

4427	   Payment is the special case that this -08 implements.  The same
4428	   completeness calculus (an authenticated extract of consumed units
4429	   reconciled to signed receipts) can apply to other consumable
4430	   resources such as compute, data, or energy.  This document does not
4431	   specify those profiles.

4433	20.  Informative Notes on Adjacent Protocols

4435	   x402 [X402] uses HTTP 402 [RFC9110] to negotiate stablecoin payment.
4436	   AP2 [AP2] uses signed mandates as verifiable credentials.  Cedulon
4437	   does not replace either protocol.  Profiles built on HTTP Message
4438	   Signatures [RFC9421] authenticate bots; they are not a spend receipt.
4439	   draft-bates-atp [BATES-ATP] is a lineage neighbor.  It does not
4440	   define rail-extract completeness.

4442	   draft-vauban-x402-stark-receipts [VAUBAN] specifies complementary
4443	   x402 receipt-format variants that a Cedulon Spend Receipt MAY carry
4444	   as a rail proof; it does not define rail-extract completeness. draft-
4445	   schrock-ep-outcome-binding [SCHROCK] compares authorized action bytes
4446	   to independently observed effects; it does not define rail-extract
4447	   completeness. draft-marques-asqav-compliance-receipts [MARQUES]
4448	   profiles access-control action receipts (the broader Acta family
4449	   includes [ACTA]); it does not define rail-extract completeness.
4450	   draft-hopley-x402-compliance-receipt [HOPLEY] records an admission-
4451	   time compliance decision; it does not define rail-extract
4452	   completeness. draft-abak-agent-control-delivery-evidence [ABAK]
4453	   states evidence requirements for a governance control - stop,
4454	   suspend, revoke - travelling toward the component expected to
4455	   constrain a runtime, and keeps emission, receiver-side observation,
4456	   enforcement outcome and observed control effect as separate results.
4457	   Its object moves the other way from this one: Cedulon reconciles a
4458	   spend that already happened against what the rail reported, and
4459	   evidences neither the delivery of a control instruction nor its
4460	   enforcement.  A denied spend leaves no portable artifact here at all
4461	   - a Decision Token encodes an allow (Section 8) - so what a Cedulon
4462	   audit says about a refusal it says through the settlement that did
4463	   not appear on the extract, which is an effect observation over a
4464	   declared population and not an acknowledgement from an enforcement
4465	   point.  Its bounded-population rule and this document's MUST-T10-18
4466	   and MUST-T10-19 are the same kind of bound on two different objects.
